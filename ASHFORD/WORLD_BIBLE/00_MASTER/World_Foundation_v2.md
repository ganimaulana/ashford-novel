## CRITICAL PRINCIPLE

**This is 1816. England. Regency Era.**

The Napoleonic Wars ended in 1815. Europe is rebuilding. Britain is the world's naval and colonial power, but domestically:
- Poverty is rampant in cities
- Class structure is rigid but shifting
- Industrial Revolution is transforming the countryside
- No electricity, no internal combustion, no telegraph
- Information travels by horse, foot, or ship
- A letter from London to a provincial town takes 1–3 days

**Ashford is NOT a fantasy town.** It is a small English market town that could exist. The supernatural world is a hidden layer beneath ordinary life.

## 1. TECHNOLOGY & INFRASTRUCTURE — WHAT EXISTS in 1816

### Lighting
- **Candles** (tallow or beeswax) — primary home lighting
- **Lamp oil** (whale oil, colza oil) — street lamps in prosperous areas
- **Fireplaces** — heat AND cooking, essential in every room
- **No electricity** — no switches, no sockets, no bulbs

### Heating
- **Fireplaces** — every major room has one
- **Stove** (cast iron cookstove) — in kitchen
- **No central heating** — people move between rooms to seek warmth
- **Chamber pots** — night toilets (emptying is someone's job)

### Transportation
- **Walking** — primary movement for everyone except wealthy
- **Horse-drawn carriages** — personal transport for wealthy
- **Diligence** (stagecoach) — public inter-town transport
- **Hansom cab / hackney carriage** — town horse carriage for hire
- **Horse-drawn wagons** — goods and freight
- **Carts** — farm and local delivery
- **Horses/mules** — personal riding
- **No railways** (first passenger railway: 1825, Stockton & Darlington)
- **No automobiles** (first: late 1800s)
- **Bicycles** (dandy horse) — extremely rare, not yet in common use

### Communication
- **Letter** — primary written communication
- **Post** — Royal Mail; letters collected at post offices (NO street pillar boxes — those arrive 1852)
- **Newspapers** — published weekly or daily in larger cities; arrive by coach
- **Telegraph** — does NOT exist yet (first practical: 1837)
- **Telephone** — does NOT exist
- **Personal visit** — most important form of communication
- **Face-to-face** — gossip travels fast within towns
- **Handwritten notes** — between people in same town

### Information & Research
- **Libraries** — subscription or private; limited collections
- **Bookshops** — sell new books, occasionally secondhand
- **Newspapers and pamphlets** — key source of current events
- **Church records** — baptism, marriage, burial (the principal vital records system)
- **Solicitor/Notary records** — deeds, wills, property transfers
- **Parish registers** — local government records
- **Gazetteers** — directories of towns and inhabitants
- **Heraldic visitations** — family genealogy records
- **Card catalogs / index books** — library research systems
- **No digital search** — Leon must PHYSICALLY visit each location

### Medicine
- **No germ theory** (Semmelweis: 1847, Pasteur: 1860s)
- **No anesthesia** (ether: 1846)
- **Bloodletting** — common treatment
- **Herbal remedies** — still widely used
- **Quack doctors** — many charlatans
- **Surgeons** — separate from physicians; performed operations
- **Apothecaries** — prepare and dispense medicines

### Law & Order
- **No professional police force** (Metropolitan Police: 1829 — not yet)
- **Constables** — unpaid parish officers; part-time, often reluctant
- **Watchmen** — night patrol, often elderly or infirm
- **Magistrates** — justices of the peace; handle minor cases
- **Sheriff** — county-level authority
- **Prisons** — local jails exist but are rudimentary
- **Transportation of convicts** — to Australia (not yet repealed)

### Economy & Society
- **Cash-based** — coins (pounds, shillings, pence) and banknotes
- **Credit** — important for shopkeepers; accounts kept in ledgers
- **Class structure:**
  - Aristocracy / Gentry (landed wealth)
  - Professional class (doctors, lawyers, clergy, merchants)
  - Artisans and shopkeepers
  - Laborers and servants
  - Poor and paupers (parish relief)

### Daily Life
- **Morning routine** — rising with dawn; fire lit first
- **Meals** — breakfast (early), dinner (midday, main meal), supper (evening)
- **Clothing** — changing is a ritual; Empire dresses for women, coats/waistcoats for men
- **Servants** — middle-class households typically have at least one servant
- **Religion** — Church of England dominant; nonconformist denominations exist
- **Entertainment** — theaters, assemblies, card games, reading, music at home
- **Writing instruments** — quill pens, ink, paper
- **Sealing wax** — for letters
- **Matchboxes** — do NOT exist yet (friction matches: 1826); use flint & steel or candle from fireplace

## 2. TRANSPORTATION & COMMUNICATION

### Inter-town Transport
- **Diligence (Stagecoach)** — fixed routes, fixed schedules, stops at inns
  - Speed: 6-8 mph; mail travels by diligence
- **Private coach** — wealthy families
- **Horse post** — faster mail, hired horses at posting houses

### Within Ashford
- **Walking** — everything within reach in a small town
- **Diligence stop** — one location for passengers and mail
- **Horse-drawn carriages** — for the wealthy
- **Market carts / freight wagons** — goods delivery

### Postal System
- **Royal Mail** — organized network; Ashford has a Post Office
- Letters posted at **originating office**, collected at destination
- **Postage paid by recipient** (prepaid stamps: 1840 — not yet)
- **Door-to-door delivery** for known addresses

### Story Implications
- News from London arrives 2-3 days after publication
- A letter to London takes overnight or next day
- Leon CANNOT call anyone in another town
- Anonymity is easy — stranger by diligence is unknown
- Misaddressed letters arriving in Ashford are plausible
- Mail arrives with the diligence coach — not daily

## 3. INFORMATION SYSTEMS — HOW LEON INVESTIGATES

### A. People (Information Sources)

| Person | Knowledge Type | Access Level |
|--------|---------------|--------------|
| **Margaret** (innkeeper) | Gossip, social knowledge, local secrets | High — hub of social exchange |
| **Daniel** (teacher) | Historical knowledge, academic networks | High for academic/historical |
| **Arthur** (antiquarian) | Antiquities, old objects, local lore | High for material culture |
| **Vicar** | Parish records, family histories | High for baptism/marriage/burial |
| **Constable** | Local incidents, suspicious persons | Moderate — limited records |
| **Physician** | Medical cases, deaths | Selective |
| **Librarian** | Index of collections, where to find documents | High for library holdings |
| **Solicitor** | Deeds, wills, property transfers | High for property history |

### B. Documents

| Document | Location | Information |
|----------|----------|-------------|
| Parish registers | Church / Vicar | Births, marriages, deaths |
| Wills & probate | Solicitor / Church court | Inheritances, relationships |
| Property deeds | Solicitor | Who owned what, transfers |
| Newspapers | Library / vendor | Events, obituaries, sales |
| Directories | Library / bookshop | Who lives where |
| Maps | Library / surveyor | Historical boundaries |
| Academy records | Academy | Who studied/taught where |
| Genealogies | Family collections / library | Lineage, family trees |

### C. Leon's Investigation Method
1. Start with what he knows — the Pembroke & Clarke letter
2. Ask people — Margaret's gossip, Daniel's knowledge, Arthur's contacts
3. Visit places — library catalog, church records, solicitor's archives
4. Cross-reference — match names, dates, addresses across sources
5. Observe physical evidence — architecture, objects, handwriting
6. Follow leads — one name leads to another

### CRITICAL: INVESTIGATION IS SLOW
- No Google, no phone calls, no instant confirmation
- Must PHYSICALLY visit each location
- Must wait for mail
- Builds natural pacing and tension

## 4. AGENCY & AUTHORITY STRUCTURES

### Law Enforcement (Pre-Police Era)
- **NO professional police** (Metropolitan Police: 1829 — not yet)
- **Parish Constables** — 2 per parish, elected annually, unpaid, part-time
- **Night Watchmen** — patrol after dark, often drunk or asleep
- **Magistrates (JPs)** — local landowners; issue warrants, commit to jail

### Story Implications
- Crime underreported, poorly documented
- Leon can circumvent authority easily
- A constable is a local farmer, not a threat
- Missing persons or old cases leave no record
- Documents can be "lost" with no one investigating

### Class Hierarchy
`
Aristocracy / Gentry (Manor family)
    │
Professional Class (doctors, solicitors, vicar, academy)
    │
Merchants & Shopkeepers (Bell, Margaret, Arthur)
    │
Artisans & Tradespeople
    │
Laborers & Servants
    │
Poor & Paupers
`

### Key Social Facts
- **First impressions matter** — dress, speech, carriage
- **Name recognition** — family reputation carries weight
- **Strangers are notable** — arriving by diligence draws attention
- **Servants know everything** — overhear conversations, see documents
- **The Church is central** — Sunday service mandatory
- **Leon's name "Ashcroft" triggers reactions and access**
- **His lack of visible wealth limits some access**
