## CRITICAL PRINCIPLE

**2006 ≠ 2026**

This is 20 years ago. Technology existed but worked differently.

**Common mistakes to avoid:**
- Smartphones (didn\'t exist yet)
- Social media as we know it (Facebook just started, UK limited)
- Apps (not a thing yet)
- Ubiquitous internet (expensive, not everywhere)
- Cloud storage (not common)
- Streaming services (Netflix was DVD-by-mail)

## MOBILE PHONES

### What Existed in 2006

**Feature phones** (NOT smartphones)

**Popular models in UK:**
- Nokia 6230
- Nokia 8800
- Motorola RAZR V3
- Sony Ericsson W810i (Walkman phone)
- Samsung D600

**Capabilities:**
- Voice calls ✅
- SMS (text messages) ✅
- Basic games (Snake, etc.) ✅
- Camera (low quality, 1-2MP) ✅ (not all phones)
- Polyphonic ringtones ✅
- WAP internet (terrible) ✅
- Bluetooth (some models) ✅
- MP3 player (some models) ✅

**NO smartphones:**
- iPhone launches June 2007 (not yet)
- Android doesn\'t exist yet
- No touchscreens (except stylus PDAs)
- No app stores
- No mobile internet as we know it

### How People Used Phones

**Texts (SMS):**
- 160 character limit
- Cost money per message (5-10p each)
- T9 predictive text (multi-tap keypad)
- No emojis (maybe :) emoticons)
- No read receipts
- No group chats (chain forward messages)

**Calls:**
- Pay-as-you-go credit or contract
- Expensive to call mobiles
- Landline cheaper
- No FaceTime/video calls
- Voicemail costs money to check

**Limitations:**
- Battery lasted 2-3 days (better than now!)
- No GPS in most phones
- No navigation
- Photos terrible quality
- No instant messaging apps

**Writing scenes dengan phones:**
- Leon would text short messages
- Costs limit frequency
- Calling still primary
- Checking phone = reading texts, not browsing

## INTERNET

### Connection Types

**Home Internet 2006:**

**Dial-up** (still common, especially outside cities):
- 56k modem
- Ties up phone line
- Slow (image loading visible)
- Pay per minute or monthly
- Disconnects easily

**Broadband** (growing but not universal):
- ADSL over phone line
- 512kbps - 8Mbps (slow by today\'s standards)
- "Always on" connection
- More expensive
- Not available everywhere

**University/Work:**
- Faster connections
- Shared networks
- Limited personal use

**Internet Cafes/Warnet:**
- Pay per hour
- Public computers
- Used for job hunting, research, gaming
- Some people don\'t have home internet

### What People Did Online

**Common activities:**
- Email (primary communication)
- Google search
- BBC News website
- eBay
- Amazon (books, CDs, DVDs)
- Forums
- MSN Messenger
- MySpace (not huge in UK)
- Downloading music (often illegally)
- Online banking (growing)

**What DIDN\'T exist or wasn\'t common:**
- Social media (Facebook US colleges only, no Twitter)
- YouTube (launched 2005, still new)
- Wikipedia (existed but less trusted)
- Streaming video (bandwidth too low)
- Cloud storage (Dropbox 2007)
- Gmail (existed but invite-only until 2007)
- Google Maps (launched 2005, basic)
- Smartphones accessing internet

### Websites in 2006

**Design:**
- Flash animations common
- Under construction GIFs
- Forums with avatars
- Guestbooks
- Hit counters
- Frames (ugh)
- Tables for layout
- Low-res images

**Speed:**
- Pages load gradually
- Images load line by line
- Click and wait
- Buffering for any video

## COMPUTERS

### Typical Specs 2006

**Desktop PC:**
- Pentium 4 or AMD Athlon
- 512MB - 2GB RAM
- 80-250GB hard drive
- CRT monitor (heavy, deep) or early LCD
- Windows XP (most common)
- No webcam (separate purchase)

**Laptop:**
- Heavier (2-3kg typical)
- Shorter battery life (2-3 hours)
- More expensive than desktop
- WiFi built-in (new feature!)
- Often no webcam

### Operating Systems

**Windows XP** (dominant):
- Released 2001, still going strong
- Vista launches 2007 (not yet)
- Blue screen of death still common

**Mac OS X** (niche):
- Tiger or early Leopard
- Only on Apple hardware
- Expensive
- Less common in UK

**Linux:**
- Geeks only
- Ubuntu exists but niche

### Software

**Common programs:**
- Microsoft Office (Word, Excel, PowerPoint)
- Internet Explorer (dominant browser)
- Firefox (growing alternative)
- Outlook Express (email)
- Windows Media Player
- iTunes (for iPods)
- Photoshop (expensive)
- Antivirus (Norton, McAfee)

**No:**
- Chrome (launches 2008)
- Modern browsers
- Cloud-based office (Google Docs beta 2006)
- Slack, Discord, Zoom

## DIGITAL PHOTOGRAPHY

### Cameras

**Digital cameras:**
- 3-8 megapixel typical
- Memory cards (SD, CompactFlash)
- LCD screen on back
- No live view
- USB cable to computer
- Battery eats charge

**Phone cameras:**
- 1-2MP if present
- Terrible quality
- No flash usually
- Not for serious photos

**Film still used:**
- 35mm film cameras still common
- Disposable cameras at events
- Processing at shops (1 hour photo)
- Professional photographers use film

### Photo Storage

**Where photos lived:**
- Computer hard drive
- CDs/DVDs burned
- Memory cards
- Physical prints
- Maybe emailed (small size)

**No cloud storage:**
- Flickr existed (limited free)
- No Google Photos
- No iCloud
- No automatic backup

- Yahoo Mail

**MSN Messenger** (dominant UK):

- Emoticons

- Snapchat (2011)

## MONEY & SHOPPING

### Banking

**ATM (Cash machines):**
- Primary way to get cash
- Check balance on screen
- Some charge fees
- Receipts

**Cards:**
- Debit cards common
- Credit cards
- Chip & PIN (introduced 2003-2006, rolling out)
- Signature still sometimes

**Online banking:**
- Existed but not universal
- Separate security device (some banks)
- Password + memorable information
- Check balance, transfer money
- Not on mobile

**Cash:**
- Still very common
- Many places cash-only atau prefer cash
- Carrying cash normal

**No:**
- Apple Pay / Google Pay
- Contactless (very rare)
- Mobile banking apps
- Venmo / PayPal common (PayPal existed but not mainstream)

### Shopping

**How people shopped:**
- Physical shops (majority)
- High Street stores
- Supermarkets
- Amazon (books, CDs, DVDs primarily)
- eBay (auctions)
- Catalogues (Argos, etc.)

**Online shopping:**
- Growing but not dominant
- Slower delivery (3-5 days normal)
- More expensive delivery
- Less trust
- Returns harder

**No:**
- Next-day delivery as standard
- Amazon everything
- Online grocery dominant
- Mobile shopping
