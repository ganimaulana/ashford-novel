# CRIMINAL UNDERWORLD — Alderwick

## Status

**Criminal network documentation** for Alderwick.
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-31
**Canon Status:** APPROVED

---

## Core Principle

> **Crime in Alderwick is not separate from politics. It is politics by other means.**

Criminal networks serve political factions. Political factions protect criminals. The line between them is deliberately blurred.

## I. Criminal Organizations

**Type:** Smuggling / Black Market
**Size:** 20–30 core members, 50+ associates

### B. The Hollow Men

**Type:** Forgery / Stolen Records / Identity Manipulation
**Size:** 5–10 core members, various specialists
**Territory:** Blackmere primarily, Alderwick operations
**Leader:** Unknown — they operate as a cell structure

#### Operations
- Document forgery: letters, certificates, receipts
- Record manipulation: parish registers, estate records, court documents
- Identity creation: dead infants, missing persons, travelers
- Information brokerage: selling secrets, blackmail material

#### Structure
```
Leader (Unknown)
        │
   ┌────┼────┐
   │    │    │
Forgery Records Identity
Specialists Theft Creation
   │    │    │
   └────┼────┘
        │
   Safe Houses
   (various locations)
```

#### Methods
1. Identify target document
2. Acquire access (theft, bribery, or infiltration)
3. Copy or alter document
4. Return original (if stolen)
5. Sell forged document or information

#### Relationships

| Radical Movement | Sell forged pamphlets, create false identities |
| Government | Some officials unknowingly use forged documents |

### C. Local Gangs

**Type:** Street Crime / Debt Collection / Theft
**Size:** Various, 5–15 members each
**Territory:** Specific neighborhoods in Alderwick
**Leaders:** Known locally, unknown to authorities (or bribed)

- **Territory:** Bellweather Lane district
- **Operations:** Pickpocketing, petty theft, protection rackets
- **Leader:** "Young Tom" (Thomas Graves, age 19)
- **Size:** 8–12 members
- **Age range:** 14–22

##### The Dock Rats
- **Territory:** Port district, warehouses
- **Operations:** Cargo theft, warehouse break-ins, crew disputes
- **Leader:** "One-Eyed Marcus" (Marcus Webb, age 34)
- **Size:** 10–15 members
- **Age range:** 20–40

##### The Mill Street Crew
- **Territory:** Mill Street, working-class district
- **Operations:** Debt collection, intimidation, gambling
- **Leader:** "Big Ned" (Edward Caulfield, age 28)

## II. Political Crime

### Definition
Crimes committed not for money, but for political advantage.

### Methods

| Method | Purpose | Who Uses It |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| Fake letters | Discredit opponents | All factions |
| Fabricated evidence | Frame radicals | Government |
| Sabotage | Disrupt meetings | Paid criminals |
| Informant networks | Monitor opponents | Government |
| Blackmail | Control officials | All factions |
| Document theft | Expose secrets | Reform Coalition |
| Archive destruction | Hide history | Unknown |
| Rumor campaigns | Shape public opinion | All factions |
| Witness payments | Secure testimony | Wealthy factions |
| Political framing | Blame opponents | All factions |

### Examples

#### Case 1: The Warehouse Fire
- **Official story:** Radical violence
- **Popular belief:** Government provocation to justify repression
- **Hidden truth:** Arson by unknown party for insurance fraud
- **Deeper truth:** The warehouse belonged to a merchant funding both sides

#### Case 2: The Stolen Parish Records
- **Official story:** Unknown thieves
- **Popular belief:** Church covering up scandal
- **Hidden truth:** Hollow Men stole records to create false identities
- **Deeper truth:** Someone needed to erase a bloodline

## V. Criminal Code

### Unwritten Rules

1. **Never involve children** (except Bellweather Boys, who recruit young)
2. **Never steal from the Church** (unless commissioned)
3. **Never kill without authorization** (unless self-defense)
4. **Never inform on associates** (punishable by death)
5. **Always pay your debts** (to anyone)
6. **Never work for free** (except ideological radicals)
7. **Keep records minimal** (for deniability)
8. **Never trust the authorities** (even bribed ones)

### Enforcement
- Violations handled by organization leaders
- Severe violations: exile or death
- Disputes between organizations: negotiated by "The Receiver" or neutral party

## VI. Law Enforcement Response

### Official Response

| Crime Type | Response | Effectiveness |
|------------|----------|---------------|
| Petty theft | Constables patrol | Low (understaffed) |
| Smuggling | Customs officials | Low (bribery) |
| Forgery | Magistrate investigation | Very Low (specialized) |
| Political crime | Government agents | High (but selective) |
| Violence | Constables + militia | Medium |

### Why Crime Persists

1. **Understaffed constables** — not enough for full coverage
2. **Bribery** — many officials are paid to look away
3. **Political protection** — powerful clients shield criminals
4. **Public sympathy** — some criminals are seen as Robin Hood figures
5. **Jurisdictional gaps** — unclear who handles what
6. **Corruption** — some officials are criminals themselves

### What Criminal Networks Provide

| Element | How Criminals Contribute |
|---------|-------------------------|

| Hidden alliances | Criminal-political connections |
| Suppressed information | Stolen documents, buried secrets |
| Access to locations | Safe houses, hidden routes |
| False leads | Framing, planted evidence |
| Genuine clues | Things criminals don't understand |
| Atmosphere | Fear, danger, moral ambiguity |

### What Criminals Don't Know

- They are often pawns in larger games
- Their clients may be using them for unknown purposes
- Some documents they steal are more valuable than they realize
- Some identities they create have hidden significance
- Some crimes they commit are connected to the central mystery
