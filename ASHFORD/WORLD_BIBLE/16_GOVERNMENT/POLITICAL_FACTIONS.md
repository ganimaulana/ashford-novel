# POLITICAL FACTIONS

**Detailed descriptions** of Ashford's political factions.
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-31

## Core Principle

> **No faction is purely good or evil.**

Each faction has:
- Valid reasons
- Blind spots
- Hypocrisies
- Genuine convictions

---

## I. Crown Conservatives

### Historical Analogue
Tory Party

### Core Values
- Crown authority
- Social order
- Church of the Crown
- Property rights
- Hierarchy
- Resistance to revolutionary politics

### Base
- Aristocracy
- Landed gentry
- Church of the Crown establishment
- Royal officials
- Landowners
- Conservative groups fearful of revolution

### Ideology
> "Order before change."

They believe rapid social change produces chaos like the revolutions in the Varellian Empire.

### Sub-Factions

#### A. Conservative Establishment
"The Crown, Church and Order"

**Supporters:** Aristocrats, magistrates, certain clergy, landowners, senior officials

**Belief:** Change must come from above, not from the streets.

**Core belief:**
> "Without the landed class, Albion falls into chaos."

**Valid concern:**
Hungry, angry masses can become dangerous mobs.

**Blind spot:**
They have never actually lived like the people they claim to protect.

**Potential abuse:**
Using "order" to silence criticism, protecting privilege, limiting freedoms.

---

#### B. Moderate Conservatives

**Pragmatic.** Recognize that without improving conditions, revolution becomes likely.

**Concerns:**
- Poverty
- Veteran welfare
- Economic instability
- Limited reform

**Role:** Bridge between government establishment and reform opposition.

**Key difference from hardliners:**
They disagree with extreme repression and recognize some reform is necessary.

---

### Fears
- Radical politics
- Republicanism
- Popular unrest
- Trade unions/combination
- Food riots
- Mass demonstrations
- Criticism of Crown

### Narrative Function
- Provide establishment perspective
- Create institutional obstacles for reform
- Represent genuine concern for stability
- Can be antagonists without being villains

---

## II. Reform Coalition

### Historical Analogue
Whig Party

### Core Values
- Parliamentary reform
- Civil liberties
- Administrative reform
- Opposition to excessive executive authority

### Base
- Segment of aristocracy
- Reform-minded gentlemen
- Intellectuals
- Certain merchants
- Groups seeking parliamentary reform
- Urban populations

### Ideology
> "Liberty through reform."

Not all revolutionaries.
Most Reform Coalition members remain aristocrats not seeking modern democracy.

### Goals
- Parliamentary reform
- Corruption limitation
- Civil liberties
- Administrative reform
- Power abuse reduction

### Sub-Factions

#### A. Moderate Reformers
Seek parliamentary and administrative reform.

Do not want revolution.

Believe system can be fixed from within.

**Core belief:**
> "Law must protect ordinary people from arbitrary power."

**Valid concern:**
Government is corrupt, system needs reform.

**Potential flaw:**
Can be opportunistic, using reform for political gain.

---

#### B. Progressive Reformers
More ambitious reform agenda.

Support broader civil liberties and administrative transparency.

May sympathize with some radical goals but reject radical methods.

---

### Narrative Function
- Provide opposition perspective
- Create political tension with establishment
- Represent genuine desire for change
- Can be allies or obstacles depending on circumstances

---

## III. Radical Movement

### Historical Analogue
British Radical movement

### Core Values
- Broader political representation
- Parliamentary reform
- Political liberty
- Reduction of aristocratic privilege

### Base
- Pamphleteers
- Printers
- Workers
- Veterans
- Radical intellectuals
- Clandestine political groups

### Ideology
> "The system is broken. Reform is not enough."

Believe Albion's political system is fundamentally broken.

### Demands
- Broader political representation
- Parliamentary reform
- Political liberty
- Reduction of aristocratic privilege

### Sub-Factions

#### A. Peaceful Radicals
Advocate for reform through pamphlets, meetings, and political pressure.

**Core belief:**
> "Those who fight for this country should not have to beg to live in it."

**Methods:** Legal (but controversial) political action.

---

#### B. Militant Radicals
Believe peaceful methods are insufficient.

May be willing to use illegal methods.

**Methods:** Clandestine meetings, illegal pamphlets, potential violence.

**Government perception:** Dangerous extremists.

**Reality:** Some are genuine reformers, others may have hidden agendas.

---

### Internal Divisions
- Some radicals are peaceful reformers
- Others may be willing to use illegal methods
- Some may be connected to underground networks
- Others may be using radicalism for purposes unrelated to reform

### Narrative Function
- Create underground political tension
- Provide mystery elements (hidden agendas, secret connections)
- Represent desperation of the poor
- Can be allies, obstacles, or red herrings

---

## IV. Church of the Crown

### Role in Politics
Not officially a political party.

But many clergy are socially close to Crown Conservative establishment.

### Political Influence
- Social legitimacy for the Crown
- Moral authority over population
- Relationship with magistrates
- Charitable functions (poor relief, education)

### Internal Divisions

#### A. Conservative Clergy
Close to Crown Conservative establishment.

**Belief:** Church must support social order.

**Political alignment:** Crown Conservative establishment.

---

#### B. Reform-Minded Clergy
Support:
- Poverty relief
- Temperance
- Education
- Charity
- Social reform

**Belief:** Church must care for the poor.

**Political alignment:** Moderate reform.

---

### Thematic Question
> Does the church maintain order because of faith — or because it is too close to power?

### Important Note
The Church of the Crown's theology is controlled by the Religion Bible.

Government documents describe the political relationship, not redefine theology.

## VI. Faction Relationships

### Power Dynamics

```
Crown Conservatives ←——→ Reform Coalition
    ↑                          ↑
    |                          |
    ↓                          ↓
Established Order ←——→ Radical Underground
```

### Alliances and Tensions

**Crown Conservatives vs Reform Coalition:**
- Parliamentary opposition
- Tension between order and reform
- Occasional cooperation on moderate issues

**Crown Conservatives vs Radical Underground:**
- Government repression of radicals
- Radicals seen as existential threat
- Potential for future confrontation

**Reform Coalition vs Radical Underground:**
- Reformers fear radicalism discredits their cause
- Radicals see reformers as insufficient
- Occasional shared goals, different methods

**Moderate Conservatives vs Hardliners:**
- Internal Conservative tension
- Moderates may support limited reform
- Hardliners resist all change

## VII. Narrative Guidelines

### 1. No Moral Binary
Every faction has valid reasons and blind spots.

### 2. Politics Serves Mystery
Political elements provide:
- Motives
- Conflicting interests
- Hidden alliances
- Institutional pressure
- Suppressed information

### 3. Show, Don't Explain
Politics enters through:
- Bread prices
- Pub conversations
- Church sermons
- Pamphlets
- Public meetings
- Police presence

### 4. Gray Characters
Political characters should be:
- Complex
- Motivated
- Flawed
- Human
