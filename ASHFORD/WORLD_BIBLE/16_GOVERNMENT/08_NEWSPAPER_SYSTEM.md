# NEWSPAPER & INFORMATION SYSTEM — Alderwick

## Status

**Media ecosystem and information flow** for Alderwick.
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-31
**Canon Status:** APPROVED

---

## Core Principle

> **Information is power. Those who control information control Alderwick.**

In 1816, newspapers are the primary means of mass communication. But they are not neutral. Each newspaper has its own bias, its own agenda, its own blind spots.

---

## I. The Newspaper Ecosystem

### A. The Albion Gazette

**Type:** National newspaper, conservative leaning
**Frequency:** Daily
**Circulation:** ~500 in Alderwick, more in Blackmere
**Political Alignment:** Crown Conservatives
**Owner:** Crown Conservative sympathizers

#### Editorial Stance
- Supports the Crown and Royal Parliament
- Defends the Grain Protection Acts
- Portrays radicals as dangerous extremists
- Supports government measures to maintain order
- Rarely criticizes the aristocracy

#### Coverage Bias
| Event | How Gazette Reports It |
|-------|------------------------|
| Warehouse fire | "Radical violence threatens social order" |
| Radical meeting | "Dangerous assembly of malcontents" |
| Bread prices rise | "Temporary adjustment due to market forces" |
| Veteran protest | "Misguided former soldiers used by agitators" |
| Government action | "Necessary measures to preserve peace" |

#### What It Ignores
- Aristocratic corruption
- Merchant exploitation
- Government overreach
- Working-class suffering (unless to blame radicals)
- Evidence that contradicts official narrative

#### Reputation
- Considered "the establishment paper"
- Trusted by Crown Conservatives
- Distrusted by Reform Coalition and radicals
- Seen as propaganda by critics

---

### B. The Reformer's Chronicle

**Type:** National newspaper, reformist leaning
**Frequency:** Three times weekly
**Circulation:** ~200 in Alderwick, more in urban centers
**Political Alignment:** Reform Coalition
**Owner:** Reform-minded merchants

#### Editorial Stance
- Supports parliamentary reform
- Criticizes aristocratic privilege
- Defends civil liberties
- Questions government overreach
- Supports broader political representation

#### Coverage Bias
| Event | How Reformer's Chronicle Reports It |
|-------|-------------------------------------|
| Warehouse fire | "Possible government provocation to justify repression" |
| Radical meeting | "Peaceful assembly exercising civil rights" |
| Bread prices rise | "Grain Protection Acts protect landowners, starve poor" |
| Veteran protest | "Justified anger of abandoned heroes" |
| Government action | "Repression disguised as order" |

#### What It Ignores
- Radical excesses
- Violence by reformers
- Complexity of aristocratic position
- Government's legitimate concerns
- Evidence that contradicts reformist narrative

#### Reputation
- Considered "the reform paper"
- Trusted by Reform Coalition
- Distrusted by Crown Conservatives
- Seen as biased by critics

---

### C. The Alderwick Herald

**Type:** Local newspaper, relatively moderate
**Frequency:** Weekly
**Circulation:** ~300 in Alderwick and surrounding area
**Political Alignment:** Moderate (officially non-partisan)
**Owner:** Local merchant family

#### Editorial Stance
- Focuses on local news
- Attempts balanced reporting (with mixed success)
- Less ideologically driven than national papers
- More practical, less philosophical
- Covers everyday concerns of Alderwick residents

#### Coverage Bias
| Event | How Alderwick Herald Reports It |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| Warehouse fire | "Investigation ongoing, cause unknown" |
| Radical meeting | "Large gathering in Market Square" |
| Bread prices rise | "Local bakers raise prices, cite supply costs" |
| Veteran protest | "Former soldiers gather to express concerns" |
| Government action | "Magistrate issues statement" |

#### What It Ignores
- Deeper political implications
- National context
- Connections to larger patterns
- What powerful people want hidden

#### Reputation
- Considered "the local paper"
- Most trusted by ordinary people
- Seen as less biased than national papers
- Sometimes criticized as too cautious

### C. Word of Mouth

**Format:** Conversation, gossip, rumor
**Distribution:** Taverns, markets, churches, homes
**Political Alignment:** Everything and nothing

#### Why It Matters
- Fastest form of information spread
- Most trusted by ordinary people
- Most distorted by retelling
- Hardest to control or suppress
- Most dangerous to the powerful

## III. Information Flow

### How News Spreads

```
Event occurs
     ↓
Witness observes
     ↓
Witness tells someone
     ↓
Information spreads through:
- Conversation
- Gossip
- Tavern discussion
- Church gatherings
- Market interactions
     ↓
Reaches:
- Newspaper reporters
- Pamphleteers
- Government informants
- Criminal networks
- Ordinary people
     ↓
Newspapers publish (with bias)
     ↓
Pamphlets respond (with counter-narrative)
     ↓
Word of mouth distorts further
     ↓
Official version vs. Popular version vs. Hidden truth
```

### Distortion at Each Stage

| Stage | What Happens |
|-------|--------------|
| Observation | Witness sees partial truth |
| Memory | Details fade or change |
| Narration | Story is shaped for audience |
| Newspaper editing | Bias filters what gets published |
| Reader interpretation | Preconceptions shape understanding |
| Retelling | Each repetition adds/removes details |
| Time | Truth becomes legend, legend becomes myth |

## IV. The Three Truths System

### For Every Major Event

| Truth Layer | Definition | Who Knows It |
|-------------|------------|--------------|
| **Official Truth** | What the government says happened | Government, Gazette |
| **Popular Truth** | What people believe happened | Ordinary people, rumors |
| **Hidden Truth** | What actually happened | Participants, investigators |
| **Deeper Truth** | Why it happened and what it means | Only a few |

### Example: The Warehouse Fire

| Truth Layer | Version |
|-------------|---------|
| Official | "Radical violence threatens social order" |
| Popular | "Government provocation to justify repression" |
| Hidden | "Arson by unknown party for insurance fraud" |
| Deeper | "The warehouse belonged to a merchant funding both sides" |

### Why This Matters

- Leon sees different versions in different papers
- Each version contains some truth
- Leon must piece together what actually happened
- Some versions are deliberately misleading
- The truth is more complex than any single narrative

## VI. Information as Weapon

### How Newspapers are Used

| User | How They Use Newspapers |
|------|-------------------------|
| Government | Shape public opinion, justify actions |
| Reform Coalition | Criticize government, mobilize supporters |
| Radicals | Spread revolutionary ideas |
| Merchants | Advertise, influence trade policy |
| Criminals | Spread disinformation, blackmail |
| Unknown networks | Plant stories, manipulate narrative |

### Information Warfare Tactics

| Tactic | Description | Who Uses It |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| Selective reporting | Only report facts that support your side | All newspapers |
| Omission | Don't report facts that hurt your side | All newspapers |
| Distortion | Report facts but twist their meaning | All newspapers |
| Fabrication | Invent stories that support your side | Desperate factions |
| Leaks | Release information strategically | Government, reformers |
| Rumors | Plant stories in word of mouth | Everyone |
| Discrediting | Attack the source, not the message | All factions |

## VII. Leon's Relationship with Information

### What Leon Can Learn

| Source | What It Provides |
|--------|------------------|
| Official newspapers | Official narrative, government perspective |
| Reform newspapers | Opposition narrative, reform perspective |
| Local newspapers | Local facts, practical concerns |
| Pamphlets | Radical perspectives, anonymous accusations |
| Word of mouth | Popular beliefs, rumors, gossip |
| Arthur's books | Historical context, old records |
| Daniel's knowledge | Academic analysis, theoretical framework |

### What Leon Must Do

1. Read multiple sources
2. Identify biases in each
3. Cross-reference facts
4. Distinguish truth from propaganda
5. Recognize what is NOT being reported
6. Follow the money and power behind information
7. Question everything, including his own assumptions

### The Danger

- Leon may believe false information
- He may miss important truths
- He may be manipulated by planted stories
- He may not recognize what he doesn't know

### What Information System Provides

| Missing information | What is NOT reported matters |

### What Information System Doesn't Know

- What happens in private
- What is deliberately suppressed
- What witnesses lie about
- What newspapers refuse to print
- What the powerful want hidden
- The central mystery itself
