## Status

**Informant and surveillance system** for Alderwick.
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-31
**Canon Status:** APPROVED

---

## Core Principle

> **In 1816, intelligence is not gathered by agencies. It is gathered by people.**

There is no formal intelligence service. Instead, a network of informants, casual observers, and paid reporters feeds information to those who can pay or compel.

---

## I. How Intelligence Works in 1816

### No Formal Agency
- No "secret service" or "intelligence bureau"
- No trained agents (except rare exceptions)
- No centralized database
- No professional surveillance

### Instead
- **Personal networks** — who you know determines what you know
- **Paid informants** — people who sell information
- **Casual observation** — people who notice things naturally
- **Institutional records** — parish, court, commercial records
- **Correspondence** — letters that can be intercepted

```
        │              │              │
   Magistrate    Constables     Political
                  │           Factions
                  │
              STREET-LEVEL
              ENFORCEMENT
```

### Types of Informants

#### Level 1: Professional Informants
- **Payment:** Regular stipend
- **Reliability:** High (paid well)
- **Risk:** High (if discovered)
- **Examples:** Tavern keepers, postal workers, clerks

#### Level 2: Occasional Informants
- **Payment:** Per-information fee
- **Reliability:** Variable
- **Risk:** Medium
- **Examples:** Servants, shopkeepers, dockworkers

#### Level 3: Casual Sources
- **Payment:** None (or small favors)
- **Reliability:** Low
- **Risk:** Low
- **Examples:** Street vendors, beggars, churchgoers

#### Level 4: Ideological Informants
- **Payment:** None (motivated by belief)
- **Reliability:** High (genuine commitment)
- **Risk:** Very High (passion makes them careless)
- **Examples:** Political sympathizers, religious believers

## V. Information Flow

### How Information Moves

```
Event occurs
     ↓
Witness observes
     ↓
Witness tells someone
     ↓
Information spreads through:
- Conversation
- Gossip
- Paid report
- Intercepted letter
     ↓
Information reaches:
- Constables (official)
- Magistrates (political)
- Merchants (economic)
- Criminals (opportunistic)
- Radicals (strategic)
     ↓
Information is:
- Acted upon
- Sold
- Suppressed
- Distorted
- Forgotten
```

### Information Distortion

| Stage | What Happens |
|-------|--------------|
| Observation | Witness sees partial truth |
| Memory | Details fade or change |
| Narration | Story is shaped for audience |
| Transmission | Each retelling adds/removes details |
| Recording | Written version may be incomplete |
| Interpretation | Listener applies own biases |
| Action | Decision based on distorted information |

## VII. The Postal System

### Why Post Office Matters

The Post Office is the single most important intelligence asset in 1816 Alderwick.

**What it controls:**
- All official correspondence
- Most business letters
- Many personal letters
- Newspaper distribution
- Pamphlet distribution

**What can be learned:**
- Who communicates with whom
- Frequency of communication
- Locations of correspondents
- Contents (if intercepted)

### Postal Intelligence Operations

| Operation | Method | Legality |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Mail watching | Observing who sends/receives | Legal |
| Letter opening | Steaming open and resealing | Illegal |
| Mail delay | Holding letters for "inspection" | Gray area |
| Mail theft | Taking letters | Illegal |
| Mail redirection | Sending to wrong address | Illegal |

### Key Postal Workers

| Position | Access | Vulnerability |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| Postmaster | All mail | High (valuable target) |
| Sorting clerks | Most mail | Medium (bribable) |
| Delivery workers | Local mail | Low (casual bribes) |
| Coastal packet workers | International mail | Medium |

| Monitoring purchases | Low | Low |
| Tracking movements | Medium | Medium |

### Limitations

- **No photography** — must rely on description
- **No telegraph** — information moves at horse speed
- **No fingerprints** — identification is visual
- **No telephones** — no real-time communication
- **Limited record-keeping** — hard to track patterns
- **Small population** — strangers are noticed

### What Intelligence Networks Provide

| Element | How Intelligence Contributes |
|---------|------------------------------|
| Hidden surveillance | Leon may be watched without knowing |
| Information leaks | Secrets don't stay secret |
| False information | Deliberate misinformation |
| Missing information | Some records are suppressed |
| Contradictory reports | Different sources say different things |
| Paranoia | Who can be trusted? |
| Access | Leon can learn things through informants |
| Vulnerability | Leon's own movements can be tracked |

### What Intelligence Networks Don't Know

- They observe behavior, not thoughts
- They see fragments, not the whole picture
- They can be deceived by counter-intelligence
- They miss what happens in private
- They don't understand what they're seeing
- They can't predict the unpredictable
