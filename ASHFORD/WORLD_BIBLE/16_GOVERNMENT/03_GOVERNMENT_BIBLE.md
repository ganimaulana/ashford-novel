## I. State Structure

### The Kingdom of Albion

Alderwick exists within the **Kingdom of Albion**, a fictional island kingdom inspired by but distinct from Great Britain.

**Capital:** Blackmere (analogue: London)
**Core Region:** Eldermere (analogue: England)
**Major Rival:** Varellian Empire (analogue: French Empire)

## II. National Political Structure

### The Crown

**King Alaric III** is the reigning monarch of Albion.

Due to the King's declining ability to personally govern, royal authority is exercised by the **Crown Regent**.

The Crown Regent does not rule alone. The **First Minister** leads the government ministry, while the **Royal Parliament** serves as the legislative institution.

### Where Power Actually Exists

Power in Albion is not concentrated in one place. It exists in tension between:

- **The Crown** — symbolic authority, royal prerogative
- **The Crown Regent** — de facto royal executive
- **The First Minister** — head of government ministry
- **The Royal Parliament** — legislative authority
- **Landed Aristocracy** — economic power, social influence
- **Merchants** — commercial wealth, urban influence
- **Church of the Crown** — spiritual authority, social legitimacy
- **Reformers** — intellectual pressure for change
- **Radicals** — underground political movements
- **Ordinary Population** — whose material conditions drive all politics

## III. National Political Factions

### A. Crown Conservatives

**Base:**
- Aristocracy
- Landed gentry
- Church of the Crown establishment
- Royal officials
- Landowners
- Conservative groups fearful of revolution

**Core Ideology:**
> "Order before change."

They believe rapid social change produces chaos like the revolutions in the Varellian Empire.

**Fears:**
- Radical politics
- Republicanism
- Popular unrest
- Trade unions/combination
- Food riots
- Mass demonstrations
- Criticism of Crown

---

### B. Reform Coalition

**Base:**
- Segment of aristocracy
- Reform-minded gentlemen
- Intellectuals
- Certain merchants
- Groups seeking parliamentary reform
- Urban populations

**Core Ideology:**
> "Liberty through reform."

Not all revolutionaries.
Most Reform Coalition members remain aristocrats not seeking modern democracy.

**Goals:**
- Parliamentary reform
- Corruption limitation
- Civil liberties
- Administrative reform
- Power abuse reduction

---

### C. Radical Movement

**Most dangerous faction.**

Believe Albion's political system is fundamentally broken.

Some advocate:
- Broader political representation
- Parliamentary reform
- Political liberty
- Reduction of aristocratic privilege

Some figures are genuinely extreme.

Provides underground political tension.

---

### D. Important: No Moral Binary

**Crown Conservatives can be right:**
- Hungry, angry masses can become dangerous mobs.

**Crown Conservatives can also abuse:**
- Using "order" to silence criticism
- Protecting privilege
- Limiting freedoms

**Reform Coalition can be right:**
- Government is corrupt, system needs reform.

**Reform Coalition can also be:**
- Opportunistic
- Aristocratic
- Indifferent to poor
- Using reform for political gain

## IV. Albion 1816 — Political Conditions

### Post-War Crisis

The Continental Wars just ended.
Albion won.
But victory did not bring prosperity.

**Conditions:**
- Veterans returning home
- Economic pressure
- High food prices
- Rising unemployment
- Industrial problems
- Poor increasingly desperate
- Government fears unrest

---

### The Grain Protection Acts

**Policy:** Restrict grain imports to protect domestic prices.

**Beneficiaries:** Landowners, farmers.

**Victims:** Urban poor — bread becomes more expensive.

**Political conflict:**
"They say Parliament protects the farmer."
"Then why is my loaf smaller?"

---

### The Blackmere Assembly — Foreshadowing

The **Blackmere Assembly** is a potential future event — a large reform gathering that could escalate into confrontation between civilians and authorities.

Alderwick begins 1816 — opportunity to build:

```
1816 → 1817 → 1818 → 1819

    ↓
radical meetings
    ↓
government repression
    ↓
demonstrations
    ↓

```

Political background grows increasingly unstable.

## VI. Church and Government

**Church of the Crown** is not officially a political party.

But many clergy are socially close to Crown Conservative establishment.

Some clergy support:
- Poverty relief
- Temperance
- Education
- Charity
- Social reform

**Thematic question:**
> Does the church maintain order because of faith — or because it is too close to power?

**Important:** The Church of the Crown's theology is controlled by the Religion Bible. Government documents describe the political relationship, not redefine theology.

## X. Principles for Alderwick

### 1. No Info-Dumps

Politics enters through:
- Bread prices
- Pub conversations
- Church sermons
- Wealthy families
- Merchants
- Officials
- Government decisions

### 2. Show, Don't Explain

**Before Leon understands:**
- He sees bread prices rising
- He hears arguments about Parliament
- He sees a veteran selling medals
- He notices constables watching a printer
- He sees torn posters
- He hears a pastor speaking to a crowd
- He sees an aristocrat pass in a carriage

**After several chapters:**
The reader realizes all these fragments connect to national political conditions.

### 3. Gray Morality

No faction is purely good or evil.

Each has:
- Valid reasons
- Blind spots
- Hypocrisies
- Genuine convictions

### 4. Politics Serves Mystery

Political tension creates:
- Hidden motives
- Secret alliances
- Foreshadowing
- Character depth
- Thematic resonance

Not a history lecture.

---

## XI. Mystery Integration

Government must serve the novel's mystery rather than become an unrelated political textbook.

Political elements may provide:
- Motives
- Conflicting interests
- Hidden alliances
- Institutional pressure
- Suppressed information
- Historical cover-ups
- Access to documents
- Surveillance
- Unreliable official records
- Social tension
- False leads
- Genuine clues

However:
DO NOT reveal the central mystery merely because a government section exists.

Information must be released according to the novel's mystery pacing.

Political world-building should normally appear through:
- newspaper fragments
- conversations
- sermons
- pamphlets
- prices
- shortages
- public meetings
- police presence
- veterans
- merchants
- magistrates

Avoid large exposition blocks explaining the entire political system to the reader.

The reader should gradually understand Alderwick's political structure through lived experience.

## XIV. AI Implementation Rules

When generating Alderwick content:

1. Always use Alderwick canon names.
2. Never accidentally substitute real-world proper nouns into narrative text.
3. Historical names may appear only in research/reference documentation unless explicitly requested.
4. Historical analogues are NOT canon.
5. Historical events are NOT canon until adapted and approved.
6. Preserve broad historical plausibility.
7. Adapt historical mechanisms rather than copying historical scenes.
8. Existing Alderwick canon overrides newly generated ideas.
9. If two canon documents conflict, flag the conflict rather than silently choosing one.
10. Do not create real historical figures as Alderwick characters unless explicitly requested.
11. Do not create modern institutions using modern terminology unless justified by the period.
12. Do not assume a fictional entity is identical to its historical analogue.
13. Preserve the distinction between inspiration and canon.
