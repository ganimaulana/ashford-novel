# ARC BEHAVIOR PATTERNS

## Status

**Framework for ARC anomaly behavior patterns.**
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-31

---

## Core Principle

> **The value of an ARC is not determined by its individual effect.**

An ARC that appears useless alone may become the foundation of a powerful combination.
ARC Synergy is the **core mechanic** of Leon's growth and the ARC system.

---

## The 12 Patterns

ARC behavior is not limited to simple cause → effect.

A single ARC may operate through multiple patterns simultaneously.
This layering creates depth, mystery, and discovery opportunities.

---

### Pattern 1 — Causality

**Core:** A causes B.

The simplest pattern:

- Someone triggers ARC-001 → something hears → phenomenon occurs.

**Characteristics:**
- Relatively easy to understand
- Testable
- Exploitable
- Usually has a clear trigger

**Development — Do not stop at "If A, then B."**

Ask:
- Must A be intentional?
- Does B always occur?
- Is there a delay?
- Does order matter?
- Can A be done indirectly?
- Does A have secondary consequences?

**Example progression:**

```
Stage 1: Ringing the bell causes phenomenon.
Stage 2: Not the sound. Someone hearing it is the trigger.
Stage 3: Not the hearing. The awareness that someone heard it is the trigger.
```

Now the rule becomes far more interesting.

---

### Pattern 2 — Correlation

**Core:** A always appears with B, but does not necessarily cause it.

No evidence that A causes B.
However: every time A appears, B also appears.

**Example:**
- Whenever a certain man is seen in Ashford, rain falls.

**The question:**
- Does the man cause the rain?
- Not necessarily.

**Possibilities:**
- Rain causes him to appear
- Both are caused by a third factor
- He only appears under certain conditions
- The relationship is coincidence
- The relationship is an effect of another ARC

**Mystery value:** Very high.

Characters and readers must distinguish:
- correlation ≠ causation

Leon may misinterpret it at first.

---

### Pattern 3 — Conditional

**Core:** A + B + C are required for D to occur.

Not: A → B
Rather: A + B + C → D

**Example:**
- ARC is only active if:
  - It is raining
  - After midnight
  - Someone speaks a specific name
  - No one else is in the room

**Why this matters:**

Strong characters may see ARC as: "Useless."

Leon thinks: "The conditions are not yet complete."

Then he creates those conditions.

This makes **intelligence** a source of power.

---

### Pattern 4 — Trigger

**Core:** A specific condition activates the phenomenon.

ARC does not always have "cause" in the ordinary sense.
It has a specific trigger.

**Example:**
- ARC activates when someone lies.
- But: it does not matter who lies.

**Or more interesting:**
- ARC activates when someone believes they are lying.

This creates a critical difference between:
- action vs condition

**Trigger categories:**
- Sound
- Touch
- Word
- Thought
- Emotion
- Time
- Location
- Action
- Presence of a specific person
- Knowledge
- **Ignorance** (lack of knowledge)

Hidden triggers create mystery.

---

### Pattern 5 — Exchange

**Core:** Gain X by giving Y.

ARC does not merely cause something.
It takes X and gives Y.

**Example:**
- ARC heals a wound.
- But: the wound is transferred to someone else.

**Or:**
- ARC gives someone one additional day of life.
- By taking one memory.

**Formula:** Gain ↔ Cost

**Why this matters:**

Prevents ARC from becoming free power.
Cost does not have to be known from the start.

The most dangerous exchange: the cost is hidden until too late.

---

### Pattern 6 — Transfer

**Core:** Something moves from one place, person, object, or condition to another.

**Example:**
- A person's shadow moves to another person.

**But more interesting:**
- Not just the shadow.
- Also transferred: memory, fear, habit, injury, identity, supernatural property.

**Leon's insight:**

> "This ARC does not create something."
> "It only moves something that already exists."

This opens tactical applications.

---

### Pattern 7 — Substitution

**Core:** ARC replaces something with something else.

**Example:**
- A mirror does not show the owner's face.
- It shows: the person who should be in their place.

**Or:**
- ARC does not erase a wound.
- It replaces "the person who was wounded" with "the person who was not wounded."

**Why this is dangerous:**

Reality does not truly "create" a new outcome.
It performs **replacement**.

This distinction matters when other changes have occurred since the "original" state.

---

### Pattern 8 — Recursion

**Core:** The effect repeats or generates itself.

**Example:**
- Someone opens a door → finds themselves opening a door → finds themselves opening a door again.

**Or:**
- ARC records an event.
- Then: the recording causes the recorded event to happen again.

**Deeper form:**

```
A causes B.
B causes A.
Therefore: A → B → A → B → A...
```

This can create:
- Loops
- Repeating events
- Self-sustaining anomalies
- Impossible histories

Ideal for high-level mystery.

---

### Pattern 9 — Feedback

**Core:** The effect strengthens or weakens its own source.

Similar to recursion, but the effect amplifies or diminishes itself.

**Example:**
- The more people believe ARC is dangerous, the more dangerous it becomes.
- Then: more dangerous → more fear → more belief → more dangerous.

**Formula:** A → B → strengthens A

**Applications:**
- Religion
- Belief
- Folklore
- Mass psychology
- Memetic phenomena

Explains how something initially small eventually becomes catastrophic.

---

### Pattern 10 — Accumulation

**Core:** Effect does not happen immediately. It builds up.

**Example:**
- Each time someone uses ARC, one second disappears from their life.
- Not noticeable at once.
- Once: 1 second.
- A hundred times: 100 seconds.
- But then Leon discovers: it is not time that disappears.
- What accumulates is **debt**.
- And debt must be paid all at once.

**Why this works:**

At first: "Nothing happens."
Readers forget.
Then dozens of chapters later: **payoff**.

---

### Pattern 11 — Threshold

**Core:** No effect until a specific amount is reached.

Not gradual. The effect appears after a specific point.

**Example:**
- No effect after 1 use.
- No effect after 5.
- No effect after 9.
- On the 10th use: something happens.

**Or:**
- The more people in a room, the stronger the phenomenon.
- But: when the count reaches seven, the phenomenon's nature changes entirely.

**Difference from Accumulation:**

| Accumulation | Threshold |
|--------------|-----------|
| Effect increases gradually | No significant change until threshold |
| Cumulative | Sudden shift |

This becomes a puzzle for Leon.

---

### Pattern 12 — Inversion

**Core:** The true effect is the opposite of what appears.

One of the most powerful patterns for mystery.

**Example:**
- ARC appears to heal.
- Actually: ARC does not heal.
- It returns the body to its pre-injured state.
- That seems the same.
- But the implications differ.

If the wound is not the only change since that state...
then ARC may also:
- Reverse age
- Erase memory
- Restore disease
- Restore emotions
- Restore identity

**Other forms:**

| Apparent | Actual |
|----------|--------|
| Gives something | Removes what prevented the recipient from having it |
| Stops time | The user stops being in the flow of time |
| Creates light | Removes darkness (different mechanism, different side effects) |
| Grants power | Removes the user's natural limitations (which may have protective functions) |

## Layering: The Key to Depth

**Do not use only one pattern per ARC.**

A well-designed ARC may have multiple patterns operating simultaneously.

**Example — ARC-X:**

```
Stage 1 (apparent): Causality
"If the bell is rung, something appears."

Stage 2 (discovered): Conditional
"Only if rung after midnight."

Stage 3 (deeper): Correlation
"Something always appears when a specific person is nearby."

Stage 4 (revelation): Inversion
"The bell does not summon something."

Stage 5 (truth): Transfer
"What happens is someone's consciousness is moved elsewhere."

Stage 6 (danger): Feedback
"The more people try to understand it, the stronger it becomes."
```

Now one ARC has **layers of mystery**, not just one power.

## Leon's ARC Analysis Method

Leon can gradually learn to test each ARC with questions:

1. What triggers it?
2. What changes?
3. What is exchanged?
4. What moves?
5. What is replaced?
6. What accumulates?
7. What happens at the threshold?
8. Does the effect repeat itself?
9. Does the effect strengthen itself?
10. Is the apparent effect actually the opposite of the real mechanism?

**The most important question:**

> **"What assumption am I making that the ARC itself never actually confirmed?"**

This question becomes a hallmark of Leon's intelligence.

In a mystery system like Ashford, the greatest mistake is not failing to know a fact — it is **mistaking interpretation for fact**.
