# ARC CLASSIFICATION

## Status

**Classification System** for ARC types and statuses.
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-31

---

## Core Principle

> **The value of an ARC is not determined by its individual effect.**

Classification is not a power ranking. An ARC classified as "R1 — Passive" may be more valuable than "R4 — Critical" when combined with the right synergy.

---

## ARC Types

### Object
Physical item with abnormal properties. Can be held, moved, or stored.
Examples: Keys, coins, clocks, photographs, clothing, jewelry, tools.

### Location
Place or area with abnormal phenomena. Fixed geographic position.
Examples: Rooms, buildings, streets, alleyways, specific addresses.

### Person
Individual experiencing or possessing abnormal conditions.
**IMPORTANT:** Use with extreme care. Do not treat people as experimental subjects.

### Event
Recurring incident with a pattern.
Examples: Sounds on specific dates, repeating incidents, weather-related phenomena.

### Phenomenon
Occurrence without fixed object or location.
Examples: Sounds without source, untraceable sensations, abnormal perceptions.

### Entity
Presence or existence not fully explained.
**IMPORTANT:** Use sparingly. Do not default to this category without justification.

### Document
Written, recorded, or visual media with abnormal properties.
Examples: Letters that change content, photographs with impossible elements, documents that cannot be destroyed.

### Ritual
A procedure or practice that produces abnormal results.
Examples: Specific sequences of actions, spoken words, symbolic arrangements.

### Conceptual
An idea, symbol, or abstract notion that exhibits abnormal properties.
Examples: Names that cause effects, symbols that propagate, thoughts that manifest.

### Composite
A combination of multiple types. Use when an ARC clearly spans two or more categories.
Format: `Category A + Category B` (e.g., `Object + Location`)

## ARC Status

### Dormant
Inactive. No current activity observed.

### Passive
Active but benign. No direct harm.

### Conditional
Active only under specific circumstances or triggers.

### Active
Currently active and observable.

### Severe
High potential for harm or significant consequences.

### Catastrophic
Wide-area impact potential. Multiple casualties possible.

### Unknown
Insufficient information to classify.

---

## Risk Classifications

### R0 — Residual
No longer active. Only traces remain.

### R1 — Passive
Active but benign. No direct harm.

### R2 — Reactive
Responds to triggers. Predictable when triggered.

### R3 — Hazardous
Direct harm potential. Requires serious precautions.

### R4 — Critical
Wide-area impact. Multiple casualties potential.

### R5 — Unknown
Insufficient data. Unpredictable behavior.
**IMPORTANT:** R5 does NOT mean "very dangerous." R5 means "we do not know yet."

### NULL
Unclassifiable. Does not fit R0-R5. Contradictory behavior.

## Numbering Rules

1. Use sequential numbering: `01_ARC_001`, `02_ARC_002`, `03_ARC_003`, ...
2. Three-digit format: `001`, `002`, ..., `020`, `021`, ...
3. Numbers are identifiers only — NOT power rankings
4. Once assigned, a number is permanent
5. Do not renumber established ARC
6. Gaps in numbering are acceptable if numbers were retired or reserved

---

## Identification Principles

1. An ARC number is assigned when the concept becomes canon
2. Proposed concepts may be listed in the index but should not receive permanent file entries until approved
3. Draft concepts reside in `Draft/` and are not canon until used in story
4. Legacy numbering (08_ARC_008, 13_ARC_013, ARC-021 in Draft/) is preserved for continuity

---

## Distinction: Danger vs Usefulness

- Threat level and usefulness are separate concepts
- A harmless ARC may be extremely important
- A catastrophic ARC may have little practical usefulness to Leon
- Classification must not conflate danger with narrative value
