## Purpose

This document defines the seasonal patterns, weather expectations, and atmospheric conditions for Alderwick during the novel's timeline (November 1816 onward). These patterns influence daily life, character behavior, and the tone of scenes. They are not rigid rules but probability-based guidelines.

---

## Overview of 1816 (The Year Without a Summer)

1816 was an anomalous year globally due to the eruption of Mount Tambora in 1815. The "year without a summer" caused crop failures, food shortages, and unusual cold across the Northern Hemisphere.

**Impact on Alderwick (1816):**
- Spring and summer were colder and wetter than usual.
- Harvest yields were poor, leading to grain shortages.
- By autumn, bread prices had risen sharply (up 50–100% from 1815).
- Fuel (coal, wood) was also in high demand and more expensive.
- Many families faced extreme hardship.

---

## Autumn 1816 (October–November)

### General Character

- **Temperature:** Chilly, often below 10°C (50°F). Frosts begin in late October.
- **Precipitation:** Frequent rain, drizzle, and damp conditions. Mist and fog common.
- **Daylight:** Shortening rapidly. By late November, sunrise ~7:30 AM, sunset ~4:30 PM.
- **Fog:** Thickest in river valleys and low-lying areas, especially after rain or on calm, clear nights.

### Progression

| Period | Weather | Atmosphere |
|--------|---------|------------|
| Early October | Mild, damp, occasional sunshine | Still feels like late summer |
| Mid-October | Cooler, more rain, leaves turning | Transition to autumn |
| Late October–Early November | Frosts begin, fog patches, shorter days | Winter feeling creeps in |
| Mid-November | Persistent cold, frequent fog, early darkness | Winter proper begins |

### Typical Conditions (Mid-November onward)

- Morning fog often lingers until noon.
- Ground frost common in sheltered areas.
- Overcast skies dominate; rain several days per week.
- Wind from the southwest, but occasional cold easterlies.
- Early darkness means lamps lit by 4:30 PM.

---

## Winter 1816–1817 (December–February)

### General Character

- **Temperature:** Cold, often near freezing. Can drop below 0°C (32°F) at night.
- **Precipitation:** Rain more common than snow, but snow possible.
- **Daylight:** Shortest around Christmas; sunrise ~8:00 AM, sunset ~4:00 PM.
- **Fog:** Common in low-lying areas; can persist for days.
- **Snow:** Rare but can occur; not heavy in most winters, but 1816 may have more due to volcanic effects.

### Progression

| Period | Weather | Atmosphere |
|--------|---------|------------|
| Early December | Cold, frosty, short days; winter takes hold | Town turns inward |
| Mid-December | Fog, rain, darkness; holiday season | Festive but strained |
| Christmas–New Year | Frost, possibly snow; cold snap likely | Bitter cold, economic strain felt |
| January | Coldest month; frosts, some snow | Deep winter, struggles intensify |
| February | Still cold, but days lengthening slightly | Hope of spring, but not yet |

## Economic Impact of Weather

The poor harvest of 1816 means that by winter:

- **Fuel is scarce and expensive.** The poor suffer most.
- **Bread prices are high.** A loaf that cost 2–3 pence in 1815 now costs 4–5 pence.
- **Tea, sugar, and other imported goods are luxuries.**
- **The poor rate rises** (as seen in Chapters 25, 31).
- **The rich feel it too** — but they can afford the increase.

## Writing Guidance

### Scene Setting

When writing a scene, consider:

- **Time of day:** Is it dark? Are lamps lit? Is fog obscuring details?
- **Weather:** Is it raining? Is the ground wet? Is there frost?
- **Senses:** The cold; the damp; the smell of coal smoke; the sight of breath in the air.
- **Activity:** People hurry indoors; windows are shut; businesses have shorter hours.

### Descriptions to Avoid

- ❌ Overusing "fog" every scene — use it intentionally.
- ❌ Making every day dark and dreary — contrast is key.
- ❌ Ignoring weather entirely — seasons affect everything.

### Descriptions to Use

- ✅ "The frost held the lane by the throat until noon."
- ✅ "The rain had been falling since dawn, and the streets were slick with mud."
- ✅ "The afternoon light was already failing behind the clouds."
- ✅ "The damp seeped through the coat sleeves, and he felt the cold in his bones."

---

## Notes on Weather as Plot Device

The weather can:

- **Isolate** characters (fog, rain keeps people indoors).
- **Create urgency** (impending snowstorm, early darkness).
- **Reveal character** (some brave the cold, others cower).
- **Heighten tension** (a search in the fog is more dangerous).
- **Symbolize mood** (cold reflects Leon's emotional state, or a thaw signals change).

But it should never feel contrived — it should arise naturally from the setting.
