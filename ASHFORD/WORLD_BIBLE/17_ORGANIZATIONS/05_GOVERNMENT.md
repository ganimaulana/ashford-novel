# PART 2 — THE CONSTABLE: INSTITUTIONAL ARCHITECTURE

## B0. THE FIRST RULE OF THIS DOCUMENT

**Do not build a police force.**

Alderwick in 1816–1817 has no police, no ranks, no uniforms, no stations, no departments, no procedures worth the name. It has **one elected part-time parish constable, a handful of men he can call on, a voluntary watch raised by a hand-bill on a church door, and a great deal of paper kept by people who are not paid to keep it.**

Everything below exists to make that *legible*, not to make it modern. If a clause here starts to feel like a chain of command, it is wrong.

**Terminology lock:** the capitalised institutional noun "the Constable" is World-Bible-only. In prose, people say *the constable*, *the office*, *the round*, *the watch*, *the bench*, *the vestry*.

**Two anachronisms already in the manuscript, logged and NOT fixed:** "bobby" (Ch11) and "the station" (Ch37). See `15_METADATA/AUDIT_POST_VALE_FOUNDATION.md`.

Leon does not join an esoteric department; he becomes the thing the office does not have, and is resented for it.

## B4. THE SEVEN AUTHORITY QUESTIONS

### 1. Who assigns cases?
The constable, personally, in speech, to whoever is standing there. He may assign to a sworn man, an enrolled watchman, or a person he simply pays. He cannot compel: his instrument is advice, wages, and the fact that he is the only one asking. — **CONFIRMED (Ch36, Ch44, Ch46).**

### 2. Who can access restricted records?
There are no clearances. **Restriction is physical.** A record is restricted if it is in a locked drawer and one man has the key. That man is the constable; the clerk holds the key to everything except the bottom drawer. The vestry clerk gatekeeps by attrition rather than refusal — "he was letting them be found without helping them, and letting them be lost without denying them" (Ch107). — **CONFIRMED, extended PROPOSED for Ingle.**

### 3. Who can classify an esoteric incident?
See `ESOTERIC_RECORD_CLASSIFICATION.md` §19–21. In short: any enrolled man may **record what he saw**; only the clerk may enter it in the book; only the constable may raise its standing; and **CONFIRMED standing cannot be assigned in Alderwick at all** — it requires the surgeon's signature at an inquest or the London office's man. — **PROPOSED.**

### 4. Who can authorise containment?
**Nobody. Containment does not exist.** There is no cell for objects, no procedure, no vocabulary. The maximum institutional response is a letter to London and a piece of advice: *"Keep it separate from your other things… not knowing is a reason to be careful, not a reason to experiment"* (Ch36). — **CONFIRMED.**

### 5. Who can seize an object?
**Nobody, unless it is evidence of a crime.** Ch36 is the governing precedent: presented with the Gear, the constable pushed it back across the table and told the civilian to keep it. Vale's private box carries no institutional claim (`Thomas_Vale.md`). The office may **request, record, and advise**. It may not take. — **CONFIRMED. Do not soften this. It is the reason Leon's private keeping is possible at all.**

### 6. Who can overrule a field officer?
The constable, by withdrawing the work or the wages. The bench, by taking the matter over — as it took the tower key (Ch19). Nobody can overrule a man's *judgement*: "Then you'll have to live with your decision" (Ch46). — **CONFIRMED.**

### 7. What is the relationship between the local office and higher authority?
**Reporting without response.** Upward is a letter; downward is silence, or a man who looks and leaves. Twice in twenty-three years. Reports sent up have come back **altered** — small things, a date, a word (Ch107). The institution is therefore not oppressive but *absent*, and the local officer's real defence is a duplicate in his own hand. — **CONFIRMED.**

## B5. THE INSTITUTIONAL DOCTRINE (Vale's rules, made structural)

Vale's maxims are the office's unwritten practice. They are habits, not regulations, and no document states them.

| Maxim | Institutional effect | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "If there is only one copy, there is no copy." | Every entry of consequence is duplicated. The second copy is **filed openly**, not hidden — "hidden things are found by people looking for hidden things; ordinary things are not looked at." | CONFIRMED (Ch45, Ch107) |
| "What I saw. What I was told. What I concluded. Keep them apart." | The three-column discipline. Direct ancestor of the E.R.C. standing ladder. | CONFIRMED (Ch45) |
| "Write what happened. Not what you think happened." | Calloway's version, given to Leon with the notebook. | CONFIRMED (Ch36) |
| "Do not lie if you can avoid it. Refuse. A lie has to be maintained; a refusal only has to be repeated." | How officers handle enquiries about what they hold. | CONFIRMED (Ch48) |
| "Four roads." | Never approach the same place twice by the same street. Vale's own practice: came at dusk, never carried paper in daylight (Ch61). | CONFIRMED |
| A constable who opens the box for curiosity finds only the curiosity. | The hereditary box is opened for cause, in writing, with the shutters closed. | CONFIRMED (Ch121) |
| "The public is better not knowing. Some things are kept only by being left unspoken." | The office's disclosure policy in one line. | CONFIRMED (Ch8) |

## B6. THE INSTITUTION'S CHARACTERISTIC FAILURES

Build these in deliberately. An institution without characteristic failures reads as a system rather than a place.

1. **It is understaffed and knows it.** Enforcement capacity is low; coverage is partial; the men are useful for thieves and drunkards.
2. **It records in order to agree.** "The thing seen is written down, and the thing written down is agreed upon, and the thing agreed upon is the town's, and not the thing's" (Ch19). Recording is how Alderwick *stops* seeing.
3. **It watched the dates and never watched the watchers** (Ch8).
4. **Its escalation channel is dead.** Two letters, two visits, no outcome.
5. **Its paper has been altered upward at least twice** (Ch107) and nobody found out by whom.
6. **It leaves decisions to successors without explaining them** — the empty file, the unopened box, the never-taken tower key.
7. **It cannot say "confirmed" about anything it has not seen a body for.**
