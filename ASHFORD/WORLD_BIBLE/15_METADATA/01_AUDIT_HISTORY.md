## 2. Blind Reader Audit

**Source:** `08_BLIND_READER_AUDIT.md`

**Method:** Reader-perspective simulation, pretending no World Bible knowledge, reading Ch1–137 as a single novel.

### Key Findings

**Central mystery:** A man inherits a house in a town where the empty house is not empty, and where generations of "keepers" have guarded a boundary, a door, and a place beneath the town while a count of thirteen signs and a silent bell measure an approaching hour (00:00).

**Reader's understanding:**
- Leon wants to understand why he was drawn to Alderwick and what he inherited.
- The supernatural system is grounded and restrained: three signs, medallions that warm, the gear, reappearing house, chamber beneath both houses, bell that rings for the keeper, count of keepers, thirteen rings.
- 00:00 is a time associated with stopped clocks, a threshold the count moves toward; its meaning is never given.
- The bell is a keeper's call signal; its tongue was sold long ago; it rings once for the keeper who must descend.
- The door is a stone door beneath the mill, opened with the gear, leading to a passage; it is "not a door" but a memory.
- Aurelius is a previous Ashcroft who lived in Bellweather House, signed the Agreement, made the lock, and left a letter; his full story remains in the margins.
- Three distinct signs (maker's/first, keeper's, Order's) with clear shapes and locations; complete meaning not given.
- Two organizations: Order of Severence (foundation; wants the door opened; factional) and a nameless preservation society (later; tests Leon; wants the tally).
- Key reader questions: What is beneath the chamber? What happens when the count completes / the bell rings / 00:00 arrives? Who is the keeper of the tally? Who sent the envelopes? Who is the boy? What did Aurelius truly intend? What are the remaining rings?

**Unintentional confusion (fixed):**
1. Ch19 author-knowledge leak ("thirteen signs / 00:00" line) – removed.
2. Formulaic taglines (Ch26/31/33/43 "The keeper's duty, the count, and the hour of the bell") – removed.
3. Boundary-stone base mark inconsistency – reconciled to first sign.
4. Earlier short chapters (104, 112) rebuilt from summaries.
5. "I I sat" typos – fixed.

**Result:** Reader's understanding is consistent with Leon's, with author knowledge never leaking ahead. Every intentional mystery is foregrounded by evidence and never by narrator omniscience.

### Migrated: Medallion → Token
- **Count:** 322 replacements across 137 chapters; 45 files changed.

- **Effect:** 278 insertions, 278 deletions. No structural changes.

- **Status:** RESOLVED. Observer is official term. Seeker is NOT used unless future canon establishes it as a separate Sign or role. Migration requires contextual audit.

### Resolved: Keeper → Holder
- **Status:** RESOLVED. Keeper remains Arc 1 terminology; Holder introduced in Arc 2+ when formal supernatural hierarchy becomes narratively relevant. No manuscript changes required for Arc 1.

### NOT Migrated: Sequence, Rank, Acting, Madness (ordinary English)
- All occurrences are ordinary English, not power-system references.

### NOT Present in Manuscript: Sanity, Sanctuary, Potion, Connection Slots
- Zero occurrences.

### Keeper→Holder Authorial Decision
- Manuscript uses "keeper" (1409 occurrences) in deeply embedded semantic cluster:
  - "the keeping" (644) – thematic creed: "the keeping is the thing, the keeper is not."
  - "first keeper" (173) – historical title.
  - "keeper's sign" (141) – Sign name.
  - "keeper who" / "keeper of" (232) – role description.
  - "keeper before" (Vale) – historical title.
  - "keeper's bell" (38), "keeper's key" / "house" / "man" (29) – compound names.
- Blanket migration would break the "keeper's sign," break "the keeping is the thing," break "first keeper," and create hybrid inconsistency.
- **Decision:** Option A (CHOSEN) – Keep "keeper" in Arc 1; introduce "Holder" in Arc 2+.
