The manuscript is broadly coherent and internally consistent across all 137 chapters. The symbol system (first sign / keeper's sign / Order's sign), the object states (medallions, gear, clocks, key, bell, book), the character cast, and the central mystery threads (door, 00:00, count, tally, Aurelius, society) hold together well.

## P0 — Critical Contradictions

### P0-1: The 13–21 November "disturbance cycle" is contradicted by continuing events into December
- **Ch8 (Calloway, verbatim):** "the disturbance returned on the same dates, year after year — from the thirteenth to the twenty-first of November. After the twenty-first it ceased, and Ashford went back to itself." And: "It ends on the twenty-first."
- **Conflicting events after 21 Nov:** Ch12 (28 Nov) bell rings twice at the corner; Ch14 (29–30 Nov) lamp and oiled gate; Ch16 (1–2 Dec) bell then lamp; Ch18 (4 Dec) "the alternation held three nights and broke on the fourth"; Ch19 (6–7 Dec) lamp; Ch22 (10 Dec) lamp; Ch23 (12–13 Dec) lamp.
- **Why it conflicts:** The single stated rule of the phenomenon — that it ceases on 21 November — is broken by the very events that carry the rest of the arc. If the lamp/bell only occur during 13–21 Nov, the December events cannot happen as written.
- **Severity:** P0
- **Recommended solution:** Either (a) correct Calloway's in-world information in an early chapter (he was told only part of the truth — the *cycle* is 13–21 Nov, but the *phenomenon* persists outside it, or the November window is when it intensifies), or (b) re-date the December chapters so they fall inside a continuing season. Option (a) is preferred and requires only a small amendment to Calloway's Ch8 speech (e.g., he says the November window is when it is *worst/known*), preserving the mystery while making the December events lawful.

## P1 — Major Inconsistencies

### P1-1: The house's number — "no house number" (Ch1) vs "number 13" (Ch5–8)
- **Ch1:** the plate above the door is described as **not a house number** — "Beneath the plate… Not a house number. A symbol." The whole opening depends on the house having no number.
- **Ch5:** "My house: 13. Opposite it: 12. The next house: 11." **Ch7:** "House number 13, mine, was one of those." **Ch8:** "The number 13 near the door was not newly affixed… Beneath it were the marks of two small holes that might have come from an older number plate."
- **Why it conflicts:** Ch1's foundational "no number" motif is contradicted from Ch5 onward.
- **Severity:** P1
- **Recommended solution:** Reconcile in Ch1 or Ch5 — e.g., Ch1's plate is *additional* to the number, or the number 13 is present but overlooked, or the "not a house number" line refers specifically to the *symbol* plate while the number exists below it. Minimal line-level fix.

### P1-2: Arrival day-of-week conflict ("Tuesday" arrival vs "A Tuesday" market)
- **Ch1 notebook:** "ASHFORD — 12 NOVEMBER 1816," arrival ~19:42; the text calls arrival night "A Tuesday."
- **Ch1 (next morning, 13 Nov):** "Market Square. A Tuesday. The market stalls were beginning to be set up."
- **Ch9 (verger):** "the first night you came to town, a Monday it was —" and Leon corrects: "A Tuesday."
- **Why it conflicts:** If 12 Nov is Tuesday, 13 Nov is Wednesday, so the market cannot be "A Tuesday." If the market day is Tuesday, arrival must have been Monday (the verger's first guess). The contradiction underpins the "return 13 November" dating.
- **Severity:** P1
- **Recommended solution:** Fix the day-of-week labels consistently (arrival Tuesday 12 Nov → market Wednesday 13 Nov, or make arrival Monday 11 Nov → market Tuesday 12 Nov).

### P1-3: Two gears exist (Ch36 + Ch46), but only one gear is ever referenced afterward
- **Ch36:** Gear found under the Mill Lane clock; Leon keeps it.
- **Ch40:** Vale tests "your gear" beside his stone fragment.
- **Ch46:** Vale opens a wooden box containing a gear and says "You already have one" — confirming a second gear exists. Vale is dying; he gives Leon the gear from the box.
- **From Ch48 onward:** the narrative refers to **"the gear"** (singular) — passed to Daniel (Ch125). The second gear's fate is never addressed.
- **Why it conflicts:** Either the "two gears" were two copies of the same concept (a drafting seam) or the second gear was silently lost.
- **Severity:** P1
- **Recommended solution:** Clarify whether there is one gear or two. If two, one can be stated as kept/guardian's copy and the other carried; if one, Ch46's "You already have one" + box scene must be reconciled (e.g., Vale's box is empty, or the box contains the *stone fragment* only, and "you already have one" refers to the gear Leon carries). This is a small edit in Ch46.

### P1-4: "Two of thirteen" medallions (Ch66) vs the later "thirteen rings" of stones
- **Ch66 (verbatim):** "Two of thirteen—and the remaining eleven would reveal themselves as the count turned toward 00:00." — implies **thirteen medallions**.
- **Ch129–137:** the "thirteen" are revealed as **rings of first-sign stones** (Ch129: "as many as thirteen such rings may exist"), and only **three** medallions ever appear (Leon's, the Bellweather one, and the third from the house on the hill, Ch134).
- **Why it conflicts:** Ch66 establishes thirteen *medallions*; the later canon establishes thirteen *rings* and three medallions. The "remaining eleven" medallions never materialize.
- **Severity:** P1
- **Recommended solution:** Amend Ch66's line to reference the thirteen *rings/signs* rather than thirteen medallions (or to "two of thirteen — and the remaining would reveal themselves"), aligning with Ch129–137. Single-sentence fix.

### P1-5: Boundary-stone marks — Order's sign on the face (Ch37) vs first sign at the base (Ch51/55) never acknowledged as coexisting
- **Ch37:** boundary stone has "a circle crossed by a short line" (Order's sign) near the lower half/face.
- **Ch51, Ch55, Ch112, Ch118, Ch119:** the **first sign** (circle + 3 lines + dot) is at the **base** of every stone, hidden by earth.
- **Why it conflicts:** These are two different symbols on two different parts of the same stone. The final canon decision (FINAL_CONTINUITY_LEDGER) holds that BOTH coexist — Order's sign on the face, first sign at the base — but no chapter in 24–47 acknowledges both marks on the same stone, so the reader may read them as contradictory drafts.
- **Severity:** P1 (canon is correct; prose does not yet make the coexistence visible)
- **Recommended solution:** In Ch37 or Ch46, add one clause noting the narrator checks the base of the stone and finds the older mark beneath the earth, foreshadowing Ch51. Optional but recommended before ARC 2.

## P2 — Minor Inconsistencies

### P2-1: Two different lamp descriptions for the night of Friday 29 November
- **Ch12 ("Friday, 29 November, evening"):** lamp in the *small window above the door*; it moves toward Leon in the open lane.
- **Ch14 (opening):** lamp at *nine in the upper window*, carried along the room, window black by ten.
- Both purport to describe the same night. Severity P2. Fix: clarify the Ch14 opening refers to a different night (e.g., "the next night").

### P2-2: "The unsound occupant of the year three" (Ch15) vs the 1804 probate
- **Ch15:** "the unsound occupant of the year three" (1803).
- **Ch12:** the probate note "The occupant is not named, he being not in a state of mind to be named at law" belongs to the 1804 probate.
- Severity P2. Fix: align the year (1803 vs 1804).

### P2-3: First lamp-sighting date referenced three incompatible ways
- **Ch9 letter:** "You looked across on Sunday night… you saw the lamp."
- **Ch9 Daniel:** "It knew before the seventeenth of November that that lamp had been seen… by you."
- **Ch5:** the sighting is an undated mid-November night.
- Severity P2. If the sighting was Sunday night, the first Sunday after arrival is 17 Nov — not "before the seventeenth."

### P2-4: The poor-house keeper's "Thursday" watch vs the official Saturday round
- **Ch14 (keeper):** "He was on your watch, was he not, Thursday?"
- **Ch14 (orders):** "the lane… Saturday nights, ten to twelve."
- Severity P2.

### P2-5: Medallion never mentioned in Ch1–47, then revealed as "carried all my life" (Ch61, Ch134)
- The medallion — the single most important object of the middle/late novel — does not appear anywhere in the foundation. Ch61 introduces it as carried since childhood; Ch134 again refers to "the box I had been given as a child."
- Severity P2. Not a hard contradiction (he simply never mentions it), but a reader may find it abrupt. Optional: seed the childhood box in Ch1–8.

### P2-6: Ch107 internal contradiction about the Book of Origins hand
- **Ch107:** first says the book's pages are "filled with a hand I had seen before — in the margins of the vestry book, in the pages of the wardens' ledger, in the letter of Aurelius Ashcroft. The same hand." Then Elias says it is **not** Aurelius's hand but "the man who came before him."
- **Ch98:** the vestry-margins hand and the letter hand are established as Aurelius's own.
- Severity P2. Either the vestry-margins hand is the first keeper's (and Ch98's attribution needs care) or the book's hand must be distinct from the letter's hand. Fix with a clarifying line in Ch107 (Elias explains the first keeper's hand was copied by later hands / or the margins were the first keeper's, and Aurelius wrote only the letter).

### P2-7: Reading-count gaps in the lamp observations (Ch24–35)
- Ch24 (9th), Ch27 (13th), Ch28 (15th), Ch31 (17th–21st) — the count sometimes skips nights without explanation.
- Severity P2/P4. Reconcile by noting nights he was out on the round, or drop explicit counts.

### P2-8: The reappearing house location — beside the lane (Ch39) vs at the common (Ch45, Ch114)
- **Ch39:** house appears "beside the lane" (western fields); **Ch45/Ch114:** house appears at the edge of the common (common meets lower pasture).
- Severity P2/P1. These are distinct locations. Fix: either Ch39's sighting is a *different* appearance (allowed) or correct the location in Ch39.

## P3 — Intentional Ambiguities (NOT problems; documented for clarity)

- **P3-1. Margaret's "a few decades ago"** for Aurelius's death vs the 1804 legal record. The text explicitly flags the town's wrong year — intentional in-world error.
- **P3-2. Whether "E. Hodge" (the occupant in the tenure) is the same Hodge who kept the keys** — explicitly left unresolved by Leon.
- **P3-3. The town-wide "voluntary association" sheet vs the Bellweather-lane watch-roll** — the two lists are treated as one but scopes differ; intentional institutional blur.
- **P3-4. Days-in-town counting ("eleven days" Ch11 vs "eighteen days" Ch15)** — arrival-inclusive vs first-full-day drift.
- **P3-5. "Aurelius was not the first keeper" (Ch44) while Aurelius signed the Agreement (Ch40)** — an intentional layered mystery: Aurelius signed/held the Agreement but the *first keeper* (before him) set the boundary, built the house, carved the first sign. Resolved forward (Ch107, Ch135).
- **P3-6. The door bears the Order's sign (circle + short line)** — intentional: the door carries both the Order's sign (the lock identity) and the keeper's sign (the key), mirrored on the two faces (Ch49 mill side: circle+line above seal, keeper's sign below; Ch53 passage side: keeper's sign above, circle below). This is the door "opened from both sides." Not a paradox.
- **P3-7. The stranger's false claim that the signs are a count of 50 years (Ch54)** — deliberately disproven by Ch55 (100+ stones) and retracted by the stranger himself in Ch84. This is an intentional misdirection, not a continuity error.
- **P3-8. "Fresh" signs in the passage (Ch53) vs "old, worn smooth" (Ch56)** — the narrator corrects his own perception; Ch56 explicitly states he had been wrong. Intentional.
- **P3-9. The fixed/unchanging count (Ch117–118) vs marks being added (Ch119–121)** — marks are added only on keeper transitions (rare), so Ferrand saw none in 15 years; the conceptual "fixity" is the reader's misreading, corrected by the watcher. Intentional progression.
- **P3-10. The four clocks "the key to the lock" (Ch97) but the lock opens with the iron key alone (Ch98)** — Ch99 explicitly concedes "their purpose had not [been proved]." Intentional loose thread.
- **P3-11. The Ch54 stranger vs the Ch121 watcher** — two separate figures; the stranger's "count running out" is a different (disproven) count; not a contradiction.
- **P3-12. "Older than the circle" applied to both the keeper's sign (Ch50) and the first sign (Ch51)** — both are older than the Order's circle; the hierarchy (first > keeper > Order) is stable. Phrasing ambiguity only.
