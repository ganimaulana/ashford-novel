## FINAL ARC STRUCTURE (what the novel actually is)

### ARC 1 — BELLWEATHER (Ch 1–47): The Foundation
A stranger (Leon Ashcroft) inherits a property in Ashford and is drawn into the town's hidden keeping: the empty-but-not-empty Bellweather House, the lamp and bell, the town's agreement to see nothing, the boundary, the gear, the stone door, the Agreement signed by Aurelius Ashcroft, the Order of Severence, and Vale's death — which makes Leon the Keeper.
- **Purpose:** establish atmosphere, the three signs, the boundary, the gear, the door, the Agreement, the keeper's duty, and the central mystery of the "place beneath."

### ARC 2 — FIRST KEEPING (Ch 48–60): The Keeper's First Duty
Leon buries Vale and takes up the keeping. He discovers the keeper's sign, the second mark, the passage, the chamber, the stranger who claims the count is running out, and defies the Order (severance). He learns the door is not opened but remembered.
- **Purpose:** Leon's first decisions; the keeper's sign; the count introduced; the door reframed as memory.

### ARC 3 — THE FIRST SIGN (Ch 61–75): Two Houses, Two Medallions, the Chamber
Leon learns of the maker's sign (the object he has carried all his life), the second medallion in the kept house, the two houses, the keeper's bell, and descends to the chamber beneath both houses where the medallion is set and the stone warms — the "place beneath."
- **Purpose:** the first sign; two medallions; the chamber; the bell rings once for Leon; the place beneath.

### ARC 4 — THE ACCOUNT (Ch 76–86): The Forgetting and the Remembering
Leon understands the town's agreement is the forgetting and his red book is the remembering; he keeps both. The Order acknowledges him; the stranger recants; the account becomes the thing that keeps.
- **Purpose:** thematic core; Leon as keeper of both; the passing foreshadowed.

### ARC 5 — THE HUNT (Ch 87–99): Four Clocks, the Key, the Lock, Aurelius's Letter
Leon hunts the four Hollis clocks (marked with the first sign), meets Ysabel Nott (the dark-coat woman, 20-year hunter), finds the key and the lock, and uncovers Aurelius's letter in the vault — the wall marks where the first sign crosses the keeper's sign; the lock is a record, not a door.
- **Purpose:** first-sign objects; Ysabel; Aurelius's role; hunting and keeping as one act.

### ARC 6 — THE SOCIETY (Ch 100–117): Three Tests, the First Keeper's Book, the Tally
A nameless society tests Leon (room, box, name). He finds the book of the first keeper (containing the tally), works with the society, and assembles three identical tallies (first keeper, Academy, Ferrand's notebook) — the fixed count.
- **Purpose:** the organization discovered through evidence; the tally; the first keeper's erasure.

### ARC 7 — THE COUNT OF KEEPERS (Ch 118–125): The Tally and the Passing
Leon realizes the tally counts the keepers, that marks are being added by the keeper of the tally, that his own mark is already in it, and that his successor is already counted. He writes the book of instruction and passes the keeping to Daniel Wren.
- **Purpose:** the central revelation (count = keepers); the passing; Leon freed.

### ARC 8 — BEYOND ASHFORD (Ch 126–137): The Thirteen Rings and the Journey
Leon walks north to the marsh (the first ring), keeps the marsh house, receives the third medallion from Miriam (keeper before the first keeper's line), and walks south to the sea, resolving that the thirteen signs are the path itself and the journey is the destination.
- **Purpose:** the count as a lived journey; the keeper-before-first backstory; the open ending.

## CHARACTER PROGRESSION
- **Leon:** stranger → watchman → keeper → hunter → society collaborator → keeper who passes → gatherer of signs → man at the sea. Every stage is caused by consequences of prior choices.

## POWER/SUPERNATURAL PROGRESSION
- No RPG leveling. The "power" is knowledge + objects + understanding: signs, medallions, gear, bell, count. Leon never gains a supernatural "level"; he gains understanding and the count's trust.
