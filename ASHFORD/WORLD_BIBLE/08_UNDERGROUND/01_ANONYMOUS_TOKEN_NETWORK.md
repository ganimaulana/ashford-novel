# ANONYMOUS TOKEN NETWORK — DESIGN FILE

**Status:** PROPOSED — ARC 2+ (not CONFIRMED, not implemented in manuscript)
**Date:** 2026-09-03
**Nature:** Design/preparation document. All concepts herein are PROPOSED unless an existing canon statement confirms them.

## A. STATUS

**PROPOSED — ARC 2+**

This document describes a future-narrative possibility: a decentralized, anonymous network of Token recipients connected by a hidden common source. It is a design framework only.

---

## B. CHARACTER ROLE — ELEANOR SHAW

**Proposed role:** First Anonymous Token Recipient — PRIMARY CANDIDATE.

See `04_CHARACTERS/characters/10_ELEANOR_SHAW.md` (ARC 2+ PROPOSAL section) for the full character proposal.

Key points:
- Eleanor is an intelligent, curious person drawn to local history, old manuscripts, folklore, antiquities, records, inscriptions, and unexplained events.
- She is NOT an occultist or supernatural expert.
- Her curiosity, not supernatural competence, draws her close to something genuinely supernatural.
- She must NOT become "female Leon," an instant expert, or a passive recipient.
- She retains full independent agency.

---

## C. MOTIVATION (Proposed)

Eleanor's motivation develops from curiosity and intellectual adventure:
1. She investigates mysteries believing most have rational/historical explanations.
2. She encounters the Token and cannot fully rationalize it.
3. Her curiosity becomes personal.
4. She must decide whether the risk is worth continuing.

---

## D. PERSONALITY (Proposed)

- Curious, historically minded, intellectually adventurous
- Capable of incorrect assumptions
- Less knowledgeable about supernatural reality than she believes
- Independent, with genuine agency
- Not flawless; makes mistakes

---

## E. KNOWLEDGE BOUNDARY (Proposed)

**Eleanor knows (after contact):**
- She received a Token
- Someone intentionally contacted her
- The sender knows something about her investigation

**Eleanor does NOT know:**
- Leon's identity
- The complete supernatural system
- The XIII Symbols
- The full meaning of the Token
- The existence/structure of any secret organization or network
- The sender's ultimate objective

---

## F. TOKEN RELATIONSHIP (Proposed)

A Token given to Eleanor must have a narrative function:
- access, identification, testing, communication, triggering a phenomenon, interacting with a supernatural mechanism, selecting/recognizing a potential recipient, or establishing controlled contact

It must lead to: a decision, an investigation, a discovery, a test, access, or a meaningful consequence.

It must NOT be an inventory object carried indefinitely without purpose.

Specific Token behavior remains PROPOSED until supported by the supernatural system canon.

---

## G. ANONYMOUS CORRESPONDENCE (Proposed)

Leon does NOT personally introduce himself, openly recruit, or initially meet her face-to-face (see OPEN QUESTION in Eleanor's profile re: prior Ch 61 acquaintance).

Historically plausible 1816 delivery methods (no modern drops):
- Letter slot
- Envelope beneath a door
- Discreetly placed letter
- Delivery through a servant or intermediary

The package may contain:
1. A Token
2. A short anonymous letter
3. Possibly a simple instruction

The first message must NOT explain: Leon's identity, the full supernatural system, the XIII Symbols, the Rank system, the existence of an organization, or the identity of other recipients.

**Mutual uncertainty:**
- Eleanor knows "someone knows something I do not."
- Eleanor does NOT know who/where the sender is, how they know about her, whether they are trustworthy, whether others are involved, whether the Token is benevolent or dangerous.
- Leon does not have perfect knowledge of her either.

---

## H. LEON'S RELATIONSHIP TO ELEANOR (Proposed)

Leon does NOT distribute Tokens because he "needs members for his organization." His motivation develops from necessity:

1. He encounters a problem he cannot solve alone.
2. He recognizes Eleanor's useful perspective, skill, or disposition.
3. He investigates her from a distance.
4. He determines whether she can be trusted.
5. He gives her controlled access to a Token.
6. He observes what she does with it.
7. Only later does he consider whether she could become part of a larger network.

He recruits because a person's existence/perspective becomes relevant to the mystery — not because he is building a conventional secret society from the beginning.

## I. FUTURE NETWORK POTENTIAL (Proposed)

Eleanor may eventually be one of several people who independently receive Tokens or instructions from the same hidden source.

- Recipients do NOT immediately know one another's identities.
- They do NOT know the complete structure, Leon's identity, the organization's true purpose, or the total number of recipients.

Conceptual model:

```
                 UNKNOWN SOURCE
                      |
          +-----------+-----------+
          |           |           |
       Person A    Eleanor     Person C
          |           |           |
       unknown     unknown     unknown
       to B/C       to A/C      to A/B
```

Leon may initially be the hidden common connection — PROPOSED only.

Members should NOT sit around a table together.

**Defining principle:** "People connected by a mystery before they are connected to one another."

# §N — ELEANOR SHAW AS SECRET CONTACT: FULL ARCHITECTURE

**Status:** PROPOSED — author-directed 2026-09-03. Not CONFIRMED. Not implemented in manuscript.
**Supersedes:** §B–§K's "first *anonymous* Token recipient" framing, in the specific respect described in §N.1.

---

## N.1 — THE ANONYMITY FRAMING IS ABANDONED

The earlier proposal made Eleanor the first *anonymous* recipient. That was already in tension with canon, and the tension does not resolve: **she initiated contact with Leon (Ch42), named him unbidden, met him twice more (Ch43, Ch61), and gave him the map from Vale's hand to his.** They know each other by sight, by name, and by history. An anonymous letter from a stranger is not available.

The replacement is stronger and requires no contrivance:

> **This is not an anonymous recruitment. It is a negotiation between two people who have each already decided the other cannot be trusted, and who each need the other anyway.**

Anonymity is retained only where canon supports it — **outward**, not between them. Nobody else knows the arrangement exists. That is the concealment that matters.

## N.2 — THE STARTING POSITION (do not soften this)

Trust as of Ch141 is **low and one-directional**, and both directions have on-page evidence:

| | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Leon → Shaw | **Ch62:** *"the Order, and Crowe, and Eleanor Shaw — they were all trying to make me remember the wrong thing."* **Ch80:** *"I had not told Eleanor Shaw."* He classed her with the opposition and then stopped telling her things. |
| Shaw → Leon | She gave him the map and **withheld its interpretation**; she warned him rather than helped him — *"If you take this map, you will not be able to tell them you have taken nothing."* She has never volunteered anything she was not holding for someone else. |

**Do NOT make Shaw instantly loyal.** The arrangement must be earned across the whole future arc, from below zero.

## N.3 — WHY LEON EVENTUALLY TRUSTS HER

Not because she is kind, and not because she helps him. Four reasons, in the order he arrives at them:

1. **She held a sealed thing for seven years and never opened it** (Ch61). This is the single most important fact about her. It is not loyalty to Leon — it is a demonstrated *property*, tested by seven years and a dead man.
2. **Vale chose her.** Of everyone in Alderwick, Vale — who trusted almost nobody, who never carried paper in daylight, who came at dusk and never twice by the same street — chose Eleanor Shaw. Leon does not have to trust her judgement. He can borrow Vale's.
3. **She is nobody's.** Once Leon works out that the Order believes she is theirs and she is not, he understands that her position is exactly his own: a person operating inside an institution's assumption about them.
4. **She told him the thing that cost her.** *"Vale did not trust the Order, and he did not trust the constabulary either"* (Ch61). She did not have to say the second half.

## N.4 — WHY SHE EVENTUALLY TRUSTS HIM

Also four, and none of them is affection:

1. **He came back.** Vale's own criterion (Ch48: *"You came back."*). Shaw's version is colder: he came back after she had given him nothing.
2. **He asked before he took** (Ch49 — Vale's other criterion). She has been asked for things by the Order for years and never asked *whether*.
3. **He is the one person who could expose her and has not.** Leon knows the Order believes she is its archivist. He has had years to use that and has not.
4. **He kept the map to himself.** Whatever she was risking by handing it over, he did not spend it. Ch80 — the chapter where he withholds from *her* — is, from her side, evidence that he withholds from everyone.

**Her motive is not friendship. It is that she has spent her life keeping other people's sealed things and has never once been told what any of them were.** Leon is the first person who might eventually tell her. That is what she wants, and it is not a nice reason, and it gives her genuine agency and a genuine capacity to defect.

## N.5 — WHAT SHE KNOWS AND DOES NOT KNOW

**Knows (CONFIRMED):** the keeper's sign is older than the Order's circle and is not theirs · Vale found the nineteenth sign and did not understand it · a map older than the vestry survey existed · the Order knows Leon has the Gear · Vale distrusted the Order and the constabulary · Vale's tradecraft, in enough detail to have concluded he was trained · people have died protecting things they could not explain.

**Does NOT know (protected):** the XIII Signs · the Ranks · the count, or that it exists · the chamber, the bell, the houses · the first keeper · Elias Marr's collection · the nameless preservation society · Vale's rank, department, or the incident · **that Leon carries a Token, or what a Token does.**

**What she learns by entering the arrangement — and no more:**
- That there is a thing Leon is keeping, that it is older than the Order, and that it responds to him and not to her
- That an object exists which behaves in a way she cannot account for
- **Nothing about the count. Nothing about the thirteen. Nothing about the Door.**

## N.6 — THE TOKEN: WHAT IT IS AND IS NOT

### Canon constraints that govern the entire design

| Constraint | Source |
|---|---|
| A Token is the physical manifestation of a Sign; **Sign = identity, Token = object** | `CANON_TERMINOLOGY.md` |
| Tokens **warm** — at thresholds, at the first sign, on approach to a kept place; warmth grows with proximity and can *lean* directionally | Ch66–Ch138 |
| **A Token does not warm for anyone but the keeper.** Ch117: *"It lay in her palm, cold." / "It does not warm for anyone I have ever known."* | **Ch117 — the hardest lock in the system** |
| Tokens carry no words, transmit nothing, and have never once conveyed information from one person to another | Ch1–141, by absence |
| Thirteen exist; three are found; they are pieces of one divided keeping | Ch138 |
| The Gear does **not** warm and is not involved in any of this | CONFIRMED, protected |

### Therefore — the three-layer design

**LAYER 1 — AUTHENTICATION (the Token's actual function).**
The Token is a **warrant, not a wire.** Its purpose in the arrangement is that it cannot be counterfeited: it bears the first sign, it is old in a way she can date by hand and by metal, and it is unmistakably the same mark as the one on the map she held for seven years. Possession of it proves the sender is the keeper and not an impostor, an Order test, or a man with a letter.
**It replaces a signature.** In 1817, that is worth more than any message.

**LAYER 2 — THE SUMMONS (movement, not warmth).**
The Token's *position* is the entire message. One bit of information per exchange:

```
Token in the agreed place        →  "come" / "there is something"
Token absent from the agreed place →  "nothing"
```

The agreed place is ordinary — a specific shelf, a numbered ledger, a book returned to the wrong sequence. **Vale's doctrine governs it:** *"Hidden things are found by people looking for hidden things. Ordinary things are not looked at"* (Ch45). Nothing is written. Nothing is carried through the town in daylight. Nothing can be intercepted, because nothing is in transit.

**This is not a supernatural mechanism at all, and that is the point.** The Token contributes only the one thing no piece of paper can: proof of who.

**LAYER 3 — THE COLD (the price, and the canon lock honoured).**
**The Token will be cold in her hand and will stay cold.** Ch117 is absolute. She holds a thing she has been told is alive and experiences dead metal.

Do not treat this as a flaw to be engineered around. It is the best thing in the design:
- It **proves** the Ch117 rule instead of breaking it
- It gives her a permanent, unresolvable unease — she is being asked to believe on someone else's testimony, which is the exact thing an archivist refuses to do
- It creates the arrangement's central asymmetry: **he has a relationship with the object; she has a relationship with him.** She cannot verify any of it, ever.

## N.7 — WHAT THE TOKEN ALLOWS AND DOES NOT ALLOW

| Allows | Does NOT allow |
|---|---|
| Proof that a summons came from the keeper | Any words, message, image, or voice |
| A binary signal by placement | Distance contact of any kind |
| A credential she can present to nobody (there is no one to present it to) | Warmth, response, or any sensation for her |
| A physical object she can examine, date, and compare by hand | Locating Leon, or Leon locating her |
| An anchor for a meeting: same place, same signal, no correspondence | Protection, access, authority, or any supernatural effect |
| Recognition of the first sign if she ever sees it elsewhere — **by eye, not by feel** | Detection of anything |

## N.8 — FREQUENCY, RISK, AND EXPOSURE

**Reasonable use:** rarely. **A handful of times a year.** The constraint is not a cooldown — it is that a book out of sequence twice in one month is a pattern, and a pattern is what a librarian notices, and she is a librarian.

**Risks of use:**
- The place is ordinary, which means anyone may disturb it by accident. A ledger reshelved by a diligent assistant reads as a summons.
- **A false signal cannot be distinguished from a real one.** There is no confirmation step and no way to build one without writing something down.
- Leon must physically visit the library. He is a known face there and Adeline Brume also keeps a table in that room (Ch105).
- **A Token out of Leon's keeping is a Token he has failed to keep.** Ch109, Ch113, Ch128 all enumerate the Tokens among the things the keeper keeps. This is the arrangement's true cost and it is *moral*, not practical.

**If someone else discovers it:**

| Who | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **The Order** | Shaw is finished — not as a traitor but as a fraud. Their belief that she is their archivist is her entire position, and it evaporates. Graves would act; the woman with the ledger might not. |
| **Calloway / the office** | Leon has given away part of the keeping to a person outside the institution, and the office has no power to take it back (no seizure, `05_GOVERNMENT.md` §B4.5) — which is worse, because it means the only sanction available is Calloway's opinion of him, and that is the sanction Leon can least afford. |
| **The nameless society** | They have watched Leon for two years already (Ch105). They would want the Token, and they would want Shaw, and they do not ask what things are for before taking them (Ch120). **The most dangerous discoverer.** |
| **Cuthbert Ingle** | UNKNOWN, and the reader should feel that. |

## N.9 — WHY NEITHER CAN GO TO AUTHORITY

**Why Leon cannot simply tell the constable:**
1. It is not the office's business and the office could not act on it. There is no seizure power, no containment, no classification for it, and CONFIRMED standing is unreachable for anything he could say about a Token.
2. The office's paper leaks upward and comes back altered (Ch107). Anything he tells Calloway becomes a document.
3. Vale's fallout is his standing lesson: *"Vale carried it for fifty years and told no one. You have carried it for a week and told the room"* (Ch53). He has already made this mistake once.
4. The keeping is not the Constable's. It never was. The Agreement predates the office and the office has no claim on it.

**Why Shaw cannot simply report Leon:**
1. **To whom?** Reporting to the Order requires her to explain how she came by it, and the answer is *the keeper gave it to me*, which ends her.
2. Reporting to the constable requires her to explain what a Token is, from a woman with no standing, about an object that does nothing in her hand.
3. **She held Vale's map for seven years and never opened it.** Her whole identity is that she does not do this.
4. **Mutual incrimination is the load-bearing mechanism.** He has given away part of the keeping. She has accepted a thing from the keeper while the Order believes she is theirs. **Neither can end the other without ending themselves.** That is what makes the arrangement hold, and it is not friendship, and it should never be mistaken for it.

## N.10 — HOW IT DEVELOPS NATURALLY (six stages, no shortcuts)

1. **The unpaid debt.** Leon has the map. He has never told her what was on it. She has never asked. Both of them know she is owed something, and neither names it.
2. **He needs an archive the parish does not have.** A record that exists only where nothing is catalogued by anyone with an interest — which is her room, and nowhere else.
3. **He pays in the one currency she wants: he tells her one true thing.** Not the count. Not the door. **One thing about the map.** It costs him materially and she registers exactly how much.
4. **She tests him without saying so** — the way Vale tested people, and she learned it from watching Vale. She gives him something slightly wrong and waits to see whether he comes back with the correction or the conclusion.
5. **The Token.** Not offered as recruitment and not accepted as loyalty. Offered as a **means of asking without writing**, because a letter can be read and a man on a stair can be seen. She takes it, finds it cold, and takes it anyway.
6. **The first real use, and the first real risk.** After which they are bound, and neither of them has said a word about trust.

**No scene in this sequence contains a declaration.** The relationship is built the way Vale's was: by accumulation, unremarked.

## N.11 — TIMING: THE ENUMERATION PROBLEM — **DEFERRED**

**This is a genuine canon obstacle and it must be resolved by an author decision before a single chapter is written.**

The manuscript enumerates exactly which Tokens Leon holds:

| Chapter | Enumeration |
|---|---|
| Ch103 | *"I put the clocks in the drawer, with the Tokens, and the key."* — **already plural** |
| Ch109, Ch113, Ch126, Ch128 | The Tokens are listed among the things the keeper keeps |
| Ch130 | *"I carried the Tokens — the one I had carried all my life, the first sign, and the one I had found in the kept house at the end of Bellweather Lane."* |
| Ch131 | *"I carried the Tokens — the one I had carried all my life, and the one from the kept house."* — **exactly two** |
| Ch138 | The third is found. *"Three Tokens. Three first signs. And I was the keeper of all three."* Ten remain in the world. |

**Consequence: no Token can be permanently given away anywhere in Ch103–Ch141 without contradicting a count that is stated on the page four times.**

### Three candidate resolutions

| # | Resolution | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The arrangement begins after Ch141.** Leon holds three; ten remain findable; a fourth-found Token can be entrusted without touching any stated enumeration. | **RECOMMENDED.** Zero contradiction. Also fits the departure canon and the Vale-past locations, which nearly all require travel. |
| **B** | **The Token is lent and returned, not given** — it passes to her and back, and the passing *is* the signal. | Works inside the Alderwick years and requires no new Token. **But it fails the moment Leon leaves Alderwick**, which he does at Ch130, so it cannot support a long arrangement. Viable only for an in-Alderwick insertion window. |
| **C** | A Token Leon acquires and never carries is given to her. | **REJECTED.** No such Token exists in Ch51–141 without inventing one, and inventing one collides with the thirteen-Token architecture of Ch138. |

**Decision required. Do not proceed on an assumption.**

## N.12 — WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN

- Shaw must not become loyal quickly, or at all in the ordinary sense
- The Token must not carry words, images, voices, sensations, or distance contact
- **The Token must not warm for her.** Ever. Under any circumstance.
- She must not learn the count, the thirteen, the Door, or the chamber
- She must not be enrolled in the Constable or re-enrolled in the Order
- She must not become a bridge between the Constable and the Order
- She must not become an occultist, a scholar of the system, or Leon's equal in knowledge
- The arrangement must not become a club, a cell, a society, or a meeting
- **She must retain the ability to defect**, and the reader must feel it
- No member of the future network may sit at a table with any other
