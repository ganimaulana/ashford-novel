# VALE AFTERMATH ARCHITECTURE

**Status:** PROPOSED — long-term architecture. Phase F.
**Date:** 2026-09-03
**Manuscript impact:** NONE. Chapters 1–141 untouched.
**Governing file:** `../04_CHARACTERS/characters/25_THOMAS_VALE.md` v4.0 — that profile is authoritative on Vale himself. This file specifies only what happens to his *legacy* over the long term.

## F0. THE ONE RULE

**Vale was never omniscient, and death must not make him so.**

He guarded a door for fifty years and did not know what was behind it. He read an inscription for fifty years and could not say what it was for. His expertise was investigative and institutional — records, verification, chains of custody, how a report gets altered — **never occult.**

The aftermath must therefore deliver **method, paper, and absence**. Not answers. Every discovery in his estate should tell Leon *how* Vale worked and leave *why* untouched.

Forbidden, permanently: Vale as secret master of the Gear · Vale as an occult scholar · Vale having understood the Door · Vale having planned Leon's path · a posthumous letter explaining anything · "Father Lucien Vale" (name and clerical status both rejected pending separate author approval).

## F2. THE JOURNAL — HOW IT PAYS OUT

Read in order, never from the end. What Ch52 and Ch128 establish:

- Garrison-town years first: the price of bread, a disliked sergeant, a horse destroyed, **a name that appears four times and then never again**
- **No supernatural content anywhere in it**
- The Alderwick transfer entry: *"The reason offered is establishment. I have read the order twice. It is correctly worded. That is what troubles me."*
- Then several blank pages
- Then ordinary parish business, *"as though a door had closed on the earlier man and the later one had agreed not to mention him"*
- Leon has read it a dozen times and still cannot place **the crossed-out names**, **the unfamiliar towns**, or **the entries that break off mid-matter and resume years later**
- Leon's conclusion: *"He wrote a great deal, and he wrote nothing about the door. I have come to think that was a decision."*

### The four extractable threads

| Thread | What it yields | Cost to hold | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The name that appears four times and stops** | A person. Alive or dead, findable or not. The cleanest single thread in the estate. | Nothing — it can be held for years | **UNKNOWN — do not name** |
| **The unfamiliar towns** | Geography. Places Leon can go, each generating a location, a person, and a local mystery. | Nothing | PROPOSED |
| **The entries that break off and resume years later** | Time. The gaps are the career; the record is the cover. | Nothing | PROPOSED |
| **The crossed-out names** | The hardest and least rewarding. Deletion by his own hand means he decided, and that decision is his and should stay his. | — | **Recommend: never resolve** |

**The omission that Leon already noticed and must be paid off eventually:** the destroyed horse appears in Vale's spoken account (Ch48) *and* in the journal's early pages (Ch52), **and in neither does he say where the road was.** Leon registered that the omission survived the change of medium. That is a deliberate, doubly-scrubbed fact — the only one in the estate — and it is the best single reserve revelation Vale has. **Do not spend it early.**

## F3. THE DISTRICT MAP — CACHE ARCHITECTURE

The map is a constabulary district map of somewhere that is not Alderwick, marked in ink in more than one shade over many years. Vale never explained it and never destroyed it.

**Intended eventual realisation (PROPOSED):** the marks correspond to places where he worked or hid records.

### Hard constraints — these are what stop it becoming a treasure hunt

1. **Not every mark is a cache.** Some are places he was. Some are places he watched. Some are places somebody else was.
2. **Not every cache is supernatural.** Most hold intelligence material: a copy of an altered report, a witness statement, a list of names, a duplicate of something that was destroyed elsewhere.
3. **Some are empty.** Recovered, spoiled, flooded, built over, or never filled.
4. **Some were found long ago** — by whoever he was hiding them from, which is the most frightening finding available.
5. **Some are fragmentary.** Half a document. A page with the top torn off.
6. **Some are irrecoverable and must stay so.**
7. **The cache doctrine is his own, stated in Ch45:** *"Hidden things are found by people looking for hidden things. Ordinary things are not looked at."* **His caches are therefore not hidden. They are ordinary.** A parcel deposited with a carrier under a wrong name. A book on a public shelf. A second copy filed openly in the run of parish paper. **This is the crucial design point and it must govern every cache: Leon's difficulty is not finding a hiding place, it is recognising an unremarkable thing.**
8. **The map must not pay off in the current arc** (`25_THOMAS_VALE.md` §Hidden Caches).

### Recommended yield ratio
Out of any ten marks: **two** hold something useful · **three** hold something that raises a new question and answers nothing · **two** are empty · **one** was emptied by somebody else · **two** are not caches at all.

## F4. THE SAYINGS — A/B/C/D/E CLASSIFICATION

The reader must initially take almost all of this as an old man's advice. **The ratio is the discipline.** Most of it is simply wisdom, and only a few items may ever resolve as professional shorthand.

**A = ordinary wisdom · B = professional habit · C = institutional survival principle · D = potentially coded information · E = unresolved**

| Saying | Chapter | Class | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| *"You do not owe the dead your life. Remember them. Learn from them. But do not become their unfinished business."* | Ch50 | **A** | Must stay ordinary. This is the arc's emotional spine and coding it would ruin it. |
| *"Never read the end first. A man who reads the conclusion first will find the evidence agrees with it."* | Ch45 | **A** | Also the reading instruction for his own journal — which is a *design* joke, not a code |
| *"A man who tells you he has nothing to hide has told you the one thing about himself he wanted you to know."* | Ch45 | **A/B** | Aimed at Graves, retrospectively |
| *"Watching is not understanding."* | Ch40 | **A** | And a statement of his own limit |
| *"What I saw. What I was told. What I concluded. Keep them apart."* | Ch45 | **B → C** | Became the office's practice and the ancestor of the STANDING ladder |
| *"You have told me what the map says. You have not told me what is there."* | Ch45 | **B** | — |
| Asks the same question twice, twenty minutes apart, buried under unrelated talk; says *"Good"* of the answerer, not the answer | Ch45 | **B** | A verification technique, not curiosity. Denby was subjected to it too. |
| *"Most men give you a refusal shaped like an answer and you go away satisfied."* | Ch45 | **B/C** | — |
| *"If there is only one copy, there is no copy."* | Ch45 | **C** | Institutional. **And D:** the exact phrasing may be a departmental formula. It resurfaces in Calloway's mouth as *"a man who has only one copy of a thing does not own it"* (Ch107) — **two forms of one rule, which is how a taught phrase behaves.** |
| *"Hidden things are found by people looking for hidden things. Ordinary things are not looked at."* | Ch45 | **C → D** | **The cache doctrine.** Reads as filing advice; is an instruction for where he put things. The most important single reclassification in this table. |

| *"Four roads. It costs you nothing to use four roads. | *"You came back." / "You ask before you take."* — on *why me* | Ch48/49 | **E** | With his own refusal to make it more: *"men who believe they were chosen stop checking their own work."* **UNANSWERABLE by instruction.** |

| *"I am not dead. It is an unpleasant habit, giving things away while you are still using them."* | Ch48 | **A** | Leave it as character. |

### The payoff discipline, stated as a rule
**Of twenty sayings, at most three may ever be revealed as professional shorthand, and at most one may ever be revealed as an actual instruction.** Confirmed echoes so far: Leon copies the transfer entry and registers that he has just made a second copy (Ch52); Vale's maxim resurfaces in Calloway's mouth twenty years on (Ch107). **That ratio is already close to its ceiling. Spend the remainder slowly.**
