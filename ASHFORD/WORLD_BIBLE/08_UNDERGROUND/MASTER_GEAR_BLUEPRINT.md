## III. CANON STATUS SUMMARY

### CONFIRMED (manuscript evidence)
- **One Gear** exists in canon (Ch36, Mill Lane clock). It is circular, dark iron, worn by use, and bears the keeper's sign (a circle crossed by a short line).
- **PHYSICAL SCALE — CONFIRMED DESIGN DECISION (2026-09-03):** diameter approximately **14–15 cm** — broad as the flat of an adult hand, thicker than a plate, substantial in mass. The Gear is **not** pocket-sized, and is not a coin, medallion, or Token-like object. The earlier manuscript phrasing "small enough to rest comfortably between two fingers" (Ch36) has been revised accordingly.
- **CARRYING LOGIC — CONFIRMED:** Leon carries the Gear **wrapped in a square of cloth, inside his coat** (established Ch38, used consistently Ch40/Ch47/Ch49/Ch52/Ch53). It is not carried loose in a trouser pocket. The cloth wrapping also links to Vale's box, where the Gear lay in a folded cloth for fifty years (Ch49).
- The Gear's temperature behaviour is canon: ordinarily **cold**; it **warms** near fractured boundary stones and the door depression (Ch38/Ch40/Ch47/Ch49).
- **Reconciliation (Ch49):** Vale's box is empty of the Gear; the gear Leon carried and the gear previously kept in the box are **the same Gear**. The box held it for 50 years before Vale set it where Leon would find it.
- **CUSTODY MODEL — CONFIRMED (2026-09-03).** Vale did **not** hand Leon the Gear as keeper equipment. Vale entrusted Leon with **one personal box** — his own private property, not a parish or Order archive — containing folded papers, a bundle of letters, a parish map with a corner missing, two stoppered bottles labelled in an unknown hand, a brass button, his wife's ring, and the empty cloth in which the Gear had lain. Vale's instruction is custody, not investiture: "These are mine. Not the parish's. Not the Order's. Mine." / "There is no one to leave them to." / "Keep the box." He states plainly that some contents are nothing, that some he never understood in fifty years, and that he cannot say which is which. He does **not** answer "Why me?"
  - The **Gear reached Leon separately** — he found it beneath the stopped clock at the mill (Ch36), before Vale's box was given. Vale had placed it "where a thing must be found and not given."
  - Leon's title is therefore **custody arising from personal trust**, never institutional ownership. "The keeper's key" phrasing is retired from the Gear (Ch129). The surviving "keeper's key" in canon is the **tower/bell key** (Ch74, 73, 80, 114) — a different object.
  - **Order/Constable claim (Ch53, Ch64):** the Order's "the gear belongs to the keeping" is **doctrine, not title**. Leon's counter is factual: the Order did not know the Gear existed until he told them (Ch53), and "a thing you did not know of is not a thing you were keeping" (Ch64). Vale's ownership ≠ Order's ownership.
  - **Ch129:** Leon passes custody to Daniel in the same register — "Vale kept it for fifty years, in a box, among his letters and his papers, and then he put it where I would find it and never told me why… Now you will carry it." Explicitly: "I did not tell him it was his. I told him he would carry it." Chain = **custody, not ownership**: Vale → personal trust → Leon → deliberate trust → Daniel.
  - **UNKNOWN:** why Vale possessed the Gear; how he obtained it; whether he understood its function; why he chose Leon; whether he expected Leon to investigate it; the Gear's status beyond Vale's private ownership.
  - **VALE BACKGROUND (CONFIRMED 2026-09-03):** Vale was a veteran, then a Constable, then a senior figure in Constable intelligence and counter-intelligence, investigating internal compromise — including indications that Order members or associates had infiltrated parts of the Constable. He was demoted and transferred to Ashford under an innocuous official reason. This reframes the Gear's concealment: **PROPOSED** — he moved it out of anywhere searchable because people connected to a compromised Constable and/or the Order were looking for it. **UNKNOWN** — whether he intended Leon specifically to find it. Intended reader question: *"Did Vale hide the Gear so Leon would find it, or so nobody else would?"* Do not answer. Full profile: `../04_CHARACTERS/characters/Thomas_Vale.md`.
  - **Constable interest in Vale's effects is deliberately double-read:** institutional recovery of records, or one person's wish to learn what Vale retained. Do not resolve.
- The Gear fits a depression in the stone door beneath the mill and is associated with the lock/mechanism (Ch57).
- The Gear is **not** the key; the keeper's sign is the key (Ch57). The Gear is the lock/marker.
- The Door is beneath the mill / chamber area (not at Leon's residence).
- The Gear is passed to **Daniel** in Ch129 — wrapped, placed in Daniel's bag, with Leon telling only part of the truth and explicitly transferring **custody rather than ownership** ("I did not tell him it was his. I told him he would carry it."). Daniel does **not** understand its function. Leon's stated reason is the continuation of the keeping; his withheld reason is that the Gear has been asked after by a stranger and that he will not always be in that room.
- **INSCRIPTION — CONFIRMED FROM Ch36.** The confirmed Gear bears a cryptic inscription from its first appearance.
  - **Canonical text:** *"The mouth speaks before the ear receives."*
  - **Status:** CONFIRMED. **Meaning:** UNKNOWN. **Function:** UNKNOWN. **Ritual relationship:** PROPOSED.
  - **Physical placement:** shallow, unusually precise characters cut in a narrow band around the outer face, following the rim; worn at the crests but legible. Compatible with the 14–15 cm diameter. Archaic letter-forms; the words themselves are readable by Leon without special knowledge, and the manuscript adds no explanation for why.
  - **Second marking:** the keeper's sign (circle crossed by a short line) near the centre, worn almost flat — unresolved at the mill (Ch36), identified under lamplight in Ch38.
  - **Ch49 (Vale):** confirms the sentence is genuine, unchanged in fifty years, and is not a name, date, or maker's mark. Neither Vale nor Leon can determine its purpose. There is **no second hidden message** and no answering inscription in the door's depression. The inscription exists; it is not explained.
  - **Ch129 (Daniel):** Daniel finds and reads the sentence. Leon states plainly that he does not know what it means, that Vale did not either, and instructs Daniel not to decide its meaning out of impatience. Leon withholds the depression's location, the second door, and his own late suspicion that the sentence sounds like one half of an exchange.
  - Do NOT interpret the sentence as ritual, instruction, or Door mechanic in canon. It may later prove to be an instruction, a ritual sequence, a resonance principle, a warning, a description of communication, or something more fundamental — all PROPOSED.
- The second door/depression in the underground passage (Ch57) is shaped to accommodate the Gear.
- **Obsolete development references:** Any older WORLD_BIBLE or development text stating "two Gears exist" is superseded by the Ch49 reconciliation. The manuscript establishes a single confirmed Gear.

## V. GEAR DEFINITION (Semantic Lock)

**Gear = a specialized Artifact category.**

- Gear is a physical supernatural/esoteric object.
- Gear is associated with the Door and the vanished ancient organization.
- Gear is NOT an ERC.
- Gear is NOT a Token.
- Gear is NOT a Sign or Rank.
- Gear is NOT a generic "Artifact" — it is a subcategory with specific narrative and functional constraints.

**ERC relationship:**
- An ERC record may document a Gear, but the Gear itself does not become an ERC.
- Correct: "An ERC record describes the Gear." Incorrect: "The Gear is an ERC."

---

## VI. GEAR vs ARTIFACT vs TOKEN vs ERC

| Term | Meaning | Category |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Artifact** | Physical supernatural/esoteric object | Object Category |
| **Gear** | Specialized Artifact associated with the Door & ancient organization | Artifact Subcategory |
| **Token** | Distinct supernatural Artifact (access/connection/organizational) | Artifact Category |
| **ERC** | Esoteric Record Classification — record/classificatory framework | Record/Classificatory Framework |

- Artifact is the broadest. Gear and Token are subsets.
- ERC is not an object category; it is a record framework.

- These labels are for development only. Characters must never say "Gear One" or "Gear B."

**Position of confirmed Gear:** UNRESOLVED. It may occupy one of the nine positions (candidate: recognition/initial interaction), but this is not established.

## IX. CRYPTIC INSCRIPTION SYSTEM

**Purpose:** Provide reader satisfaction and layered mystery.

**Design rules:**
- Every Gear bears an inscription (or set of markings).
- The inscription must not simply explain the Gear's function.
- It should be interpretable in multiple ways.
- It may reference: absence, thresholds, memory, witnesses, separation, return, names, warnings.
- Avoid: "OPENS THE DOOR", "GEAR III", "VOICE".

**Layered interpretation:**
- Layer 1: What Leon/characters see (surface reading).
- Layer 2: What an educated historian might interpret (historical/philosophical).
- Layer 3: What a knowledgeable supernatural investigator infers (clue to function).
- Layer 4: The actual meaning within the ancient organization's context.
- Layer 5: Deeper meaning revealed much later (may revise earlier assumptions).

**Implementation:** Each Gear's inscription must be recorded in its profile with these layers, but only Layer 1 is revealed at discovery; later layers appear as the story progresses.

**Script:** A consistent archaic script or language family. Variation in form (e.g., dialect, hand) is allowed.

## X. MAKER'S MOTIF

**Concept:** The Gears share a subtle recurring symbol or design signature that identifies them as part of the same system.

- It should not be immediately obvious; it should become recognizable only after multiple Gears are seen.
- Each Gear contains a variant or partial version of the motif.
- The motif may be an abstract shape, a geometric pattern, or a simplified version of the first sign (the maker's sign).
- **Author-side note:** The motif should be distinct enough that a reader can later trace it across descriptions.

**Design rule:** Do not make it a simple logo. It should feel like a ancient craftsperson's signature.

## XI. GEAR DISCOVERY FRAMEWORK

**Every Gear discovery is a story event.** It must include:
- The location and why the Gear was there.
- The appearance and immediate sensory impression.
- What makes it unusual (beyond being a Gear).
- Leon's initial reaction and any misinterpretation.
- The obstacle or complication to acquisition.
- The new question created.

**Acquisition mechanisms (variety required):**
- Auction, private collection, antiquarian dealer, abandoned property, archaeological find, inheritance, church/parish archive, faction-controlled, criminal intermediary, trade, negotiation, theft, recovery mission, unknowing owner.

**Rule:** No two Gears should be acquired in the same manner unless narratively justified.

---

## XII. ACQUISITION FRAMEWORK (Including Unknowing Owner)

**Unknowing Owner:** A person who possesses a Gear without understanding its significance. They may believe it is:
- An antique, a family heirloom, a religious object, a curiosity, worthless metal, a historical relic.

**Narrative potential:**
- Leon must investigate, negotiate, trade, or perform a favor.
- The owner may become a recurring character or even a future organization member.
- This creates information asymmetry and moral complexity.

**Transaction types:**
- Purchase, trade (another artifact/information), favor, protection, deception (allowing a harmless misunderstanding), solving a problem for the owner.

**Rule:** Leon should not simply take the Gear by force unless absolutely justified.

**Status:** PROPOSED — NOT CONFIRMED.

**Classification:** Artifact (NOT Gear, NOT Token, NOT ERC).

**Function:** Detects or indicates the direction/proximity of Gears. Imperfect by design.

**Limitations (PROPOSED):**

**Discovery/Acquisition:** Another character/ member may find it unknowingly; Leon recognizes its significance and must negotiate/trade for it.

## XIV. GEAR → DOOR PROGRESSION (PROPOSED FRAMEWORK)

The Gears progressively unlock Door capabilities. This is a **development framework**, not confirmed sequence.

| Stage | Effect | Gear Requirement |
|-------|--------|------------------|
| 1. Recognition | Door acknowledges presence; inert response | Candidate: confirmed Gear |
| 2. One-way voice | Sound can be transmitted one way | One Gear (communication-oriented) |
| 3. Two-way voice | Two-way communication | Another Gear |
| 4. Limited vision | Visual perception through the connection | Gear with sight-related function |
| 5. Remote sensing | Environmental awareness beyond the threshold | Gear for sensing |
| 6. Limited interaction | Ability to affect something beyond | Gear for interaction |
| 7. Partial crossing | Temporary/controlled crossing | Gear for threshold manipulation |
| 8. Persistent access | Stable connection over time | Gear for anchoring |
| 9. UNKNOWN | Final state — deliberately unresolved | All Gears? |

**Rules:**
- Stages are not rigid; some may be skipped or reordered.
- Each stage must carry cost/risk.
- Leon (and reader) should not know the full sequence in advance.

## XVI. GEOGRAPHIC EXPANSION

Gears are not confined to Ashford. They may be found in:
- Other towns, cities, rural estates, coastal areas, universities, archives, churches, private collections, industrial ruins.

**Rule:** Every new location must have its own local history, characters, and independent mystery. The Gear is the reason to go there; the location becomes a story in its own right.

## XVII. CHARACTER & ORGANIZATION INTEGRATION

**Future Members:** Organization members (via the anonymous Token network) may assist in Gear acquisition. They need not understand the full truth. This creates:
- Partial knowledge, trust dynamics, negotiation, mistakes, independent agency, potential betrayal.

**Factions:** Different factions may hold fragments of knowledge. The Order of Severence may view Gears as boundary relics without understanding the nine; the nameless society may have records (UNRESOLVED). Information asymmetry is intentional.

---

## XVIII. KNOWLEDGE BOUNDARIES

For every major Gear revelation, maintain:

- **Leon Knows** (what he has confirmed)
- **Leon Suspects** (his leading hypothesis)
- **Leon Does Not Know** (gaps in his understanding)
- **Leon Believes Incorrectly** (false assumptions)
- **Other Characters Know** (fragments)
- **Reader Suspects** (what a careful reader may infer)
- **Actual Truth** (author-side, may be UNRESOLVED)

This prevents accidental omniscience and maintains mystery.

## XIX. READER REVEAL ARCHITECTURE

**Phased revelation:**
1. **EARLY:** Gear appears as an unusual antique/mechanical object.
2. **MIDDLE:** Gear is connected to a Door.
3. **LATER:** Gear has individual functions; multiple Gears may exist.
4. **LATER:** Gears share a maker/tradition; connected to ancient organization.
5. **LATE:** The Gear system may not have been designed merely to open the Door.
6. **ENDGAME:** True purpose substantially clearer (never total).

**Rule:** Do not dump system explanation; let the reader discover with Leon.

## XX. ANCIENT ORGANIZATION MYSTERY

The Gears are a primary clue to the vanished ancient organization. Hypotheses (all PROPOSED, none confirmed):
- The Gears were safeguards.
- They were components of a communication system.
- They were part of a containment mechanism.
- The Door was not meant to be opened.
- The Door was designed to remember.
- The organization vanished because they opened something.
- Or they refused to open something.
- They intentionally scattered the Gears.
- They were separated after a catastrophe.

**Rule:** Maintain multiple possibilities. Reveal evidence that supports conflicting interpretations.

## XXI. FINAL ASSEMBLY POSSIBILITIES

**(All PROPOSED; author decision required.)**

| Model | Description | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|-------|-------------|-----------|------------|
| A. The Door Remembers | Completion does not open the Door; it completes the account/record | Honors "not opened but remembered"; recontextualizes Arc 1 | Risks feeling passive |
| B. The Door Opens | The Door becomes traversable | Escalation, danger | Risks conventional payoff |
| C. The Gears Were Seals | Assembly re-locks what has been loosening | Inverts the quest; separation is sacred | Requires careful seeding |
| D. Communication Mechanism | The Gears form a device to contact something beyond | Mystery of the "other side" | Could feel like a radio |
| E. Summoning | Assembly triggers a event (the bell's call, the count) | Unifies with established mysteries (count, bell, 00:00) | Risk of over-resolution |
| F. Reveal Truth | The Gears reveal the fate of the ancient organization | Strong mystery payoff | Could be exposition-heavy |
| G. Leon Does Not Know | Leon completes the system without knowing the outcome | Maximum mystery; character-driven tension | Requires disciplined withholding |

**Recommended posture:** G (Leon does not know) as the primary narrative engine, with one of A–F as the hidden truth to be revealed later.

## XXII. FAILURE / COST / CONSEQUENCE DESIGN

Every meaningful use of a Gear must carry a cost or risk:
- Information exposure (something on the other side learns about Leon).
- Psychological pressure (fatigue, disorientation, memory loss).
- Attracting attention (from factions, entities, or the watcher).
- Temporary sensory effects (blurring, hearing loss, visual distortion).
- Environmental consequences (temperature drop, stone cracking, disturbances).
- Misunderstanding (incorrect interpretation leads to wrong action).
- Dependency (Leon becomes reliant on the Gear).
- Danger from what answers from the other side.

**Avoid:** Simple "mana cost" or HP loss. Prefer narrative consequences.
