### 1. LEON ASHCROFT

**CURRENT CANON VISUAL DETAILS:** Mid-20s; average build; practical coat/vest; carries a notebook; dresses for walking. Nothing else established.

**PROPOSED VISUAL DETAILS:** 5'8"–5'10"; fair, weathered; dark brown hair; grey/hazel eyes; ordinary face; dark tailcoat, buff waistcoat, linen shirt, dark trousers, worn boots, round hat, greatcoat in winter.

**WHAT IS STRONG**
- The "forgettable face / moving eyes" tension supports a narrator who observes but is not the center of attention.
- The notebook as an "extension of his arm" is the single best visual anchor — it ties to his established behavior.
- Practical, slightly worn clothing is consistent with a modest ward who walks constantly.

**WHAT FEELS GENERIC**
- "Grey/hazel eyes" and "dark brown hair" are the default visual vocabulary. His eyes should be tied to behavior, not just colour.
- "Ordinary, not striking" risks making him visually bland in a novel where he is present in every scene.

**WHAT MAY CONFLICT WITH CHARACTERIZATION**
- He is a ward/orphan who inherited property, described as fastidious about notes and order ("things that went according to plan"). Worn clothing is fine, but he would likely keep himself tidy — neatness is part of his psychology. Avoid "dishevelled."
- "Slightly stooped from reading" could overstate; he is a walker more than a desk-bound scholar.

**WHAT MAY FEEL ANACHRONISTIC**
- "Round hat" is fine for 1816. "Dark blue or brown wool tailcoat" — in 1816 a tailcoat for a modest young man is plausible, but he would more likely wear a plain surtout/frock or a working coat. Tailcoat leans slightly formal for his class. Prefer "frock coat" or "travelling coat."

**WHAT MAY OVERSTATE THE CHARACTER'S MYSTERY**
- Nothing — Leon should NOT read as mysterious. Keep him legible.

**WHAT SHOULD BE SIMPLIFIED**
- Drop the "slightly below average height / average build / slightly stooped" triple qualification. One posture note is enough.
- Drop eye colour ambiguity (grey *or* hazel) — pick one.

**RECOMMENDED FINAL VERSION**
> Mid-20s, middling height and build; a neat, plain young man in a dark travelling coat, buff waistcoat, and much-worn boots. Brown hair kept short, no beard. His face is ordinary and would be forgotten — until one notices that his eyes are never still, moving from doorway to window to face, cataloguing. He carries a small notebook and a pencil, and his fingertips are faintly marked with ink. He stands straight, keeps himself tidy, and looks at a speaker fully when addressed.

### 2. SILAS CROWE

**CURRENT CANON:** Order of Severence field contact. No visual data.

**PROPOSED:** Late 40s; lean, not tall; sallow; dark receding hair oiled flat; heavy-lidded dark eyes; dark coat, matching waistcoat, cravat, clean boots.

**WHAT IS STRONG**
- "Lets himself into rooms without knocking" and "watches the speaker's mouth" are excellent, specific, and non-visual-cliché.
- Stillness as his signature differentiates him from Graves (who trembles and polishes spectacles).

**WHAT FEELS GENERIC**
- "Dark coat, dark hair, dark eyes" — the entire profile is built on darkness. This is exactly the "mysterious man in dark clothing" default the task warns against.
- "Sallow complexion" + "heavy-lidded eyes" is a stock sinister figure.

**WHAT MAY CONFLICT WITH CHARACTERIZATION**
- A field agent who "moves between rooms without announcing himself" would wear ordinary, forgettable clothing, NOT a matching coat-and-waistcoat ensemble that draws attention. His power is being unremarkable.
- He interacts with Leon openly and is not portrayed as sinister in the early chapters — he is a source of information and warning. Visually coding him as villainous is premature.

**WHAT MAY FEEL ANACHRONISTIC** — Nothing specific; "oiled flat" hair is period-plausible.

**WHAT MAY OVERSTATE THE CHARACTER'S MYSTERY** — Yes. The heavy-lidded, sallow, dark-coat image overstates the Order's menace before the manuscript earns it.

**WHAT SHOULD BE SIMPLIFIED** — Remove "sallow." Make his coat ordinary (grey/brown), not coordinated. Remove "oiled flat" if it implies vanity — a man who doesn't want to be noticed wouldn't oil his hair.

**RECOMMENDED FINAL VERSION**
> A man in his late forties of middling height and thin build, dressed to be passed over: a drab grey-brown coat, a plain neckcloth, boots that make no sound. His hair is dark and receding, combed without fuss. His face is ordinary; his eyes are the only unusual feature — dark, still, and given to watching the mouth of the person speaking rather than their eyes. He has a way of being present in a room without having been seen to enter it, and of holding silence as though it were a tool he carries.

### 3. JAMES CALLOWAY

**CURRENT CANON:** Constable of Alderwick; the man from the inn. Almost no visual data.

**PROPOSED:** Early 50s; solid; weathered; grey thinning hair; pale blue careful eyes; dark blue/brown coat, waistcoat, boots, round hat, concealed truncheon.

**WHAT IS STRONG**
- "Speaks slowly, as if he has learned not to commit" matches a cautious parish constable.
- Weather-beaten, planted stance reads believably official without being supernatural.

**WHAT FEELS GENERIC** — "Pale eyes, careful" is mild; fine.

**WHAT MAY CONFLICT WITH CHARACTERIZATION** — A parish constable in 1816 is a local man doing a part-time civic duty, not a uniformed officer. A "concealed truncheon" is plausible but slightly theatrical; parish constables used a staff. Keep it minimal. He should read as a tradesman-farmer who also keeps the peace, not a professional policeman.

**WHAT MAY FEEL ANACHRONISTIC** — "Truncheon" is acceptable but "constable's formal hat" must be avoided (already avoided). The dark-blue coat is fine.

**WHAT MAY OVERSTATE THE CHARACTER'S MYSTERY** — No. He should look ordinary and reliable.

**WHAT SHOULD BE SIMPLIFIED** — Reduce to: an ordinary solid man in his fifties, plainly dressed, with the unhurried wariness of local authority.

**RECOMMENDED FINAL VERSION**
> A solid, weathered man in his early fifties, plainly dressed in a dark coat and worn boots — the sort of man who farms or trades as well as keeping the peace. His hair is grey and thinning, his face broad and sun-beaten, and his pale eyes measure a person slowly before he answers. He stands with his weight spread, rubs his jaw when thinking, and gives the impression of a man who has learned that most questions have more than one answer.

### 4. NATHANIEL GRAVES

**CURRENT CANON:** Order of Severence member (Purist/elder). No visual data.

**PROPOSED:** Early 60s; spare, stooped; thin white hair; grey fixed eyes; dated dark coat, high collar, cravat, watch chain, wire spectacles; thin freckled hands; slight left-hand tremor.

**WHAT IS STRONG**
- The dated coat (a decade out of fashion) is an excellent character cue — he values the old order even in dress.
- Watch chain, spectacles removed for emphasis, and slight tremor give him age and authority without occult coding.
- Distinguishes him strongly from Crowe (still/lean/dark) — Graves is old, pale, deliberate, bookish.

**WHAT FEELS GENERIC** — "Grey fixed eyes" is the weak point; "fixed" plus "examining" is abstract. Give him a concrete habit instead.

**WHAT MAY CONFLICT** — Nothing significant. Graves leads a faction meeting; he should carry some presence despite age.

**WHAT MAY FEEL ANACHRONISTIC** — Wire-rimmed spectacles are fine for 1816.

**WHAT MAY OVERSTATE MYSTERY** — Slight risk of the "cold scholarly villain" archetype. Mitigate by keeping him visibly human (the tremor, the dated coat).

**WHAT SHOULD BE SIMPLIFIED** — Replace "grey fixed eyes" with something plainer. Keep the tremor and the dated coat.

**RECOMMENDED FINAL VERSION**
> An old man in his sixties, spare and a little stooped, in a dark coat cut in a fashion ten years out of date, worn as though it is the only coat he trusts. His hair is thin and white, his face long and lined; wire spectacles sit on his nose, and he takes them off to polish them when he wants his words to land. His left hand trembles faintly when he is tired. He holds himself like a man accustomed to being listened to.

### 5. THOMAS VALE

**CURRENT CANON:** Former constable, keeper of the Agreement, dies Ch 47. Very old. No visual data beyond "old man."

**PROPOSED:** 80s; thin, bent; papery pale; white wispy hair; pale milky-but-sharp eyes; much-mended brown coat, blanket, walking stick; knotted hands; dry whisper.

**WHAT IS STRONG** — "Eyes are the only vigorous thing about him" is excellent and supports the keeper reveal. "Holds the Agreement as if it is the only warm thing in the room" is strong.

**WHAT FEELS GENERIC** — "Papery pale," "gaunt," "white wispy hair" are the default very-old-man vocabulary. Acceptable for a deathbed figure but could be tightened.

**WHAT MAY CONFLICT** — Vale was a constable; a walking stick is fine, but avoid making him a "wise ancient" stereotype. He is guarded, withholding, and ambiguous — not merely venerable.

**WHAT MAY OVERSTATE MYSTERY** — Keep his Order/keeper affiliation ambiguous. The visual should not hint he is secretly powerful. He is a dying man who has kept a secret.

**RECOMMENDED FINAL VERSION**
> A very old man — eighty or beyond — thin and shrunken in an old brown coat that has been mended more than once. His hair is white and scant, his face deeply lined, and his hands are knotted with age. He moves little and speaks in a dry, careful voice; but when he looks at a person, his eyes are unexpectedly clear and steady, as if the only part of him that has not aged is whatever it is that watches. A folded packet of papers lies always within reach.

### 6. HELEN MARGARET ASHCROFT

**CURRENT CANON:** Housekeeper/servant figure; greets Leon on arrival; watches him "as if she already knew me." Family relationship to Leon UNRESOLVED in manuscript (name shares "Ashcroft").

**PROPOSED:** Late 40s–50s; spare; grey-streaked hair in a bun; dark plain dress, apron, cap; still posture; economical speech.

**WHAT IS STRONG** — Stillness, economy of speech, hands always occupied. Grounded and believable.

**WHAT FEELS GENERIC** — "Dark eyes that see more than they let on" is the stock servant-who-knows-secrets trope.

**WHAT MAY CONFLICT** — If Helen is a relative (shared surname) rather than a servant, "apron and cap" misreads her standing. The manuscript is ambiguous about her exact household role and relationship. The visual must not settle it.

**WHAT SHOULD BE SIMPLIFIED** — Drop "eyes that see more than they let on." Give her the quality of knowing Leon's habits before he tells her — which the manuscript already implies.

**RECOMMENDED FINAL VERSION**
> A woman of perhaps fifty, spare and composed, dressed plainly in dark wool with an apron and a white cap. She keeps herself tidy without ornament. She moves quietly, speaks little, and has a way of having things ready — the fire lit, the tea poured — before being asked. She looks at Leon fully when she speaks to him, and there is something in that look that suggests she has been expecting him for longer than he has known to come.

### 7. MARGARET ELEANOR WHITMORE (the baker)

**CURRENT CANON:** Local shopkeeper/baker; recurring town voice ("I agreed to nothing," Ch 77). Age roughly established as an older woman.

**PROPOSED:** Early 50s; sturdy; ruddy; grey hair under cap; frank blue/grey eyes; linen apron over wool dress; flour-dusted forearms; strong hands; voice that carries.

**WHAT IS STRONG** — Flour-dusted forearms, voice that carries, frankness. This avoids the "mysterious old woman" archetype completely. Keep her this way.

**WHAT FEELS GENERIC** — Almost nothing; this is the most grounded profile.

**WHAT MAY CONFLICT** — Minor: "blue or grey" eyes — pick one. She should not be given any knowing glance at the supernatural; she is a civilian who insists she agreed to nothing.

**RECOMMENDED FINAL VERSION**
> A sturdy woman in her early fifties with a round, ruddy face and grey hair escaping from under a white cap. Her sleeves are rolled and her forearms dusted with flour even when she has left the bake-house. She has strong hands, a direct gaze, and a voice used to being heard over the market noise. She stands with her arms folded when she has something to say, and she says it plainly.

### 8. ARTHUR EDMUND BELL (bookseller / verger)

**CURRENT CANON:** Bookseller; also associated with the church (verger/sexton role); established as observant with sharp eyes; spectacles; ink/dust-stained; stooped.

**PROPOSED:** 50s–60s; stooped; pale; grey thin hair; sharp observant eyes; worn coat, leather apron, wire spectacles; ink-stained fingers; tilts head when listening; touches book spines.

**WHAT IS STRONG** — The existing physical details are preserved and extended naturally (ink-stained fingers, leather apron, spectacles). Head-tilting and touching book spines are distinctive and character-true.

**WHAT FEELS GENERIC** — Very little. "Pale indoor complexion" is fine for a bookseller.

**WHAT MAY CONFLICT** — His church/verger role may mean he is out of doors more than a pure bookseller; avoid making him pasty to the point of infirmity.

**RECOMMENDED FINAL VERSION**
> A stooped man in his fifties or sixties, in a coat that carries dust and old paper in its folds, with a leather apron over his waistcoat when he is working. Wire spectacles sit on a narrow nose; his grey hair is thin and untidy. His hands are stained at the fingertips with ink, and he has a habit of tilting his head and touching the spine of a book as he speaks of it, as though every volume were an acquaintance.

### 10. YSABEL NOTT

**CURRENT CANON:** The woman in the dark coat; hunted the clocks for ~20 years. Age implied 40s+.

**PROPOSED:** Late 40s; lean, durable; weathered; dark hair streaked grey pinned back; dark searching eyes; long practical dark coat with many pockets, boots, soft hat.

**WHAT IS STRONG** — "A face that could be any age... held with authority," the coat with many pockets, and the direct gaze all work. Her darkness is justified — she is literally "the woman in the dark coat."

**WHAT FEELS GENERIC** — Dark coat + dark hair + weathered + searching eyes is a lot of "dark." But it is canon-correct (the dark coat is her identifier). Keep the coat; vary the rest.

**WHAT MAY CONFLICT** — She is a woman travelling alone for 20 years — her clothing should show repair and practicality, not drama.

**RECOMMENDED FINAL VERSION**
> A woman whose age is hard to read — perhaps late forties — with a weathered face and dark hair pulled back and streaked with grey. She wears a long, serviceable dark coat, mended at the cuffs and fitted with more pockets than a coat should need, and sturdy boots that have walked a great many roads. Her gaze is direct and unhurried; she looks at a person as though she has learned to wait for the truth of them. When she speaks she states facts without preamble.

### 11. ELIAS MARR

**CURRENT CANON:** Collector of erased objects; keeper of the book of origins. Elderly.

**PROPOSED:** 60s; slight, frail; pale; white thin uncut hair; pale grey faraway eyes; too-large inherited coat, misbuttoned waistcoat, slippers; looks at objects not people; whisper; long pale fingers.

**WHAT IS STRONG** — The misbuttoned waistcoat, looking at objects rather than people, and the whisper are distinctive and memorable.

**WHAT FEELS GENERIC** — "Pale grey faraway eyes" is the weak point. Frailty risks reading as a ghostly figure; he is a living collector, not a spectre.

**WHAT MAY CONFLICT** — He has kept the book safely for years; he is capable despite age. Do not make him seem feeble in a way that contradicts his custodianship.

**RECOMMENDED FINAL VERSION**
> An elderly man in a coat that was never his size, with a waistcoat that he buttons wrong more often than not. His hair is white and uncut, his face thin. He does not look at a visitor when he speaks; he looks at the object in his hand or on the table, as though conversation must compete with things for his attention. His voice is soft, almost a whisper, and his fingers are long and very clean.

### 13. THE OLD MAN (society elder)

**CURRENT CANON:** Presides over nameless society; older than Vale.

**PROPOSED:** Very old; thin; seated; pale; white hair; pale eyes; black coat, white cravat, blanket over knees; disciplined stillness; thin white hands.

**WHAT IS STRONG** — The "stillness of discipline, not age" is good.

**WHAT FEELS GENERIC** — Black coat + white hair + white cravat + very old = the stock "elder of secrets." Needs softening.

**WHAT MAY CONFLICT** — He should not be costumed as an occult patriarch; he runs a preservation society, closer to a scholar or antiquarian collector.

**RECOMMENDED FINAL VERSION**
> A very old man who receives visitors seated, with a shawl or blanket across his knees. He wears a dark, well-kept coat and a plain white cravat. His face is deeply lined, his hair white, but his stillness is not the stillness of frailty — it is the stillness of a man who has learned to waste nothing, not even motion. He lets visitors speak to the end before answering, and his voice, though thin, is exact.

## C. HISTORICAL CLOTHING CONCERNS (Britain, 1816)

1. **Leon's "tailcoat"** — A tailcoat (evening dress) is too formal for a modest walking young man. Use **frock coat or a plain greatcoat/surtout**. (P2)
2. **Crowe's coordinated coat + matching waistcoat** — "Matching" sets is more 1830s–1840s menswear formality. For 1816 a field agent wears contrast waistcoat (often lighter). Simpler: uncoordinated, drab. (P2)
3. **Margaret's "wooden clogs"** — Clogs were worn by some labourers/market women but were regional; "sturdy leather shoes" is safer for a baker working in a shop. (P3)
4. **Elias Marr's "slippers"** — Slippers indoors for an elderly recluse are plausible 1816. Acceptable.
5. **Helen's cap + apron** — Correct servant/housekeeper dress for 1816. Fine.
6. **Ysabel's "soft-brimmed hat"** — Plausible for travel. Fine.
7. **Calloway's "round hat"** — A parish constable off-duty would wear a round/beaver hat. Fine.
8. **"Trousers" for Leon** — Trousers (vs breeches) were increasingly common by 1816 for day wear. Acceptable.
9. **Vale's "blanket over shoulders"** — Plausible for an aged man indoors. Fine.
10. **Old man's "black coat"** — Black was formal/mourning in 1816 (not yet the universal men's colour it became later). Prefer **dark blue or dark green** for a scholar-elder, or accept black if it implies formality. (P3)

## D. GENERIC / REPETITIVE DESIGN CONCERNS

1. **"Sallow + dark + heavy-lidded" for Crowe** is the single most generic piece in the set — remove sallow, de-emphasize darkness.
2. **"Grey or hazel eyes" (Leon)** — choose one; grey eyes have become the default for protagonists.
3. **"Eyes that see more than they let on" (Helen)** — stock; replace with a behavioral cue (she has things ready).
4. **"Pale grey faraway eyes" (Elias)** — replace with the averted-gaze behavior already proposed.
5. **Avoid future reuse of:** "sharp eyes," "tired eyes," "angular face," "quiet presence," "mysterious gaze," "dark coat." After the recommended changes, only Ysabel (canonically) and the tally-keeper (intentionally) remain dark-coated.
6. **Only one character should be visually unreadable** (the tally-keeper). The stranger should be readable-but-forgettable, not mysterious.
