## E0. THE STRUCTURAL PROBLEM, STATED HONESTLY

The post-Vale period is **not empty**. It is already written.

Vale dies before dawn early in Ch51 (October 1817). Chapters 52–141 cover the five years that follow: Leon becomes keeper, the watch becomes past tense, the Order confronts and then withdraws, the nameless society tests and recruits him, Ysabel Nott arrives, the clocks and the key and the lock are found, the count is understood, the keeping is passed to Daniel Wren, and Leon walks north and ends at the sea.

Two canon facts constrain everything below:

1. **The watch is over.** From Ch81 the manuscript is consistent and explicit: *"I had done it as a watchman, before the spring"* (Ch81) · *"You were a watchman, and you watched. Now you are not a watchman, and you still watch"* (Ch87) · *"who had been a watchman before the spring"* (Ch88). There is no discharge scene; the role simply lapses. Leon's paid engagement with Calloway is last referenced retrospectively at Ch62.
2. **Leon leaves Alderwick at Ch130** and is away for Ch131–141.

**Therefore a Constable field-team arc cannot simply be appended to "after Vale."** It has to be placed. The placement options are in `../15_METADATA/POST_VALE_INSERTION_MAP.md`; this document specifies the *development* and is written to work in either window.

**Do not write a single chapter until the window is chosen.** The window changes what Leon is when the arc starts, and that changes everything on this page.

## E1. STARTING STATE

### What he has lost
Vale, in October, having been visited across eight months without either of them ever naming what it was. The recognition is **posthumous by design** (Ch51–52): the half-finished cup, the empty chair, the heavier coat refused twice and taken on the fifth day, and the realisation that Vale asked whether he had eaten every single visit and Leon only noticed once there was no one left to ask.

### How the grief is written
**Structurally, not stated.** The rules:
- **No melodrama.** No collapse, no speech about loss, no scene of weeping. Leon's grief appears as *changed procedure*: he writes more, he writes it in his own book instead of the town's (Ch52), he goes to the house at the end of the lane and does not go in.
- **The chair stays empty.** Nobody replaces Vale. **Calloway must not become the new mentor** — his register is employer and predecessor's successor, and the whole of the intimacy is that he tells Leon *about* Vale (Ch107, Ch121). He never asks whether Leon has eaten.
- **He cannot explain the loss to anyone.** Owen never met Vale. Cecily never met Vale. Orme has heard the name and thinks it dull. **This is the point.** The team's early scenes contain a man carrying something none of them can see, and the reader is the only one who can see it.
- Vale's own last instruction governs the whole arc: *"You do not owe the dead your life. Remember them. Learn from them. But do not become their unfinished business."* (Ch50) — **and Leon proceeds to spend years becoming exactly that, which is the arc's quiet irony and must never be underlined.**

### What the peers see when he arrives

| | Their reading | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| Useful | yes | yes |
| Intelligent | yes, irritatingly | yes |
| Experienced in field danger | no | no |
| Physically capable | no | no — average, walks a great deal, not athletic |
| Naturally authoritative | no | no, and never becomes so |
| Frightened | they assume not | often |
| Grieving | invisible to them | continuously |

He is not the protagonist to them. He is the constable's expensive hobby.

## E2. THE TEN STAGES

Each stage has a **gate** — the specific thing that must be true before the next stage may begin. Do not skip gates. Do not run two stages in one chapter.

### 1. He enters field operations
Not as a fighter and not as an authority. As the man Orme sends in *after*, to write down where everything was.
His first field failure is not dramatic: he takes a good record of a room that had already been tidied, and does not think to ask who tidied it. Cecily asks. He notices that she asked.
**Gate:** he has produced one document that was materially useless and knows why.

### 2. He makes a meaningful mistake
**Design requirements — all four are mandatory:**
- It is a mistake **only he could have made** — arising from his method, not from clumsiness. He follows a chronology, is right about the chronology, and wrong about what it implies.
- **Someone else pays.** Not Leon. Owen, most likely, and physically. The reader must feel that Leon's cleverness cost a body.
- **Nobody absolves him.** Orme does not forgive him; Orme was overruled and says so once. Owen does not reassure him.
- **It is arguable.** A careful reader should be able to defend Leon's reasoning. It was not stupid. It was insufficient.

The mistake's specific shape: **he treats a corroborated claim as a confirmed one** — exactly Orme's sin, committed by Leon, which is why Orme's contempt afterwards is unbearable and fair.
**Gate:** an injury, and no absolution.

### 3. He learns from it
Not by resolving to be careful. By **changing an instrument**. Vale's method, arrived at by consequence: he adds a column. *What I did not see and looked for.* The recorded null — the same discipline that produced *"In every test I could devise, it behaved as a cold piece of iron behaves"* (Ch36).
**Gate:** the change is visible on the page in the form of his notes, and he never says why he made it.

### 4. He identifies a pattern others miss
The pattern must be **available to the reader** in advance — assembled from things already on the page across several chapters. No off-page deduction. No revelation from a source nobody else could reach.
It should be a pattern in **time**, because chronology is his specific faculty: the order of events, or an interval that repeats, or a thing that happened twice with the same twenty-minute gap. Alderwick's own precedent is the November 13th–21st recurrence in Calloway's drawer of old reports (Ch8) — *"The reports only ever watched the dates. They never once watched the watchers."*
**Gate:** he is right, publicly, in front of Orme.

### 5. The team begins to trust the analysis
**Not him. The analysis.** The distinction is the whole stage.
Owen starts asking which door. Cecily starts writing her findings in a form that will fit his columns. Orme does not change, and says so.
**Gate:** somebody acts on his reading without being persuaded first.

### 6. His analysis affects tactical decisions
He does not give orders. He gives **sequence and position**: which door first, who waits, at what hour, and what the second thing will be. Orme still commands; Orme now asks first.
**Gate:** a plan is altered on his word, and it works, and nobody thanks him.

### 7. He learns practical field survival
Explicitly and only mundane. From Owen, over months, badly and reluctantly: where to stand in a room, how to leave one, how to hold a lantern so it does not blind you, how not to be between a frightened man and a door, how to fall.
From Faulds: how a hinge falls, which way a door opens, what a forced lock looks like, how long a lock actually takes.
From Denby: what an injury costs, in the specific and unsentimental sense of what it does to a body for the following six weeks.
From Vale, posthumously: four roads · never twice by the same street · never carry paper in daylight · come at dusk (Ch61).
**Gate:** he survives something without Owen having to do it for him.

### 8. He receives equipment or assistance — only when justified
Nothing is issued. There is no equipment officer and no armoury. Everything is *earned, bought, borrowed, or made*, and each item must be accounted for:
- a dark lantern
- a heavier coat — **and this one is not equipment; it is Vale's, refused twice and taken on the fifth day (Ch52), and it must never be commented on again**
- tools from Faulds, paid for
- a stick, because everyone has a stick
- **no firearm.** A pistol solves the wrong problem and would break Rule E3.
**Gate:** every item has a scene explaining where it came from.

### 9. He becomes capable in confrontation through knowledge and preparation
**Never through power inflation.** He wins by having been there in daylight the day before, by knowing which board is loose, by having arranged for someone to be at the other end of the passage, by knowing the hour the man he wants comes home, and by having chosen the room.
He remains, physically, a man of average strength who walks a great deal. **He must lose a straight fight if he is ever in one.**
**Gate:** a confrontation resolved before it begins, and the reader can see exactly how.

### 10. His analysis becomes his combat identity
```
PATTERN      what has happened before, and in what order
   ↓
PREDICTION   what will therefore happen next, and when
   ↓
POSITIONING  where each person must be standing for that to matter
   ↓
TIMING       the hour, the minute, the interval
   ↓
COORDINATION Owen stands where Leon told him, before he understands why
```
**The final gate, and the payoff of the entire arc: Owen Trant standing in the right place at the right minute, on trust, without an explanation.** That is Leon becoming dangerous. Nothing else needs to happen.

## E3. THE FOUR PROHIBITIONS

1. **He does not become the strongest fighter.** Ever. There is no training arc that ends in physical superiority.
2. **He is not promoted into leadership.** There are no ranks to be promoted into (`../17_ORGANIZATIONS/05_GOVERNMENT.md` §B0). His authority is only ever the authority of having been right recently, which decays.
3. **He gains no supernatural ability.** His Token warms — that is the *object* acting, existing canon since Ch66, and not a power he wields (`../19_SUPERNATURAL/01_ABILITY_ARCHITECTURE.md` D0).
4. **He does not become liked.** He becomes *relied upon*. These are different, and the difference is the character.

## E4. THE FIVE FAULTS THAT MUST NOT BE CURED

Leon's canon flaws are load-bearing and must survive the whole arc:

| Fault | It must keep costing him |
|---|---|
| Overthinks; hesitates to conclude | Orme goes ahead without him; someone waits too long |
| Overly cautious | He is late to the thing he predicted |
| **Tells too much when he is in company** — Ch53–54: *"Vale carried it for fifty years and told no one. You have carried it for a week and told the room"* | Every transaction with Hester Pinnock, whose price is a true fact about himself |
| Wants to trust people but is wary | He is wrong about Ingle in one direction or the other, and never finds out which |
| Cannot separate the record from the person in the next room | Cecily's findings; the family waiting for Orme's answer |

## E5. WHAT LEON LEARNS FROM WHOM, AFTER VALE

| Source | Lesson | Register |
|---|---|---|
| **Walter Denby** | **The difference between knowing a thing and being able to establish it.** Vale taught Leon to keep his categories apart; Denby teaches him what the categories are *for*. The single most important post-Vale lesson. | Refusal, repeated |
| Owen Trant | Rooms, doors, bodies, and the fact that a written word can be used against a man | Contempt, then habit |
| Cecily Pargeter | That a finding must carry its own doubt, and that being right confers nothing | Peer, equal, uncomfortable |
| Reuben Orme | That somebody has to answer to the family on Friday | Opposition, in good faith |
| Isaac Faulds | How closed things behave; capability without power | Trade, freely given |
| Hester Pinnock | That information has a price and he is paying it in the wrong currency | Transaction, fair, ruinous |
| Cuthbert Ingle | The institution, by demonstrated habit and declined explanation — Vale's teaching method, from a man who may not mean well | Ambiguous |
| James Calloway | *"To notice." / "And after that?" / "Decide."* (Ch46) — and *"Then you'll have to live with your decision"* | Employer |
| **Thomas Vale, posthumously** | The teaching set of Ch45; the journal read in order and not from the end; the second copy filed openly | Absent, and strongest |
| **Eleanor Shaw** | Nothing, for a long time. She teaches him only when he has stopped expecting her to. | Withheld |

## E6. RELATIONSHIP TO THE CLASSIFICATION FRAMEWORK

Leon's institutional arc *is* his growth into `../08_UNDERGROUND/03_ESOTERIC_RECORD_CLASSIFICATION.md` §21–25.

1. He begins believing a good argument should be enough. It is not.
2. He learns that his own conclusions are the least valuable column on the page.
3. He learns that his real function is **converting uncorroborable claims into material traces** — Owen's unwritten certainty, Cecily's sensation, Pinnock's yard talk — into something Denby could be asked to look at.
4. He learns to write **UNRESOLVED** and to sit with it, which Orme cannot and Ferrand would not.
5. And he keeps a private record that the institution will never see, because the institution cannot hold what he is actually accumulating. **That is his red book, and it already exists in canon from Ch52 onward:** *"I wrote it down. I did not write it in the town's book. I wrote it in my own."*

**The two-book structure — the office's book and his own — is Leon's post-Vale identity in one image, and it is Vale's second-copy doctrine turned inward.**
