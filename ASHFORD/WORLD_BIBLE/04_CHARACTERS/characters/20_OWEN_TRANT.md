## Origin in detail

Militia, enlisted at nineteen, discharged 1815 with the peace. Home service and garrison duty — he never fought a battle and is quietly, permanently ashamed of that, because the town assumes he did and he has never corrected anyone. What he actually learned in six years was crowd work: how a mob turns, how a frightened man moves before he moves, how to take a man off his feet without hurting him more than necessary, and how quickly a written word becomes a flogging.

Came home to a town that had got on perfectly well without him, to a trade his father had already given to a cousin.

## Personality

Direct, physical, quick, warm in the way a man is warm when he has decided to like you and unrecoverable when he has not. He fills a room without trying to. He does not brood, he does not explain himself, and he mistakes reflection for hesitation.

He is genuinely kind to frightened people and genuinely contemptuous of careful ones, and does not see the contradiction.

- **Dominant traits:** decisive, observant of people, physically confident, impatient
- **Virtues:** brave in a way that is not performance; loyal once committed; will not lie to a witness's face
- **Flaws:** acts before the picture is complete; a temper he keeps and pays for afterward; assumes the danger is a man
- **Worldview:** the world is people. Everything that happens, someone did.
- **Habit:** counts the exits on entering a room, without knowing he does it. Stands where he can see the door.
- **Speech:** short. Declarative. Does not soften. Asks one question at a time and waits.
- **Under pressure:** improves. He is better at eleven at night in a bad street than he is at noon in an office.

## THE DEFINING REFUSAL — he will not write

**Owen does not write anything down, ever, about anything he has seen.**

Not because he cannot write — he can, badly. Because in the militia he watched written words become punishments. The book was the thing that hanged you. A man said something, a clerk wrote it, and eight months later the paper was still there and the man's account of himself was not.

He will say what he saw. He will say it twice, the same way. He will not put his hand to it.

**Why this matters structurally:**
- It puts him in permanent collision with the institution's central doctrine — *"if there is only one copy, there is no copy"* (Ch45) — and with the clerk, and with Calloway, and with Leon.
- **His best evidence can never rise above OBSERVED standing** (`03_ESOTERIC_RECORD_CLASSIFICATION.md`). He does not care. Leon cannot make him care.
- It forces the arc's cruellest small betrayal: Leon starts writing Owen's evidence *for* him, in Leon's hand, over Leon's signature — which makes it Leon's word about what Owen said, the precise thing Vale warned Leon about. Owen eventually works this out. Neither of them meant it.

## Strengths

- Reads people fast and accurately: deception, fear, imminent flight, who in a room is in charge and who thinks they are
- Enters first. Restrains without escalating. Gets people out of buildings.
- Knows the town's yards, walls, footpaths, back gates and short-cuts by body rather than by map
- Militia-trained crowd handling — the single most useful skill in a parish with no police
- Can hold a door, carry a man, and stay awake until four in the morning
- Physically brave without appetite for violence

## Weaknesses

- Concludes at the second fact and defends the conclusion at the tenth
- Cannot work a document. Confronted with a ledger, he asks someone what it says and believes them.
- Contemptuous of Leon's method for the first third of the arc, loudly, in front of others
- His shame about the war makes him vulnerable to anyone who assumes he saw action — he goes along with it
- No patience for the possibility that a thing was not done by a person. **His first, second and third theory is always a man.** This is exactly how the team gets things wrong in the early cases, and it is honest, and it is his.

## Skills

Field entry · restraint · crowd management · night work · local geography by foot · reading faces · driving a cart and handling horses (family trade) · basic militia drill · rough carpentry

Cannot: read a ledger, write a report, hold a line of argument, wait.

## Ability

**NONE. Permanently. By design.**

Owen is the load-bearing demonstration that competence in this world is human. If a supernatural ability is ever proposed for him, the answer is no — his entire function is to be the man who is very good and not special, and who is therefore in real danger every time.

## Combat competence

**The team's only genuine physical asset — and deliberately capped.** Militia drill, crowd work, and six years of being large in bad rooms. He wins fights against ordinary men and loses to two of them. He has no weapon beyond a stick. He is not a duellist, not a marksman, not a brawler by temperament.

Against anything that is not a man, he is **useless and does not know it.** That is his mortal risk and the source of the arc's first real injury.

## Relationships

**Leon** — the arc's spine.
1. Dismissal: *"You'll get someone killed being clever."* Said in company. Meant.
2. Use: Leon turns out to be right about a sequence, and Owen, who is not stupid, starts asking him which door.
3. Reliance: Owen stands where Leon tells him to stand, at the time Leon tells him, **before understanding why.** This is the single moment Leon's whole development exists to earn.
4. Fracture: the written-evidence betrayal.
5. Settlement: not friendship exactly. Two men who have decided to be in the same rooms.

He never calls Leon brave and never says anything affectionate. He carries Leon's coat once, and it is the most he ever does.

**Cecily Pargeter** — protective in a way she finds patronising and occasionally useful. He believes her instantly and completely, which is worse than doubting her, because his belief is worth nothing to anybody. Two people at opposite ends of the same evidentiary problem: he won't put his name to what he knows, she puts her name to everything and is disbelieved anyway.

**Reuben Orme** — Orme trained him and Owen would follow him into anything. This is the team's real fault line: when Orme and Leon disagree, Owen's instinct goes to Orme, and Orme knows it.

**Calloway** — respectful distance. Calloway pays him and does not know him.

**Cuthbert Ingle** — mutual, cordial dislike. The clerk is everything Owen distrusts, politely.

**Thomas Vale** — **never met him.** Knew of "the old constable at the end of Bellweather Lane" as a name, the way one knows a name. This is deliberate: Owen is the person to whom Leon cannot explain the loss. He offers no condolence, notices Leon is not sleeping, and says nothing about it for a long time and then says one sentence.

**The Order of Severence** — does not know it exists. Must not learn early.

## Relationship to E.R.C.

He has no idea a classification framework exists and would consider it absurd if told. Institutionally he is the system's permanent problem: **the best witness in Alderwick who will not become a record.**

## Knowledge level

- **Knows:** the town, its people, its yards; that Leon works for the constable on odd matters; that the boundary stones cracked and it was talked about
- **Suspects:** that Leon is keeping something. He does not press. He assumes it is a woman or a debt.
- **Does NOT know:** the Agreement · the door · the Gear · the Order · the keeping · the count · anything supernatural whatsoever

## Secrets

1. He never saw action, and has let the whole town believe otherwise for two years.
2. He was offered the carting trade back on condition he stopped the watch, and refused, and has told no one, because the reason is that the watch is the only thing he has been good at since 1815.

## Character arc

**Start:** a capable man with nothing to be capable at, who thinks the new clever one is a liability.
**Middle:** injured — badly, believably, in a case where his human-first assumption was wrong. Recovers, and comes back changed in exactly one respect: he now asks *before* going in.
**Late:** becomes the instrument of Leon's method rather than its opponent. The most dangerous thing on the team is Owen Trant standing in the right place at the right minute.
**Unresolved by design:** whether he ever tells anyone about the war, and whether he ever writes his name in a book.

## Long-term potential

- Could take a round of his own; could be the man Orme leaves it to
- Could be the first person Leon has to decide whether to tell the truth to — and the first casualty of not telling
- **Mortality: genuinely available.** He is the character whose death would cost most and mean most, and he must be written as someone who can die.
