## Function

Calloway holds the office. **Orme holds the field.** He decides who goes in which door, in what order, and who waits in the yard. He trained Owen Trant. He tests Leon continuously and without malice.

He is *not* Vale's replacement and knows almost nothing about Vale beyond the name and the fact that Calloway does not discuss him.

## Personality

Solid, direct, fair in a way that is not kind. He explains an order once. He does not humiliate men and does not comfort them either. He has buried enough matters to have stopped expecting resolution from the world and to have started requiring it from himself.

- **Virtues:** physically brave, scrupulously fair with wages and blame, protective of his men, never lies to a family
- **Flaws:** **he closes cases** — see below; contemptuous of intellectual hedging; assumes a young man's caution is fear
- **Worldview:** somebody did it, somebody knows, and somebody is owed an answer by Sunday.
- **Habit:** re-ties his boot before going into a building. Every time. His men have learned to read it.
- **Speech:** flat, unhurried, complete. Never sarcastic. Says "no" as a whole sentence.
- **Under pressure:** excellent. Under ambiguity: poor.

## THE FLAW — the engine of the E.R.C. arc

**An open case is a reproach to him.**

Not because of his record. Because an open case means a man is still out there and a family is still sitting in a front room waiting for someone to knock. Orme has knocked on a great many doors with nothing to say, and he has decided that the *adequate* answer delivered on Friday is worth more to a living family than the *true* answer delivered never.

So he will take the answer that fits. Honestly. In good faith. Believing it is the kinder act. And he genuinely believes Leon's refusal to conclude is cowardice wearing rigour's coat.

**He hates the word UNRESOLVED.** He is not wrong to hate it. That is the whole point: the classification framework's most honest category is the one that costs a real person something real, and Orme is that cost with a face on it.

## Strengths

Field command · reading a street · twenty years of ordinary crime (theft, assault, arson, drink, missing men) · knows how to take a violent man without killing him · will go through a door himself rather than send a younger man · trusted by the bench, which matters institutionally

## Weaknesses

Ambiguity, patience, paper past the second page, and the possibility that the cause of a thing is not a person. He has never in his life closed a case with the word *unknown* in it and does not intend to start.

## Skills

Command · restraint and arrest · interrogation of ordinary suspects (very good) and of frightened ones (poor — he frightens them further) · knowledge of the local criminal trade · reads a room for threat, never for grief

## Combat competence

**High and unglamorous.** The most capable fighter in the senior layer. Twenty years of hard practice, no technique worth naming, and an instinct for ending things in one movement. He is also forty-nine and knows what that costs him now.

Against anything not human: he does not believe in it, will act as though it is a man, and will therefore be the one to make the catastrophic mistake if the story ever needs one made by an experienced person rather than a young one.

## Relationships

**Leon** — the arc's central institutional friction, and it must never become simple dislike. Orme respects the work and rejects the conclusion. Sequence:
1. Tolerates him as Calloway's expensive hobby
2. Uses him, resentfully, when a sequence Leon predicted comes true
3. **Overrules him at the worst possible moment, for defensible reasons, and someone is hurt** — and it is genuinely arguable whose fault it was
4. Never apologises. Changes his practice slightly and permanently and does not mention it.
He should be right at least once about Leon being wrong, or the whole dynamic is a cheat.

**Owen Trant** — trained him; Owen would follow him anywhere. **This is the team's real fault line.** When Orme and Leon disagree, Owen's body goes to Orme before his judgement does, and Orme knows it, and uses it once.

**Cecily Pargeter** — reads her honesty as timidity. Not cruel about it. Simply keeps not getting an answer from her and eventually stops asking, which is worse.

**Calloway** — mutual professional respect, no warmth. Orme thinks Calloway has gone soft in the office; Calloway thinks Orme is the reason the round still functions. Both are right.

**Cuthbert Ingle** — barely speaks to him. Regards clerks as weather.

**Walter Denby** — old sparring partners. Orme wants a signature; Denby won't give it; they have had the same argument for a decade and are almost fond of it.

**Thomas Vale** — never met. Arrived in Alderwick long after Vale had gone quiet. Has heard the name, notices Calloway will not be drawn on it, and has correctly concluded that there is something there and incorrectly concluded that it is dull.

**The Order of Severence** — unaware.

## Relationship to E.R.C.

He is the framework's most dangerous user, because he uses it *well* and for the wrong end: he will raise a claim to CORROBORATED honestly and then treat CORROBORATED as if it were CONFIRMED, because the family is waiting. He will resist UNRESOLVED to the last. **He is the reason the framework needs the distinction at all.**

## Knowledge level

- **Knows:** ordinary crime; the town's violent men; the bench's temper; that some old matters in the drawer are never discussed
- **Suspects:** that Calloway is keeping something in the bottom drawer, and that it is none of his business
- **Does NOT know:** the Agreement · the door · the Gear · the Order · the keeping · the count · anything supernatural

## Secrets

1. **Why he left his last town.** A case he closed on an adequate answer. The man he took was probably guilty of something. Orme has never checked, deliberately, for eleven years. He is not tormented by it; he has *filed* it, which is worse. **Do not resolve early. Do not make it a twist.**
2. He cannot read a long document without losing the thread and has hidden this for thirty years by asking crisp questions about the first page.

## Character arc

**Start:** the professional who has no time for the clever young man.
**Middle:** the overrule; the injury; the first case he cannot close by any honest means.
**Late:** writes UNRESOLVED in his own hand, once, and it costs him more than any fight in the book. He does not become Leon's ally. He becomes a man who has admitted a limit.
**Unresolved:** whether he ever goes back and checks the old case.

## Long-term potential

- The person who could shut Leon out of the institution entirely, and nearly does
- A candidate for the next constable if Calloway goes — and a bad one, for exactly the reasons above, which the reader should see before anyone in the book does
- Mortality available but should not be spent cheaply; he is more useful surviving with a limit than dying with a lesson
