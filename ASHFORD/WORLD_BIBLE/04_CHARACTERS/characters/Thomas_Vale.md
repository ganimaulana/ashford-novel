## Public Role

Retired parish constable of Ashford, predecessor to James Calloway (Ch40: "I was a constable. … Before Calloway."). The town regards him as an old man living quietly at the end of a lane. Calloway does not mention him to Leon unprompted — "Perhaps he thought it better not to" (Ch40).

## Military Background

Confirmed only in outline: he was a soldier before he was a constable (Ch40). The garrison-town entries in the journal (Ch48) — price of bread, a disliked sergeant, a horse destroyed, a name appearing four times and then never again — are his own record of those years. Everything specific (regiment, campaign, rank, the vanishing name) is UNKNOWN.

## Constable Career

Constable of Ashford before Calloway; overlapped with Calloway "for a short time" (Ch40). Preceded in the Ashford office by **Marcus Ferrand**, who kept the count for fifteen years and was erased from the records (Ch103, 116, 117). Vale inherited Ferrand's empty file and never wrote in it — "A man who keeps a file empty for that long has made a decision about it" (Ch103). He also inherited and passed on the **box of the constables**, which held Ferrand's notebook; he told Calloway the box held what could not be written down and declined to itemise it (Ch117).

## Intelligence Career

CONFIRMED as background, revealed only obliquely in the manuscript. Behavioural evidence seeded (see Personality). Documentary evidence: the district map in his box, "not of Ashford… the kind a constabulary prints and does not sell," marked in ink over many years in more than one shade (Ch46).

## Counter-Intelligence

CONFIRMED as background. Manuscript expression: Calloway's account of Vale keeping originals of reports that came back altered, and his maxim "a man who has only one copy of a thing does not own it" (Ch103); Vale's own instruction to Leon that records are "lost, or taken, or copied wrongly on purpose and the wrong copy is the one that survives — the three look identical after fifty years" (Ch40).

## Demotion / Transfer

Official: establishment/administrative. Journal entry (Ch48): *"To Ashford. The reason offered is establishment. I have read the order twice. It is correctly worded. That is what troubles me."* The keeper before Vale independently reports asking him and receiving the same answer — "He said the order had been correctly worded" (Ch79).

**Revelation layers (author-approved):** 1 he had once been important · 2 he had worked in intelligence · 3 he had investigated something inside the Constable · 4 the investigation ended his career · 5 he may have been sent to Ashford to be removed · 6 **or Ashford may have been the one place he could continue what he had been doing — UNDECIDED, do not resolve.**

## Ashford Years / Fifty-Year Guardianship

Kept the Agreement fifty years; kept the door closed; left no account (Ch64, 79, 124). The keeper before Vale opened the door and did not return; Vale was "the keeper who kept the door closed, because the keeper before him had opened it" (Ch71). He kept the keeper's sign from the Order deliberately (Ch50, 57). He never took the keeper's key to the tower (Ch70).

## Relationship to the Sealed Door

Guardian, not scholar. Explicitly does not know what is behind it (Ch40). Eleanor Shaw's account: he did not keep it closed because the Order told him to — "He kept it closed because he was protecting the sign," from everyone (Ch57).

## Relationship to the Gear

- The Gear was **hidden at the mill before Leon found it**; Vale: "it was set where a thing must be found and not given" (Ch46) — CONFIRMED.
- He kept it fifty years in his private box, in a folded cloth (Ch46) — CONFIRMED.
- He did **not** hand it to Leon; Leon found it (Ch36) — CONFIRMED.
- He questioned Leon twice about *where* it was found, separated by twenty minutes of unrelated talk, and treated the matching answers as a matter settled (Ch40) — a verification technique, not curiosity.
- He read the inscription for fifty years and could not say what it was for (Ch46) — CONFIRMED.
- **PROPOSED:** he moved it out of anywhere searchable because people connected to a compromised Constable and/or the Order were looking for it.
- **UNKNOWN:** whether he knew its function; whether he intended Leon specifically to find it; whether the mill was chosen for any understood reason. The intended reader question is *"Did Vale hide the Gear so Leon would find it, or so nobody else would?"* — do not answer.

## Relationship to Leon

Chose Leon as a person, not by appointment, prophecy, or supernatural selection — CONFIRMED. Ch40: "You have not agreed to anything" / "I think someone believes it is [you]" / "Do you?" "No." Ch46: "There is no one to leave them to." He does not answer "Why me?" — CONFIRMED and to remain unanswered. He assessed Leon before trusting him: "You ask questions in the right order. That is not a compliment. It means you will be difficult to lie to, and men who are difficult to lie to are told nothing at all" (Ch40).

## Private Box

CONFIRMED (Ch46). His **private property** — "These are mine. Not the parish's. Not the Order's. Mine." Size of a document case, heavier than it looks. Contents: soft-creased folded papers · a bundle of letters tied with browned tape · a **service journal** (dark leather, broken spine, pocket-dirty edges) · a **district map, not of Ashford**, of a kind constabularies print and do not sell, marked in ink in several shades over years · two stoppered bottles labelled in an unknown hand · a military brass button worn smooth · a woman's ring too small for his finger · the empty folded cloth where the Gear had lain.

He states plainly that some of it is nothing, that some he never understood in fifty years, and that he cannot say which is which. On the journal: "Read it in order. Not the end first. A man who reads the end first learns what happened and never learns why." / "There is a great deal in it I did not want anyone to find. That is why it is in a box and not in an office."

The box is **not** the Gear box, and the Constable has **no automatic ownership claim** over it. Distinct from the **box of the constables** (Ch117), which is an institutional object.

## Diaries / Archive

The service journal is CONFIRMED and partially read (Ch48): garrison-town years first, no supernatural content, a name that appears four times and stops, then the Ashford transfer entry, then several blank pages, then ordinary parish business "as though a door had closed on the earlier man and the later one had agreed not to mention him." Leon has read it "a dozen times" and still cannot place the crossed-out names, the unfamiliar towns, or the entries that break off mid-matter and resume years later (Ch124). Leon's conclusion: "He wrote a great deal, and he wrote nothing about the door. I have come to think that was a decision."

Other confirmed Vale deposits: the older-than-the-wall **map** left with Eleanor Shaw for seven years, sealed, for "the keeper who came for it" (Ch57); the hidden **vestry book** entrusted to the clerk (Ch50); the **blank red book** left on his shelf with one sentence at the end (Ch64).

## Hidden Caches

**PROPOSED — future-arc mechanism.** The district map's marks are the seed: mundane in appearance, ink applied over decades, meaning unstated. Intended later realisation: the marks correspond to places where Vale worked or hid records. Constraints: not every cache is supernatural; some hold only intelligence material; some are empty; some already found; some fragmentary. This is a future reason for Leon to travel outside Ashford and must **not** pay off in the current arc.

## Knowledge Boundaries

Vale knew more than Leon and less than the whole. His expertise is **investigative and institutional**, not supernatural — he is not an occult authority and must never become one. He does not know: what is behind the door · what the Gear does · what the inscription means · who set the original prohibition ("A predecessor." / "And who told him?" / "That is where the records become difficult."). He does know: how records are altered and suppressed · how to verify a witness · how to place a thing so that it will be found only by someone looking correctly · that the Order does not understand the keeper's sign.

## Personality / Behavioural Traits

Seated, deliberate, withholding; lets visitors finish before answering; answers in the fewest words that are still true; refuses to discuss his former position. Notices what others overlook. Asks precisely who knows what. Uncomfortable when Leon repeats information in company (Ch49–50 fallout: "Vale carried it for fifty years and told no one. You have carried it for a week and told the room"). Knows how official reports are worded. Recognises altered documents. Keeps duplicates. Never carried a paper through town in daylight; came at dusk, never twice by the same street (Ch57). Closed shutters before handing over the constables' box and watched Calloway learn where the key went (Ch117). His press was ordered systematically, with one space left empty and clean; his bed was made to a standard learned under discipline (Ch48).

The reader should read all of this as "old veteran / experienced constable" first. The pattern should resolve later.

## Secrets

**Known to the reader by Ch137:** his name; that he was a soldier before a constable; that he was posted to Ashford by an order whose correctness troubled him; that he kept private copies of altered reports; that he distributed sealed material to several keepers-by-proxy; that he kept the Gear fifty years and placed it to be found; that he read the inscription for fifty years without understanding it.

**Hidden:** the seniority and nature of his intelligence work · the investigation that ended it · what he concluded about Order penetration of the Constable · what the district map's marks are · what is in the two bottles · which papers in the box matter · why Leon.
