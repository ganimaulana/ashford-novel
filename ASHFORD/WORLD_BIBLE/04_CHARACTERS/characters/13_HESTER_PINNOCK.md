## Function

**She is 1817's entire surveillance apparatus.**

There are no records of movement in this period except the ones kept by the people who move things. The carrier's book at an inn yard is that record: who left Alderwick and on what day, what weight went with them, where it was consigned, who paid, whose name was entered wrong, which parcels went unbooked and were carried anyway for a shilling in the hand.

She also knows the things a book does not hold: which ostler was awake, who arrived on foot and left in a chaise, who came twice in a week and gave a different destination each time, and which passengers the drivers remember and why.

For an investigation that turns on **who came to Alderwick, when, and who paid for it** — and Vale's whole hidden history does — she is the only source in the town.

**She is not a supernatural detector and must never become one.**

## Canon interface

She works at the **Greyhound**, the inn from Ch36 whose innkeeper is the other Finch. Nothing about her contradicts that scene; she is in the yard, not the house, and the two need never meet on the page. No existing character is altered.

## Personality

Brisk, shrewd, unsentimental, and funnier than anyone else in the arc. She has spent sixteen years in a yard full of men who assumed she was furniture, and she has made a living out of having listened.

- **Virtues:** genuinely observant; keeps her word exactly as given; no malice at all; treats everyone identically
- **Flaws:** transactional to the bone; will not extend credit; will sell the same answer twice; **not loyal, and honest about not being loyal**
- **Worldview:** everyone is going somewhere and someone paid for it. Find out who paid.
- **Habit:** licks the pencil before she writes. Writes while you are still talking.
- **Speech:** fast, dry, elliptical. Interrupts. Uses the word "carriage" as a verb. Never says a name if a description will do.
- **Under pressure:** cheerful and immovable. She has thrown drunk men out of the yard herself.

## THE PRICE — the reason she is dangerous

**She does not take money for information. She takes information.**

Money she has. What she wants is the thing she has spent sixteen years accumulating: knowing. Every answer she gives Leon costs him **one true fact about himself** — where he was on a night, who he saw, what he was carrying, why he asked. She sets the price, she takes it in advance, and she keeps her side of the bargain scrupulously.

She does not sell it on. She does not blackmail. She has never betrayed a bargain in her life.

**But Leon is a man whose entire survival depends on compartmentalisation** (`15_LEON_ASHCROFT.md`, Layer Architecture; Vale's fallout in Ch53–54: *"You have carried it for a week and told the room"*). Every transaction with Hester Pinnock is Leon spending exactly the currency he cannot afford to spend, voluntarily, for something he needs.

**She is the leak Leon opens himself, and she is not a villain.** That is the whole of her.

## Strengths

The carrier's book, six years of it in her own hand and eleven years of her husband's before that · the drivers, ostlers, and yard boys, all of whom talk to her · an exact memory for weights, dates, and who paid · reads a consignment note the way Leon reads a ledger · nobody in Alderwick considers her important, including the Order

## Weaknesses

- Cannot leave the yard. Her knowledge is entirely local and entirely dependent on people coming to her.
- Will not lie for anyone and will not keep a secret she was not paid for
- Her price is Leon's exposure, and she will not waive it, ever, for any reason
- Blind to anything that did not arrive or leave by road. A thing that walked across the fields at night is invisible to her, and she does not know that this is a gap.

## Skills

The book · yard and road knowledge · weights and consignments · reading a traveller · negotiation · handling drunk men · a fast plain hand

## Ability

**NONE.**

## Combat competence

**Low but real.** Sixteen years of a coaching yard. She can shout a man down, put a lantern into his face, and call four ostlers. She would be no use in a field at night and would not be there.

## Relationships

**Leon** — the arc's most morally interesting working relationship. Not adversarial, not warm, entirely fair, and quietly ruinous. She gives him exactly what she promised; he pays exactly what he agreed; and over twenty transactions she assembles, without any intent to harm him, **the most complete picture of Leon Ashcroft's movements that exists anywhere.**
He knows this and keeps going back, because she is the only person who can tell him who came to Alderwick in the year Vale was transferred.

**Owen Trant** — she has known him since he was a boy in that yard. She calls him Owen. He is faintly afraid of her. **She is the only person who knows he never saw action, and she has never said so, because nobody has paid her for it.**

**Cecily Pargeter** — mutual assessment, no warmth, occasional professional dealing. Two unsworn women retained by an office that will never acknowledge either.

**Reuben Orme** — he has used her for years for ordinary work and pays in coin, which she accepts from him and not from Leon. **She has never told Orme her real price, because he has never asked a question worth it.**

**James Calloway** — knows her name and the book's value and has never gone to the yard himself.

**Isaac Faulds** — trade acquaintance. He has mended the yard gate a dozen times.

**Cuthbert Ingle** — **quietly important.** Consignment notes and the office's correspondence pass through overlapping hands. She has noticed, once or twice, that a letter left the yard and something came back that was not quite the same weight. She has never mentioned it. **Nobody has paid her for it.** Do not develop this early.

**Thomas Vale** — **the vein worth mining slowly.** Vale never used the coach and never sent a parcel through the book in fifty years — *and she knows that, because a man who never once appears in a book is a thing a book-keeper notices.* Her one piece of Vale information is an **absence**, which is exactly the shape of everything else about him.
And once, years ago, a passenger came asking the way to the end of Bellweather Lane and gave a name that was not entered. She remembers the year. She does not remember the name. **DEFERRED — do not decide the name yet.**

**The Order of Severence** — unaware of it as an organisation, but she has carried its members' luggage and could describe two of them by their boots.

## Relationship to E.R.C.

She is a producer of **material, dated, corroborable evidence** — the book itself is a document with standing, and a consignment entry in two hands is the cleanest CORROBORATED trace available in the whole setting. Institutionally she is the answer to the question the framework cannot otherwise ask: *who was here.*

She has never heard of the framework and would want paying to care.

## Knowledge level

- **Knows:** who travelled, when, with what, who paid, for six years directly and eleven years by inheritance of the book; the yard's people; the roads
- **Suspects:** that Leon is in trouble of some sort, and that it is a better class of trouble than debt
- **Does NOT know:** the Agreement · the door · the Gear · the Order · the count · the keeping · anything supernatural

## Secrets

1. Owen Trant's war. Unsold.
2. That letters have left the yard and come back the wrong weight. Unsold.
3. The unentered passenger who asked for Bellweather Lane. **Name DEFERRED.**
4. She is quietly saving to buy the carrying business outright, and has told nobody, because a woman who is known to have money in a coaching yard has less of it by spring.

## Character arc

**Start:** a source. Useful, cheap in coin, expensive in truth.
**Middle:** twenty small honest transactions, accumulating into a file on Leon that she never intended and will never use.
**Late:** the moment somebody else offers her a price for what she knows about him — and the reader has no idea what she will do, because she has been scrupulously fair all along and fairness is not loyalty.
**Unresolved:** what she does at that moment. **Do not decide it in advance.**

## Long-term potential

- The absence-of-Vale-in-the-book is a whole thread: fifty years of a man who never once sent or received anything by road
- The unentered passenger is a reserve revelation with a date already attached
- A candidate for Leon's secret contacts **only if her price is ever solved**, and it probably should not be
- Mortality: low value. She is worth far more as an unresolved risk.

The book itself is her characteristic object: canvas-backed, swollen with damp, tied with tape. Licks the pencil. **No widow's-weeds coding, no shrewd-gossip caricature.**
