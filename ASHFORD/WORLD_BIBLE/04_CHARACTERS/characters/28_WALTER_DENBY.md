## Function — the single most important structural role in the senior layer

**He is the evidence threshold with a face on it.**

Under the practice defined in `03_ESOTERIC_RECORD_CLASSIFICATION.md` §19–21, **CONFIRMED standing cannot be assigned in Alderwick by the constable, the clerk, or anyone in the office.** It requires either the London office's man — who comes twice in twenty-three years — or **a surgeon's signature given at an inquest.**

That signature is Walter Denby's. It is the only route by which anything in Alderwick becomes officially established. And it belongs to a man who will write only what he can defend in front of a coroner, a jury, and a family's solicitor, and who cannot be argued past that line by affection, urgency, or a compelling theory.

He therefore spends the arc **denying Leon the corroboration Leon needs — and being right to.**

## Personality

Dry, orderly, faintly weary, entirely without mysticism. He has opened enough bodies to have lost any appetite for wonder and to have kept a real, unsentimental tenderness for the living, which he expresses exclusively as competence.

- **Virtues:** rigorous, honest, unbribable by argument, kind to the bereaved in a practical way
- **Flaws:** intellectually arrogant within his field; dismissive of testimony he cannot examine; assumes enthusiasm is error
- **Worldview:** the body will tell you what happened to it and nothing about why. Anything beyond the body is another man's trade.
- **Habit:** washes his hands before and after, at length, and while doing it will not be spoken to.
- **Speech:** measured, technical, complete. Uses "I cannot say" as a finished statement. **Will not be hurried into a sentence.**
- **Under pressure:** slower. He has never once written something to end an argument.

## Strengths

Anatomy and injury · cause and manner of death within the limits of 1817 knowledge · inquest procedure and what a coroner will accept · will state the limits of his own knowledge unprompted, which makes him devastating as a witness · thirty-five years of Alderwick's bodies, which is a memory nobody else has

## Weaknesses

- Will not sign. Repeatedly. When it matters most.
- Underestimates Cecily Pargeter for the first third of the arc, because she is twenty-four and unqualified — and she is better than he is on doses, and he has to discover it
- No framework at all for anything the body cannot explain, and no interest in acquiring one
- His caution has, at least once in his career, let something stand as an accident that was not. He knows this. It is the source of his rigour, not an exception to it.

## Skills

Post-mortem examination · surgery · dating and distinguishing injuries · inquest testimony · reading a room for illness · writing a document that survives cross-examination

## Ability

**NONE. Absolutely and permanently.**

**Hard constraint: Denby must never become an occultist, an esoteric authority, or a believer.** The moment he accepts a supernatural explanation, the framework loses its only honest ceiling. If he ever concedes anything, it must be at the level of *the body*, phrased as a negative, and hedged: not *"this was done by something inhuman"* but *"I cannot account for this by any means I have seen in thirty-five years, and I decline to speculate."*

## Combat competence

**None.** He is fifty-six and holds knives for other purposes.

## Relationships

**Leon** — the most useful antagonism in the arc, and not personal.
Leon brings him a sequence, a chronology, three corroborating documents and a hypothesis. Denby reads it, agrees it is interesting, and declines to sign. Again. And again. He is not obstructing; he is holding the line that makes the eventual signature mean something.
**He also gives Leon the single most valuable professional lesson available after Vale's death:** the difference between *knowing* a thing and being able to *establish* it. Vale taught Leon to keep his categories apart. Denby teaches him what the categories are *for*.

**Cecily Pargeter** — the relationship that changes him. She is unqualified, unsworn, female, twenty-four, and correct about a dose he had wrong. He does not condescend and does not concede. He requires her to be exact and she is. **Late in the arc he countersigns one of her findings, with three qualifications attached, and it is the most important sentence in her story and one of the most important in his.**

**Reuben Orme** — a decade of the same argument, almost affectionately. Orme wants a signature by Friday; Denby will not give one by any date.

**James Calloway** — long professional acquaintance. Calloway is one of the few men who has never asked him to write something he did not believe, and Denby has quietly noticed.

**Owen Trant** — mild mutual incomprehension. Owen has been stitched by him twice.

**Cuthbert Ingle** — correct, distant, and Denby is faintly wary of him for reasons he has never examined. **Leave unexplained.**

**Thomas Vale** — **had a professional history with him, and it is the profile's best-hidden asset.** Denby attended one or two matters at Vale's request, years ago, and Vale asked him — twice, twenty minutes apart, buried under unrelated talk — the same question about a body. Denby recognised it as a technique at the time and found it insulting. **He also gave the same answer both times, and remembers what the question was, and has never repeated it to anyone.** One line, released very late. Do not expand it into a friendship and do not let it explain Vale.

**The Order of Severence** — unaware of it as an organisation. Has met Graves once socially and found him tedious.

## Relationship to E.R.C.

**He is the ceiling.** Nothing reaches CONFIRMED standing in Alderwick without him, and he does not know the framework exists — he simply refuses, as a matter of professional conduct, to certify what he cannot defend. That is exactly why the framework works: its highest standing is guarded by a man with no interest in it.

He is also the reason **CONFIRMED never means "explained"**: he will certify *that* a thing occurred and refuse, in the same document, to say what caused it. That distinction is his professional habit before it is anyone's rule.

## Knowledge level

- **Knows:** the bodies of Alderwick for thirty-five years; what a coroner accepts; one thing Vale asked him
- **Suspects:** nothing supernatural. He has two or three cases in his memory he could not account for and has filed them as ignorance rather than mystery, which is the more honest of the two errors.
- **Does NOT know:** the Agreement · the door · the Gear · the Order · the count · the keeping

## Secrets

1. The case he let stand as an accident, early in his career, that he no longer believes was one. He has never re-opened it and never will. **Not identified. Do not make it a twist.**
2. Vale's question, and his own answer to it, given twice. **Released late, in one line.**

## Character arc

**Start:** the professional obstacle. Correct, immovable, faintly irritating.
**Middle:** the Cecily correction; the slow discovery that Leon's method is not enthusiasm but discipline.
**Late:** one countersignature, three qualifications, and a refusal to say more. He does not become a believer. He becomes a man who has written down the edge of his own knowledge, which is the most this world's honesty permits.
**Unresolved:** the old accident.

## Long-term potential

- The witness whose late testimony makes an entire arc's evidence admissible
- The man who dies without ever having conceded, leaving one hedged document behind — the same instrument as Vale's second copy and Cecily's laying-out record
- Mortality available; his loss removes the team's only route to CONFIRMED and should be felt institutionally as well as personally
