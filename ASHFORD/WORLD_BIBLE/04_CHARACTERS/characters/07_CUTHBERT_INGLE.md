## Canon interface — a PROPOSED identification, not a change

The manuscript has an unnamed **"constable's clerk"** who writes Leon's name onto the watch sheet (Ch10, l.73) and appears again in Ch11. This profile **proposes that this man is Cuthbert Ingle.**

That is an identification of an existing on-page function, not an alteration of it. **The clerk remains unnamed in the manuscript.** No chapter is touched. If the author declines the identification, Ingle simply becomes a second, additional clerk and nothing in this file breaks.

## Function

**Nothing enters the record except through him.** He writes the fair copy, he makes the duplicate, he files both, he knows where they are, and he knows which reports went up to the bench or to London and which came back with a date or a word changed.

He has never mentioned the alterations, because mentioning them would require him to say whose hands the papers passed through, and one of those hands is his own.

## Personality

Courteous, dry, exact, and entirely without visible ambition. He has outlasted three constables and expects to outlast a fourth. He is helpful in a way that is impossible to be grateful for: he gives you precisely what you asked for and not the thing next to it.

- **Virtues:** meticulous, unfailingly polite, genuinely dutiful, never gossips
- **Flaws:** withholds by omission as a settled habit; incurious to the point of complicity; treats institutional survival as a moral good
- **Worldview:** the office outlives the men in it. What is written is what happened. What is not written did not.
- **Habit:** blots, squares the page, and sets the pen down before answering a question. Every time. It buys him two seconds and he has been buying them for forty years.
- **Speech:** formal, mildly old-fashioned, complete. Answers the question asked. **Never the question meant.**
- **Under pressure:** unchanged. This is the unnerving thing about him.

## THE AMBIGUITY — do not resolve

Vale's approved background establishes that he found indications of **Order of Severence penetration of parts of the Constable**, that this ended his career, and that the extent remains UNKNOWN (`25_THOMAS_VALE.md`).

Cuthbert Ingle has sat at that desk longer than Calloway has held the office. Every report that was ever altered passed under his hand. He is the most plausible internal compromise in Alderwick.

**He is also, and equally plausibly, exactly what he appears to be:** an old man who has spent forty years being careful, who noticed that papers came back changed, who worked out that saying so would end him, and who decided — once, long ago, without drama — to keep his head down and his copies accurate. That is not treason. It is what most people do.

**Both readings must remain completely available for as long as possible.** Every scene at the cabinet should play twice for the reader. Do not confirm. Do not eliminate. Do not have a character voice the suspicion out loud early; the reader must arrive at it alone, and then be unable to settle it.

If it must ever resolve, the most valuable resolution is the least dramatic one.

## Strengths

Forty years of institutional memory · knows which drawer, which year, which hand · a superb copyist · can date a document by paper, ink, and fold · knows the bench's correspondence habits · physically present in the office every day, which is more access than anyone else has

## Weaknesses

Physically frail. Cannot travel, cannot be in the field, cannot be hurried. Politely obstructive when he does not wish to be helpful, and there is no way to tell the difference between that and genuine slowness. Will not take a side, ever, about anything.

## Skills

Copying · filing · document dating · knowing where things are · knowing which question not to answer · a legal hand · arithmetic for the rates

## Ability

**NONE.** His uncanny quality is entirely archival.

## Combat competence

**None whatsoever.** Sixty-one, thin, and has never struck anyone.

## Relationships

**Leon** — the most productive and least trustworthy working relationship Leon has inside the institution. Ingle gives him access, correctly, promptly, and incompletely. He also — crucially — **teaches Leon the institution by accident**, in the way Vale taught him tradecraft: by demonstrating a habit and declining to explain it. When Leon eventually asks the right question, Ingle sets down his pen, blots, squares the page, and answers a different one.

**Calloway** — twenty-three years of daily courtesy and no friendship. Calloway trusts him absolutely and has never examined why.

**Reuben Orme** — barely speaks to him.

**Owen Trant** — cordial mutual dislike, perfectly polite. Owen refuses to write; Ingle exists to write. Neither will say the obvious thing.

**Cecily Pargeter** — old-fashioned courtesy, and he files her statements accurately and completely. **She is the only member of the team with no complaint about him, which should faintly trouble the reader.**

**Thomas Vale** — **the load-bearing relationship, and it is almost entirely off-page.** Ingle was a young clerk when Vale was constable. He copied Vale's reports. He was there when they came back altered. He has one line about Vale and should be permitted only that line for a very long time: something exact, courteous, and impossible to interpret.

**The Order of Severence** — UNKNOWN. Deliberately.

## Relationship to E.R.C.

**He is the mechanism.** In the practice defined in `03_ESOTERIC_RECORD_CLASSIFICATION.md` §19–21, the clerk is the only person who can raise a claim from OBSERVED to RECORDED, because RECORDED means *entered in a book by the custodian, dated, signed, and duplicated*. He cannot raise it further and would not wish to.

He is therefore the framework's gate, its guarantee, and — if the darker reading is true — its single point of failure.

## Knowledge level

- **Knows:** every document in the office; that reports have come back altered; whose hands they passed through; that Ferrand's file was left deliberately empty; that there is a bottom drawer he does not have the key to
- **Suspects:** more than he will ever say. **Content deliberately unspecified.**
- **Does NOT know:** the door · the Gear · the count · the keeping. He is an institutional man, not an esoteric one, and this must hold whichever reading of him is true.

## Secrets

1. **Whether he is compromised.** UNKNOWN — protected.
2. He kept a private copy of one document, once, forty years ago, and has never looked at it again and has never destroyed it. **Not identified. Do not identify it early.**
3. He knows exactly what Vale was doing and never told anyone — **or** he never knew and has spent forty years suspecting he should have. Which of these is true is the same open question as (1).

## Character arc

**Start:** the helpful old clerk.
**Middle:** the reader's suspicion arrives without a scene to justify it, and cannot be discharged.
**Late:** one act of either loyalty or betrayal, small, deniable, and not fully legible even afterwards.
**Unresolved by design:** all of it, for as long as possible.
