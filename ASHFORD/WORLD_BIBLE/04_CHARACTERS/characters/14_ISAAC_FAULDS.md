## Function — and why he is NOT a surveyor

Alderwick's canon already contains a boundary-and-masonry reader: **Marlow**, Ch38, who reads contour lines, distances, and masonry courses, dates the mill door as older than the mill, and is never seen again. **That niche is deliberately left alone.**

Faulds occupies a different and unclaimed one: **he is the man who can say how a thing was made to be closed, and whether it was forced or opened.**

Locks, wards, keys, hinges, hasps, staples, straps, seals, wax, solder, and the specific difference between a door that was broken and a door that was let through. In a world containing a sealed door with no keyhole (Ch38), a lock made by Aurelius Ashcroft (Ch100–101), an iron box with a strap and a first sign (Ch102), a mechanism beneath the mill that rotates and clicks under pressure (Ch49), a hereditary box with one key (Ch121), and official reports whose seals have been broken and re-made (Ch107) — the office needs exactly one man with this trade, and has never had one.

He is also **Leon's honest route to becoming dangerous without becoming powerful**: preparation, tools, and knowing which way a hinge falls. That is Phase E's requirement — capability through knowledge, not power inflation.

## Personality

Unhurried, dry, faintly amused. A craftsman's patience and a craftsman's contempt for shoddy work, including shoddy thinking. He likes being asked a technical question by someone who then listens to the whole answer, which is rarer than it should be, and it is why he tolerates Leon.

- **Virtues:** genuinely skilled; honest about what he cannot do; discreet without making a performance of it; charges fairly
- **Flaws:** incurious about meaning — he will tell you exactly how the lock works and has no interest in what it is for; pedantic; will not be hurried and does not care whose night is being wasted
- **Worldview:** things are made by men, for reasons, and the reason is usually in the workmanship.
- **Habit:** runs a thumbnail along a joint or a seam before saying anything. Talks to the object rather than to the person holding it.
- **Speech:** technical, precise, mildly wry. Answers "can you open it" with "not tonight" and means it.
- **Under pressure:** works at exactly the same speed. This has nearly got people killed and he is unrepentant.

## Strengths

- Can open most locks in Alderwick, and say honestly which he cannot
- Can tell forced from opened, picked from keyed, worn from broken, recent from old
- Reads wax and seals — whether a letter was opened and re-sealed, and how well
- Dates ironwork by method: forge marks, file marks, the fashions of wards, the hand of a particular smith
- Makes things: a hasp, a plate, a wedge, a lifting bar, a copy of a key from a wax impression
- **Physically capable in a practical way** — a smith's shoulders and a smith's grip

## Weaknesses

- Slow. Deliberately, professionally, immovably slow.
- Will not speculate. Asked what the mill door is *for*, he says he does not know and shows no sign of minding.
- Will not do anything unlawful, and is exact about where the line is. He will open a door for the constable and not for Leon.
- No stomach for the field. He will come to a building; he will not come at night; he will not go through a door he has not been told about.

## Skills

Locksmithing · whitesmithing · seal and wax examination · ironwork dating · key copying from impression · tool improvisation · mechanism reasoning (gears, wards, springs, levers)

## Ability

**NONE.** Everything unusual about him is thirty years of trade.

## Combat competence

**Low-moderate, and never used.** Strong, unafraid, and completely uninterested. He would hit someone with a bar if the alternative were being hit, and would be no good at anything beyond that.

## Relationships

**Leon** — a working friendship built on the only currency Leon has plenty of: attention. Faulds explains, Leon listens to the end and writes it down correctly, and Faulds — who has spent thirty years being interrupted — notices. He teaches Leon, incidentally and without meaning to, a great deal about how closed things behave.
**Crucially, he never asks Leon what any of it is for.** He is the one adult in the arc who supplies Leon with capability and requires nothing in return, which makes him the person Leon is most in danger of using.

**Reuben Orme** — Orme wants the door open now; Faulds will have it open in the morning. Recurring, comic, and occasionally consequential.

**Owen Trant** — Owen would kick the door in. Faulds finds this genuinely offensive. Their argument is the same argument every time and they both enjoy it.

**Cecily Pargeter** — mutual professional respect, immediate and uncomplicated. Two people who work with their hands and sign their work. **The team's only frictionless relationship — use it sparingly so it stays worth something.**

**James Calloway** — has been paying him for twenty years for hasps and window fastenings and has never once asked him a real question.

**Cuthbert Ingle** — Faulds has re-made the seal on the office cabinet twice, at Ingle's request, both times legitimately. **He remembers both occasions and has never thought about them again.** Leave this alone until it matters.

**Thomas Vale** — **the hook.** Faulds did a job for the old constable years ago, and it was an odd one, and he was paid well not to remember it and has remembered it perfectly ever since because that is what tradesmen do. He will not volunteer it. Asked directly and correctly, he will describe the *work* precisely and the *circumstances* not at all — because he was never told the circumstances. **Content DEFERRED. Do not decide what the job was yet.**

**The Order of Severence** — unaware.

## THE MARK — a controlled hook

He has seen the circle-crossed-by-a-line before. On a job. Years ago. Cut into iron, not stone.

He does not think it means anything, mentions it the way a man mentions that he has seen a maker's mark before, and **does not say where** — not because he is hiding it, but because Leon does not ask the right follow-up question the first time.

**Status: PROPOSED. Do not resolve early. Do not make him a secret keeper.** He is a witness who does not know he is one, which is the most useful kind and the hardest to write patiently.

## Relationship to E.R.C.

His evidence is the framework's best friend: **material, examinable, and reproducible.** A statement from Faulds that a lock was opened with a key and not forced is exactly the kind of trace that can raise a claim to CORROBORATED, and it is the standard Leon must learn to go looking for instead of arguing.

He has never heard of a classification framework and would find the idea of writing one down faintly ridiculous.

## Knowledge level

- **Knows:** ironwork, locks, and seals across the whole town and half the county; that he once did a strange job for Vale; that he has seen the mark on iron
- **Suspects:** nothing. Genuinely.
- **Does NOT know:** the Agreement · the door beneath the mill (he has never been taken to it) · the Gear · the Order · the count · the keeping

## Secrets

1. **The Vale job.** DEFERRED — content undecided by design.
2. Where he saw the mark. **PROPOSED, unresolved.**
3. He has kept a wax impression of every unusual lock he has ever worked on, in a drawer, for thirty years, as a professional record. **He does not consider this a secret.** It is the single most valuable archive in Alderwick and nobody has ever asked to see it.

## Character arc

**Start:** the tradesman the office calls when something is shut.
**Middle:** becomes Leon's technical education, and — through the drawer of wax impressions — an archive nobody knew existed.
**Late:** the mark, and the Vale job, arriving in that order, years apart, neither of them because he decided to tell.
**Unresolved:** whether he ever understands what he has been adjacent to. Preferred answer: no.

## Long-term potential

- The drawer of impressions is a whole investigation waiting to be asked for
- The Vale job is a reserve revelation that costs nothing to hold and can be spent at any time
- He can outfit Leon for Phase E's capability development without granting him a single supernatural advantage
- Mortality: available but wasteful. He is worth more alive and mildly annoyed.
