## Intelligence Background — Character Consequences

Vale's intelligence background manifests in specific character traits:

- **Compartmentalization:** He is accustomed to separating what he knows, what he suspects, and what is safe to disclose. He does not give all information he possesses.
- **Risk assessment:** He can recognize that a situation is dangerous without needing to understand its complete mechanism.
- **Information literacy:** He knows how records are altered, suppressed, and weaponized. He keeps duplicates. He verifies sources.
- **People reading:** He assesses who can be trusted, who is compromised, and what can be said in front of whom.
- **NOT omniscient:** He does not understand the full supernatural mechanism behind the Door, the Bell, or other anomalies. His knowledge is investigative and institutional, not supernatural.

## Constable Career

Constable of Alderwick before Calloway; overlapped with Calloway "for a short time" (Ch40). Preceded in the Alderwick office by **Marcus Ferrand**, who kept the count for fifteen years and was erased from the records (Ch107, Ch120, Ch121). Vale inherited Ferrand's empty file and never wrote in it — "A man who keeps a file empty for that long has made a decision about it" (Ch107). He also inherited and passed on the **box of the constables**, which held Ferrand's notebook; he told Calloway the box held what could not be written down and declined to itemise it (Ch121).

## Alderwick Years / Fifty-Year Guardianship

Kept the Agreement fifty years; kept the door closed; left no account (Ch68, 79, 124). The keeper before Vale opened the door and did not return; Vale was "the keeper who kept the door closed, because the keeper before him had opened it" (Ch75). He kept the keeper's sign from the Order deliberately (Ch54, 57). He never took the keeper's key to the tower (Ch74).

## Relationship to the Sealed Door

Guardian, not scholar. Explicitly does not know what is behind it (Ch40). Eleanor Shaw's account: he did not keep it closed because the Order told him to — "He kept it closed because he was protecting the sign," from everyone (Ch61).

## Relationship to Leon

Chose Leon as a person, not by appointment, prophecy, or supernatural selection — CONFIRMED. Ch40: "You have not agreed to anything" / "I think someone believes it is [you]" / "Do you?" "No." Ch48 and Ch49: "There is no one to leave them to." He does not answer "Why me?" in either chapter — CONFIRMED and to remain unanswered. He assessed Leon before trusting him: "You ask questions in the right order. That is not a compliment. It means you will be difficult to lie to, and men who are difficult to lie to are told nothing at all" (Ch40).

**The eight months (Ch41, Ch45, Ch48, Ch50) — CONFIRMED.** The relationship is built by accumulation, never declared. Neither man names it. Recurring, deliberately unremarked: Vale asks whether Leon has eaten (every visit; Leon only notices the pattern in Ch51, after there is no one left to ask); Vale keeps bread and soup for him; complains when he is late and denies noticing; sends him away and expects him back; scolds his coat and is refused. Leon begins the acquaintance with errands (Ch41, a corrected transcription) and by Ch50 arrives with nothing to ask. Vale declines to ask why — the only question he ever declines.

Leon's own recognition is **posthumous by design**. While Vale lives, Leon records the investigation and not the afternoons ("Everything I thought mattered is written down. The afternoons are not," Ch41). The realisation lands in Ch51–Ch52 through objects, not statements: the half-finished cup, the empty chair, the heavier coat he refused twice and takes on the fifth day.

**Vale never calls Leon a grandson, son, or heir, and must not.** Ch41 is as near as canon goes: "People who grow up alone learn to carry everything themselves. It is a useful skill. It is not a virtue." / "I manage." / "I know."

**Leon's orphanhood — CONFIRMED (Ch41):** both parents dead within a year; Leon was seven; six years in a household paid a small sum to have him. Vale offers no pity and closes nothing: "That is early. Late enough to remember. Early enough that no one thinks you need to be told anything."

## Private Box

CONFIRMED. **Shown twice** — first in Ch48 (August 1817), from under a folded blanket at the bottom of the press, then returned to the press at Vale's insistence ("Not yet. I am not dead. It is an unpleasant habit, giving things away while you are still using them"); handed over in Ch49 (October), already out of the press when Leon arrives, with the instruction "Look at it again. I want you to have looked at it twice."

His **private property** — "These are mine. Not the parish's. Not the Order's. Mine." (Ch48, repeated Ch49: "I told you that in August. I am telling you again because you will be told otherwise.") Size of a document case, heavier than it looks, packed rather than filled. Contents: soft-creased folded papers · a bundle of letters tied with browned tape · a **service journal** (dark leather, broken spine, pocket-dirty edges) · a **district map, not of Alderwick**, of a kind constabularies print and do not sell, marked in ink in several shades over years · two stoppered bottles labelled in an unknown hand · a military brass button worn smooth · a woman's ring too small for his finger · the empty folded cloth where the Gear had lain, with the shape still pressed into the weave.

He states plainly that some of it is nothing, that some he never understood in fifty years, and that he cannot say which is which — "The button is nothing. The ring is not nothing, but it is nothing to you… The map I could have burned thirty years ago and did not, and I have never been able to give myself a reason." On the journal: "Read it in order. Not the end first. A man who reads the end first learns what happened and never learns why." / "There is a great deal in it I did not want anyone to find. That is why it is in a box and not in an office."

In Ch48 he notices Leon has seen the cloth and neither of them remarks on it. In Ch49 he unfolds it himself.

**Concealment instruction (Ch48):** "If anyone comes here asking about my effects — the parish, or the Order, or a man with a letter — you have not seen a box." And on method: "Do not lie if you can avoid it. Refuse. A lie has to be maintained. A refusal only has to be repeated."

**"Why me?" (Ch48, asked again Ch49):** never answered completely. His nearest offers — "You came back." / "You ask before you take. I have watched how you handle things." — with an explicit refusal to make it more: "if I make it more than that you will start behaving like a man who was chosen, and men who believe they were chosen stop checking their own work." **Do not resolve.**

The box is **not** the Gear box, and the Constable has **no automatic ownership claim** over it. Distinct from the **box of the constables** (Ch121), which is an institutional object.

## Knowledge Boundaries

Vale knew more than Leon and less than the whole. His expertise is **investigative and institutional**, not supernatural — he is not an occult authority and must never become one.

### Knows
- How intelligence investigations are conducted.
- How records are altered, suppressed, and weaponized.
- How to verify witnesses and assess credibility.
- How to place objects so they will be found only by someone looking correctly.
- That the Order of Severence does not understand the keeper's sign.
- That the Agreement exists and what it requires of a keeper.
- That the Door must remain closed.
- That the Gear is significant and must be kept from certain people.
- Institutional history of the Constable and its compromises.

### Suspected
- That there is a supernatural or anomalous dimension to the Door and the boundary.
- That the Order of Severence represents a particular threat or interest.
- That his own placement in Alderwick may have been protective, operational, or both.

### Does NOT Know
- What is behind the Door.
- What the Gear actually does or how it works.
- What the inscription means.
- Who set the original prohibition or the full history of the Agreement before his custodial chain.
- The complete supernatural mechanism behind the Bell, 00:00, or other anomalies.
- The full structure or membership of the Order of Severence.
- Whether all of his superiors understood the full context of his placement.

### Safe to Disclose
Vale distinguishes between what is known, what is suspected, and what is safe to tell. He withholds information naturally, not out of mere secrecy but out of professional habit. He can know a situation is dangerous without understanding its complete mechanism.

## Personality / Behavioural Traits

Seated, deliberate, withholding; lets visitors finish before answering; answers in the fewest words that are still true; refuses to discuss his former position. Notices what others overlook. Asks precisely who knows what. Uncomfortable when Leon repeats information in company (Ch53–54 fallout: "Vale carried it for fifty years and told no one. You have carried it for a week and told the room"). Knows how official reports are worded. Recognises altered documents. Keeps duplicates. Never carried a paper through town in daylight; came at dusk, never twice by the same street (Ch61). Closed shutters before handing over the constables' box and watched Calloway learn where the key went (Ch121). His press was ordered systematically, with one space left empty and clean; his bed was made to a standard learned under discipline (Ch52).

**Observed by Leon in the eight months (CONFIRMED, read as an old man's carefulness at the time):**
- Checks the window sash "without interrupting himself, the way another man might check that his pocket was buttoned" (Ch41).
- Asks who saw Leon come up the lane, whether anyone was at the top of it, and whether they were coming up or going down (Ch41).
- Corrects a quotation Leon has paraphrased — "It is the same meaning. It is not the same sentence… It matters when a man tells you he is quoting" (Ch41).
- Approves of Leon writing things down and declines to say why (Ch41).
- Puts a subject down between them and leaves it, returning to it weeks later (Ch41, Ch48).
- Asks the same question twice, twenty minutes apart, buried under unrelated talk, to test whether the answer changes; says "Good" of the answerer, not the answer (Ch45).
- Distinguishes a refusal from an answer, and says so: "Most men give you a refusal shaped like an answer and you go away satisfied" (Ch45).
- Will not say where a thing was learned, on the grounds that knowing where breeds false confidence about why (Ch45).

The reader should read all of this as "old veteran / experienced constable" first. The pattern should resolve later.

## Teaching (CONFIRMED — Ch45, echoed after death)

Framed as ordinary caution; institutional in origin; never labelled. The set, in his own words:
- **"If there is only one copy, there is no copy."** — a record in one hand is that man's word about what you said. File the second copy rather than hide it: "Hidden things are found by people looking for hidden things. Ordinary things are not looked at."
- **"What I saw. What I was told. What I concluded. Keep them apart."** — Leon writes it into his own book in this form. Provoked by Leon reporting a map's trees as ground truth: "You have told me what the map says. You have not told me what is there."
- **Never read the end first.** — "A man who reads the conclusion first will find the evidence agrees with it. He will find that every time. He will be wrong perhaps once in five, and he will never discover which once."
- **"A man who tells you he has nothing to hide has told you the one thing about himself he wanted you to know."**
- **Four roads.** — "It costs you nothing to use four roads. It costs you a great deal to be wrong about whether anybody is counting."
- **"You do not owe the dead your life."** (Ch50) — "Remember them. Learn from them. But do not become their unfinished business."

**Payoff discipline:** most of it is simply wisdom. Only a few items should later resolve as professional shorthand, and the ratio must be preserved. Confirmed echoes so far: Leon copies the transfer entry and registers that he has just made a second copy (Ch52); Vale's own maxim resurfaces in Calloway's mouth twenty years on — "a man who has only one copy of a thing does not own it" (Ch107).
