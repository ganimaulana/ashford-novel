## A1. THE GENERATIONAL PROBLEM AND ITS SOLUTION

Every named Constable-side figure in canon is old: Calloway is sixty, Vale was in his eighties, Ferrand is dead, the clerk is about fifty, Hobbs was old and is missing, the verger and the farmers are unaged but not young. **Leon is the only young person in the institution.** That is why the post-Vale arc currently has no peer texture at all — no one to be wrong in front of, no one to be measured against, nobody his own age who thinks he is being clever.

The fix is two peers who are **not variants of each other and not variants of Leon.**

```
                    ┌──────────────────────────────┐
   THE CORE THREE   │  LEON        the record      │  what happened
   (Leon's          │  OWEN TRANT  the room        │  who is in it
    generation)     │  CECILY      the body        │  what was done to it
                    │   PARGETER                   │
                    └──────────────────────────────┘
                         │ report upward, unevenly
                         ▼
   SENIOR LAYER   ORME ── field · closes cases · hates "unresolved"
   (own jobs,     CALLOWAY ── the office · the box · the letter to London
    own history,  INGLE ── the books · the second copy · the locked drawer
    not on every  DENBY ── the body · the inquest · what may be signed
    mission)      FAULDS ── locks · hinges · seals · mechanisms
                  PINNOCK ── who travelled, when, with what, who paid
                         │
                         ╎ (outside the institution entirely)
                         ▼
   INDEPENDENT     SHAW ── the archive nobody owns · later, the secret contact
```

**Three rules for the core three:**
- They divide the *evidence*, not the *virtue*. None of them is the good one.
- They report to different people and are not always told the same thing.
- **They are never all correct at once, and never all wrong at once.**

## A2. THE CORE THREE

### LEON ASHCROFT — the record
Existing canon; see `characters/15_LEON_ASHCROFT.md` and `07_LEON_FIELD_DEVELOPMENT.md` (Phase E).

Function on the team: chronology, contradiction, cross-reference, pattern. He is the only one who reads what the others said last month. **Not a fighter, not an authority, not naturally liked.** His value is that he is difficult to lie to, and that is not a compliment (Ch40).

### OWEN TRANT — the room
**New. Full profile: `characters/20_OWEN_TRANT.md`.**

26. Enrolled watchman, then retained. Ex-militia, discharged 1815, back to a town that had got on without him. Goes first through doors. Reads *people* the way Leon reads paper — who is lying, who is frightened, who is about to run — and is right often enough to distrust anyone who needs to write it down.

**His defining refusal: he will not write anything.** In the militia he learned that written things are what men are punished with. This puts him in direct, permanent, load-bearing collision with the institution's entire doctrine ("if there is only one copy, there is no copy") and with Leon personally. It also means **his best evidence can never rise above OBSERVED standing**, and he does not care, and Leon cannot make him care.

Ability: **none, and never.** Owen is the load-bearing proof that this world's competence is human.

Relationship to Leon: contempt → grudging use → **the thing the whole arc is for**. Owen is the man who eventually stands where Leon tells him to stand, before he understands why. That is Leon's combat identity arriving.

### CECILY PARGETER — the body
**New. Full profile: `characters/05_CECILY_PARGETER.md`.**

24. Apothecary's daughter; retained per case by the constable's office as an **examiner** — the person permitted into rooms Owen and Leon may not enter: women's chambers, lyings-in, the women's side of the poor-house, the laying-out of a female corpse. Historically grounded (women were routinely used to search and examine women and to give evidence at inquests) and it gives her a field function no man on the team can perform.

Precise, physically timid, frightened most of the time and present anyway. She can date a bruise, tell a fall from a blow, tell a hand from a strap, tell what was in a cup. She writes in a small hand and signs everything, because a thing unsigned was never said.

Weakness: she over-qualifies. Every statement arrives wrapped in the doubt it deserves, which is scrupulous and useless in a room where a decision is being made now. Orme reads her honesty as timidity.

Ability: **one, optional, author-gated** — see §A5 and `../19_SUPERNATURAL/01_ABILITY_ARCHITECTURE.md`. The team functions entirely without it.

Relationship to Leon: the only person who understands what he is doing with the three-column discipline, and the only person who will tell him his conclusion is not supported. **Not a romance by default.** If it ever becomes one, it must cost them both professionally, because they are the two people whose signatures corroborate each other and that corroboration becomes worthless the moment anyone suspects it.

## A3. THE SENIOR LAYER

Senior personnel have **their own jobs**. None of them attends every case. Most of them would rather Leon went away.

### JAMES CALLOWAY — the office (EXISTING CANON, unchanged)
Sixty. ~23 years in post. Not revised, not promoted, not diminished. See `characters/06_CONSTABLE.md` (stale — see AUDIT) and `../16_GOVERNMENT/GOVERNMENT_CHARACTERS.md`.

Post-Vale function: **institutional bridge, and nothing warmer.** He hires Leon, assigns Leon, pays Leon, tells Leon "to notice" and then "decide," and accepts divergence with "Then you'll have to live with your decision" (Ch46). He holds the letter to London, the locked drawer, and the box of the constables.

**Hard constraint: Calloway must NOT become Leon's new emotional mentor.** Vale's place stays empty. Calloway's register is employer, predecessor's successor, and occasional witness — he tells Leon *about* Vale (Ch107, Ch121) and that is the whole of the intimacy. He never asks whether Leon has eaten.

### REUBEN ORME — the round
**New. `characters/22_REUBEN_ORME.md`.** 49. Senior constable under Calloway; twenty years of it somewhere else first.

The man who actually runs the field. Trains Owen, tests Leon, decides who goes in which door. Competent, physically hard, fair in a way that is not kind.

**His flaw is the engine of the whole E.R.C. arc: he closes cases.** An open matter is a reproach to him — it means a man is still out there and a family is still waiting. So he will accept the *adequate* answer over the *true* one, honestly and in good faith, and he genuinely believes Leon's refusal to conclude is cowardice dressed as rigour. **He hates the word UNRESOLVED.** He is not wrong to hate it. Somebody has to answer to the family.

He is not Vale's replacement and knows nothing about him beyond the name.

### CUTHBERT INGLE — the books
**New. `characters/07_CUTHBERT_INGLE.md`.** 61. The constable's clerk. Proposed identification with the unnamed clerk who wrote Leon's name onto the watch sheet in Ch10 — an *identification*, not a change; the manuscript's clerk stays unnamed on the page.

Nothing enters the record except through him. He makes the second copy. He knows which reports went up and which came back altered, and he has never said so, because saying so would require him to say who handled them.

**He is the plausible internal-compromise suspect, and must never be confirmed either way.** Vale's fall is tied to Order penetration of the Constable; Ingle has been at that desk longer than Calloway has held the office. He is also, equally plausibly, an old man who is simply tired and careful. **Both readings must remain fully available at all times.** Do not resolve.

Ability: none.

### WALTER DENBY — the body, officially
**New. `characters/28_WALTER_DENBY.md`.** 56. Parish surgeon; retained for examinations and inquests.

**He is the evidence threshold made human.** He will write only what he can defend in front of a coroner, a jury, and a family's solicitor, and he will not be argued past it. He therefore spends the arc *refusing Leon the corroboration Leon needs* — and being right to refuse. The one signature that can raise a claim to CONFIRMED standing in Alderwick belongs to a man who despises speculation.

Not an occultist. Never becomes one. His concession, when it finally comes, is a single sentence with three qualifications in it, and it is a triumph.

### ISAAC FAULDS — locks and mechanisms
**New. `characters/14_ISAAC_FAULDS.md`.** 44. Whitesmith and locksmith. Retained per job.

Locks, hinges, wards, seals, hasps, mechanisms, and the difference between a thing that was forced and a thing that was opened. Deliberately **not** a surveyor — Marlow's Ch38 niche (boundary stones, masonry courses, contour lines) is left alone.

Story reach: a world with a sealed door, a lock made by Aurelius (Ch100), an iron box with a strap and a first sign (Ch102), a mechanism under the mill that rotates and clicks (Ch49), and altered records with broken seals — needs one man who can say *how* a thing was made to be closed. He is also Leon's honest route to becoming dangerous without becoming powerful: preparation, tools, and knowing which way a hinge falls.

He asks no questions about the marks. He has seen the circle-and-line before, on a job, years ago, and does not volunteer where. **PROPOSED hook — do not resolve early.**

### HESTER PINNOCK — who travelled
**New. `characters/13_HESTER_PINNOCK.md`.** 38. Widow; keeps the carrier's book at the Greyhound yard.

Retained informally on Leon's own £1/week precedent. She is not sworn and would refuse to be. She knows who left Alderwick and when, what weight went with them, who paid, whose name was given wrong, and which parcels went unrecorded. That is the whole of 1817 surveillance and it is enough.

Her price is not money: **she sells information for information.** Every answer costs Leon a true fact about himself, which is exactly the wrong currency for a man keeping a secret. She is the leak that Leon opens himself.

She is not a supernatural detector. She never becomes one. Ability: none.

### ELEANOR SHAW — the archive nobody owns (EXISTING CANON, affiliation revised)
Outside the institution. Not staff, not retained, not a colleague. Her function is preserved and deepened: she is where the paper is that the parish does not have and the Order thinks it controls. See `characters/10_ELEANOR_SHAW.md` v3.0 and the secret-contact architecture in `../08_UNDERGROUND/01_ANONYMOUS_TOKEN_NETWORK.md` §N.

## A6. TEAM DYNAMICS — WHAT PRODUCES SCENES

1. **Owen won't write; the institution runs on writing.** Leon ends up writing Owen's evidence for him, which makes it Leon's word about what Owen said — the exact thing Vale warned about (Ch45). Owen eventually notices. That is a betrayal neither of them intended.
2. **Orme needs an answer; Leon needs the truth; Denby will sign neither.** Three good-faith positions, structurally irreconcilable, recurring in every case.
3. **Cecily's testimony is unimprovable.** She is the only one in the room who is certain and the only one who cannot be believed. Leon's job is to find the material trace that makes her sensation into a fact — which means Cecily's usefulness is entirely dependent on Leon's competence, and she knows it, and resents it.
4. **Ingle is either the leak or an old man.** Every scene at the cabinet is played twice by the reader.
5. **Pinnock charges in truths.** Leon's secrets are his currency and he is spending them.
6. **Calloway will not comfort anyone.** The chair Vale sat in stays empty, and the arc's grief is structural rather than stated.
