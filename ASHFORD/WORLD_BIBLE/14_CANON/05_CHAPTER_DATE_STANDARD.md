# Alderwick — CHAPTER DATE FORMAT STANDARD

## Status

**Permanent writing rule** for Alderwick chapter formatting.
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-31
**Canon Status:** LOCKED

---

## Core Principle

> **Dates are used when necessary, not by default.**

The presence of a date in the internal timeline does not mean the date must appear in the chapter.

The absence of a date from a chapter does not mean the event has no canonical date internally.

---

## 1. Default Rule

The default chapter format is:

```text
Chapter X — Title
```

NOT:

```text
Chapter X — Title
Date
```

unless the date is specifically required.

### Default

```text
Chapter 36 — The Pursuer
```

### Not Default

```text
Chapter 36 — The Pursuer
October 17, 1816
```

---

## 2. When a Date Should Be Used

An explicit date may be used when it is necessary for one or more of the following:

### A. Timeline Clarity

The exact date is important for understanding the sequence of events.

Examples:
- an event occurs immediately before another known event
- two events occur on different days and the distinction matters
- a character has a scheduled meeting
- a deadline matters
- an investigation depends on the passage of a specific number of days

### B. Historical / Political Events

A date may be explicitly stated when a major historical or Alderwick-canon event requires temporal anchoring.

Examples:
- major political gathering
- public riot
- military deployment
- government proclamation
- important election
- major religious ceremony
- major disaster
- major criminal event

Do NOT add a date merely because the event is important.

Use one only if the exact timing contributes to the narrative.

### C. Mystery / Investigation

A date may be a clue.

Examples:
- an old letter contains a date
- a death certificate contains a date
- a newspaper is dated
- an old journal entry is dated
- a historical document references a specific day
- Leon notices that two apparently unrelated events occurred on the same date

In these cases, the date belongs naturally to the object or evidence.

It does NOT necessarily need to become the chapter heading.

### D. Time-Sensitive Events

Use an explicit date when characters genuinely need to know the exact day.

Examples:
- someone is expected to arrive
- a trial is scheduled
- a military order becomes effective
- a public meeting has been announced
- a deadline is approaching

### E. Deliberate Temporal Disorientation

A date may occasionally be used as a mystery device.

Example:

```text
March 4, 1812
```

followed by information that makes the reader question why that date matters.

This should be intentional and rare.

---

## 3. When a Date Should Not Be Used

Do NOT add a date simply because:

- a new chapter begins
- the previous chapter had a date
- the chapter takes place on another day
- the writer knows the exact date
- the timeline document contains a date
- the chapter needs a sense of realism
- every chapter in an earlier draft had dates
- an AI-generated outline includes dates

The existence of a chronological timeline does NOT require every chapter to display a date.

---

## 4. Internal Timeline vs Reader-Facing Date

Maintain a strict distinction between:

### Internal Timeline

The World Bible may track events with exact dates when useful.

Example:

```text
1816-10-17 — Political meeting in Alderwick
```

This is acceptable for internal continuity.

### Reader-Facing Chapter

The chapter does not need to display:

```text
October 17, 1816
```

unless the date has narrative importance.

This distinction is REQUIRED.

The internal timeline exists for continuity.

The narrative exists for storytelling.

---

## 5. Natural Temporal Flow

When an exact date is unnecessary, communicate time naturally through prose.

Use contextual indicators such as:
- the following morning
- two days later
- by the end of the week
- several nights afterward
- the next Sunday
- before dawn
- that evening
- after several days
- later that month
- as the weather turned colder
- by the time the market reopened

Use these naturally.

Do not overuse explicit temporal markers either.

The reader should be able to understand the passage of time without seeing a calendar at the beginning of every chapter.

---

## 6. Date Consistency Rule

If a date IS used, it must be consistent with:
- MASTER CANON
- TIMELINE
- WORLD_BIBLE
- CHARACTER continuity
- historical season/weather
- day of the week when relevant
- political events
- travel time
- character movements

Never generate a date casually.

Before adding an exact date, verify that it does not contradict existing canon.

---

## 7. Date Precision

Use the minimum precision required by the story.

| Precision | When to Use |
|-----------|-------------|
| Year only (`1816`) | When only the year matters |
| Month (`October 1816`) | When the month matters |
| Day (`17 October 1816`) | When the exact day matters |
| Time (`17 October 1816, shortly before midnight`) | When the exact time matters |

Do not use greater precision than necessary.

---

## 8. Mystery Writing Principle

For Alderwick's mystery structure:

> **Time should usually be experienced before it is documented.**

Do not make the novel feel like a police report or historical chronicle.

The passage of time should primarily be conveyed through:
- environment
- weather
- routines
- character behavior
- changing circumstances
- newspapers
- letters
- appointments
- political developments
- physical changes in locations

Explicit dates should become significant precisely because they are relatively uncommon.

---

## 9. LOTM-Style Mystery Pacing

Dates must not weaken mystery pacing.

Avoid repeatedly giving the reader precise temporal information that does not contribute to the mystery.

A date can itself become:
- a clue
- a contradiction
- a pattern
- a false lead
- a connection between events
- evidence of manipulation
- evidence that a document was altered

Therefore, dates should be treated as potentially meaningful information.

---

## 10. AI Generation Rule

Every time an AI generates or revises an Alderwick chapter, it must ask internally:

> "Does the exact date materially improve the scene, mystery, timeline, or historical context?"

If NO: **Do not include a chapter date.**

If YES: **Include the minimum necessary date precision.**

Never add a date merely to follow a formatting pattern.

---

## 11. Chapter Format Standard

The canonical chapter format is now:

```text
Chapter [Number] — [Title]
```

Optional (only when narratively justified):

```text
Chapter [Number] — [Title]

[Date]
```

The optional date must never be treated as mandatory.

---

## 12. Date Status Classification

When reviewing a chapter, classify explicit dates internally as:

| Status | Definition | Action |
|--------|------------|--------|
| **REQUIRED** | The date is necessary to the narrative | Keep |
| **USEFUL** | The date provides meaningful clarity but could potentially be omitted | Evaluate based on pacing |
| **UNNECESSARY** | The date exists only because of formatting or author convenience | Remove |

---

## 13. Validation After Every Chapter

Before finalizing a chapter, check:
- Does the chapter actually need an explicit date?
- Is the date narratively meaningful?
- Is the precision appropriate?
- Does it conflict with the internal timeline?
- Does it reveal more temporal information than necessary for the mystery?
- Could natural prose communicate the same information more effectively?

If the date is not needed: **REMOVE IT.**

---

## 14. Retroactive Application

This rule applies retroactively to Chapters 1–35.

Dates should be removed from chapter headings unless they serve a narrative purpose.

Dates that are:
- meaningful clues
- belong to historical documents
- necessary for timeline comprehension
- explicitly referenced by characters
- establish an important event
- required by an existing LOCKED canon event

...should be preserved.

---

## 15. Chapter ≠ Day

A chapter does NOT equal one day. The previous convention of one chapter per day was a formatting choice, not a narrative requirement.

| Pattern | When to Use |
|---------|-------------|
| One chapter = one day | When the day contains a complete narrative unit |
| One chapter = multiple days | When events flow across days without natural breaking points |
| Multiple chapters = one day | When a single day contains multiple distinct scenes or developments |
| Time skip between chapters | When days or weeks pass without narrative significance |

Choose the structure that best serves the story. Do not force one chapter per day merely because the timeline tracks days. Do not force one day per chapter merely because chapters are short.

The reader should feel time passing naturally, not count chapters like calendar pages.

---

## 16. Final Standard

Effective immediately, Alderwick chapters must NOT follow a "one chapter = one date" or "one chapter = one day" structure.

The canonical principle is:

> **Chronology exists for continuity. Dates exist for narrative purpose. Chapters exist for storytelling.**

Internal world-building may maintain precise dates.

Reader-facing chapters should reveal dates selectively.

This rule applies retroactively to Chapters 1–35 and prospectively to all future chapters unless explicitly overridden for a specific chapter or arc.
