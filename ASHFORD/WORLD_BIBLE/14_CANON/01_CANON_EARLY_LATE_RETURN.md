## A. ESTABLISHED CANON

### A1. Leon's Actual Arrival Date
- **Date:** 12 November 1816
- **Time:** Approximately 19:42 at the stagecoach stop; approximately 19:58 at the house on Bellweather Lane
- **Source:** Chapter 1 opening; Leon's notebook entries (Chapter 1, lines 562-564)

### A2. Expected Return/Arrival Date
- **Date:** 13 November 1816
- **Source:** Helen's portrait inscription: "He would return on 13 November 1816. He was supposed to arrive tomorrow." (Chapter 1, lines 838-840)
- **Context:** The portrait depicts a boy of approximately sixteen standing before the same house on Bellweather Lane.

### A3. The Meaning of "Early"
- **Definition:** Leon arrived on 12 November, one day before the date indicated in Helen's portrait inscription (13 November).
- **Helen's statement:** "You've come earlier than expected." (Chapter 1, line 364)
- **Narrative confirmation:** Helen's internal scene confirms she was waiting for the child in the portrait to return on 13 November; today is 12 November. (Chapter 1, lines 844-860)

### A4. The Meaning of "Late"
- **Established usage:** Leon states "I only felt late" while walking through Market Square on the morning of 13 November. (Chapter 1, lines 818-820)
- **Nature:** This is a subjective feeling experienced by Leon, NOT a canonical statement that his arrival was late.
- **Status:** The feeling is recorded but unexplained. No canon document defines its cause.

### A5. "Don't be late again"
- **Source:** Leon's dream on the night of 12 November. (Chapter 1, lines 596-644)
- **Content:** A boy in a dark coat stands before the old house. He says: "Don't be late again."
- **Status:** Dream content. The phrase is recorded in Leon's notebook. Its meaning, origin, and the identity of the boy are NOT established.

### A6. Leon's Familiarity with Alderwick
- **Explicit statement:** "The name of the town felt strangely familiar on my tongue, though I was certain I had never been here before." (Chapter 1, lines 41-42)
- **Leon's verification:** "No road I remembered. No face I knew. No house I had ever seen." (Chapter 1, lines 47-48)
- **Description of feeling:** "Only a vague feeling, like hearing a melody that had once been deeply familiar without being able to remember its title." (Chapter 1, lines 49-50)
- **Status:** The familiarity is acknowledged by Leon but unexplained. It is NOT established as literal memory.

### A7. Leon Has Never Lived in Alderwick Before Chapter 1
- **Established:** Leon explicitly states he has never been to Alderwick before.
- **Source:** Chapter 1, lines 41-49.
- **Implication:** Any sense of familiarity is unexplained and must NOT be treated as factual memory of prior residence.

## B. LEON'S KNOWLEDGE (at end of Chapter 1)

Leon knows the following facts:

1. He arrived in Alderwick by stagecoach from London on 12 November 1816.
2. He is living in a house on Bellweather Lane (no number; only the street name).
3. He received a letter from Pembroke & Clarke (solicitors, Lincoln's Inn, London) containing:
   - A partial will issued in the name of Aurelius Ashcroft
   - Documents referencing a property on Bellweather Lane
   - A handwritten letter in unfamiliar script
4. He has found a recurring symbol: a circle + three lines + one dot (appears on the front door nameplate and the room key).
5. He read a notice: "Key found. Please return to the house." dated November 1798 (18 years prior).
6. He met Helen; she said he had "come earlier than expected."
7. He saw a painting of a boy on the stairs; Helen was reluctant to give a name.
8. He heard a bell toll once during the night; Helen said the bell had not rung "in a long time."
9. He had a dream: a boy in a dark coat before the old house; the boy said "Don't be late again."
10. He met a mysterious man in a woollen coat with a silver walking stick at the inn; the man seemed to know him.

## C. READER KNOWLEDGE

The reader knows the following (revealed through Helen's scene, unknown to Leon):

1. Helen holds an old miniature portrait of a boy of approximately sixteen standing before the same house on Bellweather Lane.
2. On the back of the wooden frame is written, in fading ink: "He would return on 13 November 1816. He was supposed to arrive tomorrow."
3. Today is 12 November. Tomorrow (13 November) was supposed to be the day the child in the portrait came back.
4. Helen has been waiting for this child for a long time.
5. Helen is uncertain: "Had someone finally come back? Or had someone who should never have left come home?" (Chapter 1, lines 866-868)
