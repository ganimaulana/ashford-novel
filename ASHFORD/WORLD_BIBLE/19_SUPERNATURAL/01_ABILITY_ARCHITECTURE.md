## D1. THE ARCHITECTURE — SIX RULES

**RULE 1 — Objects act. People notice.**
The default answer to "should this character have an ability?" is **no**. Capability in this world is craft, patience, access, and paying attention. Nine of the eleven post-Vale personnel have no ability at all, and that is the design, not a gap.

**RULE 2 — An ability grants perception, never force.**
No ability may damage, restrain, compel, heal, move, conceal, or win a fight. There is no combat ability anywhere in this framework and there must never be one. Leon's combat identity is `PATTERN → PREDICTION → POSITIONING → TIMING → COORDINATION` (see `../04_CHARACTERS/07_LEON_FIELD_DEVELOPMENT.md`) and it is entirely mundane.

**RULE 3 — An ability must be cheaper to decline than to keep.**
Every ability specified here has a full **mundane fallback**. If the author declines all of them, no relationship, dynamic, or arc in Phases A–G changes. This is deliberate: it means Phase 3H D7 can stand permanently.

**RULE 4 — An ability must produce a problem, not a solution.**
The test: *does it shorten an investigation?* If yes, reject it. Both proposed abilities **lengthen** investigations by producing claims that cannot be corroborated.

**RULE 5 — Every ability must have a defined interaction with STANDING.**
See `../08_UNDERGROUND/03_ESOTERIC_RECORD_CLASSIFICATION.md` §21–25. An ability that could reach CORROBORATED standing is too strong. **Both proposed abilities are capped at OBSERVED, permanently, by their own nature.**

**RULE 6 — Nobody may know abilities are a category.**
No character in 1817 has the concept "supernatural ability." There is no name for it, no community of holders, no training, no scale, and no way to look one up. A person with an ability believes they have a nervous complaint, a good eye, or a habit.

## D2. THE TWENTY-FOUR-FIELD SPECIFICATION

Any ability, existing or future, must be specified against all of these before use. An unspecified ability is not approved.

`nature · trigger · effect · what it does NOT give · range · duration · repeatability · limitation · physical cost · social cost · institutional cost · failure condition · false-positive behaviour · detectability by others · knowledge required · whether the holder understands it · possible misuse by others · possible misuse by the holder · interaction with STANDING · interaction with Token/Gear/count · what it must never become · mundane fallback if declined · who knows · status label`

## D4. THE TWO PROPOSED ABILITIES

### D4.1 — CECILY PARGETER: THRESHOLD-COLD (PROPOSED, OPTIONAL)

Full character context: `../04_CHARACTERS/characters/05_CECILY_PARGETER.md`.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Nature | Standing in a doorway, gate, stair-head, or on a boundary line, she sometimes feels a specific cold at the nape of the neck. It means: *something crossed here that should not have.* |
| Trigger | Cannot be summoned. Occurs while stationary in an opening, usually at the moment she stops moving. |
| Effect | One bit: *here, and wrongly.* |
| Does NOT give | Direction · distance · identity · time · number · image · voice · name · age of the crossing · what crossed · whether it is still present |
| Range | The opening she stands in. Not the building. |
| Duration | Seconds. Does not recur on re-entry the same day. |
| Repeatability | None. She cannot test it, ever. |
| Limitation | Cannot distinguish recent from ancient, nor the Order from the count from something unnamed |
| Physical cost | Ill the rest of the day; bad sleep; headache behind the eyes |
| Social cost | She is a nervous girl. Permanently, to men who already thought so. |
| Institutional cost | **The real cost.** Every report spends credibility she cannot replace. Three reports and she is the problem, not the evidence. |
| Failure condition | Fails when she is frightened (which is often) and when she wants it |
| False positives | **Yes, and she can never learn which.** Non-negotiable. |
| Detectability | Nothing to see. She stops and puts a hand on the door frame. |
| Knowledge required | None. Has felt it since childhood, believed it ordinary. |
| Does she understand it? | No, and never fully will |
| Misuse by others | She can be walked deliberately through doorways as an instrument. **Leon will be tempted. Doing it is a moral failure, not a tactic.** |
| Misuse by herself | She could claim it when she has not felt it, to be listened to. She will not. Once, she nearly does. |
| STANDING | **OBSERVED, permanently.** A sensation has no second witness and leaves no material trace. |
| Token / Gear / count | **No interaction. She has never touched a Token and must not be given one.** Whether her cold and the count's warmth are the same phenomenon inverted is **UNKNOWN and must not be answered.** |
| Must never become | Vision · voices · ghosts · naming the dead · tracking · combat sense · a reliable instrument |
| **Mundane fallback** | She is simply the best reader of a room in Alderwick and occasionally says *someone has been in here* on grounds she cannot articulate. **Every scene and dynamic survives intact.** |
| Who knows | Her father (told her not to speak of it) · eventually Leon · nobody else |

**Why it is worth having:** it makes the classification framework's honest cruelty concrete. She is the only person in the room who is certain and the only one who cannot be believed — and Leon's function, converting her sensation into a material fact somebody else can examine, becomes the thing the team actually needs him for.

### D4.2 — ELEANOR SHAW: RECOGNITION OF A HAND (PROPOSED, OPTIONAL)

Full character context: `../04_CHARACTERS/characters/10_ELEANOR_SHAW.md` §6.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Nature | Looking at two documents, she can tell whether they were written by the same hand — across decades, across disguise, across a copyist's imitation. Nothing more. |
| Trigger | Direct unhurried sight of both, in good light. Cannot work from memory of one. |
| Effect | Same hand / not the same hand / **cannot say** — and "cannot say" must be frequent |
| Does NOT give | Name · date · place · content · meaning · intent · authenticity |
| Limitation | Cannot detect that a document is *false* — only whether it is in the hand it claims. Fails on short samples, copperplate, and any hand taught to a standard. |
| Physical cost | None |
| Institutional cost | Her judgement is worth nothing to any authority; she cannot show her working |
| Failure condition | Fails on anything she wants the answer to |
| Detectability | She looks at paper for a long time |
| Does she understand it? | No — she believes it is twenty years of ledgers, and would be insulted by the suggestion |
| Misuse | She can be fed a forged comparison and reach a true judgement about a false pair |
| STANDING | **OBSERVED, permanently.** There is no second witness for an act of recognition. |
| Token / Gear / count | **None.** Her Ch42 detection of metal in Leon's coat is **NOT this ability and must not be explained by it.** |
| Must never become | Unrestricted memory reading · reading unwritten text · psychometry · knowing who wrote it |
| **Mundane fallback** | The best-read records hand in Alderwick, reaching the same judgements more slowly, with more errors, and with a stated margin of doubt |

**Why this and not memory:** it serves the manuscript's actual obsession — Ch111 identifies the first keeper *by hand*; Ch102 finds the marginal hand; Ch121–122 match three tallies *by hand*. It cannot solve a mystery alone, cannot be weaponised, and cannot be corroborated.

## D5. CONTESTED — JAMES CALLOWAY'S "LATENT SENSITIVITY"

`../16_GOVERNMENT/GOVERNMENT_CHARACTERS.md` carries a CANON-EXTENSION (2026-08-31) giving Calloway *"a latent sensitivity to Alderwick's old supernatural forces — a hereditary knack, never spoken of… He uses it only on cases that defy ordinary explanation, and tells no one."*

**Assessment:**
- Manuscript support: **one line** (Ch8, *"It is here… In the remembering"*), which reads perfectly well as an ordinary man's unease in a bad house.
- It cuts directly against the Calloway the manuscript actually built: *"Do you know what it is?" / "No." / "Neither do I"* (Ch36) · *"Conviction isn't part of the job." / "I want facts."* (Ch36).
- It duplicates Cecily Pargeter's proposed ability in a weaker form.
- **It also breaks Rule 4:** a sensing constable shortens investigations.

**Recommendation (AUTHOR DECISION REQUIRED):**
1. **Preferred — retire it.** Reclassify Ch8 as ordinary unease. Costs nothing; no chapter changes; Calloway becomes stronger as the reader's ground truth.
2. Acceptable — keep it strictly off-page and forbid it from ever producing information. It may make him uncomfortable in a room. It may never tell him anything.
3. Rejected — develop it. It duplicates Cecily and unbalances the framework.

**Not changed by this file.** Logged in `../15_METADATA/AUDIT_POST_VALE_FOUNDATION.md`.

## D6. FORBIDDEN — HARD LIST

Do not introduce, revive, or imply:
- Any combat, damage, restraint, healing, concealment, or compulsion ability
- **Gear warmth** · Gear as universal key · Gear as the Keeper's key · Gear inside Vale's box · Vale handing Leon the Gear · the Keeper's Door requiring the Gear
- **A Token warming for anyone but the keeper** (Ch117 lock)
- Supernatural omniscience, foresight, or occult scholarship for Thomas Vale
- Any object-response for Ysabel Nott (Ch117 lock)
- Any ability for Owen Trant, Walter Denby, Hester Pinnock, Reuben Orme, or Isaac Faulds
- A power scale, tier list, rank-up mechanic, training regime, or community of holders
- Any in-world vocabulary for "ability" · any character saying "E.R.C." or a STANDING label aloud
- SCP-style containment, object classes, or clinical formatting (§17 ANTI-SCP CONSTRAINT)
- "Father Lucien Vale" — the name and the clerical status are both rejected pending separate author approval
- Any new supernatural terminology whatsoever without a `CANON_TERMINOLOGY.md` entry
