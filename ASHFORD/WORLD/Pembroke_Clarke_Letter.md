## THE DOCUMENT

**Origin:** Messrs. Pembroke & Clarke, Solicitors, London
**Sent to:** No. 13, Bellweather Lane, Ashford
**Delivered to:** Leon [surname: Ashcroft]
**Date:** Prior to Leon's arrival in Ashford (delivered by diligence/mail)

**What Leon sees:**
- A formal letter from London solicitors
- References "Aurelius Ashcroft, No. 13"
- Concerns property at Bellweather Lane
- References a will, testament, or estate matter
- Mentions documents, inventory, or correspondence that were left
- Leon assumes this is misaddressed — his name is not Aurelius Ashcroft

**What Leon does NOT see (hidden in the letter or its attachments):**
- The document was correctly addressed to No. 13
- "Ashcroft" IS Leon's surname — he may have known this or may have been unaware
- The connection between Leon and Aurelius is NOT a coincidence
- Something Aurelius left behind requires someone's attention
- The letter's arrival is deliberate — not a mistake

---

## WHAT THE LETTER CONTAINS (Author Knowledge)

**The letter itself:**
- Formal legal language
- References a will or testament executed by Aurelius Ashcroft
- Names a property at No. 13, Bellweather Lane, Ashford
- Mentions accompanying documents or an inventory
- Requests that the addressee contact the firm or claim the property
- States that certain items require examination or are of significance

**Accompanying materials (if any):**
- Copy of will or testament
- Inventory of documents/items left by Aurelius
- A letter from Aurelius himself (handwritten, to whoever inherits or claims the property)
- Possibly something that Leon cannot yet understand — a symbol, a name, an address that seems nonsensical

**The key mystery:**
> Why was this sent to No. 13?
> Why now?
> Why to Leon?

## STORY FUNCTION

This letter is the **Mystery Catalyst**. It is the ONE document that starts everything.

It replaces the "three envelopes" concept.

**The flow:**
1. Leon receives the letter (Ch 1) — thinks it's misaddressed
2. Leon investigates because his surname IS Ashcroft — he needs to know
3. The letter leads to Margaret, Daniel, Arthur (Ch 2-4)
4. Leon's curiosity grows (Ch 5-7)
5. Archive research confirms Aurelius existed and was connected to No. 13 (Ch 8)
6. The mystery deepens — Leon decides to stay in Ashford and investigate further (Ch 9+)

---

## CRITICAL WRITING RULES

### DO:
- Make the letter feel like a real 1816 legal document
- Have Leon read it multiple times, each time noticing something new
- Let the letter be the anchor — everything Leon discovers relates back to it
- Keep the letter's full contents partially hidden — Leon may not understand everything
- Let the mystery of Aurelius grow gradually from the single document

### DON'T:
- Add more "mystery documents" that compete with the letter
- Make the letter a puzzle with an obvious solution
- Reveal the letter's full meaning too early
- Explain Aurelius through the letter alone
