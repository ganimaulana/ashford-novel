## POV RATIONALE

### NO_POV Characters — Why They Never Get POV

**Julian Ashworth (CORE-ORG-04):** The Betrayer is the most narratively dangerous character. His POV would be an unreliable narrator of the highest order -- he would make himself sympathetic, rationalize his manipulation, and undermine the devastating reveal that he has been steering Leon's entire investigation. The reader must discover Julian's manipulation through evidence, not through his own justification. Character defined by dramatic irony.

**Ruth Ashworth (CORE-ORG-05):** The Counterintelligence's power is her invisibility. Her POV would reveal her neutrality -- but the mystery of whether she is truly neutral or secretly invested is a core source of tension. Keeping her outside POV preserves this tension.

**Edward Pembroke (CORE-ORG-07):** The Finance is rational and controlled. His POV would make him too sympathetic too early. He is a bureaucratic wall Leon must navigate.

**Thomas "Tom" Bellweather (CORE-ORG-09):** The Mason thinks in terms of stone and timber, not ideas. His POV would be straightforward and unreflective -- not narratively interesting.

**Arthur Penhaligon (CORE-ORG-12):** The Journalist's POV would be reflective and narrative-driven, making him too sympathetic too early. Better as a character Leon must navigate.

**Daniel Carter (CORE-ORG-13):** The Reluctant's POV would be analytical and self-doubting, making him sympathetic and undermining the threat of his academic evaluation. Better as a character Leon must navigate.

### OCCASIONAL_POV Characters

These characters receive brief POV sections (1-2 per arc) that serve specific narrative functions:

**Silas Hawthorne:** Historical context through document-driven meditation. Texture of paper, weight of ink, silence of archives. Best for interludes between Leon's investigation.

**Nathaniel Cross:** Operational glimpses Leon never sees. Decisions, threats, consequences. Effective in brief bursts showing the organization's practical machinery.

**Margaret Whitmore:** Emotional grounding and domestic texture. Food, routines, physical reality of the house. Bridges the canon character with the proposed organization.

**Dr. Samuel Finch:** Scholarly context. Texts, observations, analysis. Best for brief interludes that decode Aurelius's intellectual world.

**Eleanor Vane:** Human experience of living in Bellweather House. Domestic details, overheard conversations, quiet moments with Aurelius. Emotional depth documents cannot provide.

**Isabella Chen:** Wider-world perspective. Flow of people and information through her establishment. Contrasts with insular Ashford.

### RECURRING_POV Characters (If Approved as Major)

**Clara Bellweather:** Most socially sophisticated POV. Every scene reads like a social maneuver. Reveals the hidden social world men cannot see. Requires careful handling to avoid making her seem manipulative without purpose.

**Isabella Chen:** Cosmopolitan counterpoint to Ashford's insularity. Every scene is about the flow of people and information. Requires careful handling to avoid making her seem omniscient.

## POV DESIGN RULES

1. **Julian Ashworth NEVER gets POV.** This is a hard rule. His narrative power is his unrevealed unreliability.
2. **Margaret Whitmore's POV must not make her seem naive.** Her warmth must coexist with professional competence.
3. **Eleanor Vane's POV must not over-explain her mystery.** Her quietness is her power. POV should deepen mystery, not resolve it.
4. **Silas Hawthorne's POV must not become a lecture.** Document-driven prose must serve character, not replace it.
5. **Nathaniel Cross's POV must not become a rant.** Terse prose must serve tension, not melodrama.
6. **Clara Bellweather's POV must not make her seem omniscient.** Her social awareness is sharp but not supernatural.
7. **Isabella Chen's POV must not make her seem cosmopolitan to the point of alienation.** She must feel like part of Ashford.
8. **All POV sections must pass the "Leon Filter":** If Leon cannot infer the same information through observation, the POV is unnecessary.

---

## POV VS. LEOPOV

All organization characters are primarily experienced through Leon's eyes. POV sections are:
- **Supplements, not replacements.** They provide what Leon cannot see.
- **Brief, not expansive.** 1-2 pages maximum per section.
- **Specific, not general.** Each POV section has a single narrative purpose.
- **Earned, not gratuitous.** A POV section is only written if Leon's investigation creates a knowledge gap that only that character's POV can fill.
