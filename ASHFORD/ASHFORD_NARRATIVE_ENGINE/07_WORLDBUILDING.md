## A. PURPOSE

Build worldbuilding through discovery, not exposition. The world is revealed through environment, documents, institutions, routines, artifacts, and conversations — not authorial information dumps.

## C. WORLDBUILDING DISCOVERY METHOD

### Primary Discovery Channels (Prefer These)

1. **Environment** — Buildings, streets, districts reveal history and class
2. **Documents** — Letters, records, newspapers contain world information
3. **Institutions** — Libraries, churches, shops show how society works
4. **Ordinary routines** — Daily life shows the world's texture
5. **Artifacts** — Physical objects carry history
6. **Conversations** — Natural dialogue reveals social structure
7. **Investigation** — Leon's research method IS worldbuilding
8. **Social behavior** — How people interact shows hierarchy and culture
9. **Consequences** — What happens when rules are tested

### Secondary (Use Only When Necessary)

- **Exposition passages** — Only when no other method works
- **Direct description** — Only when sensory grounding is already established

## E. OPERATIONAL RULES

1. **Worldbuilding must serve a scene purpose.** If the information doesn't advance character, plot, or atmosphere, reconsider its placement.
2. **Show the world through Leon's eyes.** Worldbuilding is filtered through his perspective, knowledge gaps, and interests.
3. **Build the world in fragments.** The reader should assemble understanding the same way Leon does.
4. **Era-appropriate details must be consistent.** The setting is 1816 Regency England. No railways (first: 1825), no electricity, no telegraph, no professional police force (Metropolitan Police: 1829), no automobiles, no telephones.
5. **Documents and records are the preferred worldbuilding vehicles.** Letters, parish registers, property deeds, directories.

## F. WHEN TO USE

- Every chapter should reveal at least one worldbuilding detail organically
- Investigation scenes are the primary worldbuilding vehicle
- Environmental descriptions should include worldbuilding elements
- Conversations with knowledge-holders (Daniel, Arthur, Librarian)

## G. WHEN NOT TO USE

- Don't info-dump the entire world in one scene
- Don't describe locations the reader doesn't need to know
- Don't explain world rules the character doesn't need to know yet
- Don't override character voice with authorial worldbuilding narration

## H. FAILURE MODES

| Failure | Sign | Fix |
|---------|------|-----|
| Info-dump | 3+ paragraphs of world description in sequence | Break into scene-embedded discovery |
| Over-explanation | Character explains things they shouldn't | Remove or convert to observation |
| Era inconsistency | Technology doesn't match setting | Check against canon era |
| World over character | Description replaces character moment | Restore character perspective |
