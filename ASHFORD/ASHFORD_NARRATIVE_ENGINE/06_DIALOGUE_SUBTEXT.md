# System 06 — Dialogue & Subtext

**Status:** CORE SYSTEM | **Created:** 2026-08-29
**LOTM Transfer:** Rules 8, 9, 13 (competence through analysis, expert exposition, multi-perspective)
**Evidence Confidence:** 7/10 (good character voice support)

---

## A. PURPOSE

Create dialogue that operates on multiple levels: surface meaning conveys the scene, hidden intention conveys character, and subtext conveys plot/mystery information.

## B. CORE PRINCIPLES

1. **Dialogue is never just dialogue.** Every exchange has at least one layer of meaning beyond its surface.
2. **Not every dialogue exchange carries plot information.** Some exist for relationship, atmosphere, or pacing.
3. **What is NOT said matters as much as what is said.** Silence, evasion, and implication are narrative tools.
4. **Character speech patterns are identity markers.** Different characters should sound distinct.

## C. DIALOGUE LAYERS

For each dialogue exchange, track:

| Layer | Question |
|-------|----------|
| Surface Meaning | What is being literally said? |
| Hidden Intention | What does the speaker actually want? |
| Information Transfer | What facts are being shared (deliberately or accidentally)? |
| Evasion | What is being avoided or deflected? |
| Manipulation | Is anyone trying to lead the other to a conclusion? |
| Power Dynamics | Who controls this conversation? |
| Conversational Rhythm | Fast/tense vs slow/measured — what does the pace signal? |

## D. DIALOGUE TYPES

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| Information dialogue | Conveys facts/worldbuilding | Leon asking librarian about records |
| Relationship dialogue | Develops character dynamics | Leon and Helen's measured exchanges |
| Conflict dialogue | Creates tension between characters | Confrontational moments |
| Bonding dialogue | Builds trust between characters | Leon and Daniel's academic discussions |
| Misdirection | Character hides intention | Helen's evasive answers |
| Revelation dialogue | Key information revealed | Confession scenes |

## E. OPERATIONAL RULES

1. **Distinguish dialogue types before writing.** Know if a scene's primary function is information, relationship, conflict, or atmosphere.
2. **When a character knows something Leon doesn't, show the effort of concealment.** Not lying — the ACT of holding back.
3. **Leon's dialogue pattern:** Asks questions, listens, rarely interrupts. His silence is as expressive as his words.
4. **Helen's dialogue pattern:** Measured, formal, answers with questions. Her pauses are significant.
5. **Power shifts in conversation must be visible.** Who controls the information flow shifts during exchanges.

## F. WHEN TO USE

- Every conversation between named characters
- Every scene where information is exchanged
- Every scene where relationship dynamics shift
- Pacing-release scenes can use lighter dialogue

## G. WHEN NOT TO USE

- Pure environmental/atmospheric scenes (some scenes have no dialogue)
- Internal reflection sequences
- Action sequences where dialogue would break tension

## H. FAILURE MODES

| Failure | Sign | Fix |
|---------|------|-----|
| Exposition dialogue | Characters tell each other things they already know | Make the information exchange organic to the scene |
| All dialogue carries plot | No conversational breathing room | Add relationship-only scenes |
| No subtext | Every conversation is literal | Add hidden intention beneath surface meaning |
| Indistinguishable voices | Hard to tell who is speaking | Develop distinct speech patterns per character |

## I. ASHFORD-SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATION

- Leon: asks questions, listens, formal, brief responses, never interrupts
- Helen: measured pauses, formal vocabulary, answers with questions, very formal address
- Arthur: antiquarian precision, formal but warm, enthusiastic about historical details
- Daniel: academic but approachable, teacher's habit of explaining
- Margaret: (if canon) gossipy, warm, social connector — NOT deep information source
- Dialogue in Indonesian "aku" voice must maintain period-appropriate formality

## J. CROSS-SYSTEM DEPENDENCIES

- **Character Depth** — Dialogue patterns express character
- **Information Control** — Dialogue is the primary vehicle for controlled information flow
- **Reader Knowledge** — Dialogue reveals what characters know vs hide
- **Pacing** — Dialogue rhythm affects scene pacing
- **Scene Construction** — Dialogue scenes are a subset of all scenes
