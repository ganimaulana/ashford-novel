# POLITICAL FACTIONS — Ashford Local Politics (1816)

**Status:** 📙 PROPOSED | **Created:** 2026-08-29
**Purpose:** Define political divisions, factions, and power struggles within Ashford
**Principle:** Politics is LOCAL first — gentry rivalries, class conflicts, reform agitation. National Tory/Whig politics filter down but don''t dominate.

## CORE PREMISE

**Ashford politics operates on THREE LEVELS:**

1. **LOCAL POWER STRUGGLES** — Who controls magistracy, vestry, land, business
2. **NATIONAL POLITICAL ALIGNMENT** — Tory vs. Whig, reformist vs. conservative
3. **RADICAL UNDERCURRENT** — Working/middle class demands for representation

**MOST PLOTS START LOCAL.** National politics create pressure, not primary conflict.

## SECTION 1 — LOCAL POWER FACTIONS (PRIMARY)

### A. THE GENTRY OLIGARCHY

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Definition:**
**10-15 landed gentry families** control Ashford through:
- Magistracy (2-3 resident JPs)
- Select Vestry (12-20 members, mostly gentry)
- Land ownership (estates surrounding Ashford)
- Economic power (rents, mortgages, employment)

**Internal Divisions:**

Gentry are NOT unified. They compete over:
- **Magistracy control** — who becomes JP?
- **Vestry decisions** — poor rates, church rates, road repair
- **Land disputes** — boundaries, manorial rights, enclosure
- **Business interests** — market control, mill ownership, canal/turnpike investments
- **Social status** — who has precedence at church, assemblies, county events
- **Personal grudges** — old family feuds, romantic rivalries, financial disputes

**Ashcroft Family Position (UNKNOWN):**
- Tier 1 gentry (magistrate-level) or Tier 2 (influential but not ruling)?
- Old family (ancient lineage) or new money (recent wealth)?
- Allies or enemies of current magistrates?
- Land-rich or cash-poor?

**Plot Implications:**
- Gentry rivalry creates opportunity for Leon to navigate/exploit
- Magistrate feud = selective law enforcement
- Vestry faction fight = poor relief becomes weapon
- Land dispute = violence, forgery, supernatural interference

### B. THE ESTABLISHED CHURCH FACTION

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Key Figures:**
- **Rector/Vicar** — controls parish church, sits on vestry, collects tithes
- **Church wardens** — enforce church rates, maintain church property
- **Gentry pew-holders** — front pews = social status

**Interests:**
- Protect Church of England privileges
- Collect tithes and church rates
- Suppress Dissenters (Methodists, Baptists, Quakers)
- Maintain social hierarchy ("God ordained the classes")

**Conflicts:**
- **vs. Dissenters** — religious freedom vs. Anglican monopoly
- **vs. Middle class** — church rates resented by shopkeepers/tradesmen
- **vs. Radicals** — church supports government repression
- **Internal:** Evangelical vs. High Church factions

**Plot Implications:**
- Church refuses burial to suicide victim (supernatural resonance?)
- Tithe dispute escalates to violence
- Dissenter persecution creates allies for Leon
- Rector involved in land/financial scandal

---

### C. THE MIDDLING SORT (RISING CHALLENGE)

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Composition:**
- **Professionals:** Solicitors, physicians, apothecaries, clergy
- **Merchants:** Large shopkeepers, grain dealers, wool factors
- **Master artisans:** Wealthy craftsmen employing journeymen/apprentices
- **Prosperous farmers:** Yeomen, large tenant farmers

**Characteristics:**
- Educated, literate, ambitious
- Pay heavy poor rates and church rates
- Excluded from political power (no votes, no vestry seats)
- Resent gentry privilege and church establishment

**Demands:**
- Representation on vestry
- Lower church rates (especially Dissenters)
- Fairer poor relief administration
- Expanded voting rights
- Meritocracy over aristocratic privilege

**Political Alignment:**
- Some support Whig reformism
- Some join Hampden Clubs or reform societies
- Most avoid open radicalism (risk prosecution)

**Plot Implications:**
- Middle-class reformer challenges vestry (blocked by gentry)
- Solicitor uncovers magistrate corruption (professional vs. aristocratic)
- Dissenting merchant boycotts church rates
- Physician/apothecary knows medical secrets (leverage?)

### D. THE RADICAL UNDERCURRENT

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Composition:**
- **Artisans:** Skilled workers (weavers, carpenters, printers)
- **Small tradesmen:** Shopkeepers, innkeepers
- **Literate laborers:** Self-educated workers
- **Demobilized veterans:** Soldiers/sailors radicalized by poverty

**Organizations (UNKNOWN — does Ashford have these?):**
- **Hampden Club** — reform society (middle/working class)
- **Reading society** — shares radical newspapers/pamphlets
- **Friendly society** — mutual aid (also political organizing)

**Demands:**
- Universal suffrage (or at least expanded voting)
- Secret ballot
- Annual Parliaments
- Abolition of rotten boroughs
- Repeal of Corn Laws (keep bread prices low)
- Poor relief reform

**Activities:**
- Secret meetings in taverns, private homes
- Circulate radical pamphlets (Cobbett''s Political Register, Paine''s Rights of Man)
- Attend reform rallies (risk arrest for sedition)
- Petition Parliament (usually ignored)

**Government Response:**
- Magistrates spy on meetings (informers)
- Prosecute speakers for sedition
- Suppress radical newspapers
- Intimidate employers to fire radicals

**Plot Implications:**
- Leon infiltrates radical meeting (investigate crime? or sympathizes?)
- Government spy betrays reform group
- Magistrate frames radical for crime
- Radical murdered (political assassination? or personal?)
- Supernatural event blamed on/by radicals

## SECTION 2 — NATIONAL POLITICAL ALIGNMENT (SECONDARY)

### A. TORY FACTION

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE + PROPOSED

**National Tory Party (1816):**
- In power under PM Lord Liverpool
- Support: Landed gentry, Church of England, Crown
- Ideology: Tradition, hierarchy, order, repression of reform

**Ashford Tories (PROPOSED):**

**Who:**
- Most magistrates (gentry)
- Rector/vicar
- Largest landowners
- Some wealthy merchants (especially if seeking gentry status)

**Beliefs:**
- Reform = revolution = French Terror
- Social hierarchy is God-ordained
- Poor laws are charity, not rights
- Repression is necessary to maintain order

**Actions:**
- Suppress reform meetings
- Prosecute radicals for sedition
- Enforce game laws harshly
- Support high church rates
- Resist vestry democratization

**Plot Implications:**
- Tory magistrate persecutes reformers
- Tory landowner evicts tenant for radical activity
- Tory faction blocks middle-class reformer from vestry

---

### B. WHIG FACTION

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE + PROPOSED

**National Whig Party (1816):**
- Out of power (opposition)
- Support: Some aristocrats, professionals, merchants, Dissenters
- Ideology: Gradual reform, religious toleration, constitutional monarchy

**Ashford Whigs (PROPOSED):**

**Who:**
- Minority of gentry (reformist landowners)
- Professionals (solicitors, physicians)
- Dissenting merchants
- Some middling sort

**Beliefs:**
- Moderate reform prevents revolution
- Religious toleration for Dissenters
- Meritocracy over pure aristocracy
- Poor laws need reform (but not abolition)

**Limitations:**
- Still defend property and hierarchy
- Fear working-class radicalism
- Will not support universal suffrage

**Plot Implications:**
- Whig gentleman protects reformer from Tory persecution
- Whig/Tory rivalry in vestry (poor relief becomes weapon)
- Whig reformer allied with Leon against corrupt Tory magistrate

---

### C. THE UNREFORMED PARLIAMENT PROBLEM

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Ashford has NO direct parliamentary representation.**

Ashford residents can only vote in county constituency (if qualified = freehold property worth £2+ annual rent).

**Result:**
- 96% of Ashford residents are disenfranchised
- National politics feel DISTANT and IRRELEVANT to most
- Local power (magistrates, vestry) matters MORE than Parliament

**Plot Implication:**
- National politics create CONTEXT, not primary conflict
- County election (bribery, violence) can affect Ashford indirectly
- Reformers demand representation, but Parliament ignores them
