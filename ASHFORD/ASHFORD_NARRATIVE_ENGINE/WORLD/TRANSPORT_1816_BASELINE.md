## AUTHORITATIVE PRINCIPLE

This document defines the available transportation in 1816 England.  
Any anomalous or advanced transport must be explicitly marked as EXPERIMENTAL, RARE, or IMPOSSIBLE.

---

## AVAILABLE (COMMON)

- **Walking** – the primary mode for most people
- **Horses** – riding, pack, and draft
- **Horse‑drawn carriages** – private and public coaches
- **Diligence / stagecoach** – regular long‑distance passenger service
- **Wagons and carts** – goods transport
- **River transport** – barges, ferries, small boats
- **Canals** – where built (e.g., industrial regions)
- **Sailing vessels** – coastal and overseas shipping

---

## AVAILABLE BUT RARE / EXPERIMENTAL

- **Steam engines** – stationary industrial use; early experimental locomotives
  - **No public passenger railway** (first public railway: Stockton‑Darlington 1825; Liverpool‑Manchester 1830)
  - Steam traction may exist in mines or collieries, but not as public transport
- **Hot‑air balloons** – limited to demonstrations, scientific flights, military observation
- **Hydrogen balloons** – similar, more common for scientific use

---

## IMPOSSIBLE AS NORMAL TECHNOLOGY

- **Airplanes** – no motorised heavier‑than‑air flight
- **Helicopters**
- **Steerable airships / zeppelins** – not developed until later 19th century
- **Modern rail networks** – electrified or high‑speed rail does not exist
- **Automobiles** – no internal combustion vehicles
- **Modern bicycles** – the velocipede exists only as a novelty, not common transport

---

## FOR ASHFORD

- Ashford is a market town, not a major industrial hub
- Transport is typical of a rural English town:
  - coaches to London / neighbouring cities
  - horses and carriages for local movement
  - walking for daily errands
- The Diligence (stagecoach) is mentioned in Chapter 1 – that is canon
- Any rail or steam‑powered transport in Ashford should be **experimental** and **not public**

---

## SUPERNATURAL INTERACTION

- Unusual travel abilities (teleportation, flight, etc.) are **not** part of common knowledge
- If a character can fly or move rapidly, it must be exceptional and hidden
