# System 14 — Reader Knowledge

**Status:** CORE SYSTEM | **Created:** 2026-08-29
**LOTM Transfer:** Rules 1, 3 (grounded detail, information exchange)
**Evidence Confidence:** 8/10 (strong fairness framework in canon rules)

---

## A. PURPOSE

Manage what the reader knows, thinks, suspects, and is expected to misunderstand. Protect fairness, curiosity, tension, surprise, and trust.

## B. READER STATE MODEL

Track these reader dimensions:

| Dimension | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| What Reader Knows | What facts has the reader been explicitly told? |
| What Reader Thinks | What conclusions has the reader drawn from available information? |
| What Reader Suspects | What is the reader guessing at without confirmation? |
| What Reader Is Expected to Misunderstand | What is deliberately misleading on first reading? |
| What Has Been Confirmed | What facts have been verified in-story? |
| What Has Been Disproven | What reader hypotheses have been invalidated? |

## C. READER FAIRNESS PRINCIPLES

1. **Fair play:** The reader should be able to look back and see that all answers were available from the clues.
2. **Curiosity:** Information gaps must be created continuously. Knowledge should open new questions, not close them all.
3. **Tension:** The gap between what the reader knows and what the character knows creates dramatic irony. Use it.
4. **Surprise:** Surprises must be built from established information, not introduced from outside.
5. **Trust:** Never cheat the reader. Withholding basic scene comprehension is NOT fair mystery writing.

## D. KNOWLEDGE TYPES

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| Reader-Known Fact | What the reader knows | "The symbol is circle + 3 lines + 1 dot" |
| Character-Known Secret | What character knows, reader doesn't | Helen's full identity |
| Hidden Canon | Truth known only to author | Aurelius's true purpose |
| Reader Assumption | What reader believes that may be wrong | "Leon thinks the letter was misaddressed" |
| Unconfirmed Possibility | What is plausible but unverified | "Maybe Leon is Aurelius's grandson" |

## E. OPERATIONAL RULES

1. **Track reader state at each chapter.** What does the reader know at start? What do they know at end?
2. **Never withhold basics needed for scene comprehension.** The reader must know WHERE the character is and WHAT is happening.
3. **Misdirection must be fair.** If the reader is led to believe something false, the truth must be explainable by events.
4. **Confirmations must be earned.** When a hypothesis is confirmed, it should feel satisfying, not expected.
5. **Disproven hypotheses are valuable.** Showing the reader they were wrong is a powerful narrative tool.

## F. WHEN TO USE

- Every chapter planning session
- Every major reveal
- Every time a character knows something the reader doesn't
- Every misdirection or double-meaning scene

## G. WHEN NOT TO USE

- Simple transitional scenes with no knowledge change
- Pacing-release scenes
- Pure atmosphere scenes

## H. FAILURE MODES

| Failure | Sign | Fix |
|---------|------|-----|
| Cheating the reader | Answer depends on information never shown | Plant the clue earlier |
| Fake mystery | Reader knows the answer from the start | Add genuine uncertainty |
| Withheld basics | Reader can't understand scene | Add essential context |
| Exposition reveals too early | Explanation scene spoils the reveal | Convert to discovery scene |

## I. ASHFORD-SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATION

- Reader knows: Leon's investigation progress (through his notebook and observations)
- Reader suspects: Helen's true identity (hints but no confirmation)
- Reader is expected to misunderstand: The letter's true address (reader thinks it's misaddressed along with Leon)
- Reader assumption: "This is a standard inheritance mystery" — to be challenged as story deepens
- Hidden Canon: Helen's full name (Helen Margaret Ashcroft), Aurelius's full story, the symbol's true meaning
- The reading experience should feel like: "I know what Leon knows, but I suspect there's more"

## J. CROSS-SYSTEM DEPENDENCIES

- **Information Control** — Reader knowledge is a subset of information control
- **Mystery Engine** — Reader expectations are manipulated by the mystery engine
- **Foreshadowing** — Foreshadowing shapes what the reader suspects
- **Character Depth** — Character secrets create knowledge gaps the reader tries to fill
- **Continuity** — Reader knowledge state must be tracked and preserved
