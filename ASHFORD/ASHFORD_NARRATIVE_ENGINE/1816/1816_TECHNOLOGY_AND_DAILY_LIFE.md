# 1816 Technology and Daily Life

**Status:** PROPOSED — NOT CANON

---

## LIGHTING

| Technology | Who Used It | Prevalence | Ashford Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Candles (tallow) | All classes | Universal | Bellweather House uses these |
| Candles (beeswax) | Gentry | Common wealthy | Superior, less smell |
| Oil lamps | Gentry/middle | Common urban wealthy | Possible at Bellweather |
| **Gas lighting** | Urban wealthy | Rare (Preston first Feb 1816) | NOT at Bellweather — could be discussed |
| Fireplaces | All classes | Universal | Primary heat and cooking |
| Rushlights | Poor | Common poor | Servants and laborers |

**Atmosphere:** Bellweather lit by candles and oil lamps — warm, flickering, limited. Gas would be strikingly different — brighter, steadier, with an odor.

---

## HEATING

- **Primary:** Coal or wood fires in fireplaces
- **Secondary:** Hand warmers (hot coals in metal containers)
- **Fuel:** Coal expensive; poor used wood or peat
- **Davy Lamp (Jan 1816):** Coal mining safety innovation — affects coal supply

## TRANSPORTATION

| Mode | Speed | Who Used It | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Horse (riding) | 8-10 mph | All | Fastest personal transport |
| Mail coach | 8-10 mph | Wealthy, government | London-Edinburgh: 4 days |
| Stagecoach | 5-6 mph | Wealthy | Slower, more stops |
| Canal boat | 3-4 mph | Freight | Bulk goods |
| Walking | 3 mph | Poor | Most people walked |
| River barge | 3-4 mph | Freight | Bulk goods |

**Communication:** Mail coach: London to Edinburgh 4 days. Letter from nearby town: 1-2 days. From London: 2-3 days. No telegraph in Britain.

## HOUSEHOLD

### Cooking
Open hearth. Iron pots and pans. Spits for roasting. No refrigeration (ice houses for wealthy). Preservation: salting, smoking, pickling, drying. Margaret (the cook) is central to household economy.

### Cleaning
Weekly laundry (boiling, washing, wringing, ironing with hot flat irons). Swept floors. Chimney sweeping critical. No plumbing — chamber pots, wells, pumps.

### Furnishings (Gentry)
Heavy drapes (block cold). Fire screens (direct heat). Carpets (upper), flagstones (lower). Mirrors (status). Chinese porcelain (colonial wealth). Books (status/education).

---

## CLOTHING (REGENCE ERA)

### Men's Fashion (Beau Brummell's Influence)
Dark tailored coats. White shirts (changed daily = status). Breeches/trending toward trousers. Boots. Waistcoat. Cravat. Top hat.

### Women's Fashion
Empire line (high waist, below bust). Light fabrics (muslin, cotton). Long sleeves (autumn/winter). Walking bonnets. Shawls (Indian/imitation).

### Daily Maintenance
Washing weekly. Mending constant. Well-kept clothes = status. The poor wore rags or hand-me-downs.

---

## MEDICAL PRACTICES

### Treatments
Bloodletting (very common). Purging (mercury-based). Laudanum (opium tincture — household painkiller). Blistering. Cupping.

### Diseases
Smallpox (vaccine available, not universal). Tuberculosis ("consumption" — very common). Typhus. Cholera (1817 pandemic approaching Russia). Dysentery. Infant mortality: ~30%.

### Practitioners
Physicians (university-educated, expensive). Surgeons (apprentices, no anesthesia). Apothecaries (dispensed medicines). Midwives (births). Quacks (everyone).

---

## FOOD AND DIET

### Gentry Table
Multiple courses. Roasted meats (beef, mutton, game). Pies, puddings, jellies. Fresh bread. Wine, beer, cider. Tea (colonial, expensive). Sugar (colonial, expensive).

### Poor Diet
Bread (staple). Potatoes (increasingly common due to poor harvests). Porridge. Weak beer/water. Vegetables (cabbage, turnips). Little/no meat.

### Food Adulteration (Common Scandal)
Chalk in bread. Copper greens in butter. Water in milk. Sawdust in flour.

---

## WORK AND OCCUPATIONS

**Gentry:** Landowner. Magistrate. Military officer (purchase of commissions). Clergyman (requires university).
**Middle Class:** Merchant. Lawyer. Doctor. Schoolmaster. Banker.
**Working Class:** Agricultural laborer. Artisan (weaver, smith, carpenter). Shopkeeper. Servant. Sailor (seasonal). Soldier (seasonal — demobilized 1815-1816).
**Child Labor:** Common in agriculture, factories, domestic service, mining ("trappers").

---

## MONEY AND ECONOMY

### Currency (£sd system: pound, shilling, penny; £1 = 20s, 1s = 12d)
Gold sovereigns (£1). Silver crowns, florins, shillings, sixpences. Copper pennies, halfpennies, farthings. Bank of England notes (paper, not always trustworthy). Country bank notes (local, could fail). Private tokens (small-denomination coin substitutes). **Counterfeiting = death penalty.**

### Important Prices (approx. 1816)
Loaf of bread: 4-8 pence (variable). Pint of beer: 1-2 pence. Room rent (poor): 1-3 shillings/week. Servant wages (skilled): 10-20 guineas/year + board. Servant wages (unskilled): 5-10 guineas/year + board. Gentleman's income: hundreds to thousands of pounds/year. Laborer's income: ~50-80 pounds/year.
