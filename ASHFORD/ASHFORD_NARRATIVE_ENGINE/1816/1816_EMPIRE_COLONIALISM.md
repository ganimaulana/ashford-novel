## THE BRITISH EMPIRE IN 1816

### Colonial Holdings
- **North America:** None (lost in American Revolution 1783); Canada was a separate colony
- **Caribbean:** Jamaica, Barbados, Bahamas, Trinidad (recently captured from Spain), Leeward Islands — sugar and slave economies
- **South America:** No direct holdings; Venezuela and Colombia in independence struggle (Bolivar)
- **Africa:** Trading posts (Gambia, Sierra Leone, Gold Coast); no large inland territories
- **India:** East India Company controlled Bengal, Madras, Bombay, and vast interior territories
- **Southeast Asia:** Straits Settlements (Singapore not founded until 1819); interactions with Dutch East Indies
- **Pacific:** No significant holdings (Botany Bay penal colony established 1788)
- **Europe:** Malta (captured 1800); Minorca (returned to Spain 1802)

### The East India Company
- Controlled vast Indian territories
- Employed private army of 200,000+ (larger than most European armies)
- 60,000+ British servants in India
- Single largest commercial enterprise in the world
- Charter renewed 1813: lost Indian trade monopoly but retained Chinese trade
- 1811-1812: War with Burma (recovering in 1816)
- "Nabobs" — Company agents who returned to Britain extremely wealthy
- Old aristocracy resented Company wealth

### Colonial Wealth
- Sugar, cotton, tea, coffee, indigo from colonies enriched British commerce
- Colonial goods were essential to daily life (sugar in tea, cotton in clothing)
- The wealth was real and significant but most Britons were unaware of its origins
- Colonial investors and merchants enriched London, Bristol, Liverpool, Southampton

## SLAVERY AND ABOLITION

### Legal Status (1816)
- **Slave TRADE:** Illegal in British Empire (Slave Trade Act 1807)
- **SLAVERY ITSELF:** Still LEGAL in British colonies until 1833 (Slavery Abolition Act)
- **Black Britons:** Exist in Britain (London, Bristol, Liverpool, Southampton) but face systemic racism
- **Former slaves in Britain:** Legally free after Somerset Case (1772) but social reality was different

### The Abolition Movement
- Led by William Wilberforce, Thomas Clarkson, Granville Sharp
- Society for Effecting the Abolition of the Slave Trade (founded 1787)
- Black Britons (Olaudah Equiano had died 1797; continued activism)
- Slave trade abolished 1807 but enforcement was imperfect
- British Anti-Slavery Society would be founded in 1823
- Moral and religious motivation (evangelical Christianity)
- Economic opposition: some argued free labor was more profitable

### Colonial Wealth and Moral Tension
- British economy dependent on colonial slave labor (even after 1807, existing plantations used enslaved people)
- Sugar, cotton, and tobacco from plantations were central to British trade
- Most British consumers of sugar were unaware of how it was produced
- The moral question was becoming unavoidable but was not yet resolved

## SOUTH AMERICAN INDEPENDENCE

### 1816 Developments
- **9 July 1816:** Argentina declares independence from Spain (Congress of Tucuman)
- Simón Bolívar active in Venezuela and Colombia
- Spanish Empire crumbling in the Americas
- Britain maintained official neutrality
- Britain interested in new trade opportunities with independent South America

### Implications for Britain
- Anxiety about the future of colonial trade
- East India Company monopoly questioned
- Questions about British imperial moral authority
- Parallel: if South American colonies can break from Spain, why not other colonies?

## PENAL COLONIES

### Australia (New South Wales)
- Penal colony established 1788
- Convicts transported from Britain for crimes including theft
- By 1816, the colony was established but struggling
- Transportation was the standard punishment for non-capital crimes
- A form of exile and forced labor

### Relevance to Ashford
- A character might be sent to Australia as punishment
- A character might have family in Australia
- A transported convict might return (some did, freely or escaped)
- The threat of transportation as a tool of social control
