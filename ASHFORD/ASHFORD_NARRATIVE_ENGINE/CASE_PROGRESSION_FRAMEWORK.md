## OVERVIEW

Cases are the **primary engine** for:
1. Leon''s capability development
2. Relationship network expansion
3. Information gathering
4. Aurelius convergence seed planting
5. Worldbuilding revelation
6. Reader engagement

**Core Principle:** Cases introduce characters organically, plant mystery seeds naturally, and escalate complexity systematically.

---

## A. CASE TYPES

### Type 1: Pure Investigation
**Purpose:** Test and develop Leon''s deductive abilities

**Characteristics:**
- No supernatural/anomalous elements
- Solvable through observation, logic, and evidence
- Clear resolution possible
- Low personal stakes

**Development Focus:** Investigation skill, deduction, systematic thinking

**Example Structure:**
- Property dispute with missing documents
- Theft requiring witness interviews
- Historical research request
- Genealogy question

**Convergence Potential:** LOW (but documents/people encountered may have Aurelius connections)

### Type 2: Historical Mystery
**Purpose:** Uncover past events, often Aurelius-adjacent

**Characteristics:**
- Focuses on events from decades/centuries ago
- Requires archival research and elderly witnesses
- May reveal worldbuilding about Ashford''s past
- Often indirectly connects to Aurelius

**Development Focus:** Research skills, historical knowledge, patience

**Example Structure:**
- Family wants to know what happened to ancestor
- Building has mysterious history
- Old object''s provenance unknown
- Town legend requires verification

**Convergence Potential:** HIGH (historical research naturally encounters Aurelius era)

### Type 3: Relationship Case
**Purpose:** Introduce or develop character connections

**Characteristics:**
- Case requires Leon to work with specific person
- Resolution less important than relationship development
- May be simple case but complex interpersonal dynamics
- Builds Leon''s network

**Development Focus:** Social skills, trust-building, alliance formation

**Example Structure:**
- Person needs help only Leon can provide
- Shared interest brings Leon and character together
- Favor requested, creating obligation
- Conflict requires mediation

**Convergence Potential:** VARIABLE (depends on character''s knowledge)

### Type 4: Worldbuilding Case
**Purpose:** Reveal how Ashford society/world works

**Characteristics:**
- Shows social structures, power dynamics, or world rules
- Case itself may be simple but context is rich
- Expands reader understanding of setting
- May have no direct plot relevance

**Development Focus:** Social awareness, political understanding, world knowledge

**Example Structure:**
- Property law reveals land ownership patterns
- Church matter reveals religious power structure
- Guild dispute reveals economic system
- Class conflict reveals social hierarchy

**Convergence Potential:** LOW (worldbuilding-focused, not plot-focused)

### Type 5: Convergence Case
**Purpose:** Explicitly connect multiple threads to Aurelius

**Characteristics:**
- Brings together clues from previous cases
- Leon or reader recognizes pattern
- Major revelation or recontextualization
- High emotional/narrative impact

**Development Focus:** Synthesis, pattern recognition, breakthrough thinking

**Example Structure:**
- Case involves object seen before
- Witness mentions name from previous case
- Location connects to earlier investigation
- Symbol appears in new context

**Convergence Potential:** MAXIMUM (this is the payoff)

### Type 6: Misdirection Case
**Purpose:** Create false trail or red herring

**Characteristics:**
- Seems important but leads nowhere
- Or seems unimportant but is actually crucial
- Tests Leon''s judgment
- Prevents predictability

**Development Focus:** Critical thinking, avoiding assumptions, course correction

**Example Structure:**
- Suspicious person is actually innocent
- Obvious answer is wrong
- Dramatic event is unrelated to mystery
- Planted false evidence

**Convergence Potential:** DECEPTIVE (appears high or low, reality is opposite)

### Type 7: Character Development Case
**Purpose:** Force Leon to grow in specific dimension

**Characteristics:**
- Cannot be solved with current capabilities
- Requires Leon to develop new skill or approach
- Failure is possible and meaningful
- Personal stakes, not just intellectual puzzle

**Development Focus:** Specific capability Leon needs to develop

**Example Structure:**
- Requires coordinating multiple people (leadership)
- Requires strategic planning (foresight)
- Requires trusting someone (vulnerability)
- Requires difficult moral choice (ethics)

**Convergence Potential:** VARIABLE (depends on case specifics)

## B. CASE COMPLEXITY ESCALATION

### Complexity Dimensions

Cases become more complex across multiple dimensions:

1. **Geographic Scope:** Single room → Building → District → Town-wide → Regional
2. **Character Count:** 2-3 people → 5-7 → 10-15 → 20+ → Town factions
3. **Information Sources:** 1-2 sources → Multiple sources → Conflicting sources → Unreliable sources
4. **Timeframe:** Hours → Days → Weeks → Months → Historical (decades/centuries)
5. **Resolution Clarity:** Clear solution → Partial solution → Ambiguous → Multiple interpretations
6. **Personal Stakes:** None → Low → Moderate → High → Existential
7. **Consequences:** Isolated → Limited ripple → Significant impact → Reshape relationships
8. **Required Capabilities:** One skill → Multiple skills → Coordination → Leadership → Strategic planning

### Early Cases (Chapters 1-30)

**Characteristics:**
- Small scope (single location or person)
- 2-3 characters involved
- Clear information available
- Resolved within chapter or two
- Low stakes
- Limited consequences
- Tests basic investigation skills

**Example:**
- Margaret wants to know if family heirloom is valuable
- Leon examines object, researches in library, provides answer
- Introduces Arthur (bookseller), builds relationship
- Plants seed: object has unusual maker''s mark (future convergence)

### Middle Cases (Chapters 31-80)

**Characteristics:**
- Medium scope (district or multiple locations)
- 5-10 characters involved
- Conflicting information sources
- May span several chapters
- Personal stakes emerging
- Consequences affect relationships
- Requires coordination and strategy

**Example:**
- Property dispute involves three families, church, and town hall
- Leon must interview multiple witnesses with competing claims
- Discovers old deed that contradicts current records
- Resolution affects town power dynamics
- Aurelius connection: deed references property Aurelius once owned

### Late Cases (Chapters 81+)

**Characteristics:**
- Large scope (town-wide or historical)
- 15+ characters involved
- Multiple conflicting narratives
- Spans many chapters or entire arc
- High personal stakes
- Consequences reshape Leon''s world
- Requires leadership and grand strategy

**Example:**
- Discovery that multiple prominent families descended from Aurelius''s associates
- Organization involvement becomes clear
- Leon must navigate competing factions
- Resolution reveals major Aurelius purpose
- Changes Leon''s understanding of everything

## C. CASE DESIGN TEMPLATE

Use this template when designing a new case:

```
CASE ID: [unique identifier]
CASE NAME: [brief title]
CHAPTER RANGE: [estimated chapters]

TYPE: [Pure Investigation / Historical / Relationship / Worldbuilding / Convergence / Misdirection / Character Development]

COMPLEXITY LEVEL: [Early / Middle / Late]

SUMMARY:
[1-2 sentence case description]

INITIATION:
- How does Leon encounter this case?
- Who brings it to him or how does he discover it?
- Why does Leon care enough to investigate?

KEY CHARACTERS:
- [Character 1]: Role in case, relationship to Leon, knowledge state
- [Character 2]: Role in case, relationship to Leon, knowledge state
- [etc.]

INFORMATION SOURCES:
- [Source 1]: Type (document/witness/object), reliability, what it reveals
- [Source 2]: Type, reliability, what it reveals
- [etc.]

INVESTIGATION PATH:
1. [Step 1]: What Leon does, what he learns
2. [Step 2]: What Leon does, what he learns
3. [etc.]

OBSTACLES:
- [Obstacle 1]: What blocks Leon''s progress
- [Obstacle 2]: How Leon must adapt

RESOLUTION:
- How is case resolved (or not)?
- What does Leon conclude?
- Is conclusion correct?

CONSEQUENCES:
- Immediate: What changes right away
- Relationship: Who trusts/distrusts Leon more
- Knowledge: What Leon learns
- Future: What seeds are planted

LEON DEVELOPMENT:
- Which capabilities does this case test?
- Which capabilities does Leon develop?
- Stage progression: Does this move Leon toward next stage?

AURELIUS CONNECTION:
- Connection type: [None / Subtle / Indirect / Obvious]
- Convergence seeds planted: [list]
- Payoff timing: [immediate / later chapters / much later]

READER ENGAGEMENT:
- Mystery hook: What makes reader curious?
- Clue distribution: What can reader deduce?
- Surprise factor: What subverts expectations?
```

## D. CASE INTRODUCTION PATTERNS

### Pattern 1: Direct Request
Someone asks Leon for help directly.

**Advantages:** Clear, straightforward, natural
**Use when:** Establishing Leon as investigator, relationship development

### Pattern 2: Overheard Information
Leon learns about problem through gossip or chance.

**Advantages:** Organic, shows Leon''s curiosity, reader discovers with Leon
**Use when:** Want subtle introduction, test Leon''s initiative

### Pattern 3: Personal Discovery
Leon encounters mystery while doing something else.

**Advantages:** Integrates with ongoing activities, feels natural
**Use when:** Want to show Leon''s observational skills

### Pattern 4: Escalation
Simple question evolves into complex case.

**Advantages:** Mirrors real investigation, builds complexity naturally
**Use when:** Want to demonstrate how cases deepen

### Pattern 5: External Assignment
Organization or authority figure assigns case to Leon.

**Advantages:** Shows Leon''s growing reputation, institutional involvement
**Use when:** Leon is established investigator, organization is active

## E. CHARACTER INTRODUCTION THROUGH CASES

**Principle:** Characters appear because Leon needs their expertise, not because story needs to introduce them.

### Introduction Pattern

```
Case requires specific expertise → Leon seeks specialist → Meet new character
```

### Examples

**Need:** Historical document translation
**Specialist:** Archivist or scholar
**Introduction:** Leon goes to archive/library, meets character who can read old script
**Relationship begins:** Based on shared interest in historical mystery

**Need:** Medical knowledge about old illness
**Specialist:** Physician
**Introduction:** Leon consults doctor about symptoms mentioned in documents
**Relationship begins:** Professional consultation becomes ongoing contact

**Need:** Understanding of religious practices
**Specialist:** Clergy member
**Introduction:** Leon visits church to ask about rituals mentioned in case
**Relationship begins:** Religious figure intrigued by Leon''s questions

**Need:** Underground/criminal information
**Specialist:** Street contact or tavern keeper
**Introduction:** Leon needs information not available through official channels
**Relationship begins:** Transactional initially, may develop into trust

### Multi-Function Characters

Best character introductions serve **multiple purposes:**

1. **Solve immediate case need** (expertise)
2. **Plant future convergence seed** (character knows something about Aurelius)
3. **Develop Leon''s network** (relationship continues beyond case)
4. **Reveal worldbuilding** (character''s perspective shows how world works)
5. **Challenge Leon** (character disagrees or tests Leon''s assumptions)

## F. CASE INTERCONNECTION SYSTEM

### Connection Types

**Type A: Direct Sequel**
Case 2 is direct continuation of Case 1.
- Use sparingly (too linear)
- Good for major arc cases

**Type B: Delayed Connection**
Cases seem unrelated, connection emerges later.
- Primary convergence mechanism
- Rewarding for attentive readers

**Type C: Parallel Investigation**
Multiple cases investigated simultaneously.
- Shows Leon''s growing capability
- Allows cross-pollination of clues

**Type D: Nested Cases**
Investigating Case A reveals Case B inside it.
- Natural complexity escalation
- Mirrors real investigation

**Type E: Shared Characters**
Different cases involve same people.
- Builds character depth
- Shows interconnected community

### Connection Tracking

Maintain a **Case Connection Map:**

```
CASE A (Chapter 5)
  ↓ shares witness with
CASE B (Chapter 12)
  ↓ location mentioned in
CASE C (Chapter 18)
  ↓ object from
CASE D (Chapter 25)
  ↓ all converge in
CASE E (Chapter 30) - Major Revelation
```

## G. PACING GUIDELINES

### Case Density

**Early Story:** 1 case per 1-2 chapters
- Leon can focus on one thing at a time
- Reader learns investigation process
- Build foundation

**Middle Story:** 2-3 cases active simultaneously
- Leon juggles multiple investigations
- Shows growing capability
- Allows parallel development

**Late Story:** Multiple interconnected cases
- Cases flow into each other
- Hard to separate individual cases
- Everything connects

### Resolution Timing

**Quick Resolution (1-2 chapters):**
- Simple cases
- Early relationship building
- Pacing variety

**Medium Resolution (3-6 chapters):**
- Most cases should be this length
- Allows development without dragging
- Sustains reader interest

**Long Resolution (7+ chapters, full arc):**
- Major convergence cases
- Complex multi-character situations
- High stakes investigations

## H. INTEGRATION WITH OTHER SYSTEMS

### Cases and Leon''s Trajectory

**Stage 1 (Observer):** Cases are simple, Leon learns by doing
**Stage 2 (Investigator):** Cases test investigative skill
**Stage 3 (Connector):** Cases require coordinating people
**Stage 4 (Strategist):** Cases require strategic planning
**Stage 5 (Leader):** Cases have organization-level implications

### Cases and Aurelius Mystery

Every 3-5 cases should plant at least one convergence seed.

Every 10-15 cases should have one convergence moment (seeds pay off).

Every 30-50 cases should have major revelation.

### Cases and Organization

Early: Cases are independent of organization
Middle: Organization becomes aware of Leon through cases
Later: Organization assigns cases to Leon
Late: Leon''s cases reshape organization understanding

## I. WRITING GUIDELINES

### Case Investigation Scenes

**Show, don''t tell:**
- Leon examines evidence physically
- Leon asks specific questions
- Leon records in notebook
- Leon cross-references sources

**Avoid shortcuts:**
- No magical intuition
- No convenient coincidences
- No information appearing from nowhere
- Leon must work for every piece of evidence

**Maintain 1816 constraints:**
- No phone calls or instant communication
- Research requires physical travel
- Archives are physical, not searchable databases
- Witnesses must be found and convinced to talk

### Case Resolution Principles

**Earned conclusions:**
- Leon''s conclusion must follow from evidence shown
- Reader should be able to deduce alongside Leon
- Surprise is in what conclusion means, not conclusion itself

**Consequences matter:**
- Resolution changes something (relationships, knowledge, access)
- Leon doesn''t return to exact starting state
- Cases compound over time

## J. CASE RESERVOIR SYSTEM

Maintain a **Case Idea Bank** with potential cases at various complexity levels.

### Bank Categories

**Ready to Use:**
- Fully designed cases
- Characters identified
- Convergence seeds planned
- Can be inserted when needed

**Concept Stage:**
- Basic idea only
- Needs development
- Placeholder for future

**Convergence Payoffs:**
- Cases that resolve earlier seeds
- Timing dependent on earlier cases
- Cannot be used until seeds are planted

**Emergency Cases:**
- Simple, self-contained cases
- Can be inserted for pacing
- No major plot dependencie s
