# Chapter 25 — The Rate and the Name

The Monday brought the town to its winter business, because the rate was to be asked, and a town that asks a rate is a town that has gathered itself into one room, and I went about my day with that knowledge in my pocket, and did my part of it at the desk and at the window, and let the asking go forward without me.

The poor-rate is asked in Alderwick at the vestry, because the vestry is the room the parish does its sums in, and the sums are done in December, because the winter makes the poor, and the making of them wants a paper; and the asking had been spoken of since the first of the month, and it had been spoken of the way the town speaks of a thing it has agreed to meet, and I had heard it at the pump and at Margaret's and at the door of Crane's, and I knew it would come, and did not know it would come that day.

It came in the afternoon, and it came with a name in it.

Daniel came to my door a little after three, with his hat in his hand, because that is the way he comes when the Academy has let out and the afternoon is his own, and he stood at the door the length of a breath before he spoke, which is not his way, and I took that in, and asked him in, and he came in, and stood by the fire, and said the thing he had come to say.

"There is a meeting at the vestry this afternoon," he said. "The rate. I have been at the Academy window, and I have seen them go in, and I will tell you who went in, because one of them is not of the town, and the town is asking its rate with the stranger sitting in it."

"Who," I said.

"The man from the inn," said Daniel. "The man that came in with the season, and has been at the inn this fortnight, and has been seen at the church and at the town hall and at the bench. He went in with the churchwardens, and he went in as a man goes in that has the right of it, and the town went in after him, and I thought you would want the telling of it."

I did want the telling of it, and I said so, and Daniel stood by the fire, and gave me the rest of what he had seen: that the clerk went in with his book, and the constable with the list, and the two farmers from the north end, and Michael with his hat off, and the verger last, because the verger keeps the door as well as the tower, and that the stranger sat where the wardens sit, and was spoken to by the wardens as a man spoken to at the table, and not as a man shown the room.

"The rate is the town's own business," said Daniel, "and the town has let a stranger sit at it, and I do not know why, and I thought you would want to know that it was so, because you keep the roll, and the roll is the other paper of the town."

I thanked him, and he went away into the afternoon, and I stood at the window with the knowledge of it, and put it together with what I had, and made the sum of it, and the sum came out as sums come out in this town: that the bench had come in with the season, and had been seen at the church and at the town hall, and had asked the duty of the watch, and had held the clerk's key over the Sunday of the snow, and had now gone in with the wardens at the asking of the rate, and that the coming of the bench and the coming of the season were one coming, and the town had let it come, because the town wanted what the coming brought, and what it brought was the asking of the rate, and the rate was the poor, and the poor were the winter, and the winter was the thing the town could not keep.

---

The evening brought the telling of the meeting, because a meeting is told in a town the way a thing is told, and the telling came to my door at the hour of the lamp, in the person of the verger, who came from the church by the road he always comes, and stood at my door with the lantern low, and gave me the meeting as he had kept it.

"The rate is asked," he said, "and it is asked at the figure the bench named, and the town has said yes to it, and the saying of yes was the thing that was done, and the doing of it was short. There were two that spoke against it, and the speaking of them was heard, and the hearing of it was the end of it, and the rate stands at the figure, and the paper will be made of it, and the making of it will be before the year is out."

"The bench named the figure," I said.

"The bench named it," said the verger. "And the churchwardens carried it, and the carrying of it was their part, and the stranger sat at the table where the wardens sit, and spoke the figure as a man speaks what he has come to speak, and the town said yes, because the winter is here, and the poor are here, and the figure was the figure that was wanted. That is the meeting, sir, and I have told it you because you keep the roll, and the roll is the paper the watch is kept in, and the watch is the other asking of the season."

"Was Hobbs named," I said.

The verger stood the length of a breath, and the lantern stood low with him.

"Hobbs was named," he said. "He was named at the meeting, because his name is on the poor list, and the poor list is the list the rate is asked for, and the name was asked at the meeting, and it was said at the meeting that he has not come in, and the saying of it was the saying of the meeting, and there was more said, and I will tell you the more, because you have been at the poor-house asking, and the asking of you is the asking that wants the truth of it. It was said at the meeting that the man went out on the Thursday of the bell, and has not come in since, and that his bed has stood cold, and his place at the window empty, and the town has noted it, and the noting of it is in the vestry book, and the noting is all the town will do of it, because a man that goes out of the poor-house is the poor-house's, and the poor-house has not asked for him, and the town will not ask where the poor-house does not ask."

"Who said he went out on the Thursday," I said.

"The keeper said it," said the verger. "The keeper of the house said it at the meeting, and the saying of it was kept in the book, and I stood at the door, and I heard it, and I tell it you, because you asked at the house, and because the Thursday was the Thursday of the bell, and you know what the Thursday was, and the town does not know what the Thursday was, and that is the difference of the two of us, and I keep it so."

He went away with the lantern, and I stood at the door with the night coming down, and the lamp came up at the end of the lane at the corner hour of nine, steady, at its old height, and I watched it, and understood that the town had done two things that day: it had asked the rate, and it had named Hobbs, and the two things stood in one book, and the book stood in the vestry, and the vestry stood behind the church, and the church stood over the lane, and the lane ran out to the house where the lamp burned, and the man was gone from the house where the poor are kept, and had gone on the night of the bell, and the town had noted it, and the noting of it was the end of the town's part.

I went in, and shut the door, and took the book, and wrote the day down, and put the two things together as they stood:

*The rate is asked at the figure the bench named, and the town has said yes. Hobbs is named at the meeting: gone out Thursday of the bell, not come in; bed cold, place at the window empty; noted in the vestry book. The town asks the rate for the poor, and notes the going of one of the poor, and does both in one afternoon, and the stranger sits at the table where the wardens sit, and the poor-house does not ask for the man, and the town does not ask where the poor-house does not. I asked at the house on the Monday of the snow, and I asked again on the day of the register, and the two askings are in my book, and the town's noting is in the vestry book, and the two books stand about one man, and do not say the same thing of him.*
