CHAPTER 119 — THE REALIZATION

I did not sleep that night.

I lay in the dark, with the number in my mind — the fixed number, the count that did not change, the count that the boundary was set to mark.

A count that did not change. A count of something fixed.

I turned it over in my mind. I could not let it go.

The stones did not change. The count did not change. The boundary held.

And the hairline mark on the fourth stone did not change.

I had watched the hairline mark appear, in the first year. It had appeared, and it had not grown, and I had not known what it meant.

A mark that appeared, and did not grow, and did not change.

I sat up in the dark.

The mark appeared.

The mark appeared, and it did not grow, and it did not change.

What if the mark was not a crack?

What if the mark was a tally?

A single mark. One mark. Added to a count.

I had watched the hairline mark appear. I had written in the red book that the fourth stone had cracked, that a line had appeared across its face, too straight to be natural.

What if it was not a crack?

What if it was a mark — a mark added to the stone, the way a tally-mark is added to a count?

I stood.

I lit the lamp. I took out the book of the first keeper. I opened it to the first page.

I looked at the tally.

Rows of marks. Groups of marks.

A count that did not change.

And on the fourth stone, a mark that had appeared.

I could not yet tell what it meant. But I understood that the mark on the fourth stone and the tally in the book of the first keeper might be the same kind of thing.

Marks.

Tally-marks.

A count.

---

I went to the boundary before first light.

I knelt at the fourth stone. I looked at the hairline mark across its face.

A single mark. Straight, too straight to be natural.

I put my finger to it.

It was smooth. It was not a crack. It was a mark — a mark cut into the stone, or pressed into it, or made by some means I did not understand.

A mark that had not been there in the first year, and had appeared, and had not grown, and had not changed.

I stood.

I walked the line of stones. I looked at every stone.

The stones were old. They were worn. They carried the first sign at their base, and they carried the marks of the years — the cracks, the stains, the wear of weather and time.

And on the fourth stone, a mark that had appeared.

I did not know what it meant.

But I understood that the boundary was not only a count.

It was a tally.

And the tally was being kept.

---

I went to the chamber beneath Bellweather House.

I had not been there since the winter. I had not needed to go. But I went now, with the realization in my mind, and I knelt at the trapdoor and opened it.

I went down into the passage.

The first signs marked the way. I walked past them, to the chamber.

The first sign was on the floor.

I knelt.

I looked at the first sign on the floor.

The centre of the count.

I had set the medallion in it once, and I had felt the place beneath.

I looked at the first sign now.

The circle. The three lines. The dot.

And at the centre of the sign, where the lines crossed, there was a mark.

A small mark.

A single tally-mark, cut into the stone, at the centre of the first sign.

I had not seen it before. I had not looked for it. I had set the medallion in the sign, and I had felt the warmth, and I had not looked at the centre of the sign.

I looked at it now.

A single mark, at the centre of the first sign, on the floor of the chamber.

The same kind of mark as the hairline mark on the fourth stone.

A tally-mark.

I knelt, in the dark, in the lamplight, and I looked at the mark, and I understood.

The chamber was the centre of the count.

The first sign on the floor was the tally.

And the mark at the centre was a count — a count that was being kept, one mark at a time, by someone, or something, that I did not understand.

I did not know how many marks had been made. I did not know how many were left.

But I knew that I was part of it.

I stood.

I left the chamber. I walked back along the passage, up the ladder, and out of the house.

I walked home through the streets, and I sat at my desk, and I opened the red book, and I wrote:

*The hairline mark on the fourth stone is not a crack. It is a mark — a tally-mark.*

*There is a mark at the centre of the first sign on the floor of the chamber. The same kind of mark.*

*The boundary is not only a count. It is a tally. And the tally is being kept.*

*I do not know who is keeping it. I do not know how many marks have been made. I do not know how many are left.*

*But I know that I am part of it.*

*The keeper is part of the count.*

*And the count is being kept.*
