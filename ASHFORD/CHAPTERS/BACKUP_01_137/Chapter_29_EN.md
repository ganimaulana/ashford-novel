# Chapter 29 — The Fourth Round

The Saturday came, and with it my turn of the round, and the round came round to me the way such things come round, with the week's weight in it, and I took the weight as the round takes it, which is on the arm that carries the lantern, and went out at ten into the cold.

The town had the feel of the Saturday before its great day, and the cold had come down harder in the afternoon, and there was the look of snow in the sky again, and the lane was the lane, and I did my part of it from my door to the corner, and came by the gate at the quarter past ten, and the gate was shut, and the padlock to it, and I took that in, and went on.

The round was not the round of the last time, because the round is never the same round twice, and this one had the man of the week in it, though the man was not in the lane; and I walked the round with the three tellings of the week about me, and looked at the lane with the eye the week had given me, and the eye saw what the eye had not seen before, because the eye had been changed.

I saw, at the corner, where the lane turns by the mill-yard gate, that the snow of the week before — the snow that had lain and gone off under the rain — had left the ground in the state the rain leaves ground, and the ground by the gate was marked, and the marking was the marking of feet, and the feet had come and gone by the gate since the rain, because the marking stood fresh in the ground, and the fresh marking was on the lane side of the gate, and on the yard side of it also.

I stood at it with the lantern, and took it as the round takes such things, and did not go in at the gate, because the round goes past, and I made the marking out as well as the lantern would let me: that the feet had stood at the gate, and had gone in, and had come out, and that the going in and the coming out were not the same hour, because the one marking was over the other, and the one was fresh and the other fresher. And there was one more thing, and I took it in because the eye of the week was in me: that the feet that came out went the way of the mill, and not the way of the lane.

I wrote it in the round-book, because that is what the round-book is for:

*Gate shut, padlock to it, at the quarter past ten. Ground by the gate marked with feet since the rain: one going in, one coming out; the coming out fresher; the feet that came out went the way of the mill. No person seen.*

And I went on with the round, and came round again at the quarter to eleven, and the gate stood shut, and the padlock to it, and I came round the third time at eleven, and the gate stood shut, and the padlock to it, and I understood that the gate would not stand open for me tonight, because the gate is kept by them that know the round, and the round is my round, and the gate would not be opened on the round's own night, and the knowing of that was the thing the week had given me, and I did not know whether to be glad of the giving.

---

The sound came at the quarter past eleven.

I was come round to the church end of the lane, and the sound came along it, low and full and at the ground, moving toward the corner the way it moves, along the water and the stone, and I stood still at the church end, because a man that hears the thing he has been hearing these five weeks stands still at the hearing of it, and I heard it once, and after the pause, twice, and then no more, and the lane lay still after it, and the church stood over it, and I stood at the church end with the lantern low, and listened the length of ten breaths, and heard nothing more.

It came on the lamp's night, because the lamp had burned at nine, and it came at the quarter past eleven, and it came from the corner way, and it moved toward the corner, and I was at the church end, and the sound was between me and the corner, and I did not go toward it, because the round does not go toward the sound, and I had learned what the round has learned, which is to keep the round and let the sound keep itself.

I wrote it at the end of the round, in the round-book, under the marking of the gate:

*The bell at the quarter past eleven: once, and after the pause, twice; low, at the ground, moving toward the corner. Heard from the church end. Not gone toward. The bell came on the lamp's night, as it came on the Saturday past, and the two comings stand together in the book.*

---

The round came home at the quarter to twelve, and I stood at my own door with the lantern in my hand, and looked at the end of the lane, and the house stood there with its black upper windows, and no light in them, because the light had been carried away two hours since, and the rosemary stood in its pot in the dark, and the house kept itself, and I understood that the house had kept itself through the whole of the week's asking, and the asking had gone about the town, and had not come to the house, and the house had stood at the end of the lane through the whole of it, and kept its lamp, and kept its silence, and kept its rosemary, and I went in at my own door, and shut it, and set the lantern down.

And there was that on the step inside the door, and I came on it with the setting down of the lantern, and stooped to it, because that is what a man does with what he comes on at his own door.

It was a paper. It was folded once, and it had no writing on the outside of it, and no seal, and no mark, and it had come under the door, or been put there, in the hour of the round, because it was not there when I went out at ten, and it was there when I came in at the quarter to twelve, and the round had been the round, and the lane had been the lane, and the paper had come into the house in the between of the two.

I did not open it that night. I set it on the desk with the candle, and looked at it the length of a breath, and understood that I had come home from keeping the round to find the thing the round was kept against standing on my own step, and that the keeping of the lane had not kept the house of the keeper, and that the paper lay on the desk with the outside of it up, and the inside of it kept, and the keeping of it was mine, and I would open it in the morning, with the day about me, and not in the night, with the round still on my arm.

I wrote it down, and went to bed, and did not sleep the sleep of the round that night, and lay with the paper on the desk in my head, and the marking of the feet by the gate, and the sound moving toward the corner, and the three things stood together as the things of this town stand, and I did not know yet what they stood about, and the not-knowing of it was the thing I slept on at the last.
