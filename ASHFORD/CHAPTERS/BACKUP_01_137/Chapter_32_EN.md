# Chapter 32 — The Year’s End

The day after Christmas came in quiet, the way such days come, with the town spent from the keeping of the day before, and the streets thinner, and the shops half-opened, and the whole of it settling back into itself as a thing settles that has been lifted for a day and put down again.

The lamp burned at nine on the twenty-sixth, and was carried away along the rooms, and no sound came, and I set it down as the twenty-second reading, and let it stand. And the sound did not come in the night, and the alternation stood, and the house kept its hour, and I kept mine, and the two keepings went on side by side in the lane, and did not touch, and the not-touching was the way of it, and had been the way since the first night.

I went to Margaret’s on the twenty-seventh for the week’s bread, and the shop had the look of the season’s end in it, which is the look of a thing done with and not yet cleared away, and Margaret had the counter wiped and the loaves set out, and the flour in the sacks, and the whole of it standing as it stood, and the standing was the town’s way of saying the season was over and the week was come again.

"The rate's in the bread still," said Margaret, putting the loaf on the counter. "And will be till the new year clears it. The bench's figure stands till the quarter, and the quarter is in January, and the January is the month the town pays for the December. You'll taste it in every loaf till then." She paused, and for a moment her hand lingered on the loaf. "The day after Christmas, and I've still got bread left unsold. That's not the way it used to be."

I paid the price and went out, and the cold was on the lane, and the lane was the lane, and I went home with the bread and the knowledge that the rate stood in the loaf till the quarter, and that the quarter was the new year's first business, and that the new year was four days off.

---

The twenty-eighth came, and the twenty-ninth, and the thirtieth, and the town went about the week's end with the haste of a year closing, and the shops shut earlier each day, and the streets emptied sooner, and the cold came down harder, and the sky went the colour of iron, and the town drew itself in for the year's last night.

The lamp burned each night at nine, and was carried away along the rooms, and no sound came, and I set them down as the twenty-third, twenty-fourth, and twenty-fifth readings, and let them stand. And the sound did not come on any of the nights, and the alternation stood through the year's end, and the house kept its hour as the year kept its turning, and the two keepings were the one keeping, and the one keeping was the year's, and the year was coming to its end.

I went to Arthur's on the thirtieth, because the book was due, and a book that is due is the thing a man carries back whether the year is ending or not, and Arthur took it with the hand that takes books, and set it on the counter, and looked at me the way he looks when the year is in him.

"The year goes out," said Arthur.

"It does."

"And the new one comes in. And the keeping stands in both, or it does not. That is the question of the year's end, Mr. Ashcroft. Not whether the year turns — the year always turns. But whether the keeping stands in the turning."

"And does it?"

Arthur looked at me then with the look that has the open door in it and the not-going-through, and he said, "I have kept this shop thirty years, and the keeping has stood in every turning. I do not know why this turning should be different. And yet the man is in the town, and the man asks, and the asking is new, and the newness is the thing I do not know. So I will say this: the keeping stands, or it does not, and I will keep my shop either way, and the shop will keep me, and the year will turn, and the turning will be the turning, and we will stand in it or not stand in it, and the standing is the thing."

I thanked him, and went out, and the lane was the lane, and the year was ending, and I went home with the knowledge that Arthur did not know whether the keeping would stand, and that the not-knowing was the year's gift, and the gift was the thing I carried into the new year.

---

The last day of the year came in grey and cold, and the town went about it with the quiet of a thing done, and the shops shut at noon, and the streets emptied, and the church stood in its cold with the green still up, because the green stays till the sixth of January, and the sixth is the season's true end, and the town keeps the green till the season's true end, and the true end is the thing, and the thing is the church's, and the church keeps it.

I went about my part of the day, and bought what was wanted, and came home with it, and set it in its places, and sat with the fire and the book, and wrote the year down:

*31 Dec. The year ends. I have been in the town forty-nine days. The lamp has burned twenty-six nights, and the sound has come four times, and the alternation has stood since the Saturday of the gate, with the two breaks kept by themselves. The bell was sold in the year nine to Josiah Crane, and the money is in the churchwardens' reckoning, and the bell is not. The bell left the shop before the year twelve, and the paper of the year ten says it went to the house by the church. The house at the end of the lane is not empty, and has not been empty since before I came, and the town agrees to call it empty, and the agreement is the keeping, and the keeping is the town's. The man at the inn asks where the papers are kept, and the papers are kept in the keeping, and the keeping is what he wants, and the wanting is the thing. I am on the watch roll, and the watch goes past the house, and I do not know whether the round is kept against the man or for him. I am not called. The note says it, and the note is in the desk, and the desk is in the house, and the house is in the lane, and the lane is in the town, and the town is in the year, and the year is ending, and the new year is coming, and I do not know what the new year will bring to the lane, and whether the keeping will stand in it, and whether I will stand in the keeping.*

*The five notes are in the folder. The certified copy is in its place. The round-book is on the desk. The bell-money is in the book. The bell is not. The second key is laid by. The gate is kept. The lamp burns. The sound comes when it comes. The house stands. The town stands. I stand. These are the things I carry into the new year, and the carrying is the keeping, and the keeping is the thing, and the thing is mine, and I am the town's, and the town is the year's, and the year is the keeping's, and the keeping is the house's, and the house is the lane's, and the lane is mine, and the belonging goes round, and the round is the thing, and the thing is the year's end, and the year's end is the beginning, and the beginning is the keeping, and the keeping is the thing.*

I shut the book, and let the fire burn, and the lamp came at nine, and was carried away along the rooms, and no sound came, and I set it down as the twenty-sixth reading, and let it stand, and kept the year's last night, and let the year end, and did not watch for the sound, because the sound keeps its own hour, and the hour was not come, and the not-coming was the year's last keeping, and the keeping was the thing, and I went to bed with it, and slept, and the year turned in my sleep, and I did not hear it turn.
