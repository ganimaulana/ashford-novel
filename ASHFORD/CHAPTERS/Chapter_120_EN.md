CHAPTER 120 — THE KEEPER OF THE TALLY

I could not yet tell who was keeping the tally.

I had found the marks — the hairline mark on the fourth stone, the mark at the centre of the first sign on the floor of the chamber. Marks that had appeared, and that had not grown, and that had not changed.

Marks that were being kept.

I did not know who was keeping them.

I sat at my desk, with the book of the first keeper before me, and I thought about the tally.

The first keeper had made the tally. He had kept the count. He had written that the tally was the count of the years.

But the count did not change.

A count that did not change was not a count of years.

And the marks on the stones — the marks that had appeared, and that had not grown, and that had not changed — were being added to something.

I did not know what.

I opened the book of the first keeper. I looked at the first page.

The tally. Rows of marks. Groups of marks.

And at the end of the tally, on the last page of the book, the sentence I had read many times: *I have kept my word. I have kept the keeping. I have erased myself so that the keeping will be kept for its own sake, and not for mine.*

I read the sentence again.

And I understood that I had been reading it wrong.

*I have erased myself.*

The first keeper had erased his own history. He had removed his name from the records. He had left no trace of who he was.

But he had left the tally.

The tally was not a count of years. It was a count of something else — something that the first keeper had kept, and that the keepers after him had kept, and that I was keeping now.

I did not know what it was.

But I knew that it was being kept.

---

I went to see Elias Marr.

He was at his house on the lane past the mill, among the objects without history, and he let me in.

"The tally," I said. "The marks in the book of the first keeper. You know them."

Elias was silent.

"I know them," he said. "I have known them for years."

"What are they counting?"

He was silent a long moment.

"I do not know," he said. "I have collected the objects that were scrubbed of their origin. I have kept the book of the first keeper. I have looked at the tally, and I have counted the marks. And I have not known what they are counting."

I stood in his house, among the objects without history.

"No one knows," I said.

"No one knows," Elias said. "The first keeper made the tally. He kept the count. He erased his own history. And he left the tally, without explanation, without meaning, without anything but the marks themselves."

"The marks are being added to," I said.

Elias looked at me.

"The marks?"

"There is a mark on the fourth boundary stone," I said. "A mark that appeared in the first year. A tally-mark. And there is a mark at the centre of the first sign on the floor of the chamber. The same kind of mark."

Elias was very still.

"The tally is being kept," he said.

"It is being kept," I said. "Not by me. Not by any keeper I know. But it is being kept."

We looked at each other in the lamplight.

"Then there is someone keeping the tally," Elias said. "Someone who is adding marks to the count."

"Who?"

"I do not know." He looked at me. "But if the tally is being kept, then the count is not complete. And if the count is not complete, then the keeping is not finished."

I stood in his house, among the objects without history, and I understood.

The keeping was not finished.

The count was not complete.

And I was not the last keeper.

The tally was being kept by someone — or something — that I did not know.

And the count would continue until it was finished.

---

I walked home through the dark streets.

I sat at my desk. I opened the red book. I wrote:

*The tally is being kept. There are marks on the stones, and marks on the floor of the chamber. Marks that have appeared, and that have not grown, and that have not changed.*

*Someone is adding marks to the count.*

*I do not know who.*

*The keeping is not finished. The count is not complete.*

*I am not the last keeper.*

*The tally will continue until it is finished.*

I closed the red book.

I sat in the dark.

I was the keeper. I was a hunter. I was a man who worked with a society that had no name.

And I had found that the tally was being kept.

I did not know who was keeping it.

But I would find out.

The hunt had led me to the tally.

And the tally was leading me to the keeper of the count.
