CHAPTER 47 — THE KEEPER'S VIGIL

The old constable died before dawn.

I found him in the back room of the house.

There was no struggle.

No message.

Only the chair, the cold fireplace, and the rosemary on the sill.

I closed his eyes.

Then I sat where he had sat.

For the first time, the house felt empty.

Not abandoned.

Empty.

There was a difference.

Crowe came that evening.

"You have inherited the agreement."

"I know."

"You have also inherited the choice."

"I know."

He looked at me.

"Then open the door."

"No."

He smiled.

"Not yet."

I said nothing.

He left.

I returned to the mill alone.

The stones were still.

The door was closed.

The gear lay on the table beside the bell.

I touched neither.

I opened my notebook.

For several minutes, I did not write.

Then:

The agreement has been renewed.

I paused.

The door remains closed.

Another pause.

I still do not know what is behind it.

I closed the book.

That was all.

For now.

Outside, Ashford continued as it always had.

A cart passed along the road.

Someone laughed in a nearby house.

A church bell marked the hour.

The town did not know that beneath its streets there was a door.

It did not know that someone was keeping it closed.

And it did not know that the man keeping it had finally begun to wonder whether he was protecting the town—

or protecting the thing beneath it.

I stood in the darkness and listened.

Nothing answered.

That was almost worse.
