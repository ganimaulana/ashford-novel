# Chapter 76 — The Turning

On the first of May, the old constable died. I went to the house at the end of the lane in the morning, and I found him in his bed, his eyes closed, his face still. He had died in his sleep, as he had said he would.

I sat with him for a long time, and I did not speak. I had known him for only a few months, but he had been the keeper of the agreement for fifty years, and he had passed it on to me. He had trusted me, and I had not let him down.

I closed the door of the house behind me, and I walked through the streets of Ashford with the weight of the agreement on my shoulders. I was the keeper now, and I was alone.

I went to Calloway's office, and I told him what had happened. He listened without interrupting, and when I had finished, he did not speak for a long time. "The old constable was a good man," he said at last. "He kept the agreement for fifty years, and he kept it well. You are his heir, and you must carry on his work."

I nodded, and I left his office. I went to the house at the end of the lane, and I stood at the gate, and I looked at the door. The house was empty now, and it was my house to keep.

I did not know what the future would bring, but I knew that I would keep the agreement. I would keep the bell, and I would keep the boundary, and I would keep the town safe.

I went home and wrote in my book: *1 May. The old constable is dead. I am the keeper now, and I am alone. I will keep the agreement, and I will keep the town safe.*
