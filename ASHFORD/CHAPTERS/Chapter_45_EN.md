CHAPTER 45 — THE CRACKS

The first stone cracked in May.

A thin line.

Almost invisible.

But when I touched it, the gear in my pocket became warm.

The second stone cracked two days later.

The third after that.

Something was changing.

The house at the end of Bellweather Lane appeared again.

This time it remained.

The windows were dark.

The door stood open.

Inside, the air was colder than outside.

I heard something beneath the floor.

Three knocks.

Then silence.

I did not go downstairs.

I left.

That night I wrote:

The boundary is weakening.

Then I added:

I do not yet know whether it is failing.

I stared at the sentence for a long time.

The distinction mattered.

A crack was evidence.

A conclusion required more.
