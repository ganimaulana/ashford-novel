CHAPTER 44 — THE WATCHER'S BURDEN

The rogue returned.

This time he gave me a name.

Graves.

He claimed the agreement was incomplete.

He claimed Aurelius had hidden something.

He claimed the thing beneath Ashford was not merely a danger.

It was an inheritance.

I did not believe him.

But I did not dismiss him.

That was the difference between investigation and faith.

I returned to the old constable.

"Did Aurelius know what was beneath the town?"

The old man's hands trembled.

"Enough to be afraid."

"Afraid of what?"

He looked toward the closed door.

"Of himself."

That answer stayed with me.

Crowe wanted me to open the door.

The old constable wanted me to keep it closed.

Calloway wanted me to decide for myself.

Three men.

Three answers.

None gave me certainty.

For the first time, I understood the burden of being a keeper.

It was not knowing the answer.

It was having to choose without one.
