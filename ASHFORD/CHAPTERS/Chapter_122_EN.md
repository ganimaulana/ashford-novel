CHAPTER 122 — THE NUMBER OF KEEPERS

I did not tell anyone about the keeper of the tally.

I did not tell Ysabel. I did not tell Calloway. I did not tell the society.

I sat at my desk, with the number in my mind — the number I had counted from the tally, the fixed number, the count that did not change.

A count of keepers.

I had counted the marks. I had written the number in the red book. I had not understood it.

Now I understood it.

The number was the number of keepers.

The tally was the record of the keepers — one mark for each keeper, since the first keeper set the boundary.

And the number was the count of them all.

I took out the red book. I opened it to the page where I had written the number.

I looked at it.

The number was large. Larger than I had expected.

There had been many keepers.

I sat in the dark, with the number before me, and I understood that the keeping was older than I had believed, and larger than I had believed, and that I was one keeper among many.

I could not yet tell where I stood in the count.

I did not know how many keepers had come before me.

I did not know how many would come after.

But I knew that I was part of the count.

And I knew that my mark was in the tally.

---

I went to the chamber beneath Bellweather House.

I knelt at the trapdoor. I opened it. I went down.

The first signs marked the way. I walked past them, to the chamber.

The first sign was on the floor.

I knelt.

I looked at the first sign on the floor.

The circle. The three lines. The dot.

And at the centre of the sign, the mark.

A single tally-mark.

The mark of a keeper.

I had not known what it was. I had thought it was a mark that had appeared, and that had not grown, and that had not changed.

Now I understood.

The mark at the centre of the first sign was the mark of a keeper.

My mark.

I knelt in the chamber, in the lamplight, and I looked at my mark.

It was a small mark. A single tally-mark, cut into the stone at the centre of the first sign.

The mark of the keeper who had come.

I had been in the keeping for four years. I had kept the account. I had kept the boundary. I had kept the door.

And my mark was in the tally.

I knelt there, in the chamber, and I understood that I was part of something larger than myself.

The keeping was old. The keeping was large. The keeping would continue after me.

And my mark was in the count.

---

I did not know how many marks were in the tally.

I had counted them, and I had written the number, and I had not understood it.

Now I understood it, and I did not want to count again.

The number was the number of keepers.

There had been many keepers.

And I was one of them.

I sat in the dark, and I thought about the keepers who had come before me — the first keeper, who had set the boundary and made the tally; Aurelius, who had signed the agreement; the keeper before Vale, who had opened the door and stayed in the passage; Vale, who had kept the door closed for fifty years.

And I thought about the keepers I did not know — the keepers whose marks were in the tally, whose names were not in any record.

The count of the keepers.

I did not know where I stood in the count.

But I knew that I stood in it.

I was a keeper.

My mark was in the tally.

And the keeping would continue after me.

---

I went to the reading room that night.

I sat in the chair, in the lamplight, and I thought about the keeper of the tally.

He had kept the count since the first keeper. He would keep it until the last.

He was not a keeper himself. He was the one who kept the record of the keepers.

I did not know who he was. I did not know where he came from. I did not know how long he had been keeping the count.

But I knew that he was real.

I had seen him. I had spoken to him. He had told me that the count was counting the keepers.

I sat in the reading room, in the lamplight, and I understood that there were things about the keeping that I would never fully understand.

The keeper of the tally. The count of the keepers. The marks on the stones.

Things that were older than the town, and larger than the town, and that would continue after the town was gone.

I went home.

I sat at my desk. I opened the red book. I wrote:

*The count is counting the keepers. The number I counted is the number of keepers.*

*There have been many keepers. More than I know. More than the records show.*

*My mark is in the tally.*

*The keeping will continue after me.*

*I am a keeper among many.*

*But I am a keeper.*

*And my mark is in the count.*

The count of thirteen signs moved forward, and the hour of the bell drew nearer.
