CHAPTER 41 — THE ORDER OF SEVERENCE

The first sign of the Order was not a meeting.

It was a phrase.

I found it in a document Finch had recovered from the old records.

Sever what was bound.

No signature.

No explanation.

Only the phrase.

Calloway recognised it.

"You should leave that alone."

"Who wrote it?"

He did not answer.

"Calloway."

He sighed.

"The Order of Severence."

The name was unfamiliar.

The people were not.

They had existed quietly in Ashford for years.

They believed boundaries created prisons.

They believed certain things should not remain sealed simply because previous generations had been afraid.

They were not a mob.

They were scholars, merchants, craftsmen, and educated men and women.

That made them more dangerous.

I met Eleanor Shaw at the market.

She did not introduce herself.

"You are carrying something."

I looked at her.

"Everyone carries something."

"Yours is metal."

I said nothing.

She smiled.

"I don't want it."

"What do you want?"

"To know whether you intend to keep a promise you don't understand."

She walked away.

I followed.

She stopped beside the river.

"The agreement was made for a reason," she said.

"What reason?"

"That is what you should ask."

"Do you know?"

"Some of us believe we do."

"Do you?"

She looked at the water.

"No."

That answer surprised me.

Then she said:

"But I know someone who thinks he does."

His name was Silas Crowe.

He did not threaten me.

He simply said:

"You have been looking at a locked door."

"And?"

"Eventually you will have to decide whether a locked door is protection or imprisonment."

Then he left.

I did not follow.
