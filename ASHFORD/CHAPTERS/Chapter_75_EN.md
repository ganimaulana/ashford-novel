# Chapter 75 — The Truth

On the twenty-fifth of April, I went to the house at the end of the lane to speak to the old constable. I had not seen him in a week, and I wanted to know if he had felt the same thing I had felt—the sense that something was shifting beneath the town, that the agreement was not holding as it should.

He was in the back room, as always, and he looked at me the way a man looks at a thing he has been expecting. "You have felt it," he said. It was not a question.

"I have felt it," I said. "The boundaries are still, but they are not quiet. Something is moving beneath the surface."

He nodded, and he gestured for me to sit. I sat down across from him, and he was silent for a long time. "The agreement is not a document," he said at last. "It is a choice. And a choice is only as strong as the will of the one who makes it. You have chosen to keep the agreement, and that is good. But the choice does not end when it is made. It must be made again, every day, every hour. And the thing inside the space knows that. It is waiting for the moment when the choice wavers."

He looked at me, and I saw the weight of his years in his eyes. "I have kept the agreement for fifty years," he said. "I have made the choice every day. And I am tired. I am ready to rest." He paused, and then he said, "You are the keeper now. You must make the choice. And you must make it without me."

I felt the weight of his words settle over me. "What do you mean?" I asked.

He looked at me, and I saw that he knew what he was saying. "I am going to die, Mr. Ashcroft. Not tomorrow, or next week, but soon. And when I die, the agreement will fall to you alone. You must be ready."

I did not know what to say. I sat with him for a long time, and we did not speak. When I left, I wrote in my book: *25 Apr. The old constable is dying. He has told me that the agreement will fall to me alone. I must be ready.*

I walked home through the cold, and I felt the weight of the town on my shoulders. I was the keeper, and I was the watcher, and I was alone.
