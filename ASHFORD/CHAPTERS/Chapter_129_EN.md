I did not leave the house in the marsh that night.

I built a small fire in the clearing, and I sat by it, and I looked at the house, and I thought about the first sign on the wall.

The first sign was the mark of the maker.

I had read that in the vestry book, years ago. The first sign was the sign of the maker, the sign that was set before the wall, before the boundary, before the Order.

I had carried the object all my life. I had found the first sign on the stones, and on the floor of the chamber, and on the bell, and on the clocks, and on the key, and on the box.

And now I had found it on the wall of a house in the marsh.

I could not yet tell what it meant.

But I knew that the first sign was everywhere.

And I knew that it was connected.

---

In the morning, I searched the marsh.

I walked out from the house, through the reeds, across the water, and I looked for other signs of the maker â€” other marks, other houses, other places where the first sign had been set.

I walked for hours.

The marsh was empty. There was no sign of the maker anywhere but in the house.

I came back to the house at midday.

I stood in the clearing, and I looked at the house, and I understood that I had been wrong.

The first sign was not the only thing the house had held.

I had searched the house. I had found nothing but the mark on the wall. But I had not looked beneath the house.

I knelt in the clearing.

I looked at the ground around the house.

The ground was packed earth and stone. It was the only firm ground in the marsh for as far as I could see.

I looked at the stones.

They were old. They were worn. They were set in the ground, in a pattern I had not noticed before.

A circle.

A circle of stones, around the house.

I stood.

I looked at the circle of stones.

The house stood in the centre of it. The stones were set in a ring, around the house, hidden by the reeds and the grass.

I counted them.

There were more than a hundred stones in the ring.

The same number as the boundary.

The same number as the tally.

I stood in the clearing, in the grey light, and I understood that I had not found a house in the marsh.

I had found the beginning of the boundary.

---

I knelt at the nearest stone.

I cleared the grass from its base.

The first sign was there.

The same mark. The same circle, three lines, dot. Cut into the base of the stone, hidden where the grass covered it.

I moved to the next stone.

The same sign.

The next. The next. The next.

Every stone in the ring carried the first sign at its base.

The same as the boundary in Ashford.

The same marks. The same signs. The same number of stones.

I stood in the centre of the ring, beside the house, and I understood.

The boundary in Ashford was not the only boundary.

There was a boundary here, in the marsh, older than the one in Ashford.

And the house stood at its centre.

I did not know what the house was. I did not know what the marsh boundary marked. I did not know why I had been led here.

But I knew that I had found something.

The beginning of the boundary.

The first sign.

The mark of the maker.

I stood in the ring of stones, in the grey light, and I understood that the hunt was not over.

The count had called me north.

And the count had led me to the beginning.

---

I did not leave the marsh.

I built a shelter beside the house, and I stayed, and I searched.

I walked the ring of stones. I counted them. I looked at the first sign on each one.

I searched the marsh beyond the ring. I looked for other signs, other houses, other marks of the maker.

I found nothing.

The ring of stones was the only sign of the maker in the marsh.

The house stood at its centre. The first sign was on its wall. The ring of stones circled it, each one carrying the first sign at its base.

And the count of the stones was the same as the count of the boundary.

I did not know what it meant.

But I knew that it was the beginning.

I sat by my fire, in the evenings, and I wrote in a new book â€” a book I had begun since leaving Ashford, a book of the journey, a book of what I was finding.

I wrote:

*The count has led me north. I have found a house in the marsh, with the first sign on its wall, and a ring of stones around it, each carrying the first sign at its base.*

*The count of the stones is the same as the count of the boundary in Ashford.*

*This is the beginning of the boundary. This is where the first keeper set the first sign.*

*I do not know what it means. But I know that I have found it.*

*The hunt is not over.*

*The count has led me to the beginning.*


The count of thirteen signs moved forward, and the hour of the bell drew nearer.
