CHAPTER 126 — THE PASSING

I stayed in Ashford for a year after Daniel came.

I did not keep the keeping. That was his now. I had passed it to him, and he had taken it, and the keeping was his to keep.

But I stayed.

I watched him learn. I watched him walk the boundary, and read the book, and ask the questions that I had asked, and learn the answers that I had learned.

He was a good keeper. He was not ready, when he came, but he learned. He walked the stones. He kept the account. He watched the season, and the lamp, and the bell.

I did not interfere. I did not advise, unless he asked. The keeping was his now, and a keeper must learn by keeping.

But I was there.

And when he asked, I answered.

---

In the spring, he came to me.

"I have read the book," he said. "I have walked the boundary. I have kept the account. But I do not understand the tally."

I looked at him.

"The tally is the count of the keepers," I said. "One mark for each keeper, since the first keeper set the boundary."

"I have counted the marks," he said. "The number is large. There have been many keepers."

"There have been many keepers," I said. "More than the records show."

"And my mark is in the tally?"

"Your mark is in the tally," I said. "The keeper of the tally made it when you came."

He looked at me.

"The keeper of the tally," he said. "You have seen him."

"I have seen him," I said. "He has kept the count since the first keeper. He will keep it until the last."

Daniel was silent a moment.

"And when I am done," he said, "when I have kept as long as I am meant to keep — then the tally will be complete?"

"No," I said. "The tally is never complete. The keeping is never finished. There will be keepers after you, as there were keepers before me."

I looked at him.

"You will write your own account," I said. "You will leave it for the keeper who comes after you. And the count will continue."

We stood at the boundary, in the spring light, and I understood that I had done what I could.

I had kept the keeping. I had written the account. I had prepared the instruction. I had passed the keeping.

And the keeping would continue.

---

On a day in autumn, I decided to leave Ashford.

I did not know where I would go. I had not been anywhere in five years. I had been the keeper, and the keeper stays.

But I was not the keeper now.

I had passed the keeping. The keeping was Daniel's.

I was free to go.

I went to see Ysabel. I told her I was leaving.

"Where will you go?" she said.

"I do not know," I said. "Somewhere I have not been. Somewhere I have not kept."

She looked at me a long moment.

"Will you come back?"

"I do not know," I said. "Perhaps. When the count calls."

She nodded.

"The count will call," she said. "It called you here. It will call you back."

I left Ashford on a grey morning.

I did not tell Daniel I was going. I did not want a farewell. I wanted to leave the way the first keeper had left — quietly, without ceremony, so that the keeping would be about the thing, and not about the man.

I walked out of the town by the north road.

I did not look back.

---

I walked for a long time.

I walked through towns I had not seen, and fields I had not crossed, and roads I had not walked. I carried the medallions — the one I had carried all my life, the first sign, and the one I had found in the kept house at the end of Bellweather Lane. I carried the key to the reading room. I carried the account of what I had learned, written in my own hand, in a new book that I had begun after the passing.

I did not carry the gear. I had passed it to Daniel, with the book of the keeping and the agreement.

I did not carry the red book. I had left it with Daniel, in the drawer, with the book of the keeping.

The account was his now.

I walked, and I looked at the world, and I understood that I was no longer the keeper.

I was a man who had kept the keeping, and who had passed it on.

I did not know where I would go. I did not know what I would do.

But I knew that I would be ready.

The count would call.

And when it called, I would answer.

I walked north, into the morning, and I was content.

I had been a keeper.

I had kept the account.

I had passed the keeping.

And the count would continue.

The end.
