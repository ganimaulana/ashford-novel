# Chapter 59 — The Understanding

The winter had settled into Ashford, and I had settled into the winter. I had been in the town eighty days, and I had learned to see the boundaries of things—not the boundaries of maps or property, but the boundaries between what is and what is not.

I sat at my desk and looked at the bell, and I looked at the gear, and I looked at the notes I had made over the past two months. I had investigated three cases, and I had found the pattern. I had followed the pattern to the boundary, and I had renewed the agreement.

I took out my book and wrote:

*23 Feb. The boundary is not a line. It is a space—a space that separates what is from what is not. The agreement is not a document. It is an act—an act that keeps the space intact. I have kept the act, and I have kept the space. I do not know what is on the other side of the space, but I know that it is there, and I know that I must keep it there.*

I shut the book and looked out at the lane. The house at the end of it stood with its rosemary on the sill and its glass open to the cold, and the lamp was not yet lit, because the hour was not yet come.

I had not solved the mystery of Ashford. I had only learned to see the shape of it. And that, I knew, was the beginning of the keeping.

I sat in the silence and waited for the next question, because I knew it would come.
