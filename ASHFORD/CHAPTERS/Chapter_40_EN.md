CHAPTER 40 — THE AGREEMENT

I returned two days later.

The old man finally gave me his name.

He had once served as constable.

Before Calloway.

He had guarded the house for decades.

"Guarded it from what?"

He looked at me.

"That is the wrong question."

"What is the right one?"

"Why was it sealed?"

He showed me an old document.

The handwriting was faded.

The name at the bottom was clear.

Aurelius Ashcroft.

My name.

My family.

I read the document twice.

The agreement was not a description of what lay beneath the town.

It was an instruction.

A boundary was to remain undisturbed.

Certain objects were not to be moved.

Certain doors were not to be opened.

And the keeper was to renew the agreement when the appointed time came.

"Why me?"

The old constable did not answer immediately.

"Because your family began it."

That was the first time I understood that my arrival in Ashford might not have been entirely accidental.

At midnight, I returned to the mill.

The gear fitted the depression.

I did not turn it.

I only held it there.

The ground trembled once.

Then stopped.

I removed it.

The door remained closed.

I went home.

For the first time, I understood why the old constable had been afraid.

Not because he knew what was behind the door.

Because he didn't.
