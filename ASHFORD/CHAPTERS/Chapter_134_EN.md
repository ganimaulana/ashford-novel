I walked toward the house on the hill.

The road led up from the town, past the wall of old stone, to a gate that stood open. Beyond the gate, a drive of packed earth led to the house.

I could not yet tell what the house was. I could not yet tell why the count had brought me here.

But the object was warm.

I passed through the gate. I walked up the drive.

The house was old. It was large, built of stone, with a roof of slate and windows that looked out over the town. It had been a house of some importance, once, and had fallen into a quieter age.

I came to the door.

It was closed.

I did not knock. I stood at the door, with the object warm against my chest, and I waited.

The door opened.

A woman stood in the doorway. She was older, in a plain dress, with a face that had seen many years. She looked at me for a long moment.

"You are the keeper," she said.

I did not deny it.

"You have come for the object," she said.

I stood very still.

"The object," I said. "The count has been calling it. It led me here."

She nodded.

"It has been waiting," she said. "It has been waiting for you."

She stepped back.

"Come in."

---

I entered the house.

It was quiet. The rooms were furnished, but plainly. The walls were lined with books, and papers, and the things of a long life.

The woman led me to a room at the back of the house, where a fire burned low.

There was a table in the middle of the room.

On the table, there was a box.

A wooden box, small, dark, plain.

The same size as the box that had held my object, all my life.

I looked at the box.

"Open it," the woman said.

I crossed the room. I opened the box.

Inside, on a bed of cloth, there was a object.

It was like mine. The same size. The same tarnished metal. The same cord.

And on its face, the same mark.

A circle.

Three lines through it.

A single dot.

The first sign.
