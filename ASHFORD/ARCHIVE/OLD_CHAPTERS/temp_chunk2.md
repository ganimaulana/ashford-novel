The front door opened even before I could knock.

A woman stood behind it.

She was about fifty, with short hair, wearing a grey woollen shawl and an apron still loosely tied at her waist.

Her face was firm.

But when her eyes fell on me, her expression changed.

Her gaze moved from my face to the trunk, and back to my face.

As if she were making sure of something.

"Leon Ashcroft," I said.

She did not answer at once.

She only looked at me a few seconds longer.

Then she nodded.

"You've come earlier than expected."

I looked at the clock.

"The stagecoach was actually a few minutes late."

"That's not what I meant."

I looked at her.

There was something in the way she said that.

Not a slip of the tongue.

More like something she had not meant to say had slipped out on its own.

She seemed to realise it.

A faint smile touched her face, but it did not reach her eyes.

"Come in. You must be cold."

"I don't mind if you tell me your name first."

"Helen."

I went in.

The hallway was papered with old floral wallpaper whose colours had faded. The air inside smelled of old wood and something warm from the kitchen.

The wooden floor creaked beneath my step.

An old wall clock ticked at the end of the corridor, its sound nearly drowned by the rain outside.

Helen took my trunk before I could refuse.

"Your room's on the second floor."

"I can carry it myself."

"I know."

She carried it anyway.

Halfway up the stairs, my eye was drawn to a painting on the wall.

A boy stood in front of this house.

The same house.

The canvas was old. Brown and cream dominated the painting. The boy's face was not clearly visible.

But there was something in the figure that made me stop.

"Who is the boy?"

Helen stopped.

She turned toward the painting.

For a moment her face changed.

Only a moment.

Then her expression settled again.

"The old owner."

"His name?"

"It was a very long time ago."

"That isn't an answer."

She looked at me.

"Sometimes a name doesn't matter."

Then she went on up the stairs.

I followed.

There was no use asking further.

At least for tonight.

On the second floor, Helen handed me the key to the room.

The key was old and heavy, with a round head and faint engraving along its edges.

A circle.

Three lines.

One dot.

I stared at it.

"This symbol is everywhere."

"An old symbol."

"What does it mean?"

"If I knew, perhaps I would tell you."

Her tone was lighter this time.

Almost friendly.

I looked at the key once more before closing it in my palm.

"Is there a good place to visit around here?"

"The stop. The shops. The church."

"Which church?"

"St. Bartholomew's."

She looked at me for a few moments.

Then said quietly,

"Don't come there after dark."

I gave a small laugh.

"Why?"

"Because it's a church."

The answer was so simple that I could not tell whether to take it as a joke or a warning.

Helen turned and went down the stairs.

I remained standing there for a few seconds.

My room was plain and clean.

A bed.

A table.

A wardrobe.

A bookshelf, mostly empty.

Beneath the large window, the streetlamp's light showed the trees moving in the rain.

Strangely, the room felt comfortable.

Like a room that had long been waiting for someone to fill it.

I opened my trunk and began arranging my things.

From the window I could see the town's lights in the distance, blinking beneath the dark sky.

One cluster of lights burned brighter than the rest.

Perhaps High Street.

Far behind it stood a large building with big windows only faintly visible through the rain. Farther still, the dark line of the hills vanished into the mist.

I closed the curtain.

Then I laid the town map on the table.

The place names spread across it like parts of a stage not yet fully built:

Town Centre.

Market Square.

High Street.

The Library.

St. Bartholomew's Church.

Academy Quarter.

Workshop Quarter.

I traced the map with a fingertip until I found Bellweather Lane.

I marked it.

Slowly, I would come to know this town.

Around ten o'clock the big house had fallen completely silent.

Helen had either gone to her room or gone out.

I took up my pen and opened the notebook.

ASHFORD — 12 NOVEMBER 1816

19:42 — arrived at the stop.

19:58 — arrived at the house, Bellweather Lane (no number).

Helen received me. Said I had "come earlier than expected."

Symbol: a circle, three lines, one dot.

The front door.

The room key.

The "key found" notice — November 1798.

I stopped.
