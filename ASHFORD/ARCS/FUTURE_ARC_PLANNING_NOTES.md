# IDEA 1 — ORGANIZATION ENTRANCE ARC (Recruitment by Challenge)

## Concept

A secret/semi-secret organization recruits new members without candidates knowing they are being selected. Candidates receive an anonymous letter posing a puzzle/case. They are not told this is an entrance examination. Only by solving it and following the instructions do they begin the true selection.

The organization does NOT seek the smartest person in the room. It seeks:

- the person least likely to be deceived by their own intelligence
- someone who knows when a conclusion is not yet trustworthy
- someone who acts independently, without needing the organization to provide all answers

## Core Philosophy (candidate criteria)

- **Intelligence** — able to understand a problem
- **Observation** — sees what others miss
- **Reasoning** — distinguishes fact from assumption
- **Discretion** — does not tell everyone everything
- **Judgment** — knows when to act and when not to
- **Independence** — does not need the organization to supply all answers
- **Curiosity** — but controlled curiosity

Organizational motto candidates may encounter:
> Seeing is not knowing.
> Knowing is not proving.
> And proving is not understanding.

## The 5-Stage Structure

### Stage 1 — The Invitation
Anonymous letter. No organization name. No threat. No mention of recruitment. Just:
- a case/mystery
- a few clues
- a deadline
- a location
- a reward

Example letter:
> Mr. Ashcroft,
> There is a room in Alderwick which has remained locked for seventeen years.
> Three men have entered it. None of them agree on what they saw.
> Determine which of them was telling the truth.
> If you succeed, come to the address below before midnight on Thursday.
> Your time will not be wasted.

Leon treats it as strange paid work.

### Stage 2 — The Investigation
The case must be logically solvable — NOT supernatural. Possible explanations:
- witness error
- document forgery
- coincidence
- someone deliberately creating a pattern

Leon must solve via archives, interviews, observation, maps, local history, small details, contradictions between witnesses. The reader should be able to play along.

RULE: No impossible tests. Every stage must feel like "if I were careful enough, I could actually solve this." After the reveal, the reader should think: "Oh... the clue was there all along." Not "How could Leon possibly know that?"

### Stage 3 — The First Reward
Leon arrives at the destination. No one is there. Only a box containing gold/money and a second letter:

> Your answer was correct.
> You may consider the matter concluded.
> Unless you wish to continue.

There is a next address. No explanation. Leon CAN stop. The organization tests whether he will choose to continue. (Do NOT force him to continue — that would feel like a plot device.)

### Stage 4 — The Real Selection
The second letter's challenge is much harder — not "are you smart" but "how do you USE your intelligence."

Example: a case with two solutions.
- Solution A: logically correct, but makes someone a victim.
- Solution B: harder, but saves that person.

The organization observes which choice Leon makes. He may not realize he is being watched.

### Stage 5 — The Final Meeting
Only at the end does Leon learn all of this was selection. He arrives at:
- a private library
- the back room of an academic institution
- an ordinary-looking old house

A calm, unassuming person waits — not the leader, not someone who looks powerful. They say:

> "Do you know why you were invited?"
> "I assumed you wanted someone to solve your problems."
> "No." — placing Leon's answers on the table — "We wanted to know what you would do when solving them."

Only now is the organization introduced.

## Non-candidates / Failure Rate

The organization sends letters to many people. Examples:
- 27 candidates
- 9 complete Stage 1
- 4 complete Stage 2
- 2 reach Stage 3
- 1 receives the final invitation

Leon may find evidence that his letter was not the first the organization sent. Others have received them before. Some:
- failed
- gave up
- drew the wrong conclusion
- were late
- thought it was a joke
- solved the puzzle but did not follow instructions

Good potential scene: another candidate solved a mystery faster than Leon but was rejected. When Leon asks why, the examiner replies: "Because he was too certain."

## Pacing / Revelation Structure

- Stage 1: mystery challenge
- Stage 2: anonymous patron
- Stage 3: repeated selection
- Stage 4: strange consistency (Leon realizes: "This isn't a series of unrelated problems.")
- Stage 5: someone finally meets him
- Then: organization reveal

The reader discovers the organization alongside Leon.

## Organization Public Face / Naming

- Name should be inconspicuous and not appear too early
- Publicly it may look like: scholarly society / historical society / private academy / philosophical club / antiquarian circle
- Beneath that: a far larger function
- Members do not automatically know the entire structure
- Joining ≠ understanding the organization (opens room for future arcs)

## Identity Notes (not a "Tarot Club copy")

- Very selective; does not recruit openly
- Candidates don't know they're being selected
- Challenge-based recruitment
- Each candidate evaluated individually
- The organization need not show its full face
- New members don't automatically know all secrets

# IDEA 2 — LOCATION-BASED COMBAT / SURVIVAL ARCS

## Concept

An arc where the location itself is the antagonist. Conflicts arise from the environment, not from "a powerful monster." Victory comes from observation + adaptation, NOT power escalation.

Key principle:
> The location determines how the fight happens.

## Proposed Location Arcs (a possible "Location as Character" pattern)

| Arc | Theme | Conflict | Combat style |
|-----|-------|----------|--------------|
| The Old Mine | darkness | narrow tunnels, collapse, gas, echoes, vertical spaces | close-quarter combat |
| The Marsh | uncertainty | fog, water, unstable ground, navigation, low visibility | survival / pursuit |
| The Bell Tower | height | stairs, narrow spaces, wind, old structure | vertical combat |
| The Winter Road | exposure | no shelter, enemy visible from afar | distance, stamina, weather, supplies |
| The Abandoned Estate | space | large rooms, many blind spots | doors, stairs, furniture, corridors, light |

## Marshland Arc (worked example)

Leon travels to a marshland outside Alderwick for a simple errand. No big mystery initially. Just: a small village, a marsh, bad weather, difficult roads, some missing people, a group of hunters, local guards.

Then a conflict occurs.

The marsh determines everything:
- unstable ground
- low visibility (fog)
- shallow water
- narrow paths
- sounds hard to track
- certain weapons difficult to use
- one wrong step can mean drowning

Leon may be weaker than his opponent, but he understands the terrain. He deliberately:
- lures the opponent onto soft ground
- uses sound to create false positions
- extinguishes light sources
- moves through paths only locals know

## Why This Matters for Alderwick

Prevents the story from being only "mystery → clue → mystery → clue → supernatural revelation."

Creates a rhythm of different arc types:
> Mystery Arc → Character Arc → Location/Survival Arc → Investigation Arc → Political/Social Arc → Mystery Arc

Gives readers a break from the main mystery. Proves Alderwick is a living world with many layers, not one giant puzzle where everything leads to one organization.

These arcs can be completely SEPARATE from:
- Order of Severence
- Aurelius
- the Agreement
- the gear

# DISCUSSION — MULTIPLE ORGANIZATIONS FOR LEON

## Question: Is it strange for Leon to join many organizations at once?

Answer: Not strange — potentially one of Alderwick's greatest strengths. But one big risk:
> Leon must not look like he is "collecting organizations."

If every few arcs Leon joins a new organization, readers may feel the organizations exist only to give the protagonist access to lore, characters, and abilities.

## Recommendations

### 1. Many organizations = good world-building
Each organization shows a different side of the world:
- academic group → history, research, archives
- investigative org → deduction, information networks
- religious group → theology, doctrine, ritual
- aristocratic society → politics, social class
- trade group → economy, trade routes
- secret organization → supernatural / hidden world
- criminal group → the dark side of Alderwick
- military institution → strategy, conflict
- antiquarian circle → artifacts and history

Leon learns the world by interacting with people who live in it — more natural than narrator exposition.

### 2. Do NOT make Leon a full member of all of them — use relationship levels

| Level | Status |
|-------|--------|
| 0 — Outsider | knows members, no access |
| 1 — Associate | may use some facilities |
| 2 — Candidate | undergoing selection |
| 3 — Member | official member |
| 4 — Trusted Member | limited secret access |
| 5 — Inner Circle | extremely rare |

Pattern: In 5–6 arcs, ~7 organizations appear, but Leon is a FULL member of only 2–3. The rest are: contacts, associates, rivals, informants, employers, targets, or uncomfortable relationships.

### 3. Organizations should conflict with each other
Do NOT make it "Org A gives Leon info, Org B gives Leon info, Org C gives Leon info."

Instead:
- Org A says "this happened because of X"
- Org B says "X is a lie"
- Org C says "you are both wrong"

Leon stands in the middle. No narrator tells the reader which is right. The reader must decide.

### 4. Leon must pay a price for having many connections
Membership should carry consequences:
- Org A and Org B have conflicting interests; Leon can't help both
- Or Org A learns Leon has ties to Org B → "Who do you actually serve?"

Membership becomes character conflict, not just world-building.

### 5. Not all organizations should know Leon's full identity
- Org A knows him as an investigator
- Org B knows him as a scholar
- Org C knows him only by an alias
- Org D doesn't know he's connected to A at all

→ a network of identities. But don't let this get needlessly complicated.

### 6. Additional characters are a strong reason — but give each their own agenda
Don't create characters who exist only to "explain lore to Leon from Org X." Each character should have their own goals. They may start by helping Leon, then later find their goals conflict with his — without dramatic betrayal. They simply choose different sides at some point.

### 7. Leon doesn't have to like his organizations
Membership ≠ loyalty. Leon can be a member yet:
- distrust the leadership
- disagree with the doctrine
- use their facilities
- cooperate with certain members
- secretly investigate the organization itself

### 8. Big rule: Don't make every organization connect to the main mystery
If: Org A → Aurelius, Org B → Agreement, Org C → Order, Org D → Gear, Org E → Door — readers will conclude "all organizations are one conspiracy." The world becomes small.

Instead:
- Org A has its own purpose
- Org B has its own history
- Org C doesn't even know about the supernatural
- Org D has a conflict with B
- Org E happens to know something connected to the main mystery

Then, when two organizations ARE actually connected, the reveal is far stronger.

### 9. Makes Alderwick feel like a real world
Leon starts seeing:
- Alderwick = a small town
- then: an academic society
- then: religious circles
- then: antiquarians
- then: political connections
- then: underground groups
- then: organizations not even officially recorded

Geographically it stays a small town, but Leon realizes: this town is far bigger than it looks.

### 10. Reframe the concept
Don't think: "Leon joins many organizations to gain insight."
Think: "Leon enters different worlds, and each world gives him a reason to connect with a certain organization."

The first feels like a world-building mechanism. The second feels like a story.

## Ideal multi-organization structure

- Arc A: Leon meets an organization → outsider
- Arc B: gets limited access → associate
- Arc C: completes a recruitment challenge → member
- Arc D: conflict with the organization → questions loyalty
- Arc E: enters a different environment → second organization
- Arc F: the two organizations have conflicting interests
- Arc G: Leon must choose — and his choice need not be one of the organizations. It could be: Leon chooses himself.

## Conclusion (from discussion)

- Multiple organizations concept: 9/10
- Leon = full member of 8 organizations: NOT recommended
- Leon = network of relationships with 8 organizations, full member of only 2–3: strongly recommended
- Organizations must have their own life and purpose even when Leon is not inside them
- If this principle is applied, Alderwick gains a much wider world without constantly raising the scale of the town or the main mystery

# IDEA 3 — TREASURE HUNTING / ARC ITEM COLLECTION

## Concept

Leon develops a reputation for finding valuable items in junk shops, flea markets, and auctions — items that even the sellers don't realize are valuable. This is adapted from the "treasure hunting" concept in Hunter × Hunter (Greed Island / the general concept of finding rare items that others overlook).

The core mechanic: Leon can sense something unusual about certain objects (via his Nen ability — see ARC_Item_System_Leon.md for the full Nen framework). This ability does NOT tell him what an object is or how valuable it is. It only gives a vague indication — a feeling, a cold sensation, a sense of "wrongness" or "age." He must still use:
- knowledge
- intuition
- observation
- negotiation skills

## Why This Fits Leon

1. **Parallel with Aurelius Ashcroft** — Aurelius collected many Arc Items. Instead of *telling* the reader that Leon inherits Aurelius's traits, we *show* it: Leon has the same habit of spotting objects that others ignore. He asks "Where did you get this?" not "How much is it?" The reader slowly realizes that Aurelius didn't just leave Arc Items to Leon — Aurelius's *way of seeing the world* is also awakening in Leon.

2. **Leon becomes a "hunter" in a broader sense** — not just monsters or criminals, but information, history, secrets, Arc Items. A flea market becomes as important as a dungeon.

3. **Economic mechanic** — Leon can acquire resources from this activity. Cheap item → Arc Item → information → profit → access to new places. But he also sometimes fails (buys junk for £5 and it's genuinely junk). Failure keeps the ability from feeling like cheating.

## Signature Scene Types (adapted from various anime/manga)

### 1. "Ordinary item is extraordinary" — Hunter × Hunter
Variations:
- Leon buys a cheap painting because he notices an impossible detail
- Buys a used book whose certain pages have been deliberately cut out
- Buys a broken sword that is actually a "vessel" for an Arc Item
- Buys two different items from two different sellers, realizing they are a set
- Buys an item worthless materially but containing extremely valuable information

Key principle: **market price ≠ actual value.**

### 2. Secret auction — Hunter × Yorknew City
Leon enters a rare-item auction. Everyone thinks the prize is a 17th-century manuscript. Leon knows the manuscript is irrelevant — the ink used to write it is the real treasure. Problem: he's not the only one who knows. Multiple mysterious bidders.

Scene becomes: a game of identifying who actually knows the item's value. Leon deliberately raises the price bit by bit, watching who reacts. The auction becomes an intelligence-gathering operation.

### 3. "Don't touch that item" — Made in Abyss
Arc Items feel older than human understanding. Leon's Nen says "not dangerous." Leon still doesn't touch it. Because experience has taught him: an item that is not dangerous is not necessarily safe. Builds the sense that Arc Items have their own rules.

### 4. Item identification — Frieren
In Frieren, magical items often have odd, non-combat functions. Alderwick can do the same: Leon finds a "spoon that makes food slightly saltier." Useless in combat. Cheap. Leon buys it anyway. Why? Because it's a low-grade Arc Item once owned by someone from a certain organization, and it's a clue to that organization's history.

Arc Items don't have to be legendary swords or world-destroying rings. Absurd and mundane items make the world feel more alive.

### 5. Bargaining as a psychological battle — Death Note
Not about treasure, but about how an ordinary conversation becomes a duel of information. Leon and a seller talk casually, but both are reading each other. Seller: "This came from an old house up north." Leon: "Oh." But Leon knows the seller is lying. He then asks: "The Ashcroft house?" The seller hesitates for a fraction of a second. Leon doesn't ask again. Information already obtained.

Leon's style: he doesn't win conversations by talking a lot. He wins by making others talk.

### 6. "Treasure map" that isn't a map — One Piece
Leon buys an old map. Everyone thinks it shows a treasure location. Leon realizes: it's not a geographical map. It's an underground network map, a ritual diagram, an Arc Item position map, or a map that is only correct in a certain year. The cheap item becomes the start of a whole arc.

### 7. "Owner doesn't know the item's value" — JoJo's Bizarre Adventure
Someone sells a family heirloom: "It's useless. Been in the attic for decades." Leon's Nen reacts. But not because the item is powerful. **The item recognizes Leon.** This opens a darker possibility: certain Arc Items don't just have functions. They have *relationships* with previous owners/users. The reader immediately thinks: Aurelius?

### 8. "Treasure hunt" where all participants are deceiving each other — Moriarty the Patriot / Detective Conan
A rumour spreads: "An Arc Item belonging to a century-old sorcerer will be sold in Alderwick." Five people come searching for it. Problem: the rumour was deliberately created by someone. Leon realizes: one person is looking for the item, one is looking for the buyer, one is looking for the thief, one is actually looking for the person who came looking for the item. The treasure hunt is an information war.

### 9. "Item that shouldn't exist in this era" — Steins;Gate
Leon finds an object that is technologically or materially impossible for its supposed era. A clock with a component that shouldn't have been invented yet. He doesn't explain it. He just keeps it. Dozens of chapters later, the object turns out to be from an event that was believed never to have happened. Strong mystery novel potential.

### 10. "Fake treasure" — Fullmetal Alchemist
An Arc Item is famous: the Ashen Crown. Everyone hunts it. Leon finds "the Ashen Crown." He immediately knows it's a fake. He buys it anyway. Because: if someone made such a detailed replica, that person must know where the original is. The fake becomes a clue to the real one.

### 11. "Item collection" — Pokémon (taken seriously)
Leon builds a private collection. Each Arc Item has a different reason for being kept:
| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Old ring | Power |
| Damaged book | Information |
| Dead clock | Historical clue |
| Dull knife | Connection to Aurelius |
| Ancient coin | Access |
| Painting | Hidden map |
| Child's toy | Unknown |

Leon himself doesn't always know the actual function. This makes his collection a time bomb — an item that seems unimportant today could become critical 100 chapters later.

### 12. "Treasure Hunting Loop" — the recurring pattern for Alderwick
> Rumour → market/auction → observation → negotiation → purchase → identification → mystery → consequence

Example:
1. Leon hears a rumour about an object
2. He goes to the flea market
3. Finds 20 objects; Nen reacts vaguely to 3
4. He doesn't know which is genuinely important
5. Uses knowledge + deduction
6. Buys one at a low price
7. It IS an Arc Item
8. But when he takes it home... someone starts looking for it

Treasure hunting becomes plot.

## Important Design Rule

Don't make Leon find valuable items every time. Out of 10 items bought:
- 6 are junk
- 3 are interesting
- 1 is truly extraordinary

This lets the reader play along. Every time Leon enters a flea market, the reader thinks: "Which one this time?" And when Leon says "This one," the reader knows something is there. This could become a signature Alderwick scene — like how Hunter × Hunter readers enjoy the *process* of the hunt, not just the result.

## Deeper Mystery Layer

Leon's collection gradually becomes a mirror of Aurelius's collection. At first, the reader thinks Leon is just finding random strange objects. After enough accumulate, a pattern emerges:

> Leon is not *collecting* Arc Items.
> Someone — or something — is *directing* certain items to him.

This is a new mystery layer that could become very powerful.

# IDEA 4 — TREASURE HUNTING / ARC ITEM COLLECTION (HUNTER × HUNTER-INSPIRED)

## Concept

Leon becomes a "treasure hunter" in the Hunter × Hunter sense — using his Nen ability to find valuable items in junk markets, shops, and auctions where even the sellers don't realize the items' worth. He makes low offers, buys cheap, and only later (sometimes chapters later) is it revealed the object is an Arc Item.

This fits Leon perfectly, especially to make his resemblance to Aurelius Ashcroft felt through *behavior and story mechanics* — not merely by making Leon "a young version of Aurelius."

Aurelius kept many Arc Items. Rather than telling the reader Leon inherited Aurelius's nature, we show it:
- Leon has the same habit of seeing objects others ignore
- He reads the small writing on old objects
- He asks "Where did you get this?" not "How much is it?"
- Leon may not even know why he is drawn to certain objects — as if a pattern inherited from Aurelius lives in him

The reader slowly realizes: Aurelius didn't just leave Arc Items to Leon. Aurelius's *way of seeing the world* is beginning to live in Leon.

## Core Mechanics

### The Nen ability
We don't need to make Leon's ability a "treasure detector." More interesting if the Nen ability gives only an indication, NOT an answer.

Leon can sense:
- objects with Nen residue
- objects once used by a strong Nen user
- objects storing abnormal phenomena
- objects with a certain "discordance"
- objects with a connection to Arc history

But it does NOT tell him:
- what the object is
- how valuable it is

So Leon must still use knowledge + intuition + observation + negotiation. This keeps Leon from being too easily victorious.

### The negotiation process
The interesting part isn't just finding the item — it's the bargaining.

Flea market example:
- Seller thinks an old ring is ordinary
- No other buyer sees anything special
- Leon realizes the ring is abnormal
- He does NOT show interest
- He pretends it's ordinary
- He offers low
- The seller parts with it thinking Leon is just a cheap antique buyer

Then Leon walks away with it. The reader discovers chapters later it's an Arc Item.

### Recurring location
Make the flea market a recurring Alderwick location — not just a shopping spot. Leon visits periodically. At first readers think "Leon just likes antiques." Then he buys several strange items. Then readers begin to wonder: Leon is looking for something. But what? A certain Arc Item? An Aurelius relic? A clue about the past? Or does Leon himself not yet know what he's seeking?

One day, he finds an object even his Nen cannot read properly. That could be a major story beat.

## Benefits

1. **Leon as "hunter" in a broader sense** — not always hunting monsters, criminals, or enemies. He can hunt: information → objects → history → secrets → Arc Items. Flea markets become as important as dungeons.

2. **Aurelius parallel** — show (not tell) Leon's inherited nature.

3. **Nen gives a taste, not the answer** — keeps challenge.

4. **Signature "Leon" scenes** — minimal exposition, reader curiosity.

5. **Economic mechanic** — recurring but not too frequent; Leon gains resources; occasionally he's wrong (buys £5 junk and it's genuinely £5 junk) — keeps the ability from feeling like cheating, and adds humor/characterization.

6. **Recurring location with escalating meaning** — flea market becomes a system for exploring Alderwick lore + reinforcing the Leon–Aurelius thematic link.

> Leon isn't collecting Arc Items.
> Someone — or something — is directing certain items to him.

That becomes a very strong mystery layer.

# IDEA 5 — LEON'S ACTIVITY SYSTEM / "LIFE OUTSIDE THE MAIN PLOT"

## Concept

Borrowed from Hunter × Hunter and other anime/manga: activities that make the world feel vast and give characters a life outside the main plot. For Leon, these activities make Alderwick feel alive and let him grow without every chapter being a battle or a main-plot beat.

## Primary Activities (5 pillars)

| Activity | Function |
|----------|----------|
| Treasure Hunting | Arc Items & adventure |
| Information Hunting | Mystery & investigation |
| Exploration | World-building |
| Games/Gambling | Intelligence & Nen |
| Investigation | Main plot |

## Secondary Activities

Trading → Crafting → Archaeology → Beast Hunting → Secret Societies

---

## 1. Information Brokering — hunting information

Sometimes Leon doesn't seek objects — he seeks: a name, a location, a family's history, a rumour, a Nen user's identity, an Arc Item's origin, information deliberately erased from archives.

Key idea: information also has a price. Leon can get it cheaply by finding the right person, not by paying a lot. Example: instead of asking a nobleman, he buys tea from the old servant of a house — then listens.

Information hunting can become a signature Leon activity.

## 2. Gambling / Games

Inspired by Hunter × Hunter, Kaiji, No Game No Life, Liar Game. Not constant gambling — games with strange rules.

Example: a secret club holds a card game. Leon joins not for money — the real prize is a single piece of information about Aurelius Ashcroft. The game becomes a duel of Nen + psychology + reading opponents. Shows Leon's intelligence without forcing combat.

## 3. Exploration

A large part of Alderwick's world. Leon visits: empty houses, old churches, graves, ruins, tunnels, libraries, old family mansions, closed mines, remote villages.

Key: each place has history. Exploration is NOT "dungeon → enter → fight monster." It's: "This place was used by someone 100 years ago. Why?" — much more fitting for Alderwick's mystery tone.

## 4. Investigating Missing Persons

Great for building Leon's detective side. Example: a student disappears. Police assume he ran away. Leon finds something that doesn't fit. No big fight. Leon follows: room → letter → shop → witness → book → old location. And the small case turns out to connect to an Arc Item. Episodic cases slowly build the main plot.

## 5. Monster / Beast Hunting

If Alderwick has supernatural creatures, this can be interesting — but NOT RPG-style. More interesting if every creature has rules. Example: the creature doesn't attack humans — but only attacks people who know its name. Leon must discover: rules → weakness → origin → how to confront it. Hunting remains a mystery.

## 6. Escort / Bodyguard

Leon takes simple work: escorting someone from Alderwick to another city. Seems ordinary at first. But along the way: someone follows; the client lies; the client's baggage is suspicious; a murder at the inn; someone turns out to be a Nen user. Expands the world beyond Alderwick without requiring a huge arc.

## 7. Ruins / Archaeology

Very fitting for Aurelius. Leon is drawn to relics of the past — not as an archaeologist, but because official history often differs from what Arc Items leave behind. Example: history says a manor burned in 1760. Leon finds a hidden room. On the wall: Aurelius's name. Suddenly the history he knew becomes nonsensical.

## 8. Trading / Smuggling

An underground economy: Arc Items have a market — collectors, merchants, brokers, thieves, nobles, religious organizations, Nen users, auction houses. Leon buys in one city, sells in another. Sometimes the goal isn't money — he deliberately sells cheap to gain access or information. Makes the supernatural economy feel real.

## 9. Collecting Knowledge

Not just books. Leon keeps notes: name — symbol — location — rumour — related Arc Item — people who saw it. Over time, his notes become a private encyclopedia. One day he realizes all the information he's gathered forms a pattern. That could be a major revelation.

## 10. Training / Experimentation

Not a montage. Leon has Nen; he tries something, fails, changes conditions, tries again, finds the limit. Example: "Can Nen interact with Arc Items?" He runs small experiments. The results are strange. The experiments eventually produce a new discovery about the Nen/Arc Item system. The power system grows through the character's curiosity.

## 11. Cooking / Crafting / Repairing

Simple-sounding but strong world-building. Leon finds a damaged Arc Item. Instead of using it directly, he must find: a clockmaker, a blacksmith, a bookbinder, a glassmaker, a restoration specialist. During the repair he meets people and learns the object's history. So: crafting → character → lore — not just an RPG crafting system.

## 12. Social Clubs & Secret Societies

Very fitting for an English setting. Leon enters: gentlemen's clubs, academic societies, collector groups, spiritual associations, eccentric clubs, aristocratic circles, secret societies. Each has social rules. Leon may enter just to find one piece of information — then discovers the club has a ritual that has run for decades.

---

## Connected Loop Example

All activities can connect:

1. Leon hears a rumour about a collector → Information Hunting
2. He searches for the collector → Investigation
3. The collector owns an Arc Item → Treasure Hunting
4. To obtain it, Leon must join a secret game → Game
5. The game leads Leon to an old manor → Exploration
6. Beneath the manor lies a relic of Aurelius → Lore / Main Plot

With this pattern, we don't force every chapter into combat. Leon can live, walk around, work, play, search for information, buy goods, run experiments — and without realizing it, all these activities slowly drag him closer to Aurelius's secret.

This suits Alderwick's identity far better than making Leon a protagonist who constantly moves from battle to battle.

# IDEA 6 — POST-86 CHARACTER POOL (PROPOSED, NOT CANON)

## Design Notes

- **Status:** DEVELOPMENT / PROPOSED — NOT CANON. None of these characters exist in WORLD_BIBLE yet.
- **Naming:** All names checked against WORLD_BIBLE (existing CANON, SUPERSEDED, and PROPOSED characters) to avoid conflicts. None of these names appear in current canon.
- **Diversity:** Deliberately balanced toward more women (the story has been overwhelmingly male so far). Pool: 6 women : 4 men.
- **Function:** These characters should NOT all connect to Aurelius or the main mystery immediately. Their first purpose is to make the world feel alive. Some of them may gradually become doors to Arc Items, information hunting, exploration, auctions, and organizations.

## The Pool

### EVERYDAY CAST — make the world feel alive

1. **Theo Marsh** (M, 31) — Junk & antique dealer
   - Small roaming stall between flea markets, auctions, and temporary shops.
   - Not an Arc Item expert — buys by "if it doesn't sell today, it might sell next year."
   - Doesn't know which items are supernatural.
   - Leon visits often; Theo recognizes Leon as "the man who buys what no one else buys."
   - Light friendship.
   - Potential twist: Theo has an uncanny memory for who once owned a given object — though he doesn't know why.

2. **Winifred Calder** (F, 63) — Old book dealer
   - Shop full of used books, journals, manuscripts, maps, letters, old catalogues.
   - Knows almost all local history.
   - Principle: "I sell books. I do not sell secrets."
   - Leon often finds things in her shop. Sometimes she knows the true value, sometimes not.
   - Sometimes she deliberately pretends not to know. Leon never knows if she's just a dealer or something more.

3. **Lydia Bennington** (F, 27) — Restorer of antiques
   - Repairs clocks, paintings, furniture, jewellery, books, metal objects.
   - Perfect for Arc Item identification.
   - Dry, deadpan humor. Light chemistry with Leon.
   - Scene example: Leon brings an object. "Can it be fixed?" "Yes." "How long?" "If you don't ask why this object is bleeding, two days." Leon stays silent. "You're not going to explain?" "No." "Fine."
   - May become the first person to notice that some of Leon's objects do not obey normal material laws.

4. **Rosamund Tye** (F, 48) — Innkeeper
   - The inn is where people talk: merchants, travellers, students, soldiers, nobles, hunters, criminals.
   - Rosamund hears everything. Principle: "I don't ask where my guests come from." But she remembers everyone.
   - Leon can come just for tea and leave with a rumour ("someone was asking about the old house to the north") — the adventure begins.

### ADVENTURE CAST — enable Leon's new activities

5. **Marianne Loxley** (F, 29) — Assistant librarian / researcher
   - Calm, observant, slightly obsessive. Doesn't believe in supernatural stories — believes in documents.
   - "That house is haunted." → "Where's the proof?"
   - Hunts: land records, letters, censuses, church books, death records, newspaper archives, family papers.
   - Perfect information-hunting partner for Leon.
   - Dynamic: Leon knows the supernatural is real; Marianne believes every mystery has a historical explanation. They complement each other.

6. **Rowan Vess** (M, 35) — Informal information broker
   - Neat, polite, always carries a notebook. Not a criminal in appearance.
   - Sells information — but: "I don't sell the truth. I sell the possibility that something is true."
   - Prices are not always money: sometimes a name, a book, a location, a small favour, other information.
   - Leon and Rowan can be information rivals.
   - Scene: "What are you looking for?" "Nothing." Rowan smiles. "People who are looking for nothing don't come to me."

7. **Edmund Pike** (M, 38) — Relic hunter
   - A foil to Leon: Edmund hunts ancient objects for money; Leon searches out of curiosity.
   - Aggressive, confident, experienced. Knows the Arc Item market.
   - They can meet chasing the same object. Edmund is not necessarily a villain — sometimes rivals, sometimes allies, sometimes he sells something to Leon.
   - Recurring rival.

8. **Adeline Brume** (F, 44) — Auctioneer
   - Runs small auctions. Extremely good at reading people — a social ability almost like a Nen of its own.
   - Knows: who truly wants an item, who is pretending, who has money, who came for another purpose.
   - She finds Leon amusing: "You're bad at hiding things." "I'm not hiding anything." "That's what makes you bad."
   - Door toward larger underground auctions.

9. **Marcus Ferrand** (M, 52) — Retired investigator
   - Now lives a simple life, but keeps the cases he never solved.
   - Leon sees a file: three people disappeared 27 years ago. No evidence, no bodies, no explanation.
   - But Leon notices one detail: all three victims visited the same place.
   - "I stopped looking." "Why?" "Because I finally found the answer." "And?" "I wish I'd never found it."
   - Strong candidate for a mini-arc.

### MYSTERY CAST — toward the larger plot

10. **Ysabel Nott** (F, ~40, age unclear) — "The Collector"
    - Collects a very specific kind of object: items with no history.
    - Coins with no year. Books with no author. Paintings with no signature. Photographs with no identity. Clocks with no mechanism.
    - She believes they share one thing: "Someone deliberately erased their origin."
    - Very mysterious figure.
    - Scene: Leon offers to sell her something. She looks at it. "Where did you get this?" "A market." "No." Leon falls silent. "That object was never sold."
    - Hold her back — don't explain who she is too soon.

## Ch 87 — Suggested Character Combination

Do NOT introduce all ten at once. Start with three:

1. **Theo Marsh** → brings Leon to the flea market
2. **Marianne Loxley** → gives Leon a reason to search documents/history
3. **Ysabel Nott** → appears only briefly at the end

Suggested structure:
- Leon lives an ordinary day
- Meets Theo
- Sees an interesting object; buys it cheap
- Marianne helps identify its origin
- They find something that does not fit history
- Leon returns to the market
- Theo says: "Someone else was asking about that object."
- Leon sees Ysabel. She does nothing — only looks at Leon. Then: "I thought that object was lost long ago."

Chapter ends. No battle. No new organization. No big exposition. But the reader gains something crucial: Leon's world begins to open up.
