# System 01 — Information Control

**Status:** CORE SYSTEM | **Created:** 2026-08-29 | **Updated:** 2026-08-29 for 1816 canon
**LOTM Transfer:** Rules 1, 3, 5 (sensory grounding, information exchange, structured progression)
**Era:** 1816 Regency England

---

## A. PURPOSE

Control what information is known by whom, when it is revealed, and how revelations recontextualize earlier events. Maintain reader trust through fair information handling in a 1816 information environment where information travels slowly, is physically anchored in documents, and is accessible only through personal effort.

## B. CORE PRINCIPLES

1. **Information has layers.** What is true differs from what any character knows differs from what the reader knows.
2. **Every revelation must recontextualize something.** Information should change interpretation, not just add facts.
3. **Withholding is not mystery.** Withholding without purpose creates confusion, not intrigue.
4. **Reader fairness first.** The reader should always have enough information to follow the scene, even if they don't have ALL the information.
5. **Information is physically anchored in 1816.** Every piece of knowledge must be traceable to a letter, document, conversation, or observation. No instant retrieval.

## C. MECHANISMS

### Information State Model

```
FACT (what is objectively true in the story world)
  -> KNOWLEDGE HOLDER (who knows this fact)
  -> BELIEF (what does the character think is true)
  -> EVIDENCE (what physical/documentary proof exists)
  -> UNCERTAINTY (what is plausibly true but unconfirmed)
  -> REVEAL (when and how information becomes known)
  -> RECONTEXTUALIZATION (how earlier events change meaning)
```

### Information Layers

For every major plot point, track:

| Layer | Question |
|-------|----------|
| Objective Fact | What actually happened? |
| Leon's Knowledge | What does Leon know? |
| Leon's Belief | What does Leon think is happening? |
| Other Characters' Knowledge | What do others know that Leon doesn't? |
| Reader's Knowledge | What has been revealed to the reader? |
| Intentionally Withheld | What is the author keeping back, and why? |

## D. OPERATIONAL RULES

1. **Before each chapter, define the information state:** What does each key character know? What does the reader know?
2. **Information reveals must have a mechanism:** A document found, a conversation had, an observation made — never author convenience.
3. **Information travels at 1816 speed.** Letters take 1-3 days to reach a provincial town. Characters cannot instantly know what happens in another part of town.
4. **Knowledge must have a physical source.** Letters, documents, personal observation, conversation. No "characters just know things."
5. **When Leon learns something new, show HOW he learns it.** The mechanism matters as much as the information.
6. **When information changes interpretation, explicitly connect it to the earlier scene.** The recontextualization must be visible.
7. **Physical evidence carries more narrative weight than spoken claims.** A letter, deed, diary entry > gossip.
8. **Track what Leon suspects separately from what Leon knows.** Suspicion is a narrative tool; confusion is a failure mode.

## E. WHEN TO USE

- Every chapter with investigation elements
- Every scene where characters have different knowledge
- Every time a document, artifact, or clue is introduced
- Every revelation or partial revelation
- Every scene where Leon misunderstands something

## F. WHEN NOT TO USE

- Simple transitional scenes with no information change
- Scenes focused purely on atmosphere or relationship
- Pacing-rest scenes between intense investigative sequences

## G. FAILURE MODES

| Failure | Sign | Fix |
|---------|------|-----|
| Arbitrary author secrecy | Reader cannot guess what's hidden because it was never hinted at | Plant subtle foreshadowing before withholding |
| Implausible concealment | Character acts ignorant about something they should know | Character knows but has a reason not to reveal |
| Reader confusion without payoff | Chapter ends with unresolved confusion that never resolves | Every mystery planted must resolve or deepen purposefully |
| Information dumping | Character explains everything the reader should discover | Break into multiple discovery events |
| Modern information speed | Characters know things instantly, no research needed | Use letter, visit, or gossip — not instant knowledge |

## I. Alderwick-SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATION (1816)

- **Primary information channels:** Letters (Royal Mail), personal visits, parish records at St. Bartholomew's Church, library research, conversation at Margaret's cafe, Arthur's shop
- **No telephone.** Leon must visit in person or send a letter.
- **No instant digital search.** Leon must PHYSICALLY visit library, archive, vicar, solicitor.
- **Pembroke & Clarke letter** is the master information anchor — physical document, first catalyst.
- **Leon's notebook** is the PRIMARY mechanism for tracking what he knows — also serves as information management tool.
- **Gossip network** at Margaret's cafe is how town information spreads informally.
- **Newspapers** arrive weekly — Leon reads them for current events and local notices.
- **Parish records** at St. Bartholomew's Church (per World Foundation v2, NOT St. Mary's).
- **Solicitor records** for property deeds, wills, transfers.
- **Arthur's shop** is a secondary information source — antique objects carry historical information.
- **Card catalogs / index books** — library research uses physical systems, not digital search.
- Information gaps (what Leon doesn't know) are WHERE THE MYSTERY LIVES.
- Helen always knows more than she reveals — this is her defining trait.

## J. CROSS-SYSTEM DEPENDENCIES

- **Mystery Engine** — Information reveals drive mystery progression
- **Foreshadowing** — Withheld information must have foreshadowing before reveal
- **Reader Knowledge** — Direct tracking of reader vs character knowledge
- **Character Depth** — What characters know shapes their behavior
- **Continuity** — Information state must be tracked across chapters
