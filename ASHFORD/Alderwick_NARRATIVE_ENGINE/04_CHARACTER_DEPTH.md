# System 04 — Character Depth

**Status:** CORE SYSTEM | **Created:** 2026-08-29
**LOTM Transfer:** Rules 1, 2, 7, 8 (grounded detail, progression, concealed identity, competence through analysis)
**Evidence Confidence:** 9/10 (excellent character profile support)

---

## A. PURPOSE

Create multidimensional characters revealed through action, dialogue, thought, choices, pressure, relationships, and consequences — NOT through backstory dumps.

## B. CORE PRINCIPLES

1. **Characters are revealed through behavior under pressure, not through exposition.**
2. **Contradiction creates depth.** Characters who are only one thing are flat.
3. **Backstory is not character depth.** Depth comes from present-tense behavior, not past-tense biography.
4. **Characters should surprise us in consistent ways.** Their surprises must feel true to who they are.

## C. CHARACTER DEPTH DIMENSIONS

Track each major character across:

| Dimension | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| Desire | What does this character actively want? |
| Fear | What does this character actively avoid? |
| Values | What would this character sacrifice for? |
| Contradiction | What does this character want that conflicts with their values? |
| Coping Behavior | How does this character handle stress? |
| Social Mask | How does this character present to the world? |
| Private Self | Who is this character alone? |
| Relationships | How does this character behave with different people? |
| Decision Patterns | When forced to choose, what does this character typically do? |
| Vulnerabilities | What can wound this character emotionally? |
| Competence | What is this character genuinely good at? |
| Blind Spots | What does this character refuse to see? |
| Emotional Consequences | What does this character feel most acutely? |

## D. OPERATIONAL RULES

1. **Reveal characters through action first, dialogue second, thought third.** What they do > what they say > what they think.
2. **Every character interaction must change the relationship state slightly.** Even small interactions shift dynamics.
3. **Characters must make choices under pressure.** Their choices reveal more than their words.
4. **No character should be fully understood by the reader early.** Mystery about character depth IS character depth.
5. **Characters should contradict themselves.** People say one thing and do another — especially under stress.

## E. WHEN TO USE

- Every character introduction
- Every scene with significant character interactions
- Every moment of decision or pressure
- Every relationship development

## F. WHEN NOT TO USE

- Background character interactions (they don't need depth)
- Transitional scenes with no character focus
- Pacing-release scenes

## G. FAILURE MODES

| Failure | Sign | Fix |
|---------|------|-----|
| Backstory dump | Character reveals their past through monologue | Break into multiple revealed moments |
| Single-dimensional character | Character only has one trait | Add a contradiction |
| Inconsistent behavior | Character acts differently without reason | Establish the reason or revise the behavior |
| Over-explanation | Character explains their motivations | Show through behavior instead |

## H. QA QUESTIONS

- [ ] Can I name each character's desire, fear, and contradiction?
- [ ] Does each character behave differently under pressure?
- [ ] Are characters revealed through actions, not just words?
- [ ] Does each character have at least one blind spot?
- [ ] Does this character's behavior change across the story?

## I. Alderwick-SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATION

- **Leon:** Desires truth, fears being misled, contradicts by being both cautious and curious, copes through documentation, social mask is polite distance, private self is deeply invested in understanding, decision pattern is methodical analysis
- **Helen:** Desires protection/control, fears exposure, contradicts by wanting Leon to stay while warning him away, social mask is measured courtesy, private self is deeply protective, coping behavior is watchfulness
- **Arthur:** Historical knowledge is his competence, his blind spot may be his own family connection to Ashcroft
- **Daniel:** Academic knowledge is his value, his position between Leon and the town is structural
- **Thomas Wren (deceased):** His death is a character reveal through absence — what he knew, how he died, why he left the key
- **Aurelius (deceased):** His character is revealed through documents, properties, and others' memories — never directly

## J. CROSS-SYSTEM DEPENDENCIES

- **Dialogue & Subtext** — Character depth shapes how characters speak
- **Scene Construction** — Characters are revealed through scenes
- **Character Progression** — Depth creates the baseline from which characters grow
- **Reader Knowledge** — What we know about characters shapes reader expectations
- **Continuity** — Character behavior must be consistent over time
