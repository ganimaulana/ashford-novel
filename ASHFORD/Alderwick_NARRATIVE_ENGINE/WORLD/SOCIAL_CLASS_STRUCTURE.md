# SOCIAL CLASS STRUCTURE — Alderwick Society (1816)

**Status:** 📙 PROPOSED | **Created:** 2026-08-29
**Purpose:** Define social class hierarchy, mobility, families, occupations, income, and status markers in Alderwick
**Principle:** Class is FUNDAMENTAL to 1816 society. Everyone knows their place. Mobility is rare but possible. Status anxiety is constant.

---

## CORE PREMISE

**1816 England is a CLASS SOCIETY.**

- **Birth determines status** — "gentility" is inherited, not earned
- **Wealth ≠ Status** — rich merchant < poor gentleman
- **Occupation matters** — "trade" disqualifies from gentry status
- **Visible markers** — clothing, speech, housing, servants signal class
- **Rigid but porous** — rare individuals cross class boundaries

## SECTION 1 — CLASS HIERARCHY

### TIER 1: LANDED GENTRY

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE + PROPOSED

**Definition:**
"Gentry" = families who live entirely on **landed income** (rents from estates) without working.

**Alderwick Population:** 10-15 families (out of 3,000-5,000 total population)

**Sub-Categories:**

**A. Baronets/Knights** (titled gentry)
- Hereditary title (Sir + surname)
- Largest estates (1,000+ acres)
- County-level prominence
- May NOT reside in Alderwick full-time (county town or London)

**B. Esquires** (senior gentry)
- Armigerous (entitled to coat of arms)
- Large estates (300-1,000 acres)
- Magistrates, High Sheriff, Deputy Lieutenants
- Alderwick magistrates likely come from this tier

**C. Gentlemen** (junior gentry)
- Landed income but smaller estates (100-300 acres)
- No title, but recognized gentility
- Vestry members, social leadership

**Income Range:**
- **£300-£5,000+ per year** (from rents)
- Top tier: £2,000+ (very wealthy)
- Middle tier: £500-£2,000 (comfortable)
- Lower tier: £300-£500 ("shabby genteel")

**Status Markers:**
- **No manual labor** — work disqualifies from gentry
- **Servants** — minimum 3-5; elite have 10-20+
- **Horses/carriages** — essential for status
- **Country house** — estate outside Alderwick proper
- **Education** — sons attend university (Oxford/Cambridge) or military/church
- **Speech** — "received" pronunciation, classical education evident
- **Dress** — tailored clothing, updated fashions (1-2 years behind London)
- **Leisure** — hunting, assemblies, visiting, reading

**Occupations (acceptable for gentry):**
- **None (live on rents)** — ideal
- **Church (clergy)** — younger sons become rectors/vicars
- **Military (officers)** — purchase commissions
- **Law (barrister)** — NOT solicitor (too much "trade")
- **Land management** — managing own estates acceptable

### TIER 2: PSEUDO-GENTRY & PROFESSIONALS

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Definition:**
Families with **genteel pretensions** but lacking full gentry status. The **ambiguous middle**.

**Alderwick Population:** 30-50 families

**Categories:**

**A. Clergy (Church of England)**
- **Rectors** — often younger sons of gentry; high income (£300-£800/year from tithes)
- **Vicars** — lower income (£100-£300/year); may be genteel or rising
- **Curates** — poorly paid (£50-£100/year); often not genteel

**B. Professional Men**
- **Physicians** — university-educated; income £200-£1,000/year; claim gentility
- **Barristers** — rare in small towns; genteel status
- **Solicitors** — ambiguous status; income £150-£500/year; "trade" taint
- **Apothecaries** — NOT genteel (hands-on medical work); income £100-£300/year

**C. Military Officers (half-pay/retired)**
- Former army/navy officers on half-pay
- Income £60-£200/year (insufficient to maintain status)
- Genteel background but economically struggling

**D. Large Tenant Farmers (Yeomen)**
- Farm 200-500 acres (leasehold)
- Income £150-£500/year
- Aspire to gentility (educate sons, avoid manual labor)
- NOT accepted as gentry (work the land)

**Income Range:** £100-£1,000/year

**Status Markers:**
- **Education** — sons may attend grammar school or university
- **Servants** — 1-3 (often just a maid-of-all-work)
- **Dress** — genteel but outdated fashions
- **Speech** — educated accent, some classical knowledge
- **Housing** — substantial house in Alderwick or farmhouse
- **Leisure** — reading, music, assemblies (if invited)

**Tensions:**
- **Claim gentility but not fully accepted** by Tier 1
- **Resent exclusion** from magistracy, vestry leadership
- **Ambiguous social position** — too genteel for trade, too poor for gentry

### TIER 3: MIDDLING SORT (RESPECTABLE TRADE)

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Definition:**
Prosperous **tradesmen, shopkeepers, master artisans, small farmers** — respectable but NOT genteel.

**Alderwick Population:** 150-250 families

**Categories:**

**A. Substantial Merchants**
- Grain dealers, wool factors, coal merchants
- Income £100-£400/year
- Employ clerks/laborers
- Aspire to educate sons out of trade

**B. Shopkeepers**
- Drapers, grocers, ironmongers, booksellers
- Income £60-£200/year
- Own shop + living quarters above
- Social leaders within middling sort

**C. Master Artisans**
- Employ journeymen/apprentices
- Carpenters, masons, tailors, shoemakers, blacksmiths
- Income £50-£150/year
- Own tools, workshop

**D. Innkeepers/Publicans**
- Income £60-£200/year
- Social hubs (gossip, politics)
- Ambiguous status (alcohol trade)

**E. Small Tenant Farmers**
- Farm 50-200 acres
- Income £50-£150/year
- Work alongside laborers (disqualifies from gentility)

**F. Surveyors, Engineers, Land Agents**
- Technical expertise
- Income £80-£200/year
- Rising profession (Industrial Revolution)

**Income Range:** £50-£400/year

**Status Markers:**
- **Literacy** — read, write, basic arithmetic
- **Clothing** — respectable but plain; Sunday best for church
- **Housing** — modest house or rooms above shop
- **Servants** — rare; may employ shop assistant or maid
- **Church attendance** — regular (social expectation)
- **Voting rights** — NONE (no property qualification)

**Characteristics:**
- **Respectable but not genteel**
- **Pay heavy poor rates and church rates**
- **Politically excluded** (no votes, no vestry)
- **Resent gentry privilege** but fear working-class radicalism
- **Aspire upward** — save to purchase land, educate sons

**Dissent:**
- Many middling sort are **Dissenters** (Methodists, Baptists, Quakers)
- Resent church rates (forced to fund Anglican church)
- Form alternative social networks (chapel, meeting house)

### TIER 4: WORKING CLASS (LABORING POOR)

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Definition:**
**Wage laborers** — survive by selling labor, own little/no property.

**Alderwick Population:** 800-1,500 families (majority of population)

**Categories:**

**A. Artisans (Skilled Workers)**
- Journeymen (work for master artisans)
- Weavers, carpenters, masons, tailors, shoemakers
- Income £30-£60/year (when employed)
- Some literacy (read, basic writing)
- "Aristocracy of labor"

**B. Laborers (Unskilled)**
- General laborers, porters, carters
- Agricultural laborers (harvest workers)
- Income £20-£40/year
- Mostly illiterate
- Insecure employment (day labor)

**C. Servants (Domestic)**
- Maids, footmen, cooks, grooms, stable hands
- Income £5-£20/year + room & board
- Live in employer''s house
- Better off than outdoor laborers (food, shelter)

**D. Miners, Factory Workers** (if applicable to Alderwick)
- Dangerous, low-paid work
- Income £25-£50/year
- May be children (age 8+)

**Income Range:** £5-£60/year

**Status Markers:**
- **Literacy** — varies; artisans may read; laborers mostly illiterate
- **Clothing** — patched, worn; one set for work, one for Sunday
- **Housing** — crowded rooms, shared housing, cellars
- **Diet** — bread, potatoes, occasional meat; hunger common
- **Church attendance** — irregular (work demands, no respectable clothing)

**Characteristics:**
- **Live paycheck to paycheck**
- **Vulnerable to unemployment, illness, injury**
- **No political power** (no votes, no representation)
- **Subject to poor law** (outdoor relief or workhouse)
- **Some radicalism** — artisans join reform movements

### TIER 5: PAUPERS (DESTITUTE)

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Definition:**
Cannot support themselves; **dependent on poor relief**.

**Alderwick Population:** 200-500 individuals (fluctuates with economic crisis)

**Categories:**

**A. Outdoor Relief Recipients**
- Elderly, disabled, widows with children
- Receive weekly dole (bread, coal, small cash)
- Live in own homes (if any)

**B. Workhouse Inmates** (if Alderwick has workhouse)
- Able-bodied poor (refuse outdoor relief → workhouse)
- Orphans, abandoned children
- Insane, chronically ill
- Forced labor, harsh discipline

**C. Vagrants**
- Homeless, wandering poor
- Criminalized (vagrancy laws)
- Moved on by constable or sent to house of correction

**Income:** £0-£5/year (poor relief only)

**Status Markers:**
- **No political rights**
- **Social stigma** — "undeserving poor" vs. "deserving poor"
- **Clothing** — rags; workhouse uniform (badge of shame)
- **Housing** — workhouse, poorhouse, streets

**Characteristics:**
- **Complete dependence** on vestry
- **Vulnerable to abuse** (workhouse cruelty, removal to other parish)
- **No social power**
- **1816 crisis** — numbers swell due to unemployment, harvest failure

## SECTION 2 — SOCIAL MOBILITY

### A. UPWARD MOBILITY (RARE BUT POSSIBLE)

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Mechanisms:**

**1. Purchase Land**
- Wealthy merchant/professional buys estate
- After 2-3 generations, family accepted as gentry
- **Example:** Jane Austen''s Mr. Bingley (new money buying into gentry)

**2. Marry Up**
- Heiress marries poor gentleman (her money + his status)
- Gentleman''s daughter marries wealthy merchant (his status + her money)
- **More common for women** (men preserve status through marriage)

**3. Education**
- Son of tradesman → university → clergy/medicine → pseudo-gentry
- Rare (expensive, requires patron)

**4. Military Success**
- Rare commoner rises to officer through merit (very rare)
- Officer on half-pay marries heiress

**5. Patronage**
- Gentry patron sponsors talented youth
- Rare, requires exceptional merit + luck

**Barriers:**
- **Expensive** — land costs thousands of pounds
- **Time** — takes generations to "wash off trade"
- **Social rejection** — old gentry snub new money
- **Occupation** — must stop working (live on rents only)

---

### B. DOWNWARD MOBILITY (MORE COMMON)

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Mechanisms:**

**1. Financial Ruin**
- Gambling debts
- Bad investments
- Estate mortgaged, foreclosed
- Family loses land → loses gentry status

**2. Scandal**
- Sexual scandal (illegitimate child, adultery)
- Criminal conviction
- Social ostracism → economic ruin

**3. No Heir**
- Eldest son dies → estate divided or sold
- Daughters inherit → must marry or lose status

**4. "Trade"**
- Gentleman''s son enters trade (desperate for income)
- Family loses genteel status

**5. Demographic Pressure**
- Too many sons → inheritance divided
- Younger sons without inheritance → clergy, military, or downward

**Result:**
- **"Shabby genteel"** — gentility without money
- **Pseudo-gentry** — live like gentry but must work
- **Fall into middle/working class**

## SECTION 3 — STATUS MARKERS & ETIQUETTE

### A. VISIBLE CLASS MARKERS

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**1. Clothing:**

| Class | Men | Women |
|-------|-----|-------|
| **Gentry** | Tailored coats, breeches/pantaloons, top hat, gloves, watch | Silk/muslin gowns, spencer/pelisse, bonnets, jewelry, gloves |
| **Professional** | Wool coats, less tailored, simpler fabrics | Cotton gowns, simpler bonnets, minimal jewelry |
| **Middling Sort** | Sturdy work clothes, Sunday best (plain coat) | Plain dresses, aprons for work, simple bonnets |
| **Working Class** | Smock, patched clothing, work boots | Patched dresses, shawls, no bonnets (or simple cap) |
| **Paupers** | Rags, workhouse uniform | Rags, charity clothing |

**2. Speech:**
- **Gentry:** "Received" pronunciation, classical references, French phrases, formal grammar
- **Professional:** Educated accent, formal grammar, some Latin
- **Middling Sort:** Regional accent (mild), basic grammar
- **Working Class:** Strong regional dialect, informal grammar, slang
- **Paupers:** Often illiterate (speech only)

**3. Housing:**
- **Gentry:** Country estates (5-20+ rooms), servants' quarters, stables, gardens
- **Professional:** Substantial house (4-8 rooms) in Alderwick center
- **Middling Sort:** Modest house or shop+living quarters (2-4 rooms)
- **Working Class:** 1-2 rooms, shared housing, crowded
- **Paupers:** Workhouse, cellar, streets

**4. Transportation:**
- **Gentry:** Private carriage, multiple horses
- **Professional:** Horse, occasionally hire chaise
- **Middling Sort:** Walk, occasional cart
- **Working Class:** Walk
- **Paupers:** Walk (or removed by cart to parish of settlement)

**5. Servants:**
- **Gentry:** 5-20+ servants (butler, housekeeper, maids, footmen, grooms, cook)
- **Professional:** 1-3 servants (maid, cook)
- **Middling Sort:** Occasional hired help
- **Working Class:** NONE (they ARE the servants)
- **Paupers:** NONE

**6. Literacy:**
- **Gentry:** Universal (boys and girls educated at home or school)
- **Professional:** Universal
- **Middling Sort:** 60-80% (men higher than women)
- **Working Class:** 30-50% (artisans higher; laborers lower)
- **Paupers:** 10-20%

### B. SOCIAL ETIQUETTE

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**1. Introductions:**
- **Lower rank introduced to higher rank** (never reverse)
- **Men introduced to women** (women have precedence)
- **Younger introduced to older**
- **No self-introduction** at gentry events (requires intermediary)

**2. Forms of Address:**
- **Baronet:** "Sir [First Name]" (e.g., Sir Thomas)
- **Baronet's wife:** "Lady [Surname]" (e.g., Lady Ashcroft)
- **Esquire/Gentleman:** "Mr. [Surname]"
- **Professional/Middling:** "Mr. [Surname]"
- **Clergy:** "Mr. [Surname]" (rector/vicar); "Reverend Mr. [Surname]" (formal)
- **Working Class:** First name or surname only (no "Mr.")

**3. Calling/Visiting:**
- **Gentry call on equals** (formal social ritual)
- **Lower rank may NOT call uninvited** (must wait for invitation)
- **Calling hours:** 3-5 PM (afternoon)
- **Leave calling card** if person not at home

**4. Dining:**
- **Gentry dine at 5-7 PM** ("dinner" = main meal)
- **Working class dine at noon** ("dinner" = midday meal)
- **Seating by rank** (host/hostess at ends; highest rank closest)
- **Servants serve** (gentry never serve themselves)

**5. Church Seating:**
- **Pews assigned by status** (front = gentry; back = poor)
- **Gentry own/rent pews** (exclusive right)
- **Working class stand or sit on benches** (back of church)
