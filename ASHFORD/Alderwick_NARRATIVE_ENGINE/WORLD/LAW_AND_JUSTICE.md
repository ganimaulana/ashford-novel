# LAW AND JUSTICE — Alderwick Legal System (1816)

**Status:** 📙 PROPOSED | **Created:** 2026-08-29
**Purpose:** Define court procedures, law enforcement, evidence, punishment, and legal rights in 1816 Alderwick
**Principle:** Justice is LOCAL, CLASS-BIASED, and HARSH. Law enforcement is minimal. Supernatural crimes fall outside legal framework.

---

## CORE PREMISE

**1816 English law is:**
- **Enforced locally** by magistrates, constables (no professional police)
- **Class-biased** — gentry rarely prosecuted; poor harshly punished
- **Bloody Code** — 200+ capital offenses (death penalty)
- **No presumption of innocence** — accused must prove innocence
- **Public spectacle** — trials, executions are community events

**Leon operates OUTSIDE official legal system** — investigates crimes magistrates ignore/cover up.

- **Try petty crimes** (summary jurisdiction — no jury)
- **Commit serious crimes to trial** (Quarter Sessions or Assizes)
- **Issue warrants** (arrest, search)
- **Bind over** (require bail/bond for good behavior)
- **Settle disputes** (arbitration)
- **Enforce poor law, licensing, labor regulations**

**Alderwick Magistrates (PROPOSED):**

**Limitations:**
- **No legal training** — gentry amateurs, not professionals
- **Class bias** — protect their own class

## SECTION 2 — COURTS & TRIALS

### A. PETTY SESSIONS (MAGISTRATE’S COURT)

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Jurisdiction:** Petty offenses (summary jurisdiction)

**Crimes:**
- Drunk & disorderly
- Vagrancy
- Petty theft (under 5 shillings)
- Assault (minor)
- Poaching (first offense)
- Poor law violations

**Procedure:**
- **No jury** — magistrate decides guilt/innocence
- **Informal** — no lawyers, no formal rules of evidence
- **Quick** — minutes per case
- **Public** — held in magistrate’s home, inn, or guildhall

**Punishments:**
- Fine (5s – £5)
- Whipping (10-39 lashes)
- Stocks/pillory (hours)
- House of correction (days to months)
- Bind over (bail for good behavior)

**Plot Implications:**
- **Arbitrary justice** — magistrate’s mood determines outcome
- **Class bias** — gentry never appear; poor always guilty
- **No appeal** (magistrate’s decision final)
- **Leon witnesses injustice** (motivates unofficial investigation)

### B. QUARTER SESSIONS

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE + PROPOSED

**Jurisdiction:** Serious crimes (not capital)

**Crimes:**
- Grand larceny (theft £5+)
- Burglary (if not capital)
- Assault (serious)
- Riot
- Fraud
- Poaching (repeat offense)

**Frequency:** Four times per year (hence "Quarter")

**Location:** County town (UNKNOWN — 20-40 miles from Alderwick?)

**Judges:** **Bench of magistrates** (3-12 JPs sit together)

**Procedure:**
- **Grand jury** (gentry) decides if case proceeds ("true bill")
- **Petty jury** (freeholders) hears evidence, decides guilt
- **Lawyers** may appear (but most defendants unrepresented)
- **Formal** — follows legal procedure
- **Public** — spectators, press attend

**Punishments:**
- Transportation (7-14 years to Australia)
- Whipping
- Prison (months to years)
- Fine

**Timeline:**
- Crime in Alderwick → magistrate commits to Quarter Sessions
- Wait 1-3 months for next session
- Trial (1-2 days)
- Sentence immediate

**Plot Implications:**
- **Long wait** — accused in jail for months before trial
- **County politics** — bench of magistrates includes Alderwick rivals
- **Leon travels to county town** (investigate, testify)
- **Grand jury nullification** — refuses to indict gentry

## SECTION 3 — LEGAL PROCEDURE

### A. ARREST

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**With Warrant:**
- Magistrate issues warrant (name of suspect, crime)
- Constable arrests suspect
- Brings before magistrate (within 24 hours)

**Without Warrant:**
- Constable (or any person) can arrest if:
  - Crime committed in their presence
  - "Hue and cry" (fleeing felon)
  - Breach of peace

**Citizen’s Arrest:**
- Any person can arrest felon caught in act
- Must deliver to constable/magistrate immediately

**Plot Implications:**
- **False arrest** — constable arrests wrong person
- **Unlawful arrest** — magistrate abuses warrant power
- **Leon arrests suspect** — citizen’s arrest (risky)
- **Escape from custody** — constable incompetent

---

### B. BAIL

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Magistrate decides:**
- Release on bail (accused posts bond, promises to appear)
- Remand in custody (jail until trial)

**Factors:**
- **Class** — gentry almost always bailed; poor almost never
- **Crime severity** — capital crimes usually no bail
- **Flight risk**

**Bail conditions:**
- Accused posts money (forfeited if flees)
- Sureties (friends/family guarantee appearance)

**Plot Implications:**
- **Class injustice** — rich buy freedom, poor rot in jail
- **Flight** — bailed accused disappears
- **Bail denied** — innocent person jailed for months
- **Leon posts bail** (for wrongly accused?)

---

### C. EVIDENCE

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Types of Evidence:**

**1. Witness Testimony:**
- Most important evidence
- **Competency rules** — convicted felons, atheists cannot testify
- **Accused cannot testify** (until 1898 reform)
- **Spouse cannot testify against spouse**

**2. Physical Evidence:**
- Stolen goods
- Weapons
- Clothing (bloodstains, tears)
- Documents (forged notes, letters)

**3. Circumstantial Evidence:**
- Motive, opportunity, suspicious behavior
- Often sufficient for conviction

**4. Confession:**
- Highly valued (even if coerced)
- **No exclusionary rule** — forced confessions admissible

**No Forensic Science:**
- No fingerprints, DNA, ballistics, toxicology
- Blood type unknown (all blood looks same)
- Poison detection primitive (taste, smell, symptoms)

**Plot Implications:**
- **Leon’s investigative advantage** — uses methods unknown to law
- **Medical expertise** — Leon can detect poison, determine time of death
- **Circumstantial conviction** — innocent hanged on suspicion
- **False confession** — coerced by magistrate/constable

---

### D. TRIAL PROCEDURE

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**1. Indictment:**
- Grand jury hears prosecution case only
- Decides "true bill" (proceed) or "ignoramus" (dismiss)
- **Gentry-dominated** — protects their own

**2. Arraignment:**
- Accused brought to court, asked to plead
- **Guilty** — sentence immediate
- **Not guilty** — trial proceeds
- **Mute** (refuses to plead) — peine forte et dure (torture abolished 1772; now automatic "not guilty")

**3. Trial:**
- **Prosecution** presents evidence first
- **Defense** responds (if any — most defendants unrepresented)
- **Accused cannot testify** (can give unsworn statement)
- **Judge sums up** (often biased toward conviction)
- **Jury deliberates** (no retirement — decides in courtroom)
- **Verdict** (guilty or not guilty)

**4. Sentence:**
- Immediate (no delay)
- Judge has discretion (within statutory limits)
- **No appeal** (until 19th century reforms)

**Plot Implications:**
- **Rushed trials** — 10-30 minutes for capital case
- **No defense** — poor defendants have no lawyer
- **Jury nullification** — refuses to convict despite evidence (especially if death penalty)
- **Judge bias** — directs jury toward conviction
- **Leon must prevent wrongful conviction** — no appeal possible

## SECTION 4 — PUNISHMENTS

### A. CAPITAL PUNISHMENT (DEATH)

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Crimes:** 200+ offenses ("Bloody Code")

**Common capital crimes:**
- Murder, rape, treason, arson
- Burglary, robbery, forgery
- Theft over 40s (from house), over 5s (from person)
- Poaching deer
- Cutting down trees
- Impersonating Chelsea pensioner

**Method:** Hanging (public)

**Procedure:**
- Condemned held in jail (days to weeks)
- Public execution (market day, crowd of hundreds/thousands)
- Chaplain attends (final confession)
- Body hangs 1 hour (ensure death)
- **Afterwards:** Burial, or dissection (Murder Act 1752 — murderers’ bodies given to surgeons)

**Commutation:**
- Judge can commute to transportation
- Royal pardon (rare)
- Pregnancy ("pleading the belly" — execution delayed until birth)

**Plot Implications:**
- **Public spectacle** — moral lesson, entertainment
- **Wrongful execution** — Leon must prevent
- **Body snatching** — surgeons buy executed criminals
- **Execution ghost** — supernatural consequence

### D. IMPRISONMENT

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Types:**

**1. County Jail (awaiting trial):**
- Hold accused before trial
- No separation (men/women, guilty/innocent, debtors/criminals together)
- Filthy, disease-ridden
- Jailer charges fees (room, food, removal of chains)
- Poor prisoners starve if family cannot bring food

**2. House of Correction (short sentences):**
- Petty offenses (vagrancy, disorderly conduct)
- Hard labor (oakum picking, treadmill, stone breaking)
- Weeks to months

**3. Prison (serious non-capital crimes):**
- Rare (transportation more common)
- Months to years
- Hard labor

**4. Debtors' Prison:**
- Civil debt (not criminal)
- Held until debt paid
- Must pay for own keep (paradox: imprisoned = cannot work = cannot pay)

**Conditions:**
- Overcrowded, filthy, disease
- No sanitation
- Typhus, cholera epidemics
- Prisoners must pay for food, bedding, candles
- Sexual violence, bullying
- Jailer corruption (bribes for better conditions)

**Plot Implications:**
- **Prison as death sentence** (disease kills before trial)
- **Wrongful imprisonment** (innocent suffers for months)
- **Jail break** (desperate escape)
- **Prison corruption** (jailer takes bribes, smuggles)
- **Supernatural in prison** (haunted jail, possessed prisoner)

## SECTION 5 — LEGAL RIGHTS (OR LACK THEREOF)

### A. RIGHTS OF THE ACCUSED

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Limited protections:**

**1. Habeas Corpus:**
- Cannot be held indefinitely without charge
- **BUT:** Suspended 1817-1818 (after Spa Fields Riot)
- Government can imprison without trial during suspension

**2. Trial by Jury:**
- Right to jury trial (for serious crimes)
- **BUT:** Grand jury can refuse to indict (protects gentry)
- **BUT:** Jury intimidation common

**3. No Torture:**
- Officially abolished
- **BUT:** Coerced confessions admissible
- **BUT:** Harsh prison conditions = de facto torture

**Rights DENIED:**

**1. Right to Silence:**
- No protection
- Refusal to answer = presumption of guilt

**2. Right to Testify:**
- Accused CANNOT testify under oath (until 1898)
- Can make unsworn statement (less credible)

**3. Right to Counsel:**
- No right to lawyer (until 1836)
- Can hire lawyer (if wealthy)
- Poor defendants unrepresented

**4. Presumption of Innocence:**
- Officially exists
- **Reality:** Accused must prove innocence

**5. Protection from Self-Incrimination:**
- Weak (no Miranda-style warnings)
- Magistrates coerce confessions

**Plot Implications:**
- **No protection for accused** (Leon must intervene)
- **Class injustice** (rich hire lawyers; poor die)
- **Coerced confessions** (innocent confess under pressure)
- **Wrongful conviction epidemic**

**Gentry:**
- Rarely arrested (magistrates protect their own)
- Always bailed (if arrested)
- Can hire best lawyers

**Middle Class:**
- Sometimes prosecuted

- Frequently prosecuted

**Paupers:**
- Criminalized for poverty (vagrancy)
- No bail, no lawyer, no mercy
- Maximum sentences

**Plot Implications:**
- **Two-tier justice** (rich escape, poor die)
