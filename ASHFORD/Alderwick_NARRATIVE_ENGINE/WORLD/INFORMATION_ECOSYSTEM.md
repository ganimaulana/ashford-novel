# INFORMATION ECOSYSTEM — Alderwick 1816

**Status:** 📙 PROPOSED | **Created:** 2026-08-29
**Purpose:** Define how information flows in Alderwick through letters, newspapers, gossip, rumors, and social networks
**Principle:** Information is POWER. Who knows what, when, and how determines plot dynamics. Speed and accuracy vary by class.

---

## CORE PREMISE

**Information in 1816 is:**
- **SLOW** (no telegraph, no phone, no internet)
- **FILTERED** (by class, literacy, access)
- **UNRELIABLE** (gossip, rumor, propaganda)
- **COSTLY** (newspapers, letters expensive)
- **STRATIFIED** (gentry has access poor lack)

**Information flow creates narrative opportunity:**
- **Delays** (Leon learns crucial fact too late)
- **Misinformation** (false rumor derails investigation)
- **Class barriers** (Leon cannot access working-class gossip network)
- **Secrets** (information asymmetry = power)

## SECTION 1 — FORMAL INFORMATION CHANNELS

### A. NEWSPAPERS

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE + PROPOSED

**National Newspapers (London):**

**1. The Times:**
- Most prestigious
- **Price:** 7d (includes 4d stamp duty)
- **Circulation:** ~8,000 copies/day
- **Content:** National news, Parliament, foreign affairs, advertisements
- **Bias:** Establishment, Tory-leaning

**2. Morning Chronicle:**
- Whig-leaning
- Similar price, smaller circulation

**3. Radical Press:**
- **Cobbett’s Political Register** — radical, pro-reform (2d — cheaper)
- **Black Dwarf, Republican** — underground radical papers
- **Government suppression** (seditious libel prosecutions)

**Alderwick Newspaper Access (PROPOSED):**

**Who reads newspapers?**
- **Gentry:** Subscribe to London papers (arrive 1-3 days late)
- **Middle class:** Share copies at coffee house, reading room
- **Working class:** Cannot afford (7d = half-day’s wages)
- **Radicals:** Pass around Cobbett (2d, affordable for pooled purchase)

**Local Newspaper (PROPOSED):**
- **Does Alderwick have local paper?** UNKNOWN
- If yes: Weekly (not daily), covers county news, local advertisements
- If no: Nearest paper in county town (20-40 miles away)

**Newspaper Content:**
- **National news:** War, politics, Royal family
- **Crime reports:** Assize trials, executions (moral lessons)
- **Advertisements:** Sales, auctions, wanted notices
- **NO local gossip** (too sensitive for print)

**Plot Implications:**
- **Leon reads newspapers** (stays informed on national events)
- **Advertisement as clue** (wanted notice, property sale)
- **Crime report** (similar crime elsewhere, pattern)
- **Radical paper** (found in suspect’s possession = evidence of politics)
- **Information delay** (Leon learns of national event days late)
- **Class information gap** (gentry knows news; poor does not)

### B. LETTERS (POSTAL SYSTEM)

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Royal Mail:**
- **Mail coaches** run daily (if Alderwick on route)
- **Speed:** 8-10 mph
- **Reliability:** Generally good (but weather delays)

**Postage:**
- **Recipient pays** (not sender)
- **Cost by distance and pages:**
  - Single sheet, <15 miles: 4d
  - Single sheet, 15-80 miles: 5d
  - Single sheet, 80-150 miles: 6d
  - Single sheet, 150-300 miles: 8d
  - Additional sheets: Double, triple cost
- **Expensive** (working class cannot afford)

**Letter Format:**
- **No envelopes** (fold paper, seal with wax)
- **Cross-writing** (write horizontally, then vertically to save paper)
- **Seals** (wax + signet ring = authentication)
- **Franking** (MPs can send letters free — privilege abused)

**Delivery:**
- **Alderwick:** Mail arrives daily (if on route)
- **Post office or inn** (collect mail there)
- **No home delivery** (except gentry — servant collects)

**Privacy:**
- **Letters can be opened** (government spies read radical correspondence)
- **Seals checked** (broken seal = letter read)

**Plot Implications:**
- **Letter as evidence** (handwriting, seal, content)
- **Forged letter** (common crime)
- **Intercepted letter** (spy reads, reseals)
- **Cost prevents communication** (poor cannot send letters)
- **Timing crucial** (when did letter arrive? send?)
- **Cross-writing hard to read** (clue hidden in double text)

**Plot Implications:**

## SECTION 3 — INFORMATION SPEED & ACCURACY

### A. SPEED BY CHANNEL

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Fastest to Slowest:**

**1. Local Gossip (hours to days):**
- Event happens in Alderwick → gossip spreads within hours (same class)
- Cross-class: 1-2 days
- **Example:** Murder in Market Square → whole town knows by evening

**2. Letters (1-7 days):**
- Alderwick to London: 1-3 days
- Alderwick to nearby county town: 1 day
- Alderwick to distant city: 5-7 days

**3. Newspapers (1-7 days delay):**
- London papers arrive in Alderwick: 1-3 days after publication
- Event happens → reported in paper → Alderwick reads: 3-7 days total

**4. Official Communications (days to weeks):**
- Government order → county magistrate → local magistrate: 1-2 weeks
- Legal summons, court dates: weeks

**Plot Implications:**
- **Leon may learn via gossip before official report**
- **Misinformation spreads faster than truth**
- **Class determines information speed** (gentry gets newspapers; poor gets gossip)

---

### B. ACCURACY BY SOURCE

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Most to Least Accurate:**

**1. Official Documents:**
- Legal records, wills, property deeds
- **BUT:** Can be forged

**2. Direct Witness:**
- Eyewitness testimony
- **BUT:** Memory faulty, bias, lying

**3. Letters:**
- Written record
- **BUT:** Can be forged, writer may lie

**4. Newspapers:**
- Published, edited
- **BUT:** Bias (political slant), errors, propaganda

**5. Professional Reports:**
- Solicitor, physician, clergy
- **BUT:** Professional interest may distort

**6. Gossip:**
- Oral tradition, exaggeration
- **BUT:** Sometimes accurate (people do know their neighbors)

**7. Rumor:**
- Third-hand, distorted
- **Often false or wildly exaggerated**

**Plot Implications:**
- **Leon must verify information** (cross-reference sources)
- **Rumor derails investigation** (false lead)
- **Truth hidden in gossip** (dismissed as rumor, actually accurate)
- **Official document forged** (Leon trusts, betrayed)

## SECTION 5 — RUMORS & MISINFORMATION

### A. HOW RUMORS START

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Common Sources:**

**1. Misunderstanding:**
- Partial information (overheard half a conversation)
- Misinterpretation (innocent act seen as suspicious)

**2. Exaggeration:**
- Storytelling embellishment ("I heard" becomes "I saw")
- Dramatic effect (minor event becomes major scandal)

**3. Malice:**
- Deliberate lie (damage reputation)
- Political slander (discredit opponent)

**4. Fear:**
- Paranoia creates conspiracy theories
- Supernatural explanation for natural events

**5. Class Prejudice:**
- Assumption based on class (poor assumed guilty)
- Stereotype ("gypsies are thieves")

---

### B. RUMOR DYNAMICS

**Status:** PROPOSED

**How Rumors Spread:**

**Phase 1: Origin** (hours)
- Event happens or is claimed
- First person tells second

**Phase 2: Amplification** (days)
- Second tells third, fourth, fifth
- Each retelling adds/changes details
- Reaches critical mass ("everyone knows")

**Phase 3: Solidification** (weeks)
- Rumor becomes "fact" (repeated so often, believed)
- Contradicting rumor = accused of cover-up

**Phase 4: Decline** (weeks to months)
- New rumor replaces old
- Or: Truth emerges, rumor fades
- Or: Rumor becomes legend (never fully disappears)

**Plot Implications:**
- **Leon fights rumor** (town "knows" wrong suspect guilty)
- **Rumor destroys reputation** (innocent person socially ruined)
- **Rumor as weapon** (villain spreads to frame suspect)
- **Leon uses rumor** (spreads false info to trap killer)
