## CORE PREMISE

**Religion in 1816 England:**
- **State church:** Church of England (Anglican) = established, privileged
- **Dissenters:** Methodists, Baptists, Quakers, Catholics (marginalized, penalized)
- **Universal nominal Christianity** (atheism = social/legal disqualification)
- **Church = social control** (hierarchy, morality, government support)
- **Class divide:** Gentry rational religion; poor folk belief + supernatural

**Religion shapes:**
- **Social structure** (church seating by class)
- **Political alignment** (Church supports Tory establishment)
- **Moral framework** (sin, virtue, divine justice)
- **Daily life** (Sabbath observance, church attendance)
- **Supernatural beliefs** (official theology vs. folk magic)

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE + PROPOSED

- Assistant to rector/vicar

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

- Emphasize tradition, ritual, hierarchy

- Rationalist, tolerant, undogmatic
- Enlightenment influence
- Less emphasis on doctrine
- **Politics:** Whig-leaning
- **Declining by 1816**

**Morning Prayer:**
- 10-11 AM (or 11 AM-12 PM)
- Book of Common Prayer liturgy
- Sermon (20-40 minutes)
- Psalms, hymns (limited — organs rare in small churches)

**Afternoon/Evening Prayer:**
- Optional second service
- Less attended

- Quarterly (Easter, Whitsun, Michaelmas, Christmas) — NOT weekly

**Working Class:**
- **Irregular** (work demands, lack respectable clothing)
- Back pews or standing
- Many attend Dissenting chapels instead

## SECTION 2 — DISSENTERS (NONCONFORMISTS)

### A. DISSENTING DENOMINATIONS

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE + PROPOSED

**1. Methodists:**
- Founded by John Wesley (1703-1791)
- **Beliefs:** Personal conversion, emotional worship, strict morality, perfectionism
- **Practice:** Class meetings (small groups), preaching circuits, hymn-singing
- **Social base:** Working class, some middle class
- **Growth:** Rapid in early 19th century
- **Politics:** Varies (some radical, some conservative)

**2. Baptists:**
- Adult baptism by immersion (reject infant baptism)
- **Beliefs:** Congregational governance, Bible authority
- **Social base:** Artisans, small farmers, tradesmen
- **Politics:** Often radical (support reform)

**3. Congregationalists (Independents):**
- Each congregation autonomous
- **Beliefs:** Calvinist theology, congregational polity
- **Social base:** Middle class (wealthier Dissenters)
- **Politics:** Whig, reformist

**4. Quakers (Society of Friends):**
- Silent worship, inner light, pacifism
- Reject sacraments, clergy, oaths
- **Social base:** Merchants, professionals (prosperous)
- **Politics:** Reformist (anti-slavery, prison reform)
- **Peculiarities:** Plain dress, "thee/thou" speech, refuse to doff hat

**5. Catholics:**
- **Legally marginalized** (Penal Laws still in effect 1816)
- Cannot vote, hold office, attend university
- Small minority (mostly Irish immigrants, some old gentry families)
- **Emancipation:** Not until 1829

### B. DISSENTER DISABILITIES

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Legal Penalties:**

**Test Acts (1673, 1678):**
- Must take Anglican communion to hold civil/military office
- **Dissenters excluded** from magistracy, Parliament, military commissions
- **Corporation Act** excludes from town councils

**Toleration Act (1689):**
- Allows Dissenting worship (with registration)
- **BUT:** Dissenters still cannot hold office, attend Oxford/Cambridge

**Church Rates:**
- **Forced to pay** for Anglican church upkeep
- Major grievance

**Marriage, Burial:**
- **Must marry in Anglican church** (or marriage invalid)
- **Burial:** Dissenters often denied Anglican churchyard burial

**Social Penalties:**
- **Stigma:** "Enthusiasts," lower-class association
- **Economic discrimination:** Gentry avoid Dissenting tradesmen
- **Social exclusion:** Not invited to gentry events

## SECTION 4 — SUPERNATURAL BELIEFS

### A. OFFICIAL CHURCH POSITION

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Anglican Theology (1816):**

**1. Accepts:**
- **Miracles** (Biblical — Jesus, apostles)
- **Angels, demons** (exist but rarely manifest)
- **Divine providence** (God acts in world)
- **Heaven, Hell, Purgatory (debated)**

**2. Rejects:**
- **Witchcraft** (Witchcraft Act repealed 1736 — witches don’t exist)
- **Magic** (superstition, fraud, or delusion)
- **Ghosts** (officially — souls in heaven/hell, not wandering)
- **BUT:** Some Anglican clergy believe in ghosts (haunted vicarages)

**3. Ambiguous:**
- **Possession** (madness? or demonic?)
- **Visions** (divine inspiration? or madness?)
- **Miracles today** (ceased after apostolic age? or rare?)

**Exorcism:**
- **Not part of Anglican practice** (1604 Canon forbids except with bishop permission)
- **Catholic practice** (Anglicans reject as superstition)
- **BUT:** Some clergy perform informal prayers against evil

### B. FOLK SUPERNATURAL BELIEFS

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Working-class rural England believes in:**

**1. Ghosts:**
- **Unquiet dead** (murder victims, suicides, unfinished business)
- **Haunt specific places** (scene of death, burial site, former home)
- **Visible, audible** (appear, speak, move objects)
- **Can be laid** (Christian burial, resolve grievance, exorcism)

**2. Witchcraft:**
- **Curses cause harm** (illness, death, crop failure, livestock sickness)
- **Witch = person with power** (usually old woman, outsider)
- **Counter-magic exists** (cunning folk, charms, counter-curses)
- **Physical evidence** (witch mark, familiar animal)

**3. Fairies:**
- **Diminishing belief by 1816** (but still present rurally)
- **Dangerous, capricious** (steal children, curse farms)
- **Appease with offerings**

**4. Omens:**
- **Animals** (ravens, black cats, howling dogs)
- **Events** (comets, eclipses, strange weather)
- **Dreams** (prophetic, warning)

**5. Cunning Folk:**
- **Wise women, cunning men** (magical practitioners)
- **Services:** Healing, find lost items, counter curses, love charms, identify witches
- **Not witches** ("white magic" vs. "black magic")
- **Trusted more than doctors** (cheaper, familiar)

### C. DEATH & BURIAL

**Social Significance:**
