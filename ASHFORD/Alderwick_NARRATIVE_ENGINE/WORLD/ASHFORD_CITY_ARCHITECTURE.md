- County magistrate jurisdiction
- Parish vestry (local church-based governance)

- Market charters (economic privileges only)

## SECTION 2 — LOCAL GOVERNING STRUCTURES

### A. MAGISTRATES (JUSTICES OF THE PEACE)

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Definition:**
Magistrates (Justices of the Peace, "JPs") are **unpaid local gentry** appointed by the Crown to administer justice and local government at county level.

**Qualifications (1816):**
- Must be **landed gentry** with property income of **£100+ per year**
- Appointed by **Lord Chancellor** via **Commission of the Peace**
- Usually hold office for life unless removed for misconduct
- NO legal training required (most are amateurs)

**Powers:**
1. **Judicial:**
   - Try petty offenses (theft, assault, vagrancy, poaching)
   - Commit serious offenders to county assize courts for trial
   - Issue warrants for arrest
   - Bind over individuals to keep the peace

2. **Administrative:**
   - Supervise poor relief (oversee parish overseers)
   - License alehouses and inns
   - Regulate weights and measures in markets
   - Enforce game laws and other statutes
   - Oversee highways and bridges
   - Supervise prisons and houses of correction

### B. PARISH VESTRY

**Status:** PROPOSED

**Definition:**
The **parish vestry** is the local governing body based on **St. Bartholomew''s Church parish**. It handles poor relief, church maintenance, and some local administration.

**Structure (1816):**
Two types of vestry exist in England:
1. **Open Vestry:** All male ratepayers can attend and vote
2. **Select Vestry:** Self-selecting oligarchy of wealthiest parishioners (common in larger parishes)

**PROPOSED for Alderwick: SELECT VESTRY**

Why? A select vestry fits Alderwick''s oligarchic power structure and creates better narrative tension (exclusion, corruption, class conflict).

**Vestry Membership:**
- **12-20 members** (wealthiest male ratepayers)
- **Dominated by:** Landed gentry, wealthy merchants, professionals (solicitor, apothecary, large shopkeepers)
- **Excludes:** Artisans, laborers, women, the poor
- **Chairman:** Often the **senior magistrate** or largest landowner
- **Clerk:** Parish clerk (also church clerk)

**Vestry Powers:**
1. **Poor Relief:**
   - Appoint overseers of the poor
   - Set poor rates (local property tax for poor relief)
   - Decide who receives relief and how much
   - Operate workhouse (if Alderwick has one — UNKNOWN)
   - "Remove" paupers to their parish of settlement (if not born in Alderwick)

2. **Church Maintenance:**
   - Maintain St. Bartholomew''s Church building
   - Pay church clerk, sexton, bellringers
   - Manage church property and endowments

3. **Local Administration:**
   - Appoint parish constable
   - Maintain some roads and bridges (shared with county)
   - Regulate parish affairs (burials, church rates)

**Vestry Meetings:**
- Held in **St. Bartholomew''s Church** or **Market Hall**
- **Frequency:** Monthly or quarterly
- **Open to public observation?** Technically yes, but intimidating for non-members

**Plot Implications:**
- Vestry controls **who receives poor relief** — life-or-death power over the poor
- Vestry sets **poor rates** — can burden middle-class ratepayers
- Vestry membership = **social prestige** and **local power**
- Exclusion from vestry = **political powerlessness**
- Corrupt vestries can embezzle poor relief funds
- Reformist pressure: calls for open vestries, democratic governance

## SECTION 4 — TAXATION & FINANCE

### A. LOCAL TAXES (PROPOSED)

**1816 English Local Taxation:**

**Poor Rate:**
- Property tax levied by parish vestry
- Funds poor relief (workhouse, outdoor relief, medical care for paupers)
- Assessed on **annual rental value** of property
- Paid by **property owners** (not tenants, unless lease specifies)
- Rate varies by parish needs (typically 1-3 shillings per pound of rental value)

**Church Rate:**
- Property tax for church maintenance
- Assessed similarly to poor rate
- Controversial (Dissenters object to funding Anglican church)

**Highway Rate:**
- Property tax for road maintenance (sometimes combined with poor rate)

**County Rate:**
- Property tax for county expenses (prisons, bridges, county administration)
- Collected by county, not parish

**Alderwick''s Tax Burden (PROPOSED):**

**Poor Rate:** 2 shillings per pound of annual rental value (HIGH — reflects 1816 economic distress)

**Example:**
- Property with £20 annual rental value pays £2 poor rate per year
- Property with £100 annual rental value pays £10 poor rate per year

**Church Rate:** 6 pence per pound of annual rental value

**Total Local Tax:** ~2.5 shillings per pound (~12.5% of rental value)

**Plot Implications:**
- **Tax burden** — middle-class property owners resent high poor rates
- **Vestry conflict** — disputes over setting rates, allocating funds
- **Tax resistance** — some refuse to pay church rates (Dissenters, radicals)
- **Assessment disputes** — arguments over property valuations

## SECTION 5 — COUNTY RELATIONSHIP

### A. QUARTER SESSIONS

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE + PROPOSED

**Definition:**
**Quarter Sessions** are county-level courts held **four times per year** where all county magistrates meet.

**Location:** County town (NOT Alderwick)

**Functions:**
1. **Judicial:**
   - Try serious offenses (felonies below capital level)
   - Hear appeals from petty sessions
   - Sentence offenders to transportation, imprisonment, whipping

2. **Administrative:**
   - Set county rate (county tax)
   - Supervise county prisons, bridges, lunatic asylums
   - Issue licenses for alehouses, gamekeepers, etc.
   - Coordinate magistrate activities across county

**Alderwick''s Relationship:**
- **Alderwick magistrates attend** Quarter Sessions (travel to county town)
- **Serious Alderwick crimes** are tried at Quarter Sessions
- **Alderwick must comply** with county-level regulations

**Plot Implications:**
- **Distance from county authority** — Alderwick operates semi-autonomously between sessions
- **Appeal mechanism** — victims of Alderwick magistrates can appeal to Quarter Sessions
- **County oversight** — corrupt or incompetent Alderwick magistrates can be removed by county

### B. ASSIZE COURTS

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Definition:**
**Assize Courts** are royal courts where **circuit judges** travel from London to try **capital felonies** (murder, burglary, rape, forgery, etc.).

**Frequency:** Twice per year per county

**Location:** County town

**Alderwick''s Relationship:**
- **Capital crimes in Alderwick** are tried at Assizes
- **Alderwick magistrates** commit suspects to county jail to await Assize trial
- **Transportation to Australia** or **execution** are possible sentences

**Plot Implications:**
