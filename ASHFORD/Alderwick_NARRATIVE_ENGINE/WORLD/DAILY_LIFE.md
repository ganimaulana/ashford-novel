# DAILY LIFE — Alderwick 1816

**Status:** 📙 PROPOSED | **Created:** 2026-08-29
**Purpose:** Define daily routines, food, clothing, housing, hygiene, technology, and material culture in 1816 Alderwick
**Principle:** CONCRETE DETAILS make world feel real. Every object, meal, routine tells story of class and period.

## CORE PREMISE

**1816 life is:**
- **Pre-industrial** (no electricity, no engines, no modern plumbing)
- **Class-divided** (gentry and poor live in different worlds)
- **Seasonal** (weather, daylight dictate life)
- **Slow** (travel, communication, information all slow)
- **Physical** (manual labor, no machines)

### B. MEDICAL CARE

**Status:** HISTORICAL_REFERENCE

**Medical Practitioners:**

**1. Physicians:**
- University-educated (gentlemen)
- Diagnose, prescribe (do not touch patients)
- Expensive (£1-5 per visit)
- Theory-based (humors, miasma)

**2. Surgeons:**
- Manual practitioners (originally barbers)
- Surgery, bloodletting, bone-setting
- Less prestigious than physicians
- Moderate cost (10s-£2 per procedure)

**3. Apothecaries:**
- Prepare/sell medicines
- Often diagnose/treat (cheaper than physicians)
- Common for working/middle class
- Low cost (3-10s per treatment)

**4. Midwives:**
- Attend births
- Female, traditional knowledge
- Essential (male doctors rare for childbirth)

**5. Folk healers:**
- Herbalists, wise women, cunning folk
- Working-class healthcare
- Blend medicine and magic

**Leon’s Position (PROPOSED):**
- Medical training (physician or surgeon?)
- Unusual for gentry (hands-on medicine = "trade")
- Expertise useful for investigations
- **UNKNOWN:** Does Leon practice medicine formally?

**Treatments:**
- **Bloodletting** (leeches, lancet) — standard for most illnesses
- **Purging** (laxatives, emetics) — "cleanse" body
- **Herbal remedies** (opium, laudanum, mercury, digitalis)
- **Surgery** (amputations, trepanning) — no anesthesia (until 1840s), no antiseptics

**Common Diseases:**
- **Typhus** (louse-borne, kills rapidly)
- **Tuberculosis** ("consumption" — slow death)
- **Cholera** (waterborne, epidemic)
- **Smallpox** (vaccination available 1796, but not universal)
- **Influenza, pneumonia** (deadly, especially winter)
- **Childbirth fever** (kills many women)

**Life Expectancy:**
- **Gentry:** 50-60 years (if survive childhood)
- **Working class:** 35-45 years
- **Infant mortality:** 30-40% die before age 5

**Plot Implications:**

- **Disease outbreak** (cholera, typhus epidemic)
- **Medical malpractice** (quack doctor, wrong treatment)
- **Childbirth death** (common tragedy)
- **Poison** (hard to distinguish from natural illness)

**1816 = "Year Without a Summer":**
- **Cold, wet summer** (crop failure)
- **Early frost** (September)
- **Long, harsh winter**

**Seasonal Impact:**

**Winter (Dec-Feb):**
- **Short days** (dark by 4 PM)
- **Cold** (indoor temps near freezing)
- **Limited travel** (snow, ice, mud)
- **Food scarcity** (stored food only)
- **High mortality** (cold, disease)

**Spring (Mar-May):**
- **Mud season** (roads impassable)
- **"Hungry gap"** (last year’s food gone, new harvest not ready)
- **Planting season** (agricultural labor)

**Summer (Jun-Aug):**
- **1816: Cold, wet** (abnormal)
- **Normally:** Warmth, long days, outdoor work
- **Harvest anxiety** (will crops ripen?)

**Autumn (Sep-Nov):**
- **Harvest** (make or break for year)
- **1816: Failure** (early frost, poor yield)
- **Preparations for winter** (preserve food, store fuel)

**Plot Implications:**
