# Social Reputation — Ashford, 1816

**Status:** PROPOSED — to be confirmed as canon after review.

## Overview

Reputation is paramount in 1816 Ashford. It determines trust, employment, marriage prospects, and social standing. A bad reputation can destroy a family's prospects.

---

## Components of Reputation

### Family Reputation
- **Importance:** A family's history, wealth, and respectability are passed down.
- **Effect:** A family with a scandalous past may be shunned; a noble family commands respect.

### Occupational Reputation
- **Importance:** A person's trade or profession affects their standing.
- **Effect:** Doctors and clergy are highly respected; laborers may have little standing; some trades (e.g., those associated with gambling or illicit goods) have low reputation.

### Religious Reputation
- **Importance:** Piety and adherence to religious practice are valued.
- **Effect:** A member of the New Light is seen as pious but perhaps severe; an irreligious person is distrusted.

### Political Reputation
- **Importance:** Political views can affect reputation; radicalism is seen as dangerous.
- **Effect:** A reformer may be admired by some but suspect to others; a Tory is seen as establishment.

### Financial Reputation
- **Importance:** Honest dealing and ability to pay debts are crucial.
- **Effect:** Bankruptcy ruins a reputation; a person known for paying debts is trusted.

### Romantic Reputation
- **Importance:** Chastity is highly valued, especially for women.
- **Effect:** A scandal (e.g., an illegitimate child, adultery) can destroy a woman's reputation and limit her marriage prospects.

### Criminal Suspicion
- **Importance:** Being suspected of a crime destroys one's reputation.
- **Effect:** Even unproven suspicion can lead to shunning and loss of employment.

### Military Reputation
- **Importance:** Military service can bring honor or disgrace.
- **Effect:** A decorated officer is respected; a soldier who deserted is despised.

### Academic Reputation
- **Importance:** Learning and intelligence are respected.
- **Effect:** A scholar is admired; a person known for ignorance is dismissed.

---

## Consequences of Reputation

- **Trust:** People with good reputation are trusted; those with bad reputation are distrusted.
- **Employment:** Reputation affects hiring; a person with a bad reputation may not find work.
- **Marriage:** A good reputation is essential for a suitable marriage.
- **Access:** Reputation determines who you can socialize with and what clubs or institutions you can join.
- **Information:** A trusted person is more likely to be told secrets.
- **Political Influence:** Reputation can make or break a political career.

---

## Reputation System

- **Public Knowledge:** Reputation is known through gossip and public observation.
- **Rumors:** A rumor can quickly damage a reputation, even if false.
- **Restoration:** Regaining reputation is difficult; some never recover.
