# Clothing and Appearance — Ashford, 1816

**Status:** PROPOSED — to be confirmed as canon after review.

## Overview

Clothing in 1816 Ashford is a clear marker of class, occupation, gender, age, and occasion. It is also a form of communication, signaling status, respectability, and personal identity.

---

## Class and Occupation

### Upper Class
- **Men:** Tailored coats (frock coats or tailcoats), breeches or trousers, high collars, cravats, boots or pumps, top hat or beaver hat, walking stick or cane, gloves.
- **Women:** Gowns with high waistlines (Regency style), bonnets, shawls, gloves, long skirts, elaborate hairstyles.

### Middle Class
- **Men:** Similar to upper class but less expensive fabrics; simpler cravats; boots.
- **Women:** Neat gowns, bonnets, shawls; less jewelry.

### Working Class
- **Men:** Woollen or linen shirts, breeches or trousers (often patched), waistcoats (sometimes), frock coats (if worn), boots or clogs, flat cap.
- **Women:** Plain gowns, aprons, shawls, bonnets or headscarves.

### Servants/Employees
- **Domestic Servants:** Uniforms (livery) for male servants; plain dresses and aprons for maids.
- **Laborers:** Sturdy, durable clothing for work.

---

## Gender Differences

- **Men:** Breeches or trousers; tailored coats; high collars; hats; no skirts.
- **Women:** Gowns, long skirts; bonnets; shawls; stays (corsets) for shaping.

---

## Age

- **Children:** Similar styles to adults but simpler; boys in trousers, girls in dresses; children often wear less formal clothing.
- **Youth:** Coming-of-age brings a change in clothing; boys may get their first suit; girls may get their first adult dress.

---

## Season and Weather

- **Winter:** Heavy wool, overcoats, cloaks, fur, gloves, scarves, thick boots.
- **Summer:** Lighter fabrics (cotton, linen), lighter colors; bonnets for sun protection.
- **Rain:** Umbrellas (becoming more common), oilskin capes, clogs.

---

## Occasion

- **Formal:** Ball gowns, formal suits, full dress; jewelry; wigs (still worn in some formal settings).
- **Casual:** Country clothing, hunting attire; simpler gowns.
- **Work:** Practical clothing, aprons, protective garments.

---

## Mourning

- **Widows:** Black clothing, often a dress, shawl, and bonnet; may wear a veil; period of mourning can last up to two years.
- **Other relatives:** Black armbands, black clothing for a time; less extreme for more distant relatives.

---

## Clothing as Communication

- **Status:** Wealth and rank are signaled by fabric, cut, and accessories.
- **Occupation:** Uniforms or specialized clothing indicate a person's job.
- **Identity:** Clothing choices can signal political allegiance, religious affiliation, or personal taste.
- **Mood:** In a less formal setting, clothing can reflect mood (e.g., a woman might wear a brighter gown if she is happy).
