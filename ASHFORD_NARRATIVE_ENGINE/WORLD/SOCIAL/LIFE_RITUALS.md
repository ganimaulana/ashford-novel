# Life Rituals — Ashford, 1816

**Status:** PROPOSED — to be confirmed as canon after review.

## Overview

Life in Ashford is marked by a series of rituals that accompany birth, coming of age, marriage, death, and inheritance. These rituals vary by class and religious tradition, but they provide a structure for community involvement and social status.

---

## Birth

### Old Faith
- **Rituals:** Baptism within a few weeks of birth; naming; godparents chosen.
- **Class Differences:** Upper classes may have elaborate christenings with large gatherings; working-class families hold smaller ceremonies.
- **Superstitions:** Some folk traditions may involve "charming" the baby to protect against the evil eye.

### New Light
- **Rituals:** A dedication or blessing of the child, often in a private service; naming; no godparents.
- **Class Differences:** Simple, focused on prayer.

### Unaffiliated
- **Rituals:** May attend a church service for baptism or simply have a naming ceremony at home.

---

## Coming of Age

- **For boys:** Entry into apprenticeship, university, or the family business. Often marked by a gift (e.g., a horse, a watch) and a change in clothing (from boy's to man's attire).
- **For girls:** First social season, debutante ball (for upper class), or the age when they are considered eligible for marriage (about 16–18). They may begin to attend balls and parties.

## Marriage

- **Old Faith:** Church ceremony; banns read or license obtained; vows, ring exchange, communion; wedding feast.
- **New Light:** Simple ceremony, sometimes in a meeting house; vows; minimal ritual; often no ring; a modest celebration.
- **Class Differences:** Upper-class weddings are large, expensive, and socially significant; working-class weddings are simpler, but still a community event.

---

## Pregnancy and Childbirth

- **Midwives:** Home birth is common; a midwife attends.
- **Superstitions:** Beliefs about avoiding unlucky foods, wearing protective charms, etc.
- **Support:** Women of the family and community support the mother.
- **Mortality:** High; pregnancy and childbirth are dangerous.

---

## Illness and Death

- **Old Faith:** The vicar visits the dying; last rites (if the Church of England uses them); a funeral service with prayers for the dead; burial in the churchyard.
- **New Light:** Prayers with the dying; a funeral sermon; burial in a separate burying ground, if available.
- **Class Differences:** The wealthy may have a grand funeral with a procession; the poor may have a simple burial paid for by the parish.
- **Mourning:** Widows and widowers wear black; mourning clothes may last a year or more; social restrictions apply.

---

## Funeral

### Old Faith
- **Rituals:** Service in the church; procession to the churchyard; committal prayers; burial.
- **Public Behavior:** Mourning dress; black armbands for men; weeping and display of grief.
- **Private Behavior:** In private, family may gather for a wake (in some regions); food and drink are shared.

### New Light
- **Rituals:** Simple service; sermon; burial; no prayers for the dead.
- **Public Behavior:** Modest mourning; less display of grief.

### Unaffiliated
- **Rituals:** Mixed; may follow church custom or local folk tradition.

---

## Inheritance

- **Legal:** Wills are important; property and money are passed down; the eldest son often inherits the estate (primogeniture).
- **Emotional:** Inheritances can cause family disputes, resentment, and secrecy.
- **Plot Potential:** A contested will; a hidden heir; a fortune that leads to murder.
