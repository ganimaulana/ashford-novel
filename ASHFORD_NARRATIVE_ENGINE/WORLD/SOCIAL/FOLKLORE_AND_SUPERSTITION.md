# Folklore and Superstition — Ashford, 1816

**Status:** PROPOSED — to be confirmed as canon after review.

## Overview

Folklore and superstition are deeply embedded in Ashford's rural culture. They provide explanations for the unexplained, guidance for everyday decisions, and a sense of connection to the past. While the educated classes may dismiss them as peasant superstition, many people, even among the gentry, hold to some folk beliefs.

---

## Local Superstitions

### 1. The Evil Eye
- **Belief:** Some people have the power to curse others with a malevolent look.
- **Protection:** Wear an amulet (a small bag of herbs, a piece of iron, a cross); touch wood; make a sign of the cross.

### 2. Omens
- **Sneezing:** A sneeze before breakfast is a bad omen.
- **Breaking a mirror:** Seven years' bad luck.
- **Spilling salt:** Bad luck; throw a pinch over the left shoulder to cancel it.
- **Birds:** A bird flying into a window is a death omen.
- **The New Moon:** If you see the new moon through glass, it's bad luck; turn your money over for good luck.

### 3. Cunning Folk
- **Role:** Wise women or men who use herbs, charms, and rituals to heal, protect, and identify thieves.
- **Status:** Respected and feared; sometimes consulted by clergy when all else fails.
- **Methods:** Herbal remedies, divination, incantations, "pointing" for thieves.
- **Plot Potential:** A cunning woman who knows a secret; a patient who dies mysteriously after treatment.

### 4. Ghosts and Spirits
- **Belief:** Many believe in ghosts; they are often seen as restless spirits seeking justice or completion.
- **Haunted Places:** The old manor house, the churchyard, crossroads, wells, and bridges.
- **Protection:** Iron horseshoes, rowan branches, salt.
- **Plot Potential:** A ghost that appears before a murder; a character who claims to be haunted.

### 5. Seasonal Rituals
- **May Day:** Garlanding, maypole dancing; seen as a fertility ritual.
- **Midsummer:** Bonfires, collecting herbs, fortune-telling; associated with magic.
- **Harvest Home:** Celebrations that include rituals for the last sheaf of corn.
- **Halloween (Samhain):** The veil between worlds is thin; spirits walk; fortune-telling.

---

## Distinction from Supernatural Fact

- **Known Tradition:** Folkloric beliefs that are widely held and transmitted.
- **Local Superstition:** A belief specific to Ashford or the region.
- **Rumor:** A belief that is not widely accepted but circulates.
- **Misinterpretation:** A natural phenomenon mistakenly attributed to magic.
- **Actual Supernatural Fact:** Events or entities that are genuinely supernatural within the story.

---

## Mystery Integration

- **False Leads:** A superstition can lead a character to suspect the supernatural when the truth is mundane.
- **Clue:** A superstition may hint at a real event or provide a clue (e.g., a charm left at a crime scene).
- **Misdirection:** A character may exploit a superstition to mislead.
- **Atmosphere:** Folklore can create a sense of dread or mystery.
