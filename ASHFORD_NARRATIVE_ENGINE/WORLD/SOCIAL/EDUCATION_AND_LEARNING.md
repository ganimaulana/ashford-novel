# Education and Learning — Ashford, 1816

**Status:** PROPOSED — to be confirmed as canon after review.

## Overview

Education in 1816 Ashford is stratified by class and gender, with a growing emphasis on literacy and practical skills for the working classes. The established church and dissenting groups both run schools.

---

## Childhood Education

- **Home:** Basic literacy and numeracy taught at home, especially among the middle and upper classes.
- **Dame Schools:** Private schools run by women; provide basic literacy for young children; affordable for some working-class families.
- **Parish Schools:** Run by the church; provide reading, writing, and religious instruction; often free or low-cost.
- **Sunday Schools:** Run by churches (Old Faith and New Light); teach reading, writing, and religion; very popular for working-class children.

---

## Secondary Education

### Grammar Schools
- **Availability:** Endowed schools; some offer scholarships.
- **Curriculum:** Latin, Greek, classics, mathematics; often university preparation.
- **Access:** Middle and upper class boys; some girls may attend if available.

### Academies
- **Availability:** Private schools; can be for boys or girls.
- **Curriculum:** Modern languages, literature, history, mathematics, science (for boys); etiquette, music, art, languages (for girls).
- **Access:** Middle and upper class.

---

## Higher Education

- **Universities:** Oxford and Cambridge; restricted to men; often require a classical education.
- **Other:** The "Academy" in Ashford (canon) may provide advanced instruction in classics, philosophy, and science.

---

## Apprenticeships

- **Availability:** For boys; also for some girls in trades.
- **Process:** A contract between the apprentice, master, and family; the apprentice learns a trade; often paid a small wage.
- **Duration:** Usually 5-7 years.
- **Access:** Working class and some middle class.

---

## Self-Education

- **Books:** Circulating libraries, private collections.
- **Lectures:** Public lectures on science, history, philosophy.
- **Correspondence:** Some people learn through letters and pamphlets.
- **Debating Societies:** Intellectual exchange and self-education.

---

## Class and Gender Access

| Level | Class | Gender | Typical Age |
|-------|-------|--------|-------------|
| Dame School | Working/Middle | Both | 3-7 |
| Parish School | All | Both | 5-12 |
| Sunday School | Working | Both | 5-14 |
| Grammar School | Middle/Upper | Boys | 8-18 |
| Academy | Middle/Upper | Both | 10-18 |
| University | Upper | Men | 18-21+ |
| Apprenticeship | Working/Middle | Boys (some girls) | 14-21 |

---

## Effects of Education

- **Vocabulary:** Education expands vocabulary and expressive ability.
- **Employment:** Literacy and numeracy improve job prospects.
- **Status:** Education confers social status.
- **Confidence:** Knowledge builds confidence in social situations.
- **Worldview:** Education shapes a person's understanding of the world, politics, and religion.
- **Information Access:** Educated people have access to more information networks.
