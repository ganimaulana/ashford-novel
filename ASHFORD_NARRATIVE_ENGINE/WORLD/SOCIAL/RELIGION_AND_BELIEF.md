# Religion & Belief in Ashford — 1816

**Status:** PROPOSED — to be confirmed as canon after review.

## Overview

Ashford's religious landscape is inspired by early-19th-century English society but uses fictional names for in-universe traditions. The main traditions are the Old Faith, the New Light, and unaffiliated folk religion.

---

## 1. The Old Faith (Iman Lama)

- **Worldview:** Tradition, community covenant, divine order revealed through Scripture and history.
- **Central Beliefs:** God is sovereign; salvation through faith and good works; the community is a covenant body.
- **Moral Values:** Honesty, humility, charity, obedience to lawful authority.
- **Death:** Judgment; prayers for the dead offered.
- **Charity:** A sacred duty.
- **Rituals:** Sunday worship, daily prayer, baptism (infant), marriage, funeral, harvest thanksgiving, penance.
- **Holy Days:** Christmas, Easter, Ascension, Whitsuntide, All Saints', Rogation Days.
- **Clergy:** Vicar, curate, churchwardens, sexton.
- **Architecture:** Stone churches with spires, raised pulpit, communion table.
- **Symbols:** Cross, Bible, chalice.
- **Texts:** King James Bible, Book of Common Prayer.
- **Class Distribution:** Gentry, farmers, many middle-class.
- **Social/Political Influence:** Supports establishment; moral authority; records births/marriages/deaths.
- **Internal Tensions:** High Church vs Low Church; traditional vs evangelical.
- **Supernatural Relevance:** Church can confront supernatural threats via prayer, blessing, rare exorcism.

---

## 2. The New Light (Cahaya Baru)

- **Worldview:** Personal conversion, sole authority of Scripture, egalitarian congregation.
- **Central Beliefs:** Salvation by grace through faith; every believer a priest; conversion is personal and transformative.
- **Moral Values:** Sobriety, honesty, industry, charity, separation from worldly pleasures.
- **Death:** Immediate presence with God; simple funeral.
- **Charity:** Sign of grace; emphasis on moral reform.
- **Rituals:** Sunday preaching, prayer meetings, believer's baptism (adult immersion), marriage, simple funeral.
- **Holy Days:** Christmas and Easter observed in simplified form.
- **Clergy:** Minister (elected), elders, deacons.
- **Architecture:** Plain meeting houses, no stained glass, simple pulpit.
- **Symbols:** Bible, dove.
- **Texts:** Bible, sermons, hymns.
- **Class Distribution:** Merchants, artisans, shopkeepers, some laborers.
- **Social/Political Influence:** Emphasizes education and sobriety; often supports moderate reform.
- **Internal Tensions:** Doctrine (Calvinism vs Arminianism), worship style, role of women.
- **Supernatural Relevance:** Generally skeptical of supernatural claims.

---

## 3. Folk Religion / Unaffiliated

- **Worldview:** Mix of Christian morality, local superstitions, and seasonal customs.
- **Beliefs:** Spirits, ghosts, evil eye, cunning folk (wise women/men), omens, seasonal rituals.
- **Class Distribution:** Working class, many farmers.
- **Supernatural Relevance:** May intersect with genuine supernatural events; often the source of rumors and misinterpretations.

---

## Social Infrastructure

- **Family identity** tied to religious tradition.
- **Marriage** expected within tradition; cross-tradition marriages can cause friction.
- **Funerals** follow tradition-specific rites.
- **Charity** organized by churches.
- **Education** promoted by both traditions.
- **Community trust** built through shared religion.
- **Reputation** enhanced by piety.
- **Class relations** shaped by religious affiliation.
- **Information networks** operate through church gatherings.
