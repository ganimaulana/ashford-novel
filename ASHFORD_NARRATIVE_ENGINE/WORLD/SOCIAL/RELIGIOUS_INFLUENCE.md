# Religious Influence in Ashford — 1816

**Status:** PROPOSED — to be confirmed as canon after review.

## Overview

Religious institutions and beliefs have significant influence on Ashford's political, social, and moral life. This document tracks the formal and informal power of religious groups.

---

## Formal Authority

### Old Faith (Iman Lama)

- **Legal Status:** The established church, officially recognized by the state.
- **Clergy:** Vicar (appointed by the local squire or a patron); curate; churchwardens.
- **Church Governance:** Bishops and archbishops oversee the church at a national level; locally, the vicar has authority.
- **Records:** Maintains parish registers of births, marriages, and deaths (legal records).
- **Power:** Can influence local magistrates and constables; involved in poor relief (overseers of the poor are often churchwardens).
- **Limitations:** Subject to state authority; cannot openly challenge the monarch or government.

### New Light (Cahaya Baru)

- **Legal Status:** A dissenting congregation, not established by law. By 1816, most legal restrictions have been relaxed, but they still face occasional prejudice.
- **Clergy:** Minister (elected by the congregation); elders; deacons.
- **Church Governance:** Congregational; each congregation is self-governing.
- **Records:** Keeps internal records but not legal registers.
- **Power:** Moral influence; can refuse church membership for immoral behavior; provides mutual support to members.
- **Limitations:** No official role in government or poor relief; must pay tithes to the established church despite not being members.

---

## Moral Authority

Both traditions claim moral authority, but they differ in emphasis.

- **Old Faith:** Emphasizes ordered worship, obedience, and the preservation of social order.
- **New Light:** Emphasizes personal piety, sobriety, and moral reform.

**Moral issues that create conflict:**
- **Sunday observance:** The New Light is stricter; the Old Faith is more lenient.
- **Theatre and dancing:** The New Light tends to condemn them; the Old Faith may permit them if decorous.
- **Gambling:** The New Light condemns it; the Old Faith often tolerates it among the gentry.
- **Drinking:** The New Light abstains or drinks moderately; the Old Faith may see social drinking as acceptable.
- **Charity:** Both traditions value charity, but they may differ on whether charity should be conditional on moral behavior.

---

## Social Influence

### Old Faith

- **Dominant among:** Gentry, farmers, and many middle-class families.
- **Social functions:** The church is the social center for the community; Sunday services, weddings, funerals, and churchyard gatherings are key social events.
- **Gentry influence:** The local squire often has a seat in the church and can influence the vicar's appointment.
- **Information network:** Churchgoers share news and gossip; the vicar often knows about parishioners' private lives.

### New Light

- **Dominant among:** Artisans, merchants, and some farmers.
- **Social functions:** Meeting houses offer community and mutual support; prayer meetings and Bible studies build bonds.
- **Influence:** Can mobilize members for charitable causes and political reform.
- **Information network:** Close-knit network; members are kept informed and can call on each other for help.

---

## Political Influence

- **Old Faith:** Generally supports the existing political order (Tories, or conservative Whigs). May be skeptical of radical reform.
- **New Light:** Often supports moderate reform, e.g., parliamentary reform, repeal of discriminatory laws. Can be critical of government if it violates religious liberty.

**Local politics:**
- The vicar may sit on the vestry, which manages parish affairs.
- Churchwardens oversee poor relief and local taxes.
- Dissenting congregations are involved in petitions and campaigns for reform.

---

## Charitable and Educational Influence

- **Old Faith:** Runs parish schools, provides alms to the poor, supports hospitals and charities.
- **New Light:** Runs Sunday schools, charity schools, and supports mutual aid societies.

**Impact:** Both traditions contribute to literacy, social mobility, and poverty relief. Charitable work also builds social capital and respectability.

---

## Internal Conflicts

- **Old Faith:** Tensions between High Church (ritualistic) and Low Church (evangelical) factions. Also disagreements over the role of the laity.
- **New Light:** Debates over doctrine (e.g., Calvinism vs. Arminianism), worship style, and the role of women in the congregation.

---

## Supernatural Relevance

- **Old Faith:** Some clergy have a role in confronting supernatural threats through prayer, blessing, and exorcism (rare). Church building and consecrated ground are seen as protective.
- **New Light:** Generally skeptical of supernatural claims; views them as superstition or demonic deception.
- **Folk Religion:** The unaffiliated population holds various beliefs in spirits, omens, and cunning folk, which may intersect with supernatural events.
