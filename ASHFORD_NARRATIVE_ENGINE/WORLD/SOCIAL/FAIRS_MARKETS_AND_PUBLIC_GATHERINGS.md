# Fairs, Markets, and Public Gatherings — Ashford, 1816

**Status:** PROPOSED — to be confirmed as canon after review.

## Overview

Fairs and markets are central to Ashford's economic and social life. They are places of trade, entertainment, information exchange, and occasional conflict. They also attract outsiders, making them potential sites for mystery and plot development.

---

## Regular Markets

### Weekly Market — Every Thursday
- **Location:** Market Square.
- **Goods:** Fresh produce (vegetables, meat, eggs, dairy), baked goods, clothing, household items, tools, livestock.
- **Vendors:** Local farmers, traders, craftspeople, occasionally hawkers from nearby towns.
- **Social Function:** Regular meeting place for townspeople and farmers; gossip and news exchange; a place to see and be seen.
- **Class Accessibility:** All classes; gentry may have servants do the shopping, but they may also visit for social reasons.
- **Information Value:** A prime location for rumors, political discussion, and private meetings.

---

## Annual Fairs

### Ashford Spring Fair (Late March)
- **Scale:** Large, attracts visitors from surrounding villages and even London.
- **Goods:** Livestock (cattle, horses, sheep), agricultural tools, clothing, crafts, food.
- **Entertainment:** Puppet shows, music, dancing, games of chance (bets), acrobats, fortune-tellers, public houses overflow.
- **Temporary Businesses:** Stalls, booths, tents; some vendors set up temporary market stalls.
- **Criminal Activity:** Pickpockets, petty theft, confidence tricksters.
- **Political Activity:** Pamphleteers, radical speakers, petitions.
- **Possible Plot Functions:** An outsider arrives with key information; someone disappears in the crowd; a theft or fraud occurs; a romantic meeting; a political conspiracy.

### Ashford Summer Fair (Late July)
- **Scale:** Large, similar to Spring Fair.
- **Goods:** Wool, cloth, agricultural produce, livestock.
- **Entertainment:** Music, dancing, games, horse racing (if space allows), boxing.
- **Temporary Businesses:** Stalls, booths, travelling entertainers.
- **Criminal Activity:** Similar to Spring Fair.
- **Possible Plot Functions:** A marriage proposal; a family dispute; a mysterious stranger; a scandal revealed.

### Michaelmas Fair (29 September)
- **Scale:** Medium to large.
- **Goods:** Livestock, tools, clothing, food.
- **Specialty:** Hiring fair for servants and laborers; also a time for settling debts and renewing contracts.
- **Social Function:** Labor market; a crucial economic event for the working class.
- **Possible Plot Functions:** A servant reveals a secret about a household; a contract dispute leads to violence; a missing person.

### Ashford Autumn Market (Late October)
- **Scale:** Medium.
- **Goods:** Provisions for winter (preserved food, fuel, warm clothing), crafts, tools.
- **Social Function:** Last major market before winter; a time of preparation.
- **Possible Plot Functions:** A suspicious purchase; a hidden message left among goods; a chance encounter.

## Other Public Gatherings

### Hiring Fairs
- **Occurrence:** Often coinciding with Michaelmas or other fairs.
- **Purpose:** Employment for laborers, servants, apprentices.
- **Process:** Workers gather in a designated area (often Market Square); employers inspect them; terms are negotiated; contracts are made.
- **Social Dynamics:** Can be tense; workers compete for positions; employers seek the best labor.
- **Possible Plot Functions:** A worker with a grievance; a false identity; a disappearance.

### Religious Processions
- **Occurrence:** Rogation Days, Good Friday, Easter, possibly other holy days.
- **Purpose:** Prayer, blessing, community display of faith.
- **Public Spaces:** Streets, fields, parish boundaries.
- **Possible Plot Functions:** A boundary dispute; a hidden object uncovered; a political message disguised as religious devotion.

### Civic Ceremonies
- **Occurrence:** Mayoral elections (if applicable), royal proclamations, commemorations.
- **Purpose:** Display of civic power, community unity, celebration.
- **Public Spaces:** Market Square, Town Hall, church.
- **Possible Plot Functions:** A public scandal; a challenge to authority; a hidden agenda.

---

## Social Functions of Fairs and Markets

- **Trade:** Economic exchange; also information exchange (prices, news, rumors).
- **Entertainment:** A break from work; pleasure; courtship.
- **Status Display:** Dressing in best clothes; seeing and being seen; social competition.
- **Information Network:** Gossip flows freely; news travels; political ideas spread.
- **Class Mixing:** People of different classes meet, though class boundaries still exist.
- **Criminal Opportunity:** Theft, fraud, gambling, prostitution.
- **Political Space:** Pamphlets, petitions, radical speeches.
