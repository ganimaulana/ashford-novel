# Reading, Print, and Intellectual Life — Ashford, 1816

**Status:** PROPOSED — to be confirmed as canon after review.

## Overview

Reading and intellectual life are central to the middle and upper classes in Ashford, and increasingly accessible to the working class through chapbooks, Sunday schools, and libraries. Information is a social resource, and access to knowledge confers power.

---

## Newspapers

- **Availability:** Sold by hawkers, in coffeehouses, and by subscription.
- **Content:** Political news, advertisements, local gossip, court reports.
- **Access:** Middle and upper classes; some taverns keep newspapers for patrons.
- **Social Function:** Political engagement, information, debate; also a way to stay connected to London.
- **Possible Plot:** An article that hints at a conspiracy; a letter to the editor with a hidden meaning; a newspaper that carries a coded message.

---

## Books

### Circulating Libraries
- **Availability:** Subscription libraries; a small fee per book or annual subscription.
- **Content:** Novels, travelogues, history, poetry.
- **Access:** Middle and upper classes.
- **Social Function:** Reading for pleasure, education, social status; a place to meet others.
- **Possible Plot:** A book with a hidden inscription; a library that holds a secret.

### Private Libraries
- **Availability:** Wealthy families; may have large book collections.
- **Content:** Rare books, classics, scientific works.
- **Access:** Private, but may be lent to friends.
- **Possible Plot:** A hidden diary; a clue found in a book.

### Chapbooks and Penny Dreadfuls
- **Availability:** Cheap, widely available.
- **Content:** Romance, adventure, supernatural tales.
- **Access:** Working class.
- **Social Function:** Entertainment, escapism, moral tales.
- **Possible Plot:** A chapbook that tells a local story; a clue hidden in a penny dreadful.

---

## Pamphlets and Political Writing

- **Availability:** Sold at markets, distributed at meetings; cheap.
- **Content:** Political reform, religious tracts, scandalous exposes.
- **Access:** All classes.
- **Social Function:** Political mobilization, religious debate, spreading ideas.
- **Possible Plot:** A radical pamphlet that gets someone arrested; a coded message in a political essay.

---

## Intellectual Societies

### Debating Societies
- **Availability:** Subscription and membership.
- **Participants:** Middle and upper class men; often political, philosophical, or literary.
- **Social Function:** Debate, networking, status.
- **Possible Plot:** A debate that turns into a conflict; a secret society meeting.

### Literary Societies
- **Availability:** Similar to debating societies.
- **Participants:** Men and women (sometimes).
- **Social Function:** Reading, discussion, socializing.
- **Possible Plot:** A reading that reveals a hidden message; a poet with a secret.

### Scientific Lectures
- **Availability:** Often open to the public; may require a ticket.
- **Participants:** Middle and upper classes; some working-class curiosity.
- **Social Function:** Education, entertainment, status.
- **Possible Plot:** A lecturer with a secret; a dangerous scientific discovery.

---

## Literacy

- **Class:** Literacy is higher among the middle and upper classes; working-class literacy is lower but growing.
- **Gender:** Men have higher literacy rates; women's literacy is also increasing.
- **Religion:** The New Light emphasizes literacy for Bible reading; the Old Faith also promotes education.
- **Education:** Parish schools and Sunday schools provide basic literacy.
